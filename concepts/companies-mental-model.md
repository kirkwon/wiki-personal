# Companies as a Mental Model

Companies is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world, particularly around how large organizations shape markets, consumer behavior, and economic outcomes.

## Core Idea

Treating "Companies" as a mental model means using the lens of corporate structure, strategy, and competitive dynamics to decode complex systems. This includes analyzing how firms bundle products, control distribution, and leverage network effects to achieve platform dominance.

## Connections to Galloway's Framework

Scott Galloway's work in [[the-four-scott-galloway]] and [[post-corona-from-crisis-to-opportunity]] provides the primary elaboration of this model. Key themes include:

- **The Four titans**: Apple, Amazon, Google, and Meta as dominant platform companies reshaping every industry.
- **Bundling**: Companies increasingly bundle products and services to increase switching costs and lock in consumers.
- **Distribution vs. manufacturing**: In the modern economy, controlling distribution matters more than owning manufacturing.
- **Winner-take-most dynamics**: Platform businesses tend toward monopoly or near-monopoly outcomes.

## Relationship to Other Mental Models

This model overlaps with and connects to [[platform-dominance]], [[markets]], and [[business]] as a mental model. Where [[business]] is a broad lens for navigating complexity, Companies sharpens the focus on how specific corporate strategies and structures drive systemic patterns.
