# Life audit and time tracking

Life audit and time tracking is the practice of systematically improving efficiency and output by auditing how time and attention are spent, tracking allocations, iterating on results, and scaling effective processes.

## Core Cycle

The framework follows a four-step iterative cycle:

1. **Audit** — Identify efficiency losses, track current time and attention allocation, note patterns of waste.
2. **Apply** — Restructure around high-impact activities, eliminate or delegate low-value tasks.
3. **Iterate** — Measure impact of changes, adjust based on results, build consistency through habit formation.
4. **Scale** — Standardize effective approaches, document processes for reuse, teach others to multiply impact.

## Connections to Existing Concepts

This practice extends existing wiki concepts around attention management. [[attention-budget]] treats attention as a finite resource to be allocated deliberately. [[scatterfocus]] describes unfocused, mind-wandering attention mode, while [[hyperfocus]] describes deep concentration—both of which become visible through time tracking. [[quadrant-2-focus]] and [[time-management-matrix]] provide frameworks for prioritizing important over urgent tasks, complementing the audit step.

[[active-revision-techniques]] and [[decision-tracking]] similarly emphasize measuring and adjusting based on evidence rather than intuition alone.
