# Coffee Roasting (咖啡烘焙)

## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[Beverages/Coffee]]
### Type**: Processing Method
### Category**: Heat Development
### Skill Level**: Intermediate to Advanced
### Time Required**: 10-20 minutes per batch
### Best For**: Flavor development, aroma creation, custom profiles
### Key Variables**: Temperature, time, airflow, bean density

## Summary

Coffee roasting transforms green coffee beans through the application of heat, developing the flavors, aromas, and characteristics that define the coffee experience. This complex chemical process creates over 1,000 flavor compounds through Maillard reactions, caramelization, and pyrolysis. Mastering roasting requires understanding bean characteristics, heat application, and development timing to create the perfect balance of sweetness, acidity, and body.

During roasting, amino acids and reducing sugars in the green bean react at temperatures between 285-330°F (140-165°C), producing complex flavor compounds — furans, pyrazines, thiazoles — that define roasted coffee's nutty, caramel, and chocolate notes. As temperatures rise above 355°F (180°C), caramelization of sugars begins, contributing additional sweetness and deeper color.

## Roasting Overview

### Core Principles
- **Heat Application**: Transform green beans through thermal energy
- **Chemical Reactions**: Maillard reactions, caramelization, pyrolysis
- **Development Control**: Time-temperature relationship crucial
- **Bean Characteristics**: Different beans require different profiles
- **Quality Preservation**: Maintain inherent qualities while developing flavor

### Roasting Stages
```yaml
Pre-Roast:
- Green Bean State: Raw, grassy, no developed flavor
- Moisture Content: 10-12%
- Color: Green to yellowish
- Chemical State: Chlorogenic acids intact
- Objective: Even heat application to start development

Development:
- First Crack: Beans expand, pop loudly
- Chemical Changes: Sugars caramelize, acids break down
- Color Change: Yellow to light brown
- Development Level: Light to medium roasts possible

Post-Crack:
- Second Crack: Subtle snaps, faster development
- Chemical Changes: Complex compounds form
- Color Change: Medium to dark brown
- Development Level: Dark roasts approaching

End of Roast:
- Target Temperature: Varies by roast level
- Development Complete: Desired flavor profile achieved
- Cooling Phase: Rapid cooling stops development
- Final Product: Roasted coffee beans
```

## Flavor Development

The Maillard reaction is the primary mechanism for flavor development during the early and middle stages of roasting. Amino acids and reducing sugars in the green bean react at temperatures between 285-330°F (140-165°C), producing complex flavor compounds — furans, pyrazines, thiazoles — that define roasted coffee's nutty, caramel, and chocolate notes. As temperatures rise above 355°F (180°C), caramelization of sugars begins, contributing additional sweetness and deeper color.

## Roasting Process

### Step-by-Step Process
```yaml
1. Preparation:
   - Pre-heat roaster to desired starting temperature
   - Measure green beans accurately
   - Set up cooling system
   - Prepare for recording development

2. Charging:
   - Add beans to pre-heated roaster
   - Monitor first minute of roast
   - Record drop temperature
   - Note initial bean behavior

3. Development:
   - Monitor bean temperature continuously
   - Listen for first crack
   - Record development timing
   - Adjust heat and airflow as needed

4. End Roast:
   - Target temperature reached
   - Cooling process initiated
   - Record final temperatures and times
   - Evaluate cup quality

5. Cooling:
   - Rapid cooling to stop development
   - Rest period before packaging
   - Quality assessment
   - Storage in airtight containers
```

### Temperature Control
```yaml
Roast Profile Phases:
- Drying Phase: 150-200°C (302-392°F)
- Maillard Phase: 180-220°C (356-428°F)
- Development Phase: 190-230°C (374-446°F)
- Final Drop: Varies by roast level

Temperature Monitoring:
- Bean Temperature: Internal bean temperature
- Air Temperature: Ambient roaster temperature
- Rate of Rise: How fast temperature increases
- Development Time: Total roast duration
- Critical Points: First crack, second crack

Temperature Effects:
- Low Temperature: Slow development, smooth
- High Temperature: Fast development, risk of scorching
- Consistency: Even heating essential
- Control: Ability to adjust during roast
```

### Airflow Control
```yaml
Airflow Functions:
- Heat Transfer: Moving heat to beans
- Moisture Removal: Drying phase importance
- Chaff Removal: Expelling silver skin
- Development Control: Affects evenness of roast

Airflow Settings:
- Low Airflow: Concentrated heat, scorching risk
- Medium Airflow: Balanced development, even roast
- High Airflow: Faster drying, lighter roasts
- Variable Airflow: Adjust during roast stages

Optimization:
- Bean Density: Affects airflow needs
- Roast Level: Different airflow for different roasts
- Roaster Type: Airflow requirements vary
- Quality Goals: Balance evenness and development
```

## Roast Levels

### Light Roast
```yaml
Characteristics:
- Bean Color: Light cinnamon brown
- Oil Surface: None to minimal
- Development: Before or at first crack
- Temperature: 196-205°C (385-401°F)
- Time: 8-12 minutes typical

Flavor Profile:
- Acidity: High, bright
- Body: Light to medium
- Flavor Notes: Floral, fruit, delicate, origin characteristics
- Aftertaste: Clean, sweet
- Use: Pour-over, specialty coffee, origin showcase

Common Names:
- Cinnamon Roast
- Light City
- New England Roast
- Half City
```

### Medium Roast

Characteristics:

- Oil Surface: Beginning to show
- Development: After first crack, before second

Flavor Profile:

## Bean Development Considerations

### Green Bean Characteristics
```yaml
Bean Density:

Bean Size:

- Altitude: Higher altitude beans roast differently

- Temperature Consistency: Same profile each time

```

### Common Roast Defects
```yaml
Scorching:
- Cause: High heat, poor airflow
- Symptoms: Dark spots, burnt flavor
- Prevention: Reduce heat, improve airflow
- Correction: Lower heat settings, better control

Tipping:
- Cause: High heat, uneven roasting
- Symptoms: White tips on beans
- Prevention: More even heat distribution
- Correction: Adjust heat application

Uneven Roasting:
- Cause: Poor heat circulation
- Symptoms: Mixed colors in batch
- Prevention: Proper agitation, good airflow
- Correction: Improve roaster design or technique

Under-development:
- Cause: Insufficient heat/time
- Symptoms: Sour, grassy flavors
- Prevention: Increase heat or time
- Correction: Extend roast time, increase heat

Over-development:
- Cause: Too much heat/time
- Symptoms: Bitter, burnt flavors
- Prevention: Reduce heat or time
- Correction: Shorten roast time, reduce heat
```

## Cooling & Storage

### Cooling Process
```yaml
Importance:
- Stops Development: Prevents over-roasting
- Preserves Quality: Locks in flavors
- Prevents Damage: Avoids continued cooking
- Quality Control: Consistent stopping point

Cooling Methods:
- Air Cooling: Natural air circulation
- Water Quenching: Rapid water cooling
- Rotary Cooler: Mechanical agitation with air
- Chill Rolls: Metal rolls with cooling
- Critical: Must be rapid and effective

Cooling Time:
- Standard: 2-5 minutes to target temperature
- Temperature: Below 50°C (122°F) ideal
- Consistency: Same cooling method each time
- Quality: Even cooling, no thermal shock
```

### Storage Guidelines
```yaml
Short Term (1-4 weeks):
- Containers: Airtight, one-way valves
- Environment: Cool, dark, dry
- Temperature: Stable, below 25°C (77°F)
- Humidity: Below 60% relative humidity
- Freshness: Best within 1-2 weeks of roasting

Medium Term (1-3 months):
- Freezing: Can extend to 3 months
- Thawing: Gradual thawing in container
- Quality: Some degradation expected
- Storage: Proper freezing technique essential
- Use: Rotate stock, oldest first

Long Term:
- Degradation: Flavor loss inevitable over time
- Optimal: Use within 1 month for best quality
- Investment: Small batches, frequent roasting
- Quality Focus: Freshness paramount
- Alternative: Buy green beans and roast fresh
```
