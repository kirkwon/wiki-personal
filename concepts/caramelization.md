# Caramelization

[[Caramelization]] is the thermal decomposition (or oxidation) of sugars that occurs at temperatures above 355°F (179°C), producing amber to dark brown colors and butterscotch or burnt sugar flavors, and is noted for creating nutty flavor and brown color. It is distinct from the [[Maillard reaction]], which requires both amino acids and reducing sugars and occurs at lower temperatures (285-330°F).

## Key Differences from Maillard Reaction

| Aspect | Maillard Reaction | Caramelization |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| Ingredients | Amino acids + reducing sugars | Sugars only |
| Temperature | 285-330°F (140-165°C) | Above 355°F (179°C) |
| Flavor | Nutty, meaty, roasted | Butterscotch, burnt sugar |
| Foods | Meat, bread, coffee, nuts | Onions, sugar syrups, fruits |

## Common Misconception

Foods often labeled "caramelized" — such as onions — are actually undergoing the Maillard reaction when amino acids from the onion's proteins are present alongside sugars. True caramelization involves sugars only.
