# Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion

Robert B. Cialdini's landmark work identifies six psychological principles that systematically drive human compliance and decision-making. The book argues these principles are both powerful tools and sources of vulnerability to manipulation, and that responsible use requires awareness of when influence tactics are being deployed against you.

## Six Principles of Influence
1. [[Reciprocity]] — Obligation to return favors shapes decision-making and compliance
2. [[Commitment and Consistency]] — People follow through on prior commitments as a powerful behavioral driver
3. [[Social Proof]] — Conformity to perceived group behavior
4. [[Authority]] — Deference to perceived expertise or power
5. [[Liking]] — Similarity and rapport increase persuasion
6. [[Scarcity]] — Limited availability increases desire

## Ethical Dimension
A core tension in the book is the line between ethical persuasion and manipulation. Cialdini cautions against unethical use while recognizing that influence is inherent in all human interaction. This connects to the wiki's broader theme of decision quality vs. outcome quality and the [[ethical-design-manipulation-question]].

## Relationship to Other Frameworks
Cialdini's framework complements Kahneman's dual-process theory and Dobelli's cognitive bias compendium with a structured, actionable framework rather than a bias catalog. Social Proof overlaps with [[the-wisdom-of-crowds]] and [[group-decision-polarization]]. Scarcity connects to [[fear-of-missing-out-fomo]] and [[dread-aversion]].
