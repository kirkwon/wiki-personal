# Straddle Strategies

A straddle involves simultaneously buying both a call and put option at the same strike price and expiration date. Straddles are used for volatility capture and income generation depending on market conditions.

## Types of Straddles

- **Long Straddle**: Buy call + buy put (unlimited upside, limited downside)
- **Short Straddle**: Sell call + sell put (limited upside, unlimited downside)
- **Long Straddle Spread**: Buy ATM straddle, sell OTM straddle
- **Short Straddle Spread**: Sell ATM straddle, buy OTM straddle

## Market Conditions

- **Long Straddle**: High volatility expected, neutral directional view
- **Short Straddle**: Low volatility expected, neutral market view
- **Straddle Spreads**: Limited range expectations with defined risk

## Applications

### Earnings Announcement Plays
Buy straddle 1–2 weeks before earnings expecting significant price movement. Close position if no movement after earnings. Target 50–100% return if stock moves sufficiently.

### Merger & Acquisition Plays
Buy straddle on target company around announcement date, expecting significant price movement from acquisition. Close after announcement and initial move.

### Economic Event Plays
Buy straddle on index or sector ETF around Fed meetings, economic data releases, or elections. These are short-term positions but carry high theta decay risk requiring immediate volatility.

## Theta Impact

**Long Straddle**: Double theta risk—both call and put lose value to time decay. Can lose 3–5% of premium daily in last 30 days. Need significant price movement to overcome decay. Best for 1–4 week holding periods.

**Short Straddle**: Double theta income from both call and put premium decay. Can generate 5–15% monthly returns but requires higher capital, strict stop-loss, and position sizing.
