# Battle Mental Model

Battle is a universal mental model that serves as a conceptual tool for understanding and navigating complex, adversarial, or competitive situations. It provides a mental shortcut for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world, particularly in contexts involving conflict, competition, or strategic tension.

## Definition

Battle functions as a framework for recognizing patterns and relationships that emerge in complex situations where opposing forces or competing interests are at play. It draws on the strategic principles found in Sun Tzu's *The Art of War* as its foundational reference.

## Core Principles

While the source document itself is a stub, the mental model is grounded in Sun Tzu's strategic philosophy, which includes principles such as:

- **Strategic positioning** — Understanding the landscape of forces and dynamics at play.
- **Knowledge of self and enemy** — Awareness of one's own strengths and weaknesses as well as those of competitors or adversaries.
- **Winning without fighting** — The supreme art of achieving objectives through positioning and preparation rather than direct confrontation.
- **Adaptability** — Responding fluidly to changing conditions and maintaining flexibility.
- **Economy of effort** — Deploying resources efficiently to maximize impact.

## Applicability

The model is described as universally applicable across general domains, including business strategy, personal decision-making, team dynamics, and any context where competitive or adversarial dynamics are present. It connects to existing mental models such as [[obstacles]], [[opportunities]], [[planning]], and [[solutions-mental-model]].
