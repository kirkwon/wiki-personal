# Consistency in AI Decision-Making

Consistency refers to the ability of AI systems to make reliable, predictable decisions across diverse and novel situations. [[The Alignment Problem - Brian Christian  Tom Griffiths]] describes this as a "squirrelly quest"—the challenge of building systems that are consistent enough to be trustworthy but flexible enough to handle the variability of real-world scenarios.

Key tensions:
- **Over-consistency**: A system that applies the same rule rigidly may fail in edge cases or novel situations, leading to brittleness.
- **Under-consistency**: A system that adapts too freely may behave unpredictably, making it difficult for humans to trust or oversee.
- **Alignment tradeoff**: Stricter consistency can improve reliability but may also constrain the system's ability to reflect nuanced human values in complex situations.

This concept parallels [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]]—a system can make high-quality decisions in most cases while still failing on rare but important exceptions.
