# Aperture

Aperture is the opening in a camera lens that controls how much light enters the camera. It is measured in f-stops: f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16, f/22.

## How It Works

- **Lower f-number (f/1.4)**: Larger opening, MORE light, SHALLOW depth of field (blurry background)
- **Higher f-number (f/22)**: Smaller opening, LESS light, DEEP depth of field (everything in focus)

## Common Uses by Genre

| f-stop | Use Case | Effect |
|--------|----------|--------|
| f/1.4 – f/2.8 | Portraits, low light | Blurry background (bokeh), maximum light |
| f/4 – f/5.6 | General photography | Good balance of light and depth |
| f/8 – f/11 | Landscapes | Deep depth of field, everything sharp |
| f/16 – f/22 | Bright scenes, maximum depth | Deep depth, everything in focus |

Aperture is closely tied to [[composition-techniques]], as the depth of field directly affects how subjects are isolated from backgrounds. It is one of the three pillars of the [[exposure-triangle]] alongside [[shutter-speed]] and [[iso]]. See [[exposure-triangle]] for how aperture interacts with shutter speed and ISO.
