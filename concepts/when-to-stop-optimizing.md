# When to Stop Optimizing

A distinctive and valuable element of McKeown's Essentialism is explicit guidance on when to stop optimizing. The Hermes skill flags these danger signs:

## Warning Signs
- Optimization becomes obsession and causes stress
- Productivity systems create more work than they save
- Sacrificing relationships for efficiency
- Health and well-being are being neglected
- The pursuit of productivity destroys joy in the work
- Measuring the wrong things
- Systems become rigid and inflexible
- Using productivity to avoid deeper issues
- The cost of optimization exceeds the benefit
- No longer being present in the moment

## Significance
This self-regulation principle warns against productivity obsession and provides a crucial counterbalance to the wiki's general pro-productivity orientation, flagging that optimization can become counterproductive. It complements frameworks like [[hyperfocus---chris-bailey]], [[scatterfocus]], and [[deep-work - Cal Newport]] by providing guardrails against taking any productivity approach to extremes.
