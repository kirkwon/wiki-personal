# Noise

Noise refers to unwanted variability in judgment and decision-making — the gap between how a person or system actually performs and how consistently it could perform. More broadly, noise is random variability or irrelevant information that interferes with clear signal detection, making it harder to discern true patterns.

## Key Ideas

- Noise is the counterpart to signal — it is the irrelevant or random variation that obscures accurate assessment of what is working and what is not.
- In productivity and decision-making contexts, noise can manifest as wasted effort, distractions, or misleading feedback that obscures the impact of changes.
- In the context of calibration training, noise is a mental model closely tied to aligning subjective confidence with actual accuracy.
- Reducing noise is a core goal of model selection frameworks: by auditing workflows and measuring impact systematically, practitioners aim to filter out noise and amplify signal.
- Nate Silver distinguishes noise from bias as a form of random error in predictions.

## Connection to calibration

Calibration training aims to reduce noise in personal workflows by auditing efficiency losses, restructuring around high-impact activities, and iterating on what works.
