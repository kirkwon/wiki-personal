# When to stop predicting

Knowing when prediction is futile or counterproductive. Silver argues that acknowledging the limits of prediction is more valuable than false precision.

## Key Ideas

- Stop when data is insufficient for meaningful prediction
- Stop when models become more complex than the problem warrants
- Stop when rapid change invalidates historical patterns (regime shifts)
- Stop when prediction costs exceed forecast value
- Stop when overconfidence leads to excessive risk-taking
- Stop when prediction paralysis prevents necessary action
- Embracing uncertainty is a form of intellectual honesty

## Connection to Regime Shifts

Regime shifts are a primary reason to stop predicting—when the underlying system changes, historical models become unreliable.
