# Feedback loop design

Feedback loop design is the practice of systematically improving efficiency and output by identifying, implementing, and iterating on methods, techniques, and frameworks that maximize personal or organizational productivity while maintaining sustainable work practices.^[raw/articles/Feedback loop design.md]

## Core Framework: Audit → Apply → Iterate → Scale

The central methodology follows four steps:

1. **Audit** — Identify where efficiency losses occur, track how time and attention are spent, and note patterns that lead to wasted effort.
2. **Apply** — Restructure the approach using feedback loop design principles, focusing on high-impact activities and eliminating or delegating low-value tasks.
3. **Iterate** — Measure the impact of changes, adjust based on results, and build consistency through habit formation.
4. **Scale** — Standardize effective approaches, document processes for reuse, and teach others to multiply impact.^[raw/articles/Feedback loop design.md]

## Applications

- **Personal productivity** — Planning weeks more effectively, managing competing priorities, breaking down complex projects.
- **Team coordination** — Improving meeting efficiency, enhancing decision-making processes.
- **Learning and growth** — Accelerating skill acquisition, organizing and retaining information, identifying and filling knowledge gaps.^[raw/articles/Feedback loop design.md]

## Origins and Context

The concept draws from Kevin Kelly's *Out of Control*, which explores emergent systems, self-organizing behavior, and the power of feedback loops in complex systems.^[raw/articles/Feedback loop design.md] This perspective distinguishes feedback loop design from standard PDCA or cycle improvement models by emphasizing emergent, out-of-control dynamics rather than purely mechanical optimization.
