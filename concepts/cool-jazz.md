# Cool Jazz

Cool jazz is a style of jazz characterized by a smoother, more restrained sound compared to the intensity of bebop. Miles Davis was central to its development through his Nonet recordings, which became known as the "Birth of the Cool."

## Origins
In 1949-1950, Miles Davis recorded with a nonet (nine-piece ensemble) arranged by [[Gil Evans]]. These sessions featured a cooler sound with French horn and tuba, moving away from the frenetic energy of bebop.

## Key Recordings
- **Birth of the Cool** (1949-1950) – Nonet sessions with arranged charts
- Key tracks: "Boplicity," "Moon Dreams"

## Characteristics
- Cooler, more arranged sound
- Use of unusual instruments (French horn, tuba)
- Greater emphasis on arrangement and composition
- Less virtuosic display than bebop

## Legacy
The cool jazz movement that emerged from these recordings influenced a generation of musicians and established the idea that jazz could be understated and lyrical. This aesthetic connects to Davis's broader philosophy of economy and the use of space.
