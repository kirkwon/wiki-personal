# Work-Life Integration

Work-life integration, as articulated in David Allen's *Making It All Work*, is the practice of achieving coherence across all domains of life rather than treating work and personal life as separate spheres to be balanced. The book argues that rigid separation often leads to neither domain being fully lived.

## Key Ideas

- Integration replaces balance: instead of splitting time and energy, seek alignment between professional ambitions and personal values.
- Technology plays a dual role—streamlining tasks but also introducing distractions that fragment attention.
- Sustainable productivity requires that systems do not sacrifice relationships, health, or joy.
- Knowing when to stop optimizing is a core principle: when the cost of optimization exceeds the benefit, step back.
- Environment design—shaping physical and digital spaces—supports integration by reducing friction between work and personal modes.

## When to Stop

The book provides explicit criteria for disengaging from optimization:
- When optimization becomes obsession and causes stress
- When productivity systems create more work than they save
- When relationships are sacrificed for efficiency
- When health and well-being are neglected
- When the pursuit of productivity destroys joy
