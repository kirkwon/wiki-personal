# Flavor Theory

## Overview

Flavor theory explores how flavors are built and layered, emphasizing the role of aromatic bases as the starting point for flavor construction. By using staged addition strategies, cooks create complex flavor profiles that balance depth, integration, and freshness.

## Flavor Layering

- **Early addition**: Base flavor – aromatic bases go in first.
- **Mid‑cooking**: Integrated flavor – aromatic bases combine with proteins to produce new layers.
- **Late addition**: Fresh impact – garlic and green onions added in the last 30 seconds provide freshness.

## The Holy Trinity

- **Chinese Holy Trinity**: Ginger, garlic, and scallion are the core practice of Chinese flavor theory, creating balanced flavors through varying proportions and timing. Adjusting proportions based on protein type is an important application of flavor theory.
- **Other cuisines**: French Mirepoix, Spanish Sofrito, Portuguese Refogado, and Louisiana Holy Trinity are each the aromatic base starting points of their respective flavor systems, illustrating the universality of flavor theory across cultures.

## Applications

- Common use cases include building layered dishes, adjusting aromatic ratios to protein types, and integrating cultural aromatic bases into modern cooking techniques.
