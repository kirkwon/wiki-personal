# RAW vs JPEG

RAW files contain unprocessed sensor data with maximum editing flexibility, while JPEG files are compressed and processed in-camera with limited adjustment range.

## Key Differences

- **RAW**: Greater editing flexibility, non-destructive workflow, more data per pixel
- **JPEG**: Smaller file size, ready to share, limited adjustment range
- Post-processing benefits significantly from shooting RAW because adjustments have more headroom

## Why RAW Matters for Post-Processing

The source emphasizes that RAW files provide more editing flexibility. Not shooting RAW is listed among common mistakes in post-processing, as it limits the ability to recover detail in highlights and shadows.
