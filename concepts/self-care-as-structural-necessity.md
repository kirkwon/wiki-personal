# Self-Care as Structural Necessity

Self-care, as presented in Manson's framework, is not indulgence or luxury—it is a structural necessity for sustaining performance, well-being, and long-term resilience. This concept reframes self-care from a reactive measure (something you do when you're burnt out) to a proactive, ongoing commitment that undergirds all other life activities.

## Definition

Self-care as a structural necessity means treating the maintenance of physical, emotional, and mental health as a foundational requirement for effective living, similar to how infrastructure maintenance is required for any system to function.

## Key Principles

- **Ongoing commitment** – Self-care is not a one-time intervention but a lifelong practice.
- **Sustained performance** – Without self-care, performance in all domains (work, relationships, personal goals) degrades over time.
- **Prevention over cure** – Self-care prevents burnout and breakdown rather than merely addressing symptoms.
- **Structural, not optional** – Like sleep or nutrition, self-care is a prerequisite for functioning, not a reward for achievement.

## Connection to Other Concepts

This concept supports [[effort]] and [[perseverance]] mental models by ensuring the capacity to sustain effort over time. It also relates to [[stress]] management, where managing stress is framed as a productive tool rather than something to eliminate entirely.
