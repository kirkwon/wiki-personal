# Resting Meat

Resting meat is the technique of allowing cooked meat to sit after removal from heat before cutting or serving. This allows the juices, which have been driven to the center by cooking heat, to redistribute evenly throughout the meat, resulting in a juicier, more flavorful final product.

## Key Points

- **Juice redistribution** — Resting allows trapped juices to settle back into the muscle fibers
- **Temperature equalization** — Internal temperature continues to rise slightly (carryover cooking) then stabilizes
- **Timing** — Generally 5-10 minutes for small cuts, up to 20 minutes for large roasts
- **Connection to technique** — Resting is the counterpart to high-heat cooking methods like [[wok-hei]] and [[maillard-reaction]], where proper timing is essential for optimal results

This technique is particularly relevant for dishes like [[salmon-collars]] and [[recipe:glazed-salmon-collars]].

## Overview

Letting cooked meat rest before cutting.
