# Heat Sensitivity of Herbs

Herbs like basil are highly sensitive to heat. Prolonged cooking causes loss of color, development of bitterness, and degradation of volatile aromatic compounds that define their flavor.

## Key Principles
- **High Heat**: Causes immediate color loss and bitterness
- **Medium Heat**: Add in the last 1-2 minutes of cooking
- **No Heat**: Best for garnishes and raw applications
- **Warm Oil**: Infusing basil in warm (not hot) oil preserves flavor

## Practical Implications
When making pesto, basil should be added to warm rather than hot ingredients. In stir-fries and sauces, basil is typically added in the final seconds. This principle applies broadly to fresh aromatic herbs and distinguishes their treatment from dried herbs, which can tolerate longer cooking times.
