# Mental Model Library

A mental model library is a curated collection of frameworks, heuristics, and principles that can be applied to decision-making, problem-solving, and learning across domains.

## Core Idea
Having a diverse toolkit of mental models allows you to see problems from multiple angles. The key insight — central to Weinberg's *Super Thinking* — is that combining multiple models yields better results than relying on any single framework.

## Key Models in the Library
- **Epistemic models**: [[bayesian-thinking]], [[first-order-and-second-order-thinking]], [[inversion-thinking]], [[first-principles-thinking]]
- **Decision models**: [[expected-value-ev]], [[sunk-cost-fallacy]], [[opportunity-cost]], [[margin-of-safety]]
- **Strategic models**: [[game-theory-and-strategic-analysis]], [[network-effects]], [[competitive-advantage]]
- **Systems models**: [[feedback-loops]], [[emergent-properties]], [[equilibrium]], [[law-of-unintended-consequences]]
- **Social models**: [[reciprocity]], [[authority-bias]], [[social-proof]], [[in-group-out-group-bias]], [[empathy]]
- **Learning models**: [[feynman-technique]], [[spaced-repetition]], [[deliberate-practice]], [[growth-mindset]], [[dunning-kruger-effect]]

## Guardrails
Weinberg emphasizes important guardrails from [[super-thinking-gabriel-weinberg]]:
- Avoid analysis paralysis
- Don't force models where they don't fit
- Don't use complexity to sound smart
- Don't let models become a crutch
