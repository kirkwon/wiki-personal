# PARA Categories

The PARA Method organizes digital information into four distinct categories, each serving a different purpose in managing your digital life.

## Projects

Active efforts with a clear outcome and deadline. These are the things you are working on right now. A project has a defined end state and a target completion date. Examples include writing a report, planning a vacation, or completing a course.

## Areas

Ongoing responsibilities that require regular maintenance but have no specific deadline. These represent domains of your life that need continuous attention. Examples include health and fitness, financial management, home maintenance, and professional development.

## Resources

Topics of interest, reference material, or inspiration that may be useful someday. Resources are organized by topic rather than by project. This category captures notes, articles, books, and ideas that don't belong to an active project or area.

## Archives

Completed or inactive items that are worth keeping for reference. Once a project is finished or an area becomes inactive, it moves to Archives. This keeps the active categories clean and reduces cognitive load.

## Key Insight

The power of PARA lies in its simplicity. By asking "Is this a project, area, resource, or archive?" you can quickly categorize any piece of information or action item, reducing decision fatigue and creating clarity about what demands your attention.
