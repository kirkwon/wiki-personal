# Income as Mental Model

Income functions as a universal conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern and relationship recognition. At its core, it provides a mental shortcut for making sense of how value is generated, exchanged, and sustained in various contexts.

The model draws from Timothy Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Workweek*, which distinguishes between active income (traded for time), passive income (generated without direct labor), and portfolio income (from investments and assets). Ferriss emphasizes designing income systems that decouple earning from hours worked, focusing on outcomes and leverage rather than time inputs.

This mental model connects to broader wiki concepts:
- **Leverage** — Income is closely tied to finding and applying leverage to multiply output.
- **Rates** — Understanding income patterns involves analyzing rates of return, growth, and decay.
- **Investment strategies for life satisfaction** — Ferriss's framework is fundamentally about optimizing for desired outcomes rather than traditional metrics.
- **Personal finance** — Income types, diversification, and decoupling from time are core personal finance themes.

The model is applicable across domains — from business strategy to personal career design to understanding economic systems. Its utility lies in forcing clarity about what generates value and how that value flows.
