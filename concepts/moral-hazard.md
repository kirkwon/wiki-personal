# Moral Hazard

Moral hazard occurs when a party insulated from risk behaves differently than if they were fully exposed to the risk. Taleb identifies moral hazard as a central problem in systems where decision-makers face no downside for their choices. Without skin in the game, individuals and institutions are incentivized to take excessive risks, shift costs to others, and avoid accountability.

This concept is central to Taleb's argument that accountability structures—where participants bear the consequences of their decisions—are essential for fair and responsible systems.

## Key Points

- Moral hazard arises when decision-makers face no downside
- Insulated parties take excessive risks at others' expense
- Skin in the game eliminates moral hazard by aligning incentives
- Applies to finance, governance, insurance, and institutional design
- Historical crises (Great Depression, subprime crisis) exemplify moral hazard
