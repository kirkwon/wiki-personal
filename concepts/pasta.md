# Pasta

Pasta is the foundation of Italian cuisine — versatile, affordable, and beloved worldwide. From long strands to short shapes, fresh to dried, each pasta shape has its ideal sauce and regional tradition.

## Fresh vs. Dried
- **Fresh (pasta fresca)**: Soft, tender, cooks quickly; for special occasions and stuffed pasta
- **Dried (pasta secca)**: Firm, al dente, shelf-stable; for everyday cooking

## Classic Dishes
- [[carbonara]]: Roman classic with guanciale, eggs, Pecorino Romano, black pepper — no cream
- [[cacio-e-pepe]]: Roman classic with Pecorino Romano, black pepper, pasta water emulsion
- [[amatriciana]]: Roman classic with guanciale, tomato, onion, Pecorino Romano
- [[ragu-alla-bolognese]]: Emilia-Romagna slow-cooked meat ragù with tomato, onion, celery, carrot, wine, milk
- [[pesto-alla-genovese]]: Genovese basil pesto with pine nuts, Pecorino, Parmesan, garlic, olive oil
- [[pasta-alla-norma]]: Sicilian eggplant, tomato, basil, ricotta salata
- [[spaghetti-alle-vongole]]: Southern clams with garlic, olive oil, parsley, white wine

## Key Techniques
- [[al-dente]]: Firm doneness standard; cook 1-2 minutes less than package time
- [[fresh-pasta-making]]: Flour, eggs, kneading, resting, rolling process
- [[pasta-shapes-and-sauces]]: Matching shape to sauce for optimal adhesion and mouthfeel
- Reserve starchy pasta water for sauce binding (especially [[cacio-e-pepe]])

## Common Mistakes
- Overcooking pasta
- Not salting water sufficiently (water should taste like the sea)
- Rinsing pasta (removes starch needed for sauce)
- Wrong sauce for shape
- Not tossing pasta in sauce

## Where to Learn More
Bologna for fresh pasta, Naples for dried pasta, Genoa for pesto.
