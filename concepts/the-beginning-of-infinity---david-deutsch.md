# The Beginning of Infinity

*The Beginning of Infinity* is a book by David Deutsch that argues human progress is driven by the creation of explanatory knowledge and the capacity to identify and correct errors. Deutsch posits that the "beginning of infinity" marks the point at which humans developed the ability to create such knowledge, enabling unlimited progress.^[raw/articles/World.md]

## Core Thesis

Deutsch distinguishes between two types of problems:
- **Problems that are soluble in principle** — These are problems that have correct answers and can be solved through conjecture and criticism.
- **Problems that are unsolvable** — These are problems that lack correct answers or are beyond human capability.

His central claim is that optimism — the methodological belief that problems are solvable — is what drives progress. This is not naive positivity but a commitment to fallibilism: the recognition that we may be wrong, but that we can always improve our understanding through error correction.^[raw/articles/World.md]

## Key Concepts

- **Error-correction mechanisms** — The processes by which humans identify and fix mistakes in their knowledge and practices.
- **Explanatory knowledge** — Knowledge that provides explanations rather than mere predictions, enabling understanding and progress.
- **Optimism principle** — The methodological commitment to solving problems through conjecture and criticism.
