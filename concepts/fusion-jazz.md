# Fusion Jazz

Fusion jazz is the genre that emerged from Miles Davis's electric experiments, combining jazz improvisation with rock rhythms, electric instruments, and studio production techniques. It represents one of the most significant genre shifts in 20th-century music.

## Origins
Fusion began with Miles Davis's work in the late 1960s, particularly [[Bitches Brew]] (1970), which introduced electric instruments, rock-influenced rhythms, and extensive studio experimentation into the jazz context.

## Key Characteristics
- Electric instruments (guitars, electric piano, synthesizers)
- Rock and funk rhythms alongside jazz harmony
- Extended improvisation with dense, layered textures
- Studio experimentation and production techniques

## Influential Artists and Bands
- Weather Report
- Mahavishnu Orchestra
- Return to Forever
- Herbie Hancock (post-Miles work)
- John Scofield

## Cross-Genre Influence
Fusion opened pathways for jazz's influence on rock (Prince, Radiohead), hip-hop (Madlib, Flying Lotus), and electronic music. Miles Davis's willingness to absorb outside sounds while maintaining improvisational integrity made fusion a viable artistic direction.
