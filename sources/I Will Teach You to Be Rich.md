# I Will Teach You to Be Rich

A six-week action program for automating personal finances, authored by Ramit Sethi. The core philosophy replaces traditional budgeting with a **Conscious Spending Plan** organized into four buckets: fixed costs, investments, savings, and guilt-free spending. The central thesis is that automation—setting up automatic transfers, payments, and investments—eliminates the need for daily willpower and yields compounding benefits over decades.

## Core Philosophy

The central claim is that consistency through systems beats perfection through willpower. A six-week automated financial setup eliminates the need for ongoing budgeting decisions, allowing guilt-free spending on what matters while savings and investments happen automatically. Two to three hours of initial setup can result in $100,000+ over 30 years.

## Core Principles

- **Automation over willpower**: Systems handle decisions so humans don't have to.
- **Employer 401(k) match as priority**: Contributing enough to get the full 401(k) match is the single highest-priority financial action—a guaranteed 50-100% return.
- **Debt avalanche vs. debt snowball**: The avalanche method (paying highest interest rate first) saves the most money; the snowball method (smallest balance first) provides psychological momentum.
- **Index fund investing**: Buy-and-hold index funds (VTSAX, VFIAX, VBTLX, target-date funds) outperform 80-90% of active funds. Dollar-cost averaging removes emotion and market timing risk.
- **The 4% Rule / Freedom Number**: Withdrawal rate of 4% annually requires 25x annual expenses saved. This defines "rich" as financial freedom, not a specific number. Example: $60,000 annual expenses → $1.5 million needed.
- **Rule of 110**: 110 minus your age equals the percentage to hold in stocks for asset allocation.
- **Big wins over small wins**: Refinancing, negotiating salary, shopping insurance annually, and auditing subscriptions move the needle more than daily frugality.

## The Four Buckets (Conscious Spending Plan)

Spend extravagantly on what you love, cut mercilessly on what you don't. No guilt because spending is planned.

| Bucket | Allocation |
|---|---|
| Fixed costs | 50-60% |
| Investments | 10-20% |
| Savings | 5-10% |
| Guilt-free spending | 20-35% |

## Six-Week Program

1. **Week 1**: Credit cards—check reports, clean up errors, choose card strategy (rewards/cash back/low interest), negotiate APR, automate payments.
2. **Week 2**: Bank accounts—open high-yield savings (4-5% vs 0.01%), set up automatic transfers, create sub-savings accounts.
3. **Week 3**: Retirement accounts—get 401(k) match, open Roth IRA, choose target-date funds or index funds.
4. **Week 4**: Conscious spending—track spending, create four-bucket plan, optimize fixed costs, automate everything.
5. **Week 5**: Debt payoff—list all debt, choose avalanche or snowball, automate extra payments, consider balance transfers.
6. **Week 6**: Investing—buy index funds, automate monthly contributions, dollar-cost averaging, ignore financial news.

## Big Wins

1. Automate everything ($100,000+ over 30 years)
2. Get employer match (free money)
3. Negotiate salary ($5,000-10,000/year)
4. Refinance high-interest debt ($200-500/month)
5. Shop insurance annually ($100-300/year)
6. Subscriptions audit ($50-150/month)

## Key Concepts

- **Conscious Spending Plan**: Four-bucket allocation replacing traditional budgeting.
- **Freedom Number**: 25x annual expenses needed for retirement at 4% withdrawal rate.
- **Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball**: Strategies for prioritizing debt payoff.
- **Index Fund Investing**: Passive, diversified, low-cost approach to investing.

## Key Takeaways

- Automate everything — willpower fails, systems work
- Conscious spending — spend on what matters, cut what doesn't
- Invest early and often — time is the greatest asset
- Use index funds — don't try to beat the market
- Get the employer match — free money
- Negotiate everything
- Ignore financial noise
- Start today
