# Options Trading

Options are derivative contracts giving the right (but not obligation) to buy or sell an asset at a predetermined price (strike) by a specific date (expiration). Used for speculation (leverage), hedging (protection), and income generation (selling premium). Complex instruments with significant risk, especially for sellers.

## Option Basics
- Call Option: Right to BUY asset at strike price. Profitable when asset price > strike price + premium paid.
- Put Option: Right to SELL asset at strike price. Profitable when asset price < strike price - premium paid.
- 1 option contract = 100 shares.
- Implied Volatility / Historical Volatility
- Leverage and Time Decay (Theta)

## The Greeks
- Delta (Δ): How much option price moves for $1 change in stock
- Gamma (Γ): How much delta changes for $1 change in stock
- Theta (Θ): Daily time decay
- Vega (ν): Sensitivity to volatility changes
- Rho (ρ): Sensitivity to interest rates

## Option Strategies
- Covered Call: Own stock + sell call option
- Protective Put (Married Put): Own stock + buy put option
- Long Straddle: Buy call + buy put at same strike
- Vertical Spread: Buy option + sell option at different strike
- Iron Condor: Bull put spread + bear call spread

## Risk Management
- Position sizing: Don't risk >2-5% of portfolio per trade
- Greeks limits
- Stop losses
- Avoid naked options, binary options, overleveraging

## Tax Treatment
- Section 1256: 60% long-term, 40% short-term for non-equity options
- Wash sale rule: 30-day window
- Short-term vs long-term capital gains

## Common Mistakes
- Buying OTM options
- Ignoring time decay
- Selling naked options
- Overleveraging
- Not understanding Greeks

## Best Practices
Start small, understand math, respect time decay, manage Greeks, use stops, avoid naked selling, trade liquid options, consider volatility, plan for assignment, paper trade first.
