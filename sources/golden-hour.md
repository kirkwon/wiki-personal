# Golden Hour - Photography Lighting Guide

Practical guide covering the golden hour period—warm, soft light shortly after sunrise and before sunset—and related lighting concepts.

## Definition and Characteristics
Golden hour is a period of warm, soft light shortly after sunrise and before sunset. It provides ideal lighting conditions for photography due to its low angle and warm color temperature. The **blue hour** is an extension of shooting time after sunset, characterized by a deep blue sky.

## Timing and Variations
The exact timing of golden hour varies based on:
- **Season**: Duration and time change with the seasons.
- **Latitude**: Location on the globe affects the angle and duration.
- **Elevation**: Higher elevations can shift the timing.

## Planning and Tools
Proper planning is essential for capturing golden hour shots.
- **Apps**: Use tools like [[PhotoPills]], [[The Photographer's Ephemeris]], and [[Sun Surveyor]] to calculate exact times.
- **Weather**: Always check weather forecasts to avoid cloud cover blocking the light.
- **Location Scouting**: Research and visit locations in advance to find the best compositions.

## Equipment and Settings
- Use a tripod for stability during low-light conditions.
- Adjust camera settings for the soft light, often involving wider apertures or slower shutter speeds.
- Equipment recommendations include cameras and lenses suitable for the golden hour's dynamic range.

## Techniques
Master various lighting techniques to utilize golden hour light effectively:
- **Backlighting**: Place the sun behind the subject for a glowing rim light.
- **Side Lighting**: Use the sun at a 90-degree angle for strong shadows and texture.
- **Front Lighting**: Shoot with the sun behind the camera for even illumination.
- **Silhouettes**: Expose for the background to create dark, defined subject outlines.

## Subjects
Golden hour is versatile and suits many subjects:
- Portraits
- Landscapes
- Cityscapes
- Beach scenes

## Common Mistakes
- Not accounting for weather changes.
- Arriving too late and missing the optimal light.
- Ignoring the transition into the blue hour.

## Summary
Golden hour offers a brief window of magical light for photography. By understanding timing, planning with apps, and mastering techniques like backlighting and silhouettes, photographers can capture stunning images. The blue hour provides an additional shooting window after sunset.
