# Cumin (Cuminum cyminum)

Comprehensive spice profile for cumin covering flavor profile, culinary applications across Mexican, Indian, Middle Eastern, and Mediterranean cuisines, growing/selecting/storage, cooking techniques (toasting, blooming, infusion, grinding), recipes, pairing guide, regional blend systems, substitutions, common mistakes, health benefits, and global trade history.

## Key Takeaways
- Cumin is one of the world's most important spices with a distinctive earthy, nutty, warming flavor. Whole seeds retain flavor 2-3x longer than ground; toast 2-3 minutes max to avoid bitterness.
- 1 tsp ground cumin per pound of food is a useful rule of thumb.
- Cumin-coriander base is globally universal across all four cuisines.
- Flavor profile: earthy, nutty, warming, slightly bitter, musky.
- Primary cuisines: Mexican, Indian, Middle Eastern, Mediterranean.
- Best uses: chili, curries, tacos, spice blends.
- Complements: coriander, chili, garlic, turmeric.
- Quality indicators include uniform brown color, warm nutty aroma, and clean taste.
- Whole seeds should be toasted before use for maximum aroma.

## Regional Blend Systems
- **Mexican**: cumin + chili + oregano
- **Indian**: cumin + coriander + turmeric + garam masala
- **Middle Eastern**: cumin + coriander + cinnamon + allspice
- **Mediterranean**: cumin + paprika + oregano + garlic
