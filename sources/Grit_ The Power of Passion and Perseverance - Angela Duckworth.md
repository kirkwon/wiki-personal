## Key Concepts

- **Grit** – Defined as passion + perseverance for long-term goals, distinct from talent/intelligence. Core concept, already in wiki.
- **Growth Mindset** – Fixed vs. growth mindset; growth mindset enables grit. Already in wiki.
- **Goal setting for grit** – Clear goals provide purpose and measurable progress. New angle on existing [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] and [[quadrant-2-focus]].
- **Limits of grit** – Over-grittiness can be counterproductive; balance needed. This is a nuance not strongly represented in existing wiki pages; worth flagging.
- **Passion and perseverance as learnable traits** – Grit is actionable, not fixed. Already partially covered via [[grit-concept]] and [[embracing-failure-for-personal-growth]].

## Main Arguments & Findings

- **Core claim**: Grit (passion + perseverance) is a more reliable predictor of success than talent or intelligence.
- **Evidence**: Historical research evolution from IQ studies to grit studies; case studies of gritty individuals; neuroscience of passion/perseverance; growth mindset linkage.
- **Evidence strength**: Moderate — the book is largely narrative and pop-psychology; rigorous longitudinal datasets are limited. The "limits of grit" chapter adds important caveats.
- **Key takeaway**: Grit can be cultivated through goal-setting, growth mindset, and systematic habit development, but excess grit without balance causes harm.
