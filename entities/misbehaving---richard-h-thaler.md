# Misbehaving - Richard H. Thaler

*Misbehaving* is Richard H. Thaler's account of behavioral economics, demonstrating how humans systematically deviate from the predictions of rational-agent models. The book covers topics including loss aversion, mental accounting, anchoring, and the limitations of traditional economic theory.

Key themes:
- Humans are not rational actors; cognitive biases shape decisions
- People treat identical outcomes differently depending on reference points
- Traditional economic models fail to predict real-world behavior
- Understanding these deviations can improve decision-making frameworks

Thaler's work is referenced alongside Taleb's *Fooled by Randomness* in the [[data-mental-model]] entry as a cross-source perspective on how people misinterpret data and narrative.
