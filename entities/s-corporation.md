# S Corporation

An S Corporation is a small business corporation with pass-through taxation while maintaining corporate formalities. It combines the limited liability protection of a corporation with the tax benefits of a partnership or LLC.

## Formation

- First formed as a C Corporation, then file S Election via **Form 2553** with the IRS
- Maximum 100 shareholders (no corporations, partnerships, or non-resident aliens)
- One class of stock only
- Domestic corporation only

## Legal Characteristics

- Limited liability protection like C Corporation
- Pass-through taxation (no entity-level tax)
- Must maintain corporate formalities (board meetings, shareholder meetings)
- Distributions taxed as ordinary income (not qualified dividends)
- Must pay reasonable compensation to employee-shareholders

## Advantages

- **Reduced Self-Employment Taxes**: Only payroll taxes on salary, not distributions
- **Pass-Through Taxation**: Avoiding double taxation
- **Credibility**: Perception of corporate structure
- **Perpetual Existence**: Regardless of shareholder changes
- **Estate Planning Benefits**: Potential advantages for succession planning

## Disadvantages

- Strict eligibility requirements (100 shareholders max, one class of stock)
- Must pay reasonable compensation to employee-shareholders
- Less flexibility in profit allocation than LLC
- Limited ability to sell stock to raise capital
- Distributions taxed as ordinary income (not capital gains)

## Tax Optimization

The key tax strategy for S Corps is optimizing the **salary vs. distribution split**. By paying yourself a reasonable salary and taking the remainder as distributions, you avoid self-employment tax on the distribution portion. See [[self-employment-tax-optimization]] for detailed strategies.

## IRS Filings

- **Form 2553**: S Election form filed with IRS
- Must be filed within 75 days of formation or tax year election

## Best For

- Small businesses with growth potential
- Self-employed individuals with significant earnings
- Businesses wanting liability protection with pass-through taxation
