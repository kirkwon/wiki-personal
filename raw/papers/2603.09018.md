Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence
================================================

Yixiong Chen Xinyi Bai Yue Pan Zongwei Zhou Alan Yuille 

###### Abstract

Multi-modal large language models (MM-LLMs) have shown strong performance in medical image understanding and clinical reasoning. Recent medical agent systems extend them with tool use and multi-agent collaboration, enabling complex decision-making beyond single-pass inference. However, these systems rely almost entirely on proprietary frontier models (e.g., GPT), whose API-based deployment incurs high cost, high latency, and privacy risks that conflict with on-premise clinical requirements. We present Meissa, a lightweight 4B-parameter medical MM-LLM that brings full agentic capability offline. Instead of imitating static answers, Meissa learns both _when_ to engage external interaction (strategy selection) and _how_ to execute multi-step interaction (strategy execution) by distilling structured trajectories from frontier agent systems. To be specific, we propose: (1) Unified trajectory modeling: trajectories (reasoning and action traces) are represented within a single state–action–observation formalism, allowing one model to generalize across heterogeneous medical environments. (2) Three-tier stratified supervision: the model’s own errors trigger progressive escalation from direct reasoning to tool-augmented and multi-agent interaction, explicitly learning difficulty-aware strategy selection. (3) Prospective–retrospective supervision: pairing exploratory forward traces with hindsight-rationalized execution traces enables stable learning of effective interaction policies. Trained on ∼{\sim}40K curated trajectories, Meissa matches or exceeds proprietary frontier agents in 10 of 16 evaluation settings across 13 medical benchmarks spanning radiology, pathology, and clinical reasoning. Using over 25× fewer parameters than typical frontier models like Gemini-3, Meissa operates fully offline with ∼22×{\sim}22\times lower end-to-end latency compared to API-based deployment. Data, models, and environments are released at https://github.com/Schuture/Meissa.

1 Introduction
--------------

Multi-modal large language models (MM-LLMs) can combine medical images with free-form clinical text, enabling unified visual understanding and clinical reasoning[yao2022react, schick2023toolformer, shen2023hugginggpt]. When extended with _agentic capabilities_, the ability to take external actions such as invoking tools, requesting intermediate evidence, and coordinating specialist roles, these models can solve clinical tasks that are difficult for single-pass prediction systems[kim2024mdagents, fallahpour2025medrax, schmidgall2024agentclinic]. However, today’s strongest medical agents are usually powered by proprietary frontier models (e.g., GPT[hurst2024gpt], Gemini[team2023gemini], Claude[anthropic2024claude3]) through cloud APIs. This deployment pattern is often incompatible with real clinical constraints: patient data must remain on-premise, repeated API calls increase cost, and multi-step interaction adds latency that can disrupt clinical workflows.

This paper is motivated by a simple tension: frontier agents are capable, but hard to deploy. We ask: _Can we transfer the action-taking behavior of frontier medical agents into a model that can run fully offline?_ Here, _agentic behavior_ refers to a model’s _interaction policy_: deciding whether to answer directly or to take external actions, and if it acts, choosing a sequence of actions based on observed outcomes. This differs from prior works distilling single-pass _reasoning ability_[wei2022chain, gunasekar2023textbooks, zhou2023lima, saab2024capabilities] from larger models to smaller models, and from those learning _tool-use_ in a single fixed pipeline[zheng2025deepeyes, hong2025deepeyesv2, jiang2025ophiuchus], because practical medical settings involve multiple interaction modes (direct reasoning, tool chains, visual feedback loops, role-based consultation, and multi-turn clinical questioning).

The core challenge is the lack of structured supervision for agentic behavior. Although frontier models exhibit strong agentic behaviors, there is currently no publicly available training corpus designed to teach models how to behave as medical agents. Existing benchmarks provide final answers but do not specify whether external interaction was necessary or how actions should evolve based on intermediate observations. On the other hand, existing agent systems generate agentic trajectories (reasoning and action traces) within isolated environments, each with its own format and protocol, making it difficult to construct unified supervision across heterogeneous interaction modes.

To address this gap, we design a data synthesis pipeline that systematically constructs supervision for agentic behavior along three dimensions. First, we introduce a unified _state–action–observation_ representation that models multiple interaction modes within a single formalism. Therefore, we avoid environment-specific formats and enable cross-environment learning within one model. Second, to teach _when_ to act, we propose a _three-tier stratification strategy_ driven by the model’s own errors. Queries that the model already solves become direct-reasoning trajectories (tier 1); queries solvable by a stronger frontier model without tools become enhanced reasoning trajectories (tier 2); and only the hardest residual samples trigger full agent environments (tier 3). This stratified supervision implicitly encodes the decision boundary between direct inference and external interaction. Third, to teach _how_ to act within environments, we construct complementary _prospective_ and _retrospective_ trajectories. Prospective traces are recorded during forward interaction and capture exploratory decision-making under real observations. Retrospective traces preserve the same action sequence but provide clean hindsight explanations. Together, they provide stable execution supervision across heterogeneous medical agent environments.

Based on this pipeline, we train Meissa 1 1 1 Named after λ\lambda Orionis (Meissa), the central anchor of the Orion constellation, our model functions as a unified core that coordinates heterogeneous tools and sub-agents in various medical agent environments., a 4B-parameter multi-modal medical agent initialized from Qwen3-VL-4B model and distilled from approximately 40K frontier multimodal agent’s (Gemini-3-flash) trajectories. Training requires only ∼{\sim}12 hours on 8×\times A6000 GPUs. Despite its lightweight size, Meissa is competitive with frontier models using over 25× fewer parameters across 13 medical benchmarks ([Sec.˜4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S4.SS2 "4.2 Meissa Is Competitive with Frontier Models ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")), matches or exceeds them in 10 of 16 evaluation settings, achieves near-oracle strategy selection, and reduces end-to-end latency by approximately 22×\times ([Sec.˜4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S4.SS3 "4.3 Strategy Selection: Learning When to Act ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")) compared to cloud-based agent deployment.

In summary, our work makes three contributions: (1) we introduce a unified trajectory representation and targeted synthesis pipeline for constructing supervision of medical agentic behavior across heterogeneous environments; (2) we show Meissa, a lightweight medical agentic model, can make complex agentic behaviors available for offline deployment with low cost and inference latency; (3) we provide extensive empirical evidence that Meissa learns meaningful policy and matches closed-source models on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmarks.

2 Related Work
--------------

### 2.1 Medical Multi-modal Large Language Models

Medical MM-LLMs have evolved from specialized architectures[li2023llava, luo2023biomedgpt, chen2024chexagent, lin2023pmc, wu2024pmc, luo2022biogpt] to adaptations of general-purpose foundations[saab2024capabilities, yang2024advancing, tu2024towards]. LLaVA-Med[li2023llava] demonstrated biomedical VQA through instruction-tuned visual chat[liu2023visual], CheXagent[chen2024chexagent] specialized in chest X-ray interpretation, and HuatuoGPT-Vision[chen2024towards] injected medical visual knowledge at scale. Med-PaLM[singhal2023large, singhal2025toward], Med-Gemini[tu2024towards], and RadFM[wu2025towards] further pushed performance through model scaling and proprietary data. However, these models operate as single-pass reasoners without the ability to invoke tools or adaptively route queries based on difficulty.

### 2.2 Tool-Augmented Reasoning and Its Medical Applications

ReAct[yao2022react] established reasoning-action interleaving; FireAct[chen2023fireact] and AgentTuning[zeng2024agenttuning] showed that fine-tuning on agent trajectories generalizes agentic capabilities across tasks. In the visual domain, DeepEyes[zheng2025deepeyes] incentivized “thinking with images” via RL, and Ophiuchus[jiang2025ophiuchus] adapted this for medical imaging through cold-start SFT plus agentic RL. In the medical domain, MedRAX[fallahpour2025medrax] integrated specialized tools for chest X-ray reasoning. Closely related, STeP[chen2025training] and STAR[wu2026internalizing] train student models from teacher trajectories via trajectory-aligned SFT, and MedVistaGym[lu2026medvistagym] frames tool-augmented medical reasoning as an RL problem. Our work differs in distilling agentic behavior _across four heterogeneous environments_ into a single model, and in showing that SFT alone can match RL pipelines at substantially lower compute.

### 2.3 Multi-Agent Systems for Medicine

MDAgents[kim2024mdagents] dispatches queries to solo experts or multi-agent debate panels based on difficulty; MedAgentSim[almansoori2025medagentsim] and AgentClinic[schmidgall2024agentclinic] created simulated clinical environments for multi-turn diagnosis. Park et al.[park2023generative] demonstrated emergent behaviors from generative agent simulations, Du et al.[du2024improving] showed debate improves factual accuracy, and Society of Mind[zhuge2025mindstorms] explored hierarchical organization. All require frontier LLM backends at inference. We instead distill multi-agent interaction policies into a single model.

### 2.4 Learned Routing, Selective Prediction, and Early Exit

A model that can invoke external tools must also learn _when not to_. RouteLLM[ongroutellm] and cascade routing[dekoninckunified] train lightweight routers to select between a cheap and an expensive LLM; confidence-token methods[chuang2025learning] embed routing inside the model itself. Selective classification[geifman2017selective] provides theoretical grounding for risk-coverage trade-offs. Our work differs in learning routing _implicitly_ via stratified trajectory supervision. The model’s generated reasoning trajectory determines interaction depth without an explicit router, and in treating routing as a continuous choice over depth (T=0 T{=}0 to T>10 T{>}10) across four heterogeneous environments, a setting not addressed by existing frameworks.

3 Method
--------

We frame the training of Meissa as _agentic behavior distillation_: learning a policy over _interaction depth_, which is the number of external actions an agent takes before producing an answer. This single variable unifies direct reasoning (d​e​p​t​h=0 depth=0), single-tool queries (d​e​p​t​h=1 depth=1), multi-step tool chains, multi-agent debate, and clinical simulation into a continuous spectrum. We first define a trajectory formalism that captures this spectrum ([Sec.˜3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.SS1 "3.1 Agentic Behavior Modeling ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")), then describe how stratified trajectories teach strategy selection ([Sec.˜3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.SS2 "3.2 Behavior Distillation via Stratified Trajectory Supervision ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")), the agent environments that serve as trajectory sources ([Sec.˜3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.SS3 "3.3 Agent Environments and Trajectory Sources ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")), and the prospective-retrospective supervision that teaches strategy execution ([Sec.˜3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.SS4 "3.4 Prospective-Retrospective Supervision ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")). [Figure˜1](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.F1 "In 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence") provides an overview.

### 3.1 Agentic Behavior Modeling

We model all agent interactions from direct reasoning to complex multi-agent collaboration within a unified trajectory formalism. An _agentic trajectory_ is a sequence:

τ=[(s 0,a 0,o 1),(s 1,a 1,o 2),…,(s T−1,a T−1,o T)],\tau=[(s_{0},a_{0},o_{1}),(s_{1},a_{1},o_{2}),\ldots,(s_{T-1},a_{T-1},o_{T})],(1)

where state s t s_{t} is the conversation context up to step t t, a t a_{t} is an action, and o t o_{t} is the observation returned by the environment. Concretely, each action a t a_{t} is serialized as either a <|call|> turn (a JSON-formatted action) or an <|assistant|> turn for the final answer. <|observation|> turn is returned by tools or sub-agents in JSON format. Inference terminates when the model emits an <|assistant|> turn or reaches T max T_{\max}; full schemas are in the supplementary.

The _interaction depth_ T T of a trajectory is the key variable that unifies all agent behaviors. Direct reasoning corresponds to T=0 T{=}0 (a single final-answer). For Meissa, we set T max=4 T_{\max}=4 for continuous tool calling; T max=6 T_{\max}=6 for interleaved image-text reasoning; and multi-agent collaboration and clinical simulation produce the deepest trajectories (T max=12 T_{\max}=12). Learning _when_ to produce a trajectory of a given depth, and _how_ to act at each step, is the core objective of agentic behavior distillation.

We train the policy π θ​(a t∣s t)\pi_{\theta}(a_{t}\mid s_{t}) based on trajectories generated by a frontier model, where next-action prediction reduces to conditional language modeling. This separates the _learning problem_ from the _environment details_, enabling a single model to generalize across environments. Critically, behavioral cloning over _ordered_ trajectories learns causal decision-making: the model predicts each action conditioned on preceding observations. (Verified on [Sec.˜4.4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S4.SS4.SSS2 "4.4.2 Policy Robustness: Causal Decision-Making vs. Pattern Imitation. ‣ 4.4 Ablation and Robustness: Learning How to Act ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")).

### 3.2 Behavior Distillation via Stratified Trajectory Supervision

The key insight for teaching strategy selection is that not all queries require the same interaction depth. Rather than uniformly generating complex agentic trajectories for all samples, we stratify trajectory supervision using the model’s own errors as a curriculum signal[bengio2009curriculum]:

Tier 1: Direct Reasoning Trajectories. We first run the student model ℳ S\mathcal{M}_{S} (Qwen3-VL-4B[bai2025qwen3]) directly on the training set 𝒟 train\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}. Samples answered correctly yield direct reasoning trajectories (T=0 T{=}0, no external interaction):

𝒟 direct={(x,τ direct)∣(x,y)∈𝒟 train,ℳ S​(x)=y}.\mathcal{D}_{\text{direct}}=\{(x,\tau_{\text{direct}})\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}},\;\mathcal{M}_{S}(x)=y\}.(2)

These trajectories supervise the model to answer efficiently when its parametric knowledge suffices, learning _when not to act_.

Tier 2: Enhanced Reasoning Trajectories. The student’s incorrect samples are re-processed by a stronger teacher model ℳ T\mathcal{M}_{T} (Gemini-3-flash [team2023gemini]), again without external interaction:

𝒟 enhanced={(x,τ enhanced)∣(x,y)∈𝒟 train∖𝒟 direct,ℳ T​(x)=y}.\mathcal{D}_{\text{enhanced}}=\{(x,\tau_{\text{enhanced}})\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\setminus\mathcal{D}_{\text{direct}},\;\mathcal{M}_{T}(x)=y\}.(3)

These trajectories demonstrate stronger reasoning that the student could not produce, but remain direct reasoning (T=0 T{=}0).

Tier 3: Agentic Trajectories. The hardest residual samples are beyond both the student’s and teacher’s direct reasoning capacity. They are processed by the teacher within agent environments ([Sec.˜3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.SS3 "3.3 Agent Environments and Trajectory Sources ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")):

𝒟 agentic={(x,τ agentic)∣(x,y)∈𝒟 residual,𝒜 T​(x)=y},\mathcal{D}_{\text{agentic}}=\{(x,\tau_{\text{agentic}})\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{residual}},\;\mathcal{A}_{T}(x)=y\},(4)

where 𝒜 T\mathcal{A}_{T} denotes the teacher operating within an agent environment and residual erroneous samples 𝒟 residual=𝒟 train∖(𝒟 direct∪𝒟 enhanced)\mathcal{D}_{\text{residual}}=\mathcal{D}_{\text{train}}\setminus(\mathcal{D}_{\text{direct}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\text{enhanced}}). Only these samples produce full multi-step trajectories with external interaction.

The complete data generation procedure, including retry logic, per-tier statistics, and discard handling, is detailed in Algorithm 1 in supplementary material. The final dataset contains ∼{\sim}40K trajectories: 8.2K direct (Tier 1), 9.8K enhanced (Tier 2), and 23.9K agentic (Tier 3).

The combined supervision teaches strategy selection as an emergent behavior: simple queries are associated with direct trajectories, complex queries with multi-step ones, naturally inducing a difficulty-aware routing policy. At inference, the model’s first generated action implicitly determines the strategy.

### 3.3 Agent Environments and Trajectory Sources

The agentic trajectories in 𝒟 agentic\mathcal{D}_{\text{agentic}} are collected from four agent environments, each contributing trajectories with distinct interaction patterns ([Fig.˜2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S3.F2 "In 3.3.1 Visual information in trajectories. ‣ 3.3 Agent Environments and Trajectory Sources ‣ 3 Method ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")). These environments serve as _trajectory sources_ rather than architectural components of Meissa itself. Their diversity ensures that the learned policy generalizes.

#### 3.3.1 Visual information in trajectories.

In tool-calling trajectories, the initial state s 0 s_{0} contains the input image as <image> tokens; observations return structured text (classification labels, bounding boxes, reports). In interleaved image-text trajectories, observations additionally contain _new images_ like cropped regions or segmentation overlays injected as additional <image> tokens, creating a multi-round visual reasoning chain. Multi-agent environments provide <image> tokens for lead agent and each sub-agent at the beginning of the debate. For clinical simulation, Meissa can request either structured examination results or images as <image> tokens as needed in each call.

#### 3.3.2 Action Space of the Agent Environments.

*   •Continuous Tool Calling[fallahpour2025medrax]. The action space consists of eight medical imaging tools: a t∈{a_{t}\in\{Classifier, Segmentation, CheXagentVQA, LLaVA-Med, PhraseGrounding, ReportGenerator, DICOMProcessor, ImageVisualizer}\}, each serialized as a <|call|> turn with JSON arguments. Observations o t+1 o_{t+1} return structured text: classification probabilities, bounding box coordinates, segmentation statistics, or free-text reports. 
*   •Interleaved Thinking with Images[zheng2025deepeyes, hong2025deepeyesv2, jiang2025ophiuchus]. Actions select from vision manipulation tools: a t∈{ZoomIn,SAM2,BioMedParse,OCR,Terminate}a_{t}\in\{\texttt{ZoomIn},\allowbreak\texttt{SAM2},\allowbreak\texttt{BioMedParse},\allowbreak\texttt{OCR},\allowbreak\texttt{Terminate}\}. Unlike tool calling, observations o t+1 o_{t+1} contain _new images_, creating multi-round visual reasoning chains. The trajectory terminates when the model emits Terminate with the final answer. 
*   •Multi-Agent Collaboration[kim2024mdagents]. Actions are structured role-based queries: a t a_{t} consists of a difficulty assessment, expert recruitment, expert analysis, multi-round debate contributions, or moderator synthesis. Observations o t+1 o_{t+1} aggregate expert responses (analysis or debate argument). Debate protocols, persona prompts, and synthesis rules are in the supplementary. 
*   •Clinical Simulation[schmidgall2024agentclinic, almansoori2025medagentsim]. Actions select from clinical information-gathering tools: a t∈{RequestPhysicalExam,RequestTest,Terminate}a_{t}\in\{\texttt{RequestPhysicalExam},\allowbreak\texttt{RequestTest},\allowbreak\texttt{Terminate}\}. The initial state s 0 s_{0} contains the patient presentation (demographics, history, symptoms). Observations o t+1 o_{t+1} return clinical findings (e.g., “Auscultation: bilateral basal crackles”), lab results (e.g., “Troponin I: 0.4 ng/mL”), or radiology images. The trajectory terminates with Terminate for the final diagnosis. 

### 3.4 Prospective-Retrospective Supervision

Learning _when_ to act is addressed by stratified trajectories; here we address learning _how_ to act effectively within agent environments. The key insight is that a single correct trajectory can be decomposed into two complementary supervision signals that teach different aspects of execution policy.

Prospective (forward) trajectories are recorded during real-time inference: the teacher reasons forward, potentially revising strategy upon unexpected observations. Only correct trajectories are retained, teaching an _exploration policy_.

Retrospective (backward) trajectories are generated _given_ each prospective trajectory: a recap agent re-narrates the reasoning while preserving the original action sequence, producing a clean post-hoc narrative that teaches an _optimal execution policy_. The combined training set is:

𝒟 SFT=𝒟 direct∪𝒟 enhanced∪{τ pro​(x),τ retro​(x,y)∣(x,y)∈𝒟 agentic}.\mathcal{D}_{\text{SFT}}=\mathcal{D}_{\text{direct}}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\text{enhanced}}\cup\{\tau^{\text{pro}}(x),\,\tau^{\text{retro}}(x,y)\mid(x,y)\in\mathcal{D}_{\text{agentic}}\}.(5)

During auditing, retrospective recaps that are over-simplified or over-complicated are discarded; approximately 8% of generated recaps are filtered. We did not observe significant gains from depth- or difficulty-based weighting in preliminary experiments. A detailed comparison of the two supervision modes and trajectory validation details (structural and behavioral filtering) are in the supplementary.

### 4.4 Ablation and Robustness: Learning _How_ to Act

#### 4.4.1 Distillation Strategy Ablations.

[Table˜4](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S4.T4 "In 4.3 Strategy Selection: Learning When to Act ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence") shows all three tiers are necessary: Tier 1 preserves parametric knowledge; Tier 2 bridges direct and agentic reasoning (5.5 pt gain over Tier 1+3); Tier 3 provides agentic exemplars. 5 Discussion and Conclusion
---------------------------

We presented Meissa, a 4B-parameter medical agent trained through agentic behavior distillation. Stratified trajectory supervision teaches _when_ to act; prospective-retrospective supervision teaches _how_ to act; a unified trajectory formalism enables learning across tool calling, visual reasoning, multi-agent debate, and clinical simulation. Trained on ∼{\sim}40K trajectories, Meissa is competitive with frontier models across 13 benchmarks while operating fully offline.

Why SFT over RL? Our framework is intentionally SFT-only. While RL can sharpen stopping behavior via outcome-based rewards[lu2026medvistagym], it faces two practical limitations. First, existing RL approaches are each tied to a single agent framework: DeepEyesV2 optimizes tool-calling via RL[hong2025deepeyesv2], and MARTI addresses multi-agent coordination[zhang2025marti]; none can jointly learn policies across the four heterogeneous environments Meissa operates in. Second, RL requires extensive online rollouts with live tool environments, incurring high compute cost and training variance that hinder reproducibility. Our stratified SFT achieves near-oracle routing ([Tab.˜3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#S4.T3 "In 4.2 Meissa Is Competitive with Frontier Models ‣ 4 Experiments ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")) with deterministic, compute-efficient training (∼{\sim}12 GPU-hours), and naturally scales across environments through trajectory unification.

Deployment considerations. Deploying Meissa clinically requires human-in-the-loop safeguards: the model currently lacks calibrated uncertainty estimation and an abstention mechanism for out-of-distribution queries. Integrating selective prediction[geifman2017selective] to enable principled deferral is an important future direction.

The MDAgents environment implements adaptive complexity routing with three levels:

*   •Basic: Single-expert direct answer. 
*   •Intermediate: Multi-agent debate with recruited specialists. Includes expert recruitment, individual analysis, multi-round debate, and moderator synthesis. 
*   •Advanced: Multidisciplinary team consultation. 

Trajectories from this environment produce 8 distinct SFT types (difficulty assessment, expert recruitment, expert analysis, debate, synthesis, plus retrospective variants for the first two and last).

#### 0.A.3.4 Clinical Simulation (AgentClinic)

The simulation environment models doctor-patient encounters in OSCE (Objective Structured Clinical Examination) format:

*   •RequestPhysicalExam: Returns findings from a specified physical examination. 
*   •RequestTest: Returns results of a diagnostic test (labs, imaging). 
*   •Terminate: Issues the final diagnosis. 

Patient agents are scripted from structured clinical vignettes (MedQA →\to OSCE conversion via Gemini, MIMIC-IV EHR records). These produce the longest trajectories (up to 10+ actions) as the doctor agent gathers clinical evidence iteratively.

### 0.A.4 Prospective vs. Retrospective Supervision

[Table˜9](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#Pt0.A1.T9 "In 0.A.4 Prospective vs. Retrospective Supervision ‣ Appendix 0.A Supplementary Material ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence") provides a detailed comparison of the two supervision modes.

Table 9: Prospective vs. retrospective trajectory comparison.

| Dimension | Prospective (Forward) | Retrospective (Backward) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generation time | During real-time inference | After correct answer is known |
| Answer knowledge | Agent does _not_ know y y | Agent is given y y as context |
| Uncertainty level | High (planning, hypotheses, contingencies) | Low (hindsight rationalization) |
| Error content | May contain wrong assumptions, backtracking | Clean, logically consistent |
| Reasoning style | Process-driven, exploratory | Result-driven, decisive |

| ID vs. OOD | Better OOD generalization | Better ID performance |

Retrospective generation process. Given a prospective trajectory τ pro\tau^{\text{pro}} with correct answer y y, the recap agent receives:

1.   1.The original question x x, 
2.   2.The correct answer y y, 
3.   3.The sequence of observations from τ pro\tau^{\text{pro}} (tool outputs, agent responses, patient replies). 

The agent then re-narrates the reasoning while preserving the original action sequence. The same tools are called in the same order, but the reasoning between actions is tightened into a clean, post-hoc narrative that explains _why_ each action was taken given what was ultimately learned.

### 0.A.5 Trajectory Validation Pipeline

All generated trajectories undergo a two-stage validation pipeline before inclusion in the training set.

#### 0.A.5.1 Structural Filtering

Structural filters verify format compliance:

*   •Correctness: Only trajectories arriving at the correct answer are retained (answer​(τ)=y\text{answer}(\tau)=y). 
*   •Turn-type integrity: Conversations must follow the human→\to (function_call→\to observation)∗→\to gpt pattern. 
*   •Tool call validity: Each function_call must reference a tool defined in the system prompt with valid JSON arguments. 
*   •Terminate presence: Every trajectory must end with a termination action (tool-specific: Terminate for thinking with image and clinical simulation, [FINAL] marker for MedRAX, synthesis turn for MDAgents). 
*   •Image alignment: The number of <image> tokens must match the number of entries in the images list. 
*   •Length bounds: Trajectories exceeding 10,000 characters total or containing >>4 tool calls (MedRAX) / >>6 rounds (thinking with image) / >>12 rounds (multi-agent collaboration & clinical simulation) are discarded. 

#### 0.A.5.2 Behavioral Filtering

Behavioral filters detect semantic quality issues:

*   •Hallucination detection: Scan <think> blocks for medical keywords (pathology names, anatomical terms) not present in the original question or tool outputs. If a medical term appears in reasoning but not in any input or observation, the trajectory is flagged and reviewed. 
*   •Repetition loops: Consecutive duplicate tool calls (same tool, same arguments) are detected and the trajectory is discarded. 
*   •Truncation detection: Per-type checks for unclosed markdown formatting, missing terminal punctuation, or incomplete JSON structures indicate truncated API responses. 
*   •Class balance: Downsample majority classes (e.g., “yes” answers in binary VQA are kept at 1/3 rate) to prevent trivial shortcut learning. 
*   •Content length: Individual turns with <<10 characters of meaningful content (excluding formatting) are rejected. 
*   •Thought field presence: For agentic trajectories, each function_call must contain a non-empty <think> block articulating reasoning. 

Quality audit. After automated filtering, a random 10% sample of remaining trajectories is reviewed by Claude Opus[anthropic2024claude3] for hallucinations, logical inconsistencies, and low-quality reasoning. Trajectories flagged by the audit are manually inspected and discarded if confirmed.

#### 0.A.7.2 Trajectory Serialization

All trajectories are serialized in ShareGPT format with four turn types:

*   •human: Initial state containing the user question and optional <image> tokens. 
*   •function_call: Agent action, containing a <think> reasoning block followed by a JSON tool invocation ({"name": "...", "arguments": {...}}). 
*   •observation: Environment feedback, containing tool output text and optionally new <image> tokens (for Thinking with Image). 
*   •gpt: Final action, containing a <think> reasoning block followed by the answer (marked with [FINAL] in MedRAX). 

Tool definitions are embedded in the tools field as JSON schemas, and image paths are listed in the images field. The system prompt specifies the agent’s role and available tools.

*   •Inference: vLLM serving on a single A6000. Average inference time per query: 1.2s (direct) to 10.6s (agentic).

##### Pattern 2: Recursion Limit from Infinite Tool-Calling Loops.

Example. Using the same chest X-ray as Pattern 1 ([Fig.˜6](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#Pt0.A1.F6 "In Pattern 1: Over-Invocation of Tools on Simple Questions. ‣ 0.A.10.1 Tool-Calling Environment (MedRAX on ChestAgentBench) ‣ 0.A.10 Qualitative Analysis: Failure and Recovery Patterns ‣ Appendix 0.A Supplementary Material ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence")), a different question asks: “Which of the following is the most likely explanation for the normal appearance of the chest X-rays in this patient?” (correct: bronchiolitis obliterans can present with normal X-rays). The model entered a tool-calling loop, repeatedly invoking tools searching for abnormalities that do not exist. After 25 iterations (the LangGraph recursion limit), the agent was terminated without producing an answer.

Analysis. The model failed to recognize that the question itself implies the X-ray is normal, and no tool would find abnormalities. This illustrates a failure mode where the model cannot learn to _stop_ tool calling when the answer requires reasoning about the absence of findings rather than detecting their presence.

The trajectory exhausted the maximum round limit with no answer produced.

This failure mode is largely resolved in Meissa’s trained version, where 96% of queries are resolved in ≤\leq 1 round.

The question “Is gastrointestinal tissue present?” ([Fig.˜10](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.09018#Pt0.A1.F10 "In Pattern 6: Tool False Positives Overriding Correct Visual Judgment. ‣ 0.A.10.2 Interleaved Image-Text Environment (Thinking with Image on PathVQA) ‣ 0.A.10 Qualitative Analysis: Failure and Recovery Patterns ‣ Appendix 0.A Supplementary Material ‣ Meissa: Multi-modal Medical Agentic Intelligence"); ground truth: no) led Meissa to call BioMedParseTextSeg with query “gastrointestinal tissue.” The tool returned a large segmented area (121,312 pixels). ### 0.A.11 MDAgents Trajectory Examples

We present representative multi-agent collaboration trajectories from Meissa’s inference on PubMedQA, demonstrating the learned debate protocol.

#### 0.A.11.1 Example: Zero CLABSI Rate Sustainability (Correct, Unanimous)

Question: “Is zero central line-associated bloodstream infection rate sustainable?” (Context: a NICU achieved zero CLABSI for >>370 consecutive days, then >>600 days after a brief uptick.)

Ground truth: Yes. Prediction: Yes (correct).

Difficulty Assessment (a 0 a_{0}): The model classifies this as intermediate, reasoning: “This question requires synthesis of statistical trends and operational policy management across multiple domains.”

Expert Recruitment (a 1 a_{1}): Four specialists are recruited: (1)Pediatrician, (2)Neonatologist, (3)Infectious Disease Specialist, (4)Critical Care Specialist.

Expert Analysis (a 2 a_{2}–a 5 a_{5}):

*   •_Pediatrician_: “Yes – the study demonstrated sustained zero CLABSI for over 370 consecutive days after implementing evidence-based practice bundles and checklists.” 
*   •_Neonatologist_: “Yes – despite a brief 3.3 per 1000 line-days increase in Q1 2013, the rate was successfully re-stabilized at zero after targeted interventions.” 
*   •_Critical Care Specialist_: “Yes – a 77% overall reduction over the 5-year study period demonstrates high sustainability.” 

Synthesis (a 6 a_{6}): “The implementation of evidence-based care bundles resulted in a sustained 600-day zero-rate, accompanied by a 77% overall reduction. Despite a temporary increase requiring targeted corrections, the protocols maintained efficacy through interdisciplinary collaboration.” Final answer: Yes.

#### 0.A.11.2 Example: Non-HDL-C as Cardiovascular Risk Predictor (Correct)

Question: “Is non-HDL-cholesterol a better predictor of long-term outcome after acute myocardial infarction compared to LDL-cholesterol?” (Context: Thai patients post-AMI; non-HDL-C >>130 mg/dL had HR 3.15 for MACE, while LDL-C >>100 mg/dL paradoxically had HR 0.42.)

Ground truth: Yes. Prediction: Yes (correct).

Expert Recruitment: Cardiologist, Cardiometabolic Specialist, Epidemiologist.

Cross-Expert Debate: All three experts independently concluded “Yes.” The Cardiometabolic Specialist highlighted the paradoxical LDL-C finding (HR 0.42) as a key reason to prefer non-HDL-C: “Elevated LDL-C was unexpectedly associated with reduced MACE risk, undermining its utility as a standalone predictor.” The Epidemiologist reinforced: “Non-HDL-C showed a 3.15-fold increased risk, far exceeding LDL-C’s predictive power.”

Moderator: Confirmed unanimous consensus.

#### 0.A.11.3 Example: Carotid Endarterectomy Risk in Octogenarians (Disagreement)

Question: “Are octogenarians at high risk for carotid endarterectomy?” (Context: study of 103 procedures in 95 octogenarians with only 4 neurologic complications and 0 deaths.)

Ground truth: No. Prediction: Yes (incorrect).

Expert Analysis: Cardiologist, Geriatrician, and Vascular Surgeon all concluded “No,” citing the 1.94% complication rate as far below the commonly cited 15–25% risk. However, during the debate phase, the Cardiologist briefly wavered, misinterpreting “selected high-risk patients” in the text as a diagnostic label rather than an enrollment criterion, before reverting to “No.”

Moderator Override: Despite the 3-to-0 expert consensus of “No,” the Moderator concluded “Yes,” overriding the correct expert majority. This represents a failure mode where the synthesis agent fails to faithfully implement the specified majority-vote protocol.

### 0.A.12 Clinical Simulation Trajectory Examples

#### 0.A.12.1 Example: Pulmonary Tuberculosis (Correct, 5 Tool Calls)

Patient Presentation: 55-year-old male with chronic cough (8 weeks), productive of clear sputum, unintentional weight loss (10 lbs/3 months), night sweats, decreased appetite. Former smoker (20 pack-years, quit 5 years ago).

Ground truth: Pulmonary Tuberculosis. Prediction: Tuberculosis (correct).

1.   1.a 0 a_{0}: RequestPhysicalExam(Vital_Signs) →\to o 1 o_{1}: Temperature 36.8∘C, BP 130/80, HR 82, RR 18.

> Reasoning: “Vital signs are within normal limits. No acute systemic infection or shock. The chronic cough, weight loss, and night sweats remain concerning for TB or lung cancer.” 
2.   2.a 1 a_{1}: RequestPhysicalExam(Respiratory_Examination) →\to o 2 o_{2}: Bilateral basal crackles (right-predominant), dullness at right base, increased tactile fremitus on right lower lobe.

> Reasoning: “These findings are highly suggestive of consolidation or mass in the right lower lobe, consistent with pneumonia, tuberculosis, or lung cancer.” 
3.   3.a 2 a_{2}: RequestTest(Chest X-Ray) →\to o 3 o_{3}: Right upper lobe consolidation with cavitation, no pleural effusion.

> Reasoning: “Right upper lobe consolidation with cavitation – a classic radiographic finding in TB. Cavitation is more commonly seen in TB than in malignancy, which typically presents with solid masses.” 
4.   4.a 3 a_{3}: RequestTest(Sputum Analysis) →\to o 4 o_{4}: AFB smear positive, culture pending.

> Reasoning: “AFB smear positivity is diagnostic for TB in the context of clinical symptoms and radiographic findings. No further tests required.” 
5.   5.a 4 a_{4}: Terminate(diagnosis=‘‘Tuberculosis’’). 

Analysis. The trajectory demonstrates systematic clinical reasoning: vitals →\to physical exam →\to imaging →\to confirmatory lab. A differential diagnosis (TB vs. lung cancer) is maintained throughout and resolved by the chest X-ray findings (cavitation favoring TB) and AFB smear confirmation.

#### 0.A.12.2 Example: Iron Deficiency Anemia (Correct, 3 Tool Calls)

Patient Presentation: 55-year-old male with persistent fatigue (2 months), unintentional weight loss (10 lbs), occasional headaches, general weakness. Smoker (10 cigarettes/day). History of hypertension.

Ground truth: Iron Deficiency Anemia. Prediction: Iron deficiency anemia (correct).

1.   1.a 0 a_{0}: RequestTest(Complete_Blood_Count) →\to o 1 o_{1}: WBC 6,500, Hgb 10.2 g/dL (low), Hct 31% (low), Plt 290K.

> Reasoning: “Microcytic anemia identified. Differential: iron deficiency, chronic disease, or hemolytic processes.” 
2.   2.a 1 a_{1}: RequestTest(Basic_Metabolic_Panel) →\to o 2 o_{2}: Glucose 85, Na 138, K 4.2, Cr 1.0 (normal).

> Reasoning: “BMP normal, ruling out metabolic causes. Iron studies needed to confirm iron deficiency.” 
3.   3.a 2 a_{2}: RequestTest(Additional_Tests) →\to o 3 o_{3}: Serum iron 25 μ\mu g/dL (low), TIBC 400 μ\mu g/dL (high), ferritin 10 ng/mL (low).

4.   4.a 3 a_{3}: Terminate(diagnosis=‘‘Iron deficiency anemia’’). 

Analysis. The model efficiently skips physical exam (appropriate for fatigue/weight-loss presentations) and proceeds directly to lab workup. Each test narrows the differential systematically: CBC confirms anemia, BMP excludes metabolic causes, iron studies confirm the specific etiology.

#### 0.A.12.3 Example: Acute Myocardial Infarction (Correct, 6 Tool Calls)
