###### Abstract

Function calling (FC) empowers large language models (LLMs) and autonomous agents to interface with external tools, a critical capability for solving complex, real-world problems. As this ability becomes increasingly central to advanced AI systems, the need for high-quality, multi-turn training data to develop and refine it cannot be overstated. Existing data synthesis methods, such as random environment sampling or multi-agent role-playing, are not powerful enough to generate high-quality data in real-world environments. Practical challenges come in three folds: targeted data synthesis, hard query construction, and multi-turn logical dependency. To address these structural deficiencies, we present FunReason-MT, a novel data synthesis framework for real-world multi-turn tool use. FunReason-MT resolves the complexity barrier in multi-turn FC data by employing 1) _Environment-API Graph Interactions_ to gather varied high-quality trajectories with targeted tool, 2) _Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis_ to simplify hard query construction, and 3) _Guided Iterative Chain_ for sophisticated CoT generation. Evaluations on Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard (BFCLv3) demonstrate the power of our framework: a 4B model built upon FunReason-MT generated data achieves state-of-the-art performance among comparable-sized models. Further performance improvements on BFCLv4 confirm that FunReason-MT provides a reliable and robust source for agentic learning.

1 Introduction
---

![Image 6: Refer to caption](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2/x6.png)

Figure 2: We summarize the core challenges in multi-turn function-calling data generation into three aspects.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are profoundly transforming human-computer interaction, largely through Function Calling (FC), the pivotal technology enabling LLMs to interface with external tools (WANG et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib10)). Although contemporary foundation models possess inherent tool-use capability, unlocking more advanced applications in this domain is hindered by a critical bottleneck: The construction of effective, high-quality data, particularly for multi-turn trajectories. Existing data generation research predominantly relies on constrained methodologies, such as random sampling within environments or Multi-Agent System (MAS) role-playing simulations (Huang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib4); Prabhakar et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib8); Liu et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib6); Zeng et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib12)). Crucially, random sampling fails to capture rare, complex events, while MAS role-playing often defaults to simplistic “happy-path” scenarios or suffers from a lack of true diversity (Han et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib1)). Thus these approaches inherently produce data that is limited in both effectiveness and diversity, resulting in a substantial training performance ceiling.

As illustrated in Figure [2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#S1.F2 "Figure 2 ‣ 1 Introduction ‣ FunReason-MT Technical Report: Advanced Data Synthesis Solution for Real-world Multi-Turn Tool-use"), we categorize the challenge in multi-turn FC data generation into three critical, interconnected issues that diminish data quality and model stability. 1) Targeted Data Synthesis. Existing random sampling techniques fail to controllably and targetedly construct trajectories requiring the collaborative use of a target complex tool with others. 2) Hard Query Construction. Due to the modular inputs of individual tools rather than holistic integration, direct environmental sampling and MAS role-playing struggle to synthesize hard, logical-jump queries. 3) Multi-Turn Logical Dependency. In complex multi-turn dialogues, each step of the Chain-of-Thought (CoT) is a validation and reconstruction of the reliance on prior logic. Current Reasoning Large Language Models (RLLMs) often falter when generating CoT in unexplored environments, preventing the acquisition of complete and accurate multi-turn trajectories.

To fundamentally resolve these structural limitations, we introduce the Fun ction Call Reason ing M ulti-T urn (FunReason-MT) data synthesis framework. Our framework prioritizes robustness and efficiency, and incorporates three specific core components to tackle the outlined challenges.

*   •Environment-API Graph Interactions. We establish an API graph based on interdependencies between tools and environments, enabling the collection of multi-turn trajectories centered around a targeted complex API within varied scenarios, ultimately training models for targeted tool mastery and collaborative use. 
*   •Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis. We use the tool-state pairs collected during Environment-API Graph Interactions to construct an advanced tool that eliminates the need to input the modularized sub-tools when queries are generated in reverse. This inherently makes the execution of prerequisite tools implicit. 
*   •Guided Iterative Chain. We input the hard query, the set of sub-tools, and the advanced tool description to obtain CoT. We then implement an iterative feedback loop: The analysis identifying the specific reasons for any failure to match the ground truth is continuously integrated as a corrective hint until the CoT successfully leads to the right FC or meets the maximum attempts. 

We validate the FunReason-MT framework on the challenging Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard (BFCLv3), encompassing both single-turn and multi-turn tests ([Patil et al.,](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib7)). Experiments demonstrate that our data construction method consistently outperforms approaches based on MAS role-playing or random environmental sampling, and achieves state-of-the-art performance among comparable-sized models. This success affirms that FunReason-MT effectively overcomes the complexity barrier in multi-turn FC data generation, establishing an essential knowledge supplement for future LLM environment exploration. We also evaluate our models on BFCLv4, an out-of-distribution (OOD) benchmark, to further assess their agentic ability. Although our data is unrelated to the tasks evaluated by BFCLv4 (Web Search and Memory), our models still show improvement in these specific areas. We believe our study is well-positioned to lay a robust and enduring foundation that facilitates the future progression of agentic learning (Zhang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib13); Ke et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib5)).

2 Motivation
---

Our motivation stems from the insight that the identified “performance ceiling” is not merely a data quantity issue, but a fundamental failure of the existing data generation paradigm. We recognized that “bottom-up” approaches like random sampling and MAS role-playing are structurally incapable of producing the high-complexity, long-tail trajectories needed for true mastery. These methods merely hope for complexity to emerge from simple interactions, suffer from low controllability and poor reliability.

This drove us to devise a new, “top-down” construction methodology that can explicitly direct the model to master a specific complex tool within a targeted complex scenario. Our approach is motivated by the “why” and “how” of this shift: instead of hoping for targeted complex tool involved trajectories, we engineer them by modeling the environment’s structural dependencies (the API graph). Instead of sampling simple tool combinations, we guarantee complex, logical-jump queries by generating them in reverse. And finally, instead of accepting flawed reasoning, we enforce CoT accuracy through a guided feedback loop, thus directly targeting the three core challenges that plague existing methods and corresponding to solving the issues of reliability and complexity respectively.

4 Methodology
-------------

As shown in Figure [3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#S4.F3 "Figure 3 ‣ 4.1 Phase I: Environment-API Graph Interactions ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ FunReason-MT Technical Report: Advanced Data Synthesis Solution for Real-world Multi-Turn Tool-use"), our proposed FunReason-MT framework comprises three core phases: (1) Phase I: Environment-API Graph Interactions constructs a valid multi-step execution trace by sampling tool calls from the API Relation Graph (§[4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#S4.SS1 "4.1 Phase I: Environment-API Graph Interactions ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ FunReason-MT Technical Report: Advanced Data Synthesis Solution for Real-world Multi-Turn Tool-use")); (2) Phase II: Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis reverse-engineers a hard-to-solve data sample and synthesizing a corresponding challenging Hard Query (Q hard Q_{\text{hard}}) (§[4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#S4.SS2 "4.2 Phase II: Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ FunReason-MT Technical Report: Advanced Data Synthesis Solution for Real-world Multi-Turn Tool-use")); (3) Phase III: Guided Iterative Chain utilizes an iterative, self-correction loop to refine the CoT and ensure its logical consistency with the ground truth answer (§[4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#S4.SS3 "4.3 Phase III: Guided Iterative Chain ‣ 4 Methodology ‣ FunReason-MT Technical Report: Advanced Data Synthesis Solution for Real-world Multi-Turn Tool-use")).

### 4.1 Phase I: Environment-API Graph Interactions

A central challenge in generating multi-turn tool-use trajectories is to devise a sampling strategy that is both executable and purposefully directed. To achieve this, FunReason-MT framework is designed to satisfy two critical constraints simultaneously: execution correctness (_i.e._, respecting tool dependencies) and goal-directed efficiency (_i.e._, prioritizing T a T_{a}). We detail these two components below.

#### Formalizing Tool Call Legality

To ensure a valid execution sequence, we introduce a strict dependency constraint: Tool T i T_{i} is only callable if all its necessary preconditions, Prerequisite​(T i)\text{Prerequisite}(T_{i}), are satisfied by the set of already executed tools, 𝒯 called\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}}. We formalize this essential constraint using the tool legality check I I:

I​(T i,𝒯 called)=𝟏{Prerequisite​(T i)⊆𝒯 called},I(T_{i},\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}})=\mathbf{1}_{\{\text{Prerequisite}(T_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}}\}},(1)

where 𝟏{⋅}\mathbf{1}_{\{\cdot\}} is the indicator function, returning 1 if the condition is true and 0 otherwise.

#### Tool Sampling with Priority

While random sampling over the tool graph can satisfy execution correctness, it often fails to reach the target tool T a T_{a} efficiently. To address this, we introduce a Directed Sampler, denoted as Sampler, which explicitly biases the sampling process toward T a T_{a}. Specifically, Sampler adopts a greedy heuristic strategy that operates over the set of legal tools 𝒯 legal\mathcal{T}_{\text{legal}} and prioritizes those minimizing the graph distance dist​(⋅,⋅)\text{dist}(\cdot,\cdot) to T a T_{a}. The sampling policy of Sampler is formally defined as follows:

T s=Sampler​(T a,𝒯 called)={rand​(𝒯 legal),if​T a∈𝒯 called,T a,if​I​(T a,𝒯 called)=1∧T a∉𝒯 called,argmin T k​{dist​(T k,T a)},otherwise.T_{s}=\text{Sampler}(T_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}})=\begin{cases}\text{rand}(\mathcal{T}_{\text{legal}}),&\text{if }T_{a}\in\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}},\\ T_{a},&\text{if }I(T_{a},\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}})=1\land T_{a}\notin\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}},\\ \underset{T_{k}}{\operatorname{argmin}}\left\{\text{dist}(T_{k},T_{a})\right\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}(2)

The chosen tool T s T_{s} is executed using sampled parameters P s P_{s} as the call C s=(T s,P s)C_{s}=(T_{s},P_{s}). This action yields environmental feedback E i E_{i}, triggers an update to the system state 𝒮 j\mathcal{S}_{j}, and consequently updates the API Graph 𝒢\mathcal{G}, legal tools 𝒯 legal\mathcal{T}_{\text{legal}} and executed tools 𝒯 called\mathcal{T}_{\text{called}}. The resultant single-turn execution sequence is recorded as the trace: Turn i=(C 1,E 1,C 2,E 2,…,C M,E M)\text{Turn}_{i}=(C_{1},E_{1},C_{2},E_{2},\dots,C_{M},E_{M}).

### 4.2 Phase II: Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis

The execution trace Turn i\text{Turn}_{i} ensures correct tool execution, yet it fails to capture the abstract intent behind those operations. So we need a mechanism that encourages the model to reinterpret the entire trace as a single high-level abstraction. To realize this objective, Phase II first abstracts the trace into an advanced tool and then constructs a hard query that depends on this abstraction.

1.   1.Advanced Tool Generation. Given the execution trace Turn i\text{Turn}_{i}, the _Tooling Agent_ (A T A_{T}) synthesizes a single composite abstraction, denoted as the advanced tool T adv T_{\text{adv}}:

T adv=A T​(Turn i),T_{\text{adv}}=A_{T}\left(\text{Turn}_{i}\right),(3)

where A T A_{T} abstracts the multi-step execution trace into a unified high-level operation that encapsulates the functionality and interdependencies of all sub-tools within Turn i\text{Turn}_{i}. 
2.   2.Hard Query Construction. Conditioned on the synthesized advanced tool T adv T_{\text{adv}}, the _Querying Agent_ (A Q A_{Q}) generates a challenging hard query Q hard Q_{\text{hard}} that explicitly requires the use of T adv T_{\text{adv}} for resolution. Formally, the construction process is expressed as:

Q hard=A Q​(T adv,ϵ),Q_{\text{hard}}=A_{Q}\left(T_{\text{adv}},\epsilon\right),(4)

where ϵ\epsilon denotes a synthesis noise that increases task difficulty and encourages generalization. 

### 4.3 Phase III: Guided Iterative Chain

The construction of Q hard Q_{\text{hard}} promotes higher-level reasoning, but it also increases the risk of logical inconsistency or incomplete Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation. To mitigate these issues, Phase III introduces the Guided Iterative Chain, a feedback-driven refinement process that iteratively improves CoT quality and coherence until the reasoning converges to a reliable solution.

#### Initial Reasoning Attempt and Correction Loop

The _Reasoning Agent_ (A R A_{R}) begins by attempting to resolve the hard query, Q hard Q_{\text{hard}}, utilizing both the advanced tool description Des​(T adv)\text{Des}(T_{\text{adv}}) (denoted as Prompt(1)\text{Prompt}^{(1)} in the first attempt) and its primitive components 𝒯 sub\mathcal{T}_{\text{sub}}.

The k t​h k^{th} attempt yields the output {FC(k),CoT(k)}=A R​(Q hard,𝒯 sub,Prompt(k))\{\text{FC}^{(k)},\text{CoT}^{(k)}\}=A_{R}(Q_{\text{hard}},\mathcal{T}_{\text{sub}},\text{Prompt}^{(k)}), comprising a CoT and a Function Call. The generated function call (FC(k)\text{FC}^{(k)}) is subsequently validated against the known ground truth G G ( G=(C 1,C 2,…,C M)G=(C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{M}) ) using a verification function Validate:FC(k)×G→{Pass,Fail}\text{Validate}:\text{FC}^{(k)}\times G\to\{\text{Pass},\text{Fail}\}.

1.   1.Error Analysis (Upon Failure): If the current attempt fails, the _Critiquing Agent_ (A C A_{C}) analyzes the incorrect function call (FC(k)\text{FC}^{(k)}) with respect to the ground truth G G, identifies the specific error, and generates targeted corrective hint feedback: Error(k)=A C​(FC(k),G)\text{Error}^{(k)}=A_{C}(\text{FC}^{(k)},G). 
2.   2.Self-Correction: In this step, the _Reasoning Agent_ (A R A_{R}) first updates its context using the corrective hint Error(k)\text{Error}^{(k)} from the previous turn, forming an augmented prompt that explicitly conditions the next attempt on the identified error: Prompt(k+1)=Concat​(Prompt(k),Error(k))\text{Prompt}^{(k+1)}=\text{Concat}(\text{Prompt}^{(k)},\text{Error}^{(k)}). Given this augmented prompt, A R A_{R} is then re-invoked to generate a refined solution, {FC(k+1),CoT(k+1)}=A R​(Q hard,𝒯 sub,Prompt(k+1))\{\text{FC}^{(k+1)},\text{CoT}^{(k+1)}\}=A_{R}(Q_{\text{hard}},\mathcal{T}_{\text{sub}},\text{Prompt}^{(k+1)}). This self-correction step, together with error analysis, induces an iterative feedback loop that progressively improves subsequent attempts. 

The maximum allowed limit for this iterative correction process is K max K_{\max} attempts. A data sample is only retained if the validation function Validate​(FC(k),G)\text{Validate}(\text{FC}^{(k)},G) returns Pass before the iteration cap is reached.

### 4.4 Scaling from Single-Turn Trace to Multi-Turn Trajectory

The full pipeline is iterated N N times to scale the single-turn generation into a comprehensive, high-quality multi-turn trajectory, Traj.

The overall data generation process starts by initializing Traj as an empty set. The framework then sequentially executes the three phases for each of the N N required data rounds:

1.   Step 1:Environment API-Graph Interactions: A target tool T a T_{a} is chosen from the primitive tool set 𝒯\mathcal{T}. This choice dictates the generation of a complete execution trace Turn 1\text{Turn}_{1} within the simulation environment, following the procedure outlined in Phase 1. 
2.   Step 2:Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis: The successful trace is then gave to the LLM agents A T A_{T} and A Q A_{Q}. These agents collaborate to synthesize the Advanced Tool (T adv T_{\text{adv}}) and its corresponding Hard Query (Q hard Q_{\text{hard}}), thereby forming the core of the new data sample. 
3.   Step 3:Guided Iterative Chain: Immediately following synthesis, the data sample undergoes the crucial Guided Iterative Chain verification process. This step employs the reasoning and critiquing agents (A R,A C A_{R},A_{C}) to attempt solving the hard query and validate results against the ground truth answer G G. 

To ensure the resulting multi-turn trajectory is reliable and complex, the entire process detailed above will be repeated N N times.

5 Experiments
-------------

### 5.1 Experimental Setup

Training Details. Considering the length of multi-turn conversation and CoT, we use Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507 with 256K long-context (Yang et al., [2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib11)) as our base model. The collected trajectories are split at each assistant response and the model is only trained on the CoT and answer at current turn. We collect 17000 multi-turn samples, and to enhance the diversity of the dataset, we also jointly train model with function calling data from APIGen. We use Llama-Factory (Zheng et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib14)) and Verl (Sheng et al., [2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib9)) library to train models with implementations from Hao et al. ([2025b](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib3)) and Hao et al. ([2025a](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24645v2#bib.bib2)).

Benchmarks. Our primary evaluation is performed on the BFCLv3, which is designed to assess the model’s performance on both Single-Turn and Multi-Turn tool-calling tasks. To further evaluate the data influence on the model’s agentic capability, we extend our assessment to the Held-out (OOD) BFCLv4 benchmark. BFCLv4 is engineered for the evaluation of agentic performance with Web Search and Memory. We present all the results in terms of accuracy (%).

6 Conclusion
------------

In this technical report, we introduce FunReason-MT, a novel data synthesis framework designed to address critical bottlenecks in multi-turn function calling data generation. We identified that existing methods, such as random sampling and MAS role-playing, are insufficient, producing data that lacks diversity and fails to capture complex, logically-dependent trajectories. Our framework overcomes these limitations through three core components: Environment-API Graph Interactions for targeted data generation, Advanced Tool-Query Synthesis for creating logically complex queries, and a Guided Iterative Chain for robust CoT generation. Empirical validation on the BFCLv3 benchmark demonstrates that our approach achieves state-of-the-art performance among comparable-sized models. Furthermore, our model shows promising out-of-distribution generalization on BFCLv4, underscoring the robustness of our method. This research paves the way for developing more robust and stable models capable of complex reasoning and environment exploration.
