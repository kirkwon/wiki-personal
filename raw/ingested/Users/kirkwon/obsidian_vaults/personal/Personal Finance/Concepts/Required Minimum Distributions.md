# Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

## Quick Reference

### Field**: Personal Clients
### Type**: Retirement Tax Planning
### Purpose**: Understand RMD rules and strategies
### Key Concepts**: RMD calculation, penalty avoidance, strategic withdrawals
### Related Concepts**: [[tax-torpedoes]], [[retirement-planning]]

## What Are RMDs?

Required Minimum Distributions are the minimum amounts you must withdraw from your retirement accounts annually once you reach a certain age.

## Current RMD Rules

### Age Threshold
- **Previous Rule**: 70½ (traditional)
- **Current Rule**: 73 (born 1950-1959)
- **Future Rule**: 75 (born 1960+)

### Account Types Subject to RMDs
- Traditional IRAs
- 401(k), 403(b), 457(b) plans
- SEP IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs
- Inherited retirement accounts
- **NOT Subject**:
  - Roth IRAs (during original owner's lifetime)
  - Roth 401(k) (if still with employer)
  - Roth conversions in progress

## RMD Calculation Formula

```
RMD = Account Balance ÷ Distribution Period
```

### Calculation Examples

**Example 1: Single Account**
- Account value: $500,000
- Age 73, single life expectancy: 17.34
- RMD = $500,000 ÷ 17.34 = $28,840

**Example 2: Joint Life Expectancy**
- Account value: $500,000
- Age 73, spouse is 70 (life expectancy: 21.8)
- RMD = $500,000 ÷ 21.8 = $22,936

## RMD Deadlines and Timing

### Annual Deadline
- **Deadline**: December 31st of each year
- **Exception**: First RMD due April 1st of the year you turn 73
- **Penalty**: 25% of shortfall (reduced to 10% if corrected promptly)

### Quarterly Timing
- You can take RMDs in quarterly installments
- Total must be taken by December 31st
- Each quarter withdrawal is 25% of annual RMD

## RMD Strategies

### 1. Roth Conversion Strategy
**Before RMDs Start:**
- Convert traditional IRA to Roth to reduce future RMDs
- Pay taxes during working years when income is lower
- Remove RMD burden from heirs

**After RMDs Start:**
- Convert up to top of your tax bracket
- Be mindful of provisional income

### 2. Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs)
- Up to $105,000 per year (2024)
- Count toward RMD requirement
- Reduce taxable income
- Must be paid directly to charity

### 3. Aggressive Withdrawal Strategy
- Withdraw more than RMD during good market years
- Withdraw less during poor market years
- Maintain consistent income stream
- Avoid selling assets at market bottoms

### 4. Asset Location Strategy
- Hold growth assets in Roth accounts (tax-free growth)
- Hold income assets in traditional accounts (tax-deferred)
- Withdraw from traditional accounts first (lower tax rates)

### 5. Inherited IRA Strategy
- Beneficiaries must take RMDs based on their life expectancy
- Stretch IRA rules changed for non-spousal beneficiaries
- Most beneficiaries must deplete account within 10 years

## Common RMD Mistakes

### ❌ Forgetting RMDs
- Set automatic calendar reminders
- Use multiple accounts, forget one account
- Penalty is 25% - serious consequence

### ❌ Incorrect Calculations
- Using wrong life expectancy table
- Missing account balance cutoff date
- Including non-RMD accounts

### ❌ Timing Errors
- Taking too much/too little
- Missing December 31st deadline
- Incorrect quarterly amounts

### ❌ Misunderstanding Rules
- Thinking Roth IRAs have RMDs
- Ignoring inherited account rules
- Mixing up first-year rules

## Planning Strategies by Age

### Ages 50-60 (Pre-RMD Planning)

- Consider mega backdoor Roth

### Ages 60-70 (Transition Planning)
- Final Roth conversions
- Start planning account balances
- Consider QCDs

### Ages 70-73 (Pre-RMD Implementation)
- Final year for Roth conversions
- Account balance optimization
- Consider partial QCDs
- Estate planning

### Ages 73+ (RMD Management)
- Systematic withdrawal planning
- Tax optimization
- Legacy planning
- Healthcare expense planning
