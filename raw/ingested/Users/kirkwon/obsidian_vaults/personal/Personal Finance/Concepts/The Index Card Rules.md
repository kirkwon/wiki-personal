# The Index Card Rules


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[personal-finance]]
### Type**: Financial Framework
### Purpose**: Simple, actionable personal finance rules
### Key Principles**: Simplicity, automation, low costs
### Related Concepts**: [[the-psychology-of-money]], [[the-algebra-of-wealth]], [[i-will-teach-you-to-be-rich]]



## Summary
The Index Card approach demonstrates that all the financial advice you need can fit on a 4x6 index card. The philosophy is that personal finance doesn't have to be complicated - it just needs to be simple enough to actually follow.

## The 10 Rules

### Rule 1: Save 10-20% of Your Income
**Why:** Building wealth requires saving consistently

**How:**
- Start with 1% if 10% feels impossible
- Increase by 1% each month
- Automate the transfer
- Save in tax-advantaged accounts first

**Priority Order:**
1. Emergency fund (3-6 months expenses)
2. Employer 401(k) match (FREE money)
3. Roth IRA (tax-free growth)
4. Remaining 401(k) contributions
5. Taxable investment account

**Tips:**
- Treat savings like a bill you must pay
- Save automatically on payday
- Start small, build gradually

### Rule 2: Pay Off Credit Cards in Full Every Month
**Why:** Credit card debt destroys wealth (18-25%+ interest)

**How:**
- Set up automatic payment for full balance
- Use credit cards like debit cards (only spend what you have)
- If carrying balance: stop using cards until paid off

**Emergency Strategy:**
- Transfer to 0% APR card
- Pay minimum + as much as possible
- Never miss a payment (credit score impact)

**Prevention:**
- Track spending weekly
- Use debit or cash for problem categories
- Remove saved cards from browsers

### Rule 3: Max Out Tax-Advantaged Accounts
**Why:** Free money from tax breaks and employer matches

**Accounts to Max:**
- **401(k)/403(b):** Up to \$23,000 (2024) + employer match
- **Roth IRA:** Up to \$7,000 (2024) - tax-free growth
- **HSA:** Up to \$4,150 (2024) - triple tax advantage
- **529 Plans:** For education expenses (state tax benefits)

**Priority:**
1. Employer match (401k)
2. HSA (if eligible)
3. Roth IRA
4. Remaining 401(k)
5. Taxable accounts

**Why This Order:**
- Employer match = 100% return
- HSA = triple tax advantage
- Roth IRA = tax-free growth forever

### Rule 4: Never Buy Individual Stocks
**Why:** Picking winners is incredibly difficult; diversification is key

**What to Do Instead:**
- Buy low-cost index funds
- Total stock market index
- S&P 500 index
- Target-date funds (set and forget)

**Why Index Funds Win:**
- Instant diversification (hundreds/thousands of companies)
- Low fees (0.03% vs 1%+ for active funds)
- Outperform most pros over time
- No stock picking required

**Example:**
- **Individual stock:** High risk, high stress
- **S&P 500 index:** Owns 500 largest companies

### Rule 5: Buy Low-Cost Index Funds
**Why:** Fees destroy returns over time

**Recommended Funds:**
- **VTSAX:** Vanguard Total Stock Market (0.04% expense ratio)
- **VFIAX:** Vanguard S&P 500 (0.04%)
- **FXAIX:** Fidelity S&P 500 (0.015%)
- **SWTSX:** Schwab Total Stock Market (0.03%)

**Target Date Funds:**
- **VTTHX:** Vanguard 2055 (appropriate allocation for age)
- Automatically adjusts risk as you age
- Set and forget - perfect for most investors

**The Math of Fees:**
- 0.04% fee (index fund): Keep 99.96% of returns
- 1% fee (active fund): Keep 99% of returns
- Over 30 years: 1% fee costs you 25%+ of final balance

### Rule 6: Rebalance Your Portfolio Annually
**Why:** Maintain target allocation, manage risk

**How:**
- Pick a date (birthday, New Year's)
- Check allocation percentages
- Sell high, buy low to rebalance
- Or use target-date funds (auto-rebalancing)

**Sample Allocation by Age:**
- **20s:** 90% stocks, 10% bonds
- **30s:** 80% stocks, 20% bonds
- **40s:** 70% stocks, 30% bonds
- **50s:** 60% stocks, 40% bonds
- **60s:** 50% stocks, 50% bonds

**Simple Approach:**
- Use target-date fund for your retirement year
- Fund handles rebalancing automatically

### Rule 7: Avoid Financial Products That Sound Too Good to Be True
**Why:** They usually are

**Red Flags:**
- Promises of high returns with low risk
- Complex fee structures
- Pressure to buy NOW
- Exclusive or "insider" opportunities
- Annuities (generally terrible for most people)

**Products to Avoid:**
- Whole life insurance (expensive, low returns)
- Private placements (illiquid, high fees)
- Leveraged ETFs (decay over time)
- Most hedge funds (underperform after fees)

**Rule of Thumb:**
- If you don't understand it, don't buy it
- If they're pushing hard, walk away
- Simple index funds beat complex products

### Rule 8: Buy a Home You Can Afford
**Why:** Housing is your biggest expense; don't overextend

**Guidelines:**
- Maximum 2-3x annual income
- Total housing cost < 28-30% of gross income
- Include: mortgage, taxes, insurance, maintenance

**Example:**
- \$100k income
- Max home price: \$200k-300k
- Max monthly payment: \$2,333 (28% of \$100k/12)

**Hidden Costs to Budget:**
- Maintenance: 1-2% of home value annually
- Property taxes: varies widely
- Utilities: often higher than renting
- Repairs: always something needs fixing

**When to Rent vs Buy:**
- Buy if: staying 7+ years, stable job, want control
- Rent if: might move, uncertain job, flexibility needed

### Rule 9: Insurance: Protect Against Catastrophe
**Why:** Prevent financial ruin from unlikely but devastating events

**Essential Insurance:**
- **Health Insurance:** One accident = bankruptcy
- **Disability Insurance:** More likely than death to prevent working
- **Auto Insurance:** Required by law, get good liability limits
- **Home/Renters Insurance:** Protect your stuff
- **Life Insurance:** Only if others depend on your income

**Term Life Insurance:**
- Buy term, not whole life
- Coverage: 10-12x annual income
- Duration: until kids are grown or retirement
- Term is simple and cheap

**Insurance You Probably Don't Need:**
- Extended warranties (usually overpriced)
- Credit card insurance (limited benefit)
- Flight insurance (already covered by other policies)
- Cancer insurance (health insurance covers this)

### Rule 10: Create a Will and Advance Directives
**Why:** Protect your family and your wishes

**Essential Documents:**
- **Will:** Distributes your assets, names guardian for kids
- **Power of Attorney:** Someone to make financial decisions if you can't
- **Healthcare Proxy:** Someone to make medical decisions if you can't
- **Living Will:** Your wishes for end-of-life care

**When to Update:**
- Marriage/divorce
- Having children
- Major asset changes
- Every 5 years

**Digital Assets:**
- Include passwords to digital accounts
- Document cryptocurrency access
- List online accounts to close/maintain

## Implementation Strategy

### Month 1: Foundation
- [ ] Set up automatic savings (1% to start)
- [ ] Pay off credit card balance
- [ ] Open high-yield savings account for emergency fund
- [ ] Track spending for 30 days

### Month 2: Protection
- [ ] Get employer 401(k) match
- [ ] Review insurance coverage
- [ ] Create basic will (use online service if simple)
- [ ] Build emergency fund to \$1,000

### Month 3-6: Build
- [ ] Increase savings to 10% (1% per month)
- [ ] Open Roth IRA
- [ ] Buy first index fund (VTSAX or similar)
- [ ] Build emergency fund to 3 months expenses

---
### Month 7-12: Optimize
- [ ] Increase savings to 15-20%
- [ ] Max out HSA if eligible
- [ ] Rebalance portfolio
- [ ] Review and adjust allocations

## Common Objections

### "I don't have enough money to save"
- Start with 1% - you won't miss it
- Increase gradually as income grows
- Every little bit compounds over time

### "The stock market is too risky"
- Risk is not investing and losing to inflation
- Time horizon matters: 20+ years = stocks historically win
- Diversification reduces risk

### "I need a financial advisor"
- Most advisors don't beat index funds
- They charge fees that reduce your returns
- Index funds = DIY, simple, effective

### "It's too complicated"
- That's why there are only 10 rules
- Automate everything
- Spend < 1 hour per year on finances

### "I'll start when I make more money"
- Lifestyle inflation will eat raises
- Start now with whatever you have
- Building the habit matters most

## The Philosophy

### Why It Works
1. **Simple enough to follow** - Complexity leads to inaction
2. **Evidence-based** - Rules based on research, not opinions
3. **Low-cost** - Minimizes fees that destroy returns
4. **Automated** - Removes willpower from equation
5. **Diversified** - Reduces risk through broad exposure

### The Problem with Most Financial Advice
- Too complicated to follow
- Changes constantly (what to do this month?)
- Benefits the advisor, not you
- Focuses on picking "winners" (impossible consistently)
- Ignores psychology and behavior

### Why "Boring" Wins
- Boring = reliable
- Boring = low fees
- Boring = no stress
- Boring = actually doing it

## Key Takeaways

1. **Simplicity beats complexity** - 10 rules > 1000 books
2. **Automate everything** - Remove willpower from equation
3. **Index funds work** - Stop trying to beat the market
4. **Fees matter** - Low costs = higher returns
5. **Save first, spend later** - Reverse the typical approach
6. **Protect against catastrophe** - Insurance prevents ruin
7. **Time is your ally** - Start early, stay consistent
8. **Boring is beautiful** - Reliability beats excitement
