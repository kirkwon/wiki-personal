# Chinese Cuisine

## Overview

Chinese cuisine is one of the world's oldest and most diverse culinary traditions, spanning thousands of years of history and encompassing numerous regional styles. Characterized by its emphasis on balance, harmony, and technique, Chinese cooking has influenced cuisines worldwide.

## Key Principles

### Balance of Flavors (Yin & Yang)
- **Yin foods**: Cool, soft, moist (e.g., cucumber, tofu)
- **Yang foods**: Hot, spicy, fried (e.g., ginger, garlic, chili)
- Goal: Harmonious balance in every meal

### Five Elements
Each flavor corresponds to an element:
| Flavor | Element | Examples |
|--------|---------|----------|
| **Sweet** | Earth | Cane sugar, fruit, root vegetables |
| **Sour** | Wood | Vinegar, pickled foods |
| **Bitter** | Fire | Bitter melon, tea |
| **Spicy/Pungent** | Metal | Ginger, chili, garlic |
| **Salty** | Water | Soy sauce, salted vegetables |

### Texture Variety
Chinese cuisine emphasizes diverse textures:
- **Crisp** (stir-fried vegetables)
- **Tender** (braised meat)
- **Silky** (egg whites, tofu)
- **Crunchy** (cashews, water chestnuts)
- **Jelly-like** (sea cucumber, certain mushrooms)

### Color Harmony
Dishes should balance colors:
- **Red** (roast pork, chili)
- **Green** (vegetables)
- **Yellow** (egg, corn)
- **White** (tofu, rice, fish)
- **Black** (black mushrooms, wood ear)

## Regional Styles

| Region | Flavor Profile | Key Dishes |
|--------|----------------|------------|
| **Sichuan** (川) | Numbing (má), spicy (là), aromatic | Mapo tofu, kung pao chicken, hot pot |
| **Cantonese** (粤) | Mild, fresh, umami, delicate | Dim sum, roast duck, steamed fish |
| **Hunan** (湘) | Spicy, sour, savory | Hunan-style smoked pork, spicy fish |
| **Shandong** (鲁) | Salty, savory, rich | Braised sea cucumber, sweet and sour carp |
| **Jiangsu/Zhejiang** (苏浙) | Sweet, light, fresh | Lion's head meatballs, West Lake vinegar fish |
| **Fujian** (闽) | Light, savory, umami | Buddha jumps over the wall, braised abalone |
| **Anhui** (徽) | Wild, rustic, braised | Red-braised pork, stewed turtle |

## Staple Ingredients

### Aromatics
- **Ginger** - Fresh, young ginger for mild flavor, old ginger for pungency
- **Garlic** - Aromatic base, lightly browned in oil
- **Scallions/Green onions** - Garnish, aromatic base
- **Chilies** - Fresh, dried, Sichuan peppercorns for numbing

### Sauces & Condiments
- **Soy sauce** - Light (coloring, umami) and dark (color, richness)
- **Oyster sauce** - Sweet, savory seafood flavor
- **Hoisin sauce** - Sweet, thick, bean-based
- **Vinegar** - Black (Chinkiang), red, white
- **Sesame oil** - Nutty finish, used sparingly
- **Bean paste** - Fermented bean sauces (toban djan, broad bean)

### Staples
- **Rice** - Long-grain (Hunan), short-grain (Cantonese), sticky rice
- **Noodles** - Wheat, rice, glass (mung bean)
- **Tofu** - Silken, soft, firm, extra firm, fried
- **Vegetables** - Bok choy, Chinese broccoli, lotus root, wood ear mushroom

## Eating Traditions

### Family-Style Dining
- Dishes shared communally
- Individual rice bowls
- Lazy Susan on round tables
- Elders served first

### Chopstick Etiquette
- Never point chopsticks at people
- Don't stick vertically in rice (resembles funeral offering)
- Pass food to others' plates (never directly mouth to mouth)
- Use chopstick rest when not eating

### Meal Structure
1. **Cold dishes** - Appetizers
2. **Main dishes** - Meat, vegetables, seafood
3. **Soup** - Usually one per meal
4. **Starch** - Rice or noodles
5. **Dessert** - Fresh fruit, sweet soups
