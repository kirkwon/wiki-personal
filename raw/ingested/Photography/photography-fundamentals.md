## Overview

Photography is the art and science of capturing light to create images. Understanding the fundamentals - exposure, composition, and camera settings - is essential for creating compelling photographs.

## Exposure Triangle

The exposure triangle consists of three elements that control light:

### Aperture

**What it is:** The opening in the lens that controls how much light enters.

**Measured in:** f-stops (f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16, f/22)

**How it works:**
- **Lower f-number (f/1.4)**: Larger opening, MORE light, SHALLOW depth of field (blurry background)
- **Higher f-number (f/22)**: Smaller opening, LESS light, DEEP depth of field (everything in focus)

**Common uses:**
| f-stop | Use Case | Effect |
|--------|----------|--------|
| f/1.4 - f/2.8 | Portraits, low light | Blurry background (bokeh), maximum light |
| f/4 - f/5.6 | General photography | Good balance of light and depth |
| f/8 - f/11 | Landscapes | Deep depth of field, everything sharp |
| f/16 - f/22 | Bright scenes, maximum depth | Deep depth, everything in focus |

### Shutter Speed

**What it is:** How long the camera's shutter stays open.

**Measured in:** Seconds or fractions of a second (1/1000, 1/500, 1/250, 1/60, 1/30, 1/15, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1")

**How it works:**
- **Faster shutter (1/1000s)**: FREEZES motion, less light
- **Slower shutter (1/15s)**: MOTION BLUR, more light

**Common uses:**
| Shutter Speed | Use Case | Effect |
|---------------|----------|--------|
| 1/1000 - 1/2000 | Sports, wildlife | Freezes fast motion |
| 1/500 - 1/1000 | Street, action | Freezes most motion |
| 1/125 - 1/250 | General photography | Good balance |
| 1/60 | General, handheld | Minimum for handheld without shake |
| 1/15 - 1/30 | Low light, intentional blur | Motion blur, need stabilization |
| 1" - 30" | Night, light painting | Long exposures |

### ISO

**What it is:** Camera sensor sensitivity to light.

**Measured in:** Numbers (100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200, 6400, 12800, 25600)

**How it works:**
- **Lower ISO (100)**: LESS sensitive, CLEAN images, needs more light
- **Higher ISO (6400)**: MORE sensitive, NOISY images, needs less light

**Common uses:**
| ISO | Use Case | Effect |
|-----|----------|--------|
| 100 - 200 | Bright daylight, tripod | Cleanest images |
| 400 - 800 | Overcast, indoor | Good balance |
| 1600 - 3200 | Low light, indoor | Acceptable noise |
| 6400 - 12800 | Very low light | Visible noise |
| 25600+ | Extreme low light | Heavy noise |

### The Relationship

```
Proper Exposure = Aperture + Shutter Speed + ISO

If you change one, you must adjust another:
  ↓ Aperture (larger opening, more light) → ↑ Shutter speed (faster) or ↓ ISO
  ↑ Aperture (smaller opening, less light) → ↓ Shutter speed (slower) or ↑ ISO
  ↑ Shutter speed (faster, less light) → ↓ Aperture (larger) or ↑ ISO
  ↓ Shutter speed (slower, more light) → ↑ Aperture (smaller) or ↓ ISO
  ↑ ISO (more sensitive) → ↓ Aperture or ↓ Shutter speed
  ↓ ISO (less sensitive) → ↑ Aperture or ↑ Shutter speed
```

## Camera Modes

### Automatic (Auto/Full Auto)
- Camera makes all decisions
- **Use for:** Beginners, quick shots
- **Limitations:** No creative control

### Program Mode (P)
- Camera selects aperture and shutter
- **Use for:** Quick shooting, want some control
- **Limitations:** Less creative control than manual

### Aperture Priority (Av/A)
- **YOU choose aperture**, camera chooses shutter speed
- **Use for:** Portraits, landscapes, control depth of field
- **Common:** Most photographers use this mode

### Shutter Priority (Tv/S)
- **YOU choose shutter speed**, camera chooses aperture
- **Use for:** Sports, action, intentional motion blur

### Manual Mode (M)
- **YOU choose everything**: aperture, shutter speed, ISO
- **Use for:** Complete creative control, difficult lighting
- **Requires:** Understanding of exposure triangle

## Focus

### Autofocus (AF)
- Camera focuses automatically
- **Modes:**
  - **Single Shot (AF-S)**: One-time focus, good for stationary subjects
  - **Continuous (AF-C)**: Continually adjusts, good for moving subjects
  - **Automatic (AF-A)**: Camera chooses between single and continuous

### Manual Focus (MF)
- You focus manually
- **Use for:** Macro, low light, creative effects
- **Requires:** Practice and patience

## Metering

How the camera measures light:

| Mode | What it measures | Best for |
|------|------------------|----------|
---
| **Evaluative/Matrix** | Entire scene, analyzes brightness distribution | Most situations |
| **Center-weighted** | Center of frame | Portraits, backlit subjects |
| **Spot** | Small area (usually center) | Precise metering, high contrast |

## Common Settings by Genre

### Portrait Photography
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 - f/4 (blurry background)
- **Shutter:** 1/125 - 1/250 (freeze motion)
- **ISO:** 100 - 800 (keep clean)
- **Focus:** AF-S (single shot)
- **Metering:** Center-weighted or spot

### Landscape Photography
- **Aperture:** f/8 - f/16 (deep depth of field)
- **Shutter:** 1/60 - 1/125 (or use tripod, slower)
- **ISO:** 100 - 200 (cleanest images)
- **Focus:** Manual or AF-S
- **Metering:** Evaluative

### Street Photography
- **Aperture:** f/8 - f/11 (wide depth of field for candid shots)
- **Shutter:** 1/250 - 1/500 (freeze motion)
- **ISO:** Auto (up to 3200)
- **Focus:** Zone focus or AF-C (continuous)
- **Metering:** Evaluative

### Low Light/Night Photography
- **Aperture:** f/2.8 - f/4 (let in light)
- **Shutter:** 1/60 - 30" (use tripod for slow speeds)
- **ISO:** 800 - 6400 (or higher if needed)
- **Focus:** Manual
- **Metering:** Spot

## Equipment

### Camera Types

| Type | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|------|------|------|----------|
| **DSLR** | Good battery life, optical viewfinder, many lenses | Heavy, complex | Serious photographers |
| **Mirrorless** | Compact, electronic viewfinder, fast autofocus | Battery life, expensive | Most photographers |
| **Compact** | Portable, simple | Limited control | Beginners |
| **Phone** | Always with you, convenient | Limited optics, sensor size | Casual photography |

### Lens Types

| Focal Length | Field of View | Best for |
|--------------|----------------|----------|
| **Wide (14-24mm)** | Very wide | Landscapes, architecture |
| **Standard (24-35mm)** | Normal view | Street, travel |
| **Normal (50mm)** | Similar to human eye | Portraits, general |
| **Telephoto (70-200mm)** | Narrow | Sports, wildlife, portraits |
| **Super Telephoto (300mm+)** | Very narrow | Wildlife, sports |

### Essential Accessories

- **Tripod**: For long exposures and stability
- **Memory cards**: Fast cards for burst shooting
- **Extra batteries**: Always carry spares
- **Cleaning kit**: For sensor and lens cleaning
- **Camera bag**: Protection and organization

## Composition Basics

While composition is covered in [[Composition Techniques]], fundamentals include:

- **Rule of Thirds**: Divide frame into 3x3 grid, place subject at intersections
- **Leading Lines**: Use lines to guide viewer's eye
- **Framing**: Use elements to frame your subject
- **Symmetry and Patterns**: Create balance and interest
- **Backgrounds**: Keep backgrounds simple and uncluttered

## Post-Processing

Basic post-processing includes:

- **Cropping**: Improve composition
- **Exposure adjustment**: Brighten or darken
- **Contrast**: Enhance tonal range
- **White balance**: Correct color cast
- **Clarity/Dehaze**: Enhance details

See [[Post-Processing]] for more details.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to Fix |
|---------|------------|
| Camera shake | Use faster shutter or tripod |
| Blurry subject | Use faster shutter speed |
| Overexposed images | Lower ISO, faster shutter, smaller aperture |
| Underexposed images | Higher ISO, slower shutter, larger aperture |
| Noisy images | Use lower ISO, more light |
| Wrong focus mode | Choose AF-S for still subjects, AF-C for moving |
| Cluttered backgrounds | Move to cleaner background or use wider aperture |

## Practice Exercises

### Daily Practice
1. **Shoot in Manual mode**: Practice adjusting settings
2. **Master one setting at a time**: Focus on aperture, then shutter, then ISO
3. **Shoot in different lighting**: Bright day, overcast, indoor, night
4. **Review EXIF data**: See what settings you used for good photos

### Weekly Practice
1. **Pick one genre**: Portraits, landscapes, street - practice for a week
2. **Experiment with extremes**: Wide aperture, narrow aperture, fast/slow shutter
3. **Focus on one technique**: Depth of field, motion blur, etc.
4. **Compare with examples**: Study photos by others, try to replicate
