## Overview

Post-processing is the act of editing your photos after capture. While photography is about capturing the moment, post-processing is about enhancing that capture to convey your vision.

## Philosophy

### What is Post-Processing?

**Definition**: Editing photos after capture to enhance, correct, and style images.

**Goals:**
- **Correct technical issues**: Exposure, white balance, color
- **Enhance visual impact**: Contrast, clarity, saturation
- **Create consistent style**: Develop personal aesthetic
- **Tell the story**: Guide viewer's eye, create mood

### Film vs. Digital

**Film era:**
- Darkroom processing (developing, printing)
- Limited manipulation (mostly exposure, contrast)
- Fixed look (film type predetermined)

**Digital era:**
- Unlimited possibilities
- Non-destructive editing
- Each photographer's look

**Key insight**: Great photos start with great captures. Post-processing enhances, doesn't fix bad photos.

## Workflow

### Typical Post-Processing Workflow

1. **Import**: Load photos into editing software
2. **Culling/Selection**: Choose best photos
3. **Global adjustments**: Overall image corrections
4. **Local adjustments**: Specific areas of photo
5. **Crop and straighten**: Compositional refinements
6. **Export**: Save edited image

### Step-by-Step Guide

#### 1. Import

**Import photos from:**
- Camera via USB cable
- Memory card via card reader
- Phone (if using mobile)

**Organize as you import:**
- Add keywords
- Add to collections/albums
- Star or flag favorites

#### 2. Culling/Selection

**Rate photos:**
- **5 stars**: Portfolio-worthy
- **4 stars**: Very good, keep
- **3 stars**: Good, maybe
- **2 stars**: Average, rarely use
- **1 star**: Poor, usually delete

**Delete rejects:**
- Out of focus
- Bad exposure (beyond recovery)
- Bad composition (can't fix in editing)
- Duplicates (choose best)

**Target:** Keep only best 10-20% of shots.

#### 3. Global Adjustments

**Apply to entire image:**

| Adjustment | What It Does | When to Use |
|------------|--------------|-------------|
| **Exposure** | Brighten or darken overall image | Over/underexposed |
| **Contrast** | Difference between light and dark | Flat image |
| **Highlights** | Adjust bright areas | Blown highlights |
| **Shadows** | Adjust dark areas | Blocked shadows |
| **Whites** | Adjust brightest tones | Need more contrast |
| **Blacks** | Adjust darkest tones | Need more contrast |
| **Clarity** | Local contrast, midtones | Punch up image |
| **Vibrance** | Saturates less-saturated colors | Boost color without oversaturation |
| **Saturation** | Overall color intensity | Boost color for impact |
| **Texture** | Enhance fine details | Portraits, landscapes |

#### 4. White Balance

**Correct color cast:**

| Preset | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| **Auto** | Quick correction, often accurate |
| **Daylight** | Outdoor, daylight |
| **Cloudy** | Overcast days |
| **Shade** | Shade, shadowed areas |
| **Tungsten** | Indoor, incandescent lighting |
| **Fluorescent** | Office lighting |
| **Flash** | Flash photography |
| **Custom** | Manual white balance picker |

**How to use custom white balance:**
---
1. **Click white balance picker**
2. **Click neutral color** in image (gray, white)
3. **Software corrects** color temperature

#### 5. HSL (Hue, Saturation, Luminance)

**Adjust specific colors:**

**Hue:** Shift color (e.g., make green more yellow-green)
**Saturation:** Increase or decrease color intensity
**Luminance:** Brighten or darken specific colors

**Common uses:**
- **Skin tones**: Adjust oranges to look natural
- **Blue sky**: Increase saturation for dramatic sky
- **Green foliage**: Adjust to look natural

#### 6. Tone Curve

**Fine-tune tonal range:**

- **Highlights**: Upper right of curve
- **Lights**: Upper middle
- **Darks**: Lower middle
- **Shadows**: Lower left

**Create S-curve** for contrast:
- Pull up highlights
- Pull down shadows
- Creates more contrast

#### 7. Lens Corrections

**Fix lens issues:**
- **Distortion**: Barrel/pincushion distortion
- **Vignette**: Darker corners
- **Chromatic aberration**: Color fringing

Most software has automatic lens correction (detect lens, fix).

#### 8. Effects

**Add stylistic effects:**

| Effect | Description | Use |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| **Vignette** | Darker corners | Draw attention to center |
| **Grain** | Add film-like noise | Texture, retro look |
| **Dehaze** | Remove atmospheric haze | Landscapes, flat scenes |
| **Split toning** | Add color to highlights and shadows | Creative styling |

#### 9. Crop and Straighten

**Crop for composition:**
- Improve rule of thirds
- Remove distractions
- Change aspect ratio

**Straighten:**
- Level horizons
- Correct perspective

**Aspect ratios:**
- **3:2**: Full-frame camera standard
- **4:3**: Micro Four Thirds, some phones
- **16:9**: Widescreen, video
- **1:1**: Square (Instagram)
- **2:3**: Portrait orientation

#### 10. Local Adjustments

**Adjust specific areas:**

| Tool | Use for |
|------|---------|
| **Adjustment brush** | Paint adjustments on specific areas |
| **Gradient filter** | Apply adjustments linearly (sky, foreground) |
| **Radial filter** | Apply adjustments circularly (subject, vignette) |
| **Color range mask** | Select by color |
| **Luminance mask** | Select by brightness |

**Common uses:**
- Brighten subject's face
- Darken bright sky
- Enhance eyes
- Reduce highlights on skin

#### 11. Export

**Save edited image:**

**File formats:**
- **JPEG**: Standard, smaller file, lossy compression
- **TIFF**: Lossless, larger file
- **PNG**: Lossless, transparency support
- **PSD**: Photoshop format, layers preserved

**Export settings:**
- **Resolution**: 300 DPI for print, 72 DPI for web
- **Quality**: 80-90% for JPEG
- **Color space**: sRGB for web, Adobe RGB for print
- **Size**: Resize for intended use (web, print, social media)

## Advanced Techniques

### HDR (High Dynamic Range)

**What it is:** Combine multiple exposures for extended dynamic range.

**How to do it:**
1. **Shoot bracketed exposures**: -2, 0, +2 EV
2. **Import into HDR software**: Lightroom, Aurora HDR, Photomatix
3. **Merge**: Software combines exposures
4. **Tone map**: Adjust for natural look

**Best for:** High contrast scenes (landscapes with bright sky and dark foreground)

### Panoramas

**What it is:** Stitch multiple photos together for wide view.

**How to do it:**
1. **Shoot series**: Overlap each photo by 30-50%
2. **Keep camera level**: Use tripod or level
3. **Merge in software**: Lightroom, Photoshop, PTGui
4. **Crop and adjust**: Clean up edges, adjust exposure

### Focus Stacking

**What it is:** Combine multiple photos at different focus distances for extended depth of field.

**Best for:** Macro photography, landscapes

### Compositing

**What it is:** Combine elements from multiple photos.

**Software:** Photoshop (required)
**Examples:** Replace sky, add moon, combine people

## Developing Your Style

### Consistent Processing

**Goal:** Recognizable look across your photos.

**How to achieve:**
1. **Use presets**: Create starting point
2. **Consistent adjustments**: Similar contrast, color treatment
3. **Aspect ratio**: Use same ratio
4. **Color palette**: Prefer certain color treatments
5. **Vignetting**: Use or avoid consistently

### Presets and Profiles

**What they are:** Saved adjustments that can be applied to other photos.

**How to create presets:**
1. **Edit a photo** to desired look
2. **Save as preset** (Lightroom, etc.)
3. **Apply to other photos** for consistent look

**Tips:**
- Create multiple presets for different situations
- Tweak presets for each photo (don't rely entirely)
- Share and learn from others' presets

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to Fix |
|---------|------------|
| Over-editing | Subtle is usually better |
| Oversaturation | Back off saturation |
| Too much clarity | Subtle enhancement, not harsh |
| Ignoring histogram | Watch for clipping (blown highlights, blocked shadows) |
| Not shooting RAW | RAW has more editing flexibility |
| Editing bad photos | Start with good captures |
| Overusing filters | Use filters tastefully, as starting points |

## Practice Exercises

### Daily Practice
1. **Edit at least one photo** from every shoot
2. **Experiment with one adjustment**: See what it does
3. **Create preset**: Start building your style

### Weekly Practice
1. **Edit a series**: Consistent look across multiple photos
2. **Before and after**: Compare to see your progress
3. **Learn new technique**: HDR, panorama, etc.

### Monthly Practice
1. **Portfolio curation**: Select best photos, edit consistently
2. **Style challenge**: Mimic a photographer's style
3. **Backup edited photos**: Ensure you don't lose your work

## Final Tips

1. **Shoot in RAW**: More flexibility in editing
2. **Backup original files**: Never lose your captures
3. **Subtle is better**: Small adjustments have big impact
4. **Learn shortcuts**: Speed up your workflow
5. **Develop your style**: Consistent look across photos
6. **Organize your photos**: Easy to find, manage
7. **Practice regularly**: Editing takes practice
8. **Review before/after**: Learn from your edits

**Post-processing is about enhancing, not fixing. Start with great captures, then elevate them through thoughtful editing.**
