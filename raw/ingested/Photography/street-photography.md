## Overview

Street photography is candid photography of public life. It captures unplanned, unposed moments in public spaces, telling stories about people, culture, and urban life.

## Philosophy

### What Is Street Photography?

**Definition**: Capturing unposed, unplanned moments of public life.

**Characteristics:**
- **Candid**: Subjects are unaware or unposed
- **Public**: In public spaces (streets, parks, markets)
- **Storytelling**: Images convey narrative or emotion
- **Documentary**: Captures slice of life, era, culture

### Ethics

| Consideration | Guideline |
|---------------|-----------|
| **Permission** | Legal in most public spaces (varies by country) |
| **Respect** | Don't photograph vulnerable people (children in distress, homeless, etc.) |
| **Context** | Consider cultural norms and local laws |
| **Subjects' comfort** | If someone objects, don't photograph them |
| **Privacy** | Don't photograph in private spaces (homes, businesses) |

**Bottom line**: Be respectful, consider context, know your rights.

## Equipment

### Camera Choice

| Camera Type | Pros | Cons | Best for |
|-------------|------|------|----------|
| **Mirrorless/DSLR** | Fast autofocus, good lenses | Large, conspicuous | Serious street photographers |
| **Compact camera** | Portable, discreet | Smaller sensor | Most street photographers |
| **Film rangefinder** | Quiet, classic | No preview, film costs | Traditional approach |

| Focal Length | Field of View | Why Good for Street |
|--------------|----------------|-------------------|
| **35mm** | Wide normal | Context + intimacy, classic street length |
| **28mm** | Wide | Environmental portraits, lots of context |
| **50mm** | Normal | Natural perspective, good compression |
| **24mm** | Very wide | Environmental, lots of context |

**Street photography sweet spot**: 35mm (context + intimacy)

## Settings

### Recommended Street Photography Settings

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---------|-------|-----|
| **Mode** | Manual or Aperture Priority | Creative control |
| **Aperture** | f/8 - f/11 | Wide depth of field, everything in focus |
| **Shutter** | 1/250 - 1/500 | Freeze motion, candid shots |
| **ISO** | Auto (up to 3200) | Adapt to changing light |
| **Focus** | Zone focus or AF-C | Fast, pre-focused |
| **Drive mode** | Continuous | Capture series |
| **Metering** | Evaluative | General street scenes |

### Zone Focus

**What it is:** Pre-focus to a distance, then shoot without focusing.

**How to do it:**
1. **Set aperture**: f/8 - f/16 for wide depth of field
2. **Set distance**: 6-10 feet (typical street distance)
3. **Switch to manual focus**: Don't refocus
4. **Shoot**: Subjects in that range will be in focus

**Why use it:**
- Faster than autofocus
- No delay between seeing and shooting
- Discreet (no beep or focus light)

### Hyperfocal Distance

**What it is:** The closest distance at which lens can focus while keeping infinity sharp.

**How to use:**
1. **Calculate or use app**: Many apps exist
2. **Set focus**: At hyperfocal distance
3. **Everything from half that distance to infinity is in focus**

**Why use it:**
- Maximum depth of field
- Pre-focus, shoot freely

## Techniques

### The "Decisive Moment"

**Coined by Henri Cartier-Bresson: The perfect moment when all elements come together.**

**How to capture:**
1. **Anticipate**: Watch behavior, predict next action
2. **Position yourself**: Best vantage point
3. **Wait**: Be patient for moment to unfold
4. **Shoot**: Capture quickly when moment happens

**Example:** Pedestrian stepping into pattern of shadows, woman's hat catches wind.

### Zone Focusing + Waiting

**Technique:**
1. **Pre-focus** to street distance (6-10 ft)
2. **Set aperture** for depth of field (f/8 - f/11)
3. **Find interesting location**: Intersection, doorway, pattern
4. **Wait**: For interesting person to enter frame
5. **Shoot**: When moment is right

---
**Why works:** Discreet, fast, you don't miss moments.

### Street Portraits

**Two approaches:**

**Candid portraits:**
- Photograph people without them knowing
- Requires quick shutter, discreet camera
- Often environmental (show context)

**Asked portraits:**
- Ask people if you can photograph them
- Requires confidence, friendliness
- Often more personal, posed but natural

**How to ask:**
1. **Approach respectfully**: Say hello, explain project
2. **Be specific**: "I love your style, can I photograph you?"
3. **Be brief**: Don't waste their time
4. **Offer to share**: If they want, offer to email photo

### The "F8 and Be There"

**Famous quote attributed to Weegee.**

**Meaning:**
- **f/8**: Aperture wide enough for depth of field
- **Be there**: Be out on the street, ready to shoot

**Philosophy:** The most important thing is being there, ready to capture moments. Technical skills matter less than presence.

### Layered Scenes

**Technique:** Multiple layers creating depth and interest.

**How to create:**
1. **Foreground element**: Person close to camera
2. **Middle ground**: Main subject
3. **Background**: Context, environment

**Example:** Person walking past with another person in background, city skyline beyond.

### Use of Shadows and Light

**Street photography lighting:**
- **Golden hour**: Best light (early morning, late afternoon)
- **Hard light**: Harsh shadows, dramatic
- **Soft light**: Overcast, flattering
- **Shadows**: Use as graphic element, frame subjects

**Techniques:**
- **Photograph in shadow**: Soft, even light
- **Use harsh shadows**: Graphic, dramatic
- **Backlight**: Silhouettes, drama

## Composition in Street Photography

### Leading Lines

**Use street elements as leading lines:**
- Roads, sidewalks, building edges
- Shadows, reflections
- Lines of people

### Framing

**Use urban elements:**
- Doorways, windows, alleys
- Overpasses, tunnels
- Signs, street lights

### Reflections

**Use reflective surfaces:**
- Store windows, car windows
- Puddles, water features
- Mirrors, polished metal

### Patterns and Repetition

**Find patterns:**
- Rows of windows, doors
- Repeating shapes (bicycles, people)
- Geometric patterns in architecture

## Processing Street Photos

### Minimal Processing

**Street photography is about the moment, not heavy editing.**

**Basic adjustments:**
- **Exposure**: Adjust brightness
- **Contrast**: Enhance tonal range
- **Black and white**: Often better for street
- **Crop**: Minor compositional improvements

### Black and White

**Why B&W for street:**
- Removes distraction of color
- Emphasizes light and shadow
- Timeless, classic look
- Focuses on emotion and moment

**Convert to B&W when:**
- Scene lacks interesting color
- You want to emphasize mood
- Color distracts from subject

See [[post-processing]] for more on editing.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to Fix |
|---------|------------|
| Being too conspicuous | Use smaller camera, be discreet |
| Missing the moment | Anticipate, have camera ready |
| Asking too many questions | Be brief, respect subject's time |
| Being inattentive | Pay attention, observe behavior |
| Not getting close enough | Get closer, engage with scene |
| Ethical concerns | Know your rights, be respectful |
| Technical overthinking | Focus on moment, not settings |

## Practice Exercises

### Daily Practice
1. **Walk the same route**: Observe changes, patterns
2. **Set a theme**: Shadows, reflections, people with hats
3. **Limit yourself**: Use one lens, one location
4. **Time limit**: 30 minutes, then review

### Weekly Practice
1. **Project approach**: Work on a theme for a week
2. **Study a master photographer**: Analyze their work
3. **Review and cull**: Look at your photos, edit ruthlessly
4. **Visit new location**: Explore different neighborhood

### Monthly Practice
1. **Create series**: Related photos, narrative
2. **Photograph event**: Festival, market, protest
3. **Critique**: Get feedback on your work
4. **Learn from others**: Study great street photography
