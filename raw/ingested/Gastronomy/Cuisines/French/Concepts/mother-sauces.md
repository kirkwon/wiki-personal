# The Five Mother Sauces

## Overview

The five mother sauces (sauces mères) are the foundation of French cuisine, codified by Auguste Escoffier in the early 20th century. Master these five, and you can create hundreds of derivative sauces, forming the basis for countless dishes.

## The Five Mother Sauces

### 1. Béchamel (White Sauce)

**The simplest and most versatile**

**Base:**
- Milk
- White roux (butter + flour)

**Ratio:**
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp flour
- 1 cup milk

**Process:**
1. Melt butter over medium heat
2. Whisk in flour (roux)
3. Cook 1-2 minutes (don't brown)
4. Gradually whisk in warm milk
5. Simmer until thickened
6. Season with salt, white pepper, nutmeg

**Common uses:**
- Mornay sauce (béchamel + cheese)
- Soubise sauce (béchamel + onions)
- Gratin dishes
- Lasagna, mac and cheese
- Creamed dishes

**Derivatives:**
| Sauce | Addition |
|-------|----------|
| **Mornay** - Gruyère cheese |
| **Soubise** - Cooked onions |
| **Nantua** - Crayfish butter |
| **Suprême** - Cream, chicken stock |

### 2. Velouté (Blond Sauce)

**Light, golden sauce**

**Base:**
- Chicken, fish, or veal stock
- Blond roux

**Ratio:**
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp flour
- 1 cup stock

**Process:**
1. Make roux (butter + flour)
2. Cook 2-3 minutes (light golden)
3. Whisk in hot stock
4. Simmer until thickened
5. Strain (optional)
6. Season

**Common uses:**
- Sauce over chicken, fish
- Base for cream sauces
- Pot pie filling
- Soups

**Derivatives:**
| Sauce | Base |
|-------|------|
| **Suprême** - Chicken velouté + cream |
| **Allemande** - Velouté + egg yolks, cream |
| **Normande** - Fish velouté + cream, mushrooms |
| **Poulette** - Chicken velouté + cream, egg yolks, herbs |

### 3. Espagnole (Brown Sauce)

**Rich, deeply flavored**

**Base:**
- Brown stock (beef, veal, game)
- Brown roux
- Mirepoix (onions, carrots, celery)

**Ratio:**
- 1 tbsp butter
- 1 tbsp flour
- 1 cup brown stock

**Process:**
1. Make mirepoix, cook until brown
2. Make brown roux (cook until dark)
3. Add brown stock
4. Simmer for hours (develops flavor)
5. Skim fat
6. Strain

**Common uses:**
- Gravy
- Stews
- Base for demi-glace

**Derivatives:**
| Sauce | Addition |
|-------|----------|
| **Demi-glace** - Espagnole reduced by half |
| **Bordelaise** - Red wine, shallots |
| **Mushroom** - Mushrooms |
| **Madeira** - Madeira wine |
| **Chasseur** - Mushrooms, white wine, tomatoes |

### 4. Hollandaise (Egg Yolk Emulsion)

**Rich, buttery, delicate**
---

**Base:**
- Egg yolks
- Butter
- Acid (lemon juice or vinegar)

**Ratio:**
- 3 egg yolks
- 1 lb butter (clarified)
- 1 tbsp lemon juice or vinegar

**Process:**
1. Clarify butter
2. Whisk egg yolks with acid over double boiler
3. Drizzle in melted butter (whisking constantly)
4. Until thick and emulsified
5. Season with salt, white pepper, cayenne

**Common uses:**
- Eggs Benedict
- Steamed vegetables (asparagus)
- Grilled fish
- Steak sauce

**Derivatives:**
| Sauce | Addition |
|-------|----------|
| **Béarnaise** - Shallots, tarragon, chervil |
| **Choron** - Béarnaise + tomato |
| **Maltaise** - Blood orange |
| **Mousseline** - Whipped cream |

### 5. Tomato Sauce

**Tomato-based sauce**

**Base:**
- Tomatoes (fresh or canned)
- Mirepoix
- Optional: roux

**Process:**
1. Sauté mirepoix in oil
2. Add garlic (optional)
3. Add tomatoes (crushed, diced, or paste)
4. Simmer 30 min - 2 hours
5. Season with salt, pepper, herbs

**Common uses:**
- Pasta
- Pizza
- Ratatouille
- Shakshuka

**Variations:**
- Fresh tomatoes (seasonal)
- Canned tomatoes (year-round)
- Tomato paste (intense)

## Making Roux

**Ratio: 1:1 (butter:flour)**

| Type | Cooking Time | Use |
|------|--------------|-----|
| **White roux** - 1-2 minutes | Béchamel |
| **Blond roux** - 2-3 minutes | Velouté |
| **Brown roux** - 4-5 minutes | Espagnole |

**Technique:**
1. Melt butter over medium heat
2. Whisk in flour
3. Cook to desired color
4. Use immediately

## Common Mistakes

| Sauce | Mistake | How to Fix |
|-------|---------|------------|
| **Béchamel** - Lumpy sauce | Whisk flour into melted butter gradually, add milk slowly while whisking |
| **Velouté** - Too thick | Add more stock |
| **Espagnole** - Not flavorful | Simmer longer, reduce |
| **Hollandaise** - Separated | Keep whisking, don't overheat |
| **Tomato** - Too acidic | Add sugar or pinch of baking soda |
