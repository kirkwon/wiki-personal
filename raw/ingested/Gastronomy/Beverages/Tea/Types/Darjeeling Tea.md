# Darjeeling Tea (大吉岭茶)


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[Beverages/Tea]]
### Type**: Tea Category
### Oxidation Level**: 80-100% (full oxidation, light processing)
### Processing**: Orthodox processing, light oxidation
### Caffeine Level**: Medium-High (40-60mg per 8oz)
### Color Profile**: Light to medium brown, amber liquor
### Best For**: Afternoon tea, delicate flavors, connoisseur tea



## Summary

Darjeeling tea is a premium black tea grown in the Darjeeling district of West Bengal, India, often called the "Champagne of Teas" for its exceptional quality and unique characteristics. Grown at high altitudes in the Himalayan foothills, Darjeeling offers a distinctive muscatel flavor that sets it apart from other black teas. The tea's character varies dramatically with the season, creating different flushes with unique flavor profiles that tea connoisseurs prize worldwide.

## Flush System

### First Flush (春摘 - Chūn Zhāi)
```yaml
Timing:
- Period: March to April
- Harvest: Young, tender leaves and buds
- Weather: Cool temperatures, light mist
- Quality: Highest quality of the year
- Production: Limited quantities, premium pricing

Characteristics:
- Appearance: Light, tippy leaves, silver buds
- Color: Pale coppery liquor, light infusion
- Flavor: Delicate, floral, muscatel
- Aroma: Fresh, floral, light
- Body: Light, elegant
- Astringency: Light, crisp finish

Best Brew:
- Temperature: 195-205°F (90-96°C)
- Time: 2-3 minutes
- Multiple Infusions: Possible, light and delicate
- Serving: Small cups, elegant presentation
- Pairing: Light snacks, scones, delicate pastries
```

### Second Flush (夏摘 - Xià Zhāi)
```yaml
Timing:
- Period: May to June
- Harvest: Mature leaves, more developed
- Weather: Warmer temperatures, regular rain
- Quality: Most sought after by connoisseurs
- Production: Moderate quantities, premium pricing

Characteristics:
- Appearance: Darker leaves, fewer tips
- Color: Coppery to amber liquor
- Flavor: Classic muscatel, complex, fruity
- Aroma: Pronounced muscatel, fruity
- Body: Medium to full
- Astringency: Balanced, pleasant finish

Best Brew:
- Temperature: 200-212°F (93-100°C)
- Time: 3-4 minutes
- Multiple Infusions: 2-3 infusions possible
- Serving: Standard teacups
- Pairing: Light meals, afternoon tea
```

## Processing Method

### Orthodox Processing
```yaml
Traditional Steps:
1. **Withering**: 12-18 hours to reduce moisture
2. **Rolling**: Orthodox rolling, gentle pressure
3. **Oxidation**: 2-4 hours, controlled oxidation
4. **Firing**: Final heat treatment to stop oxidation
5. **Sorting**: By size, quality, and appearance

Quality Control:
- Gentle Processing: Preserves delicate flavors
- Low Oxidation: Maintains characteristic notes
- Hand Sorting: Removes defects carefully
- Consistency: Strict quality standards
- Garden Character: Preserves terroir expression
```

## Brewing Recommendations

### Optimal Brewing Methods
```yaml
Western Style:
- Equipment: Porcelain teapot or mug
- Water: Fresh, filtered, boiling
- Temperature: 200-212°F (93-100°C)
- Time: 3-4 minutes
- Ratio: 1 tsp per 8 oz water
- Multiple Infusions: 2-3 possible for premium flushes

Gongfu Style:
- Equipment: Small teapot or gaiwan
- Water: Boiling, good quality
- Temperature: 200-212°F (93-100°C)
- Time: 30-60 seconds per infusion
- Ratio: 4-6g per 100ml water
- Multiple Infusions: 3-5 infusions possible

Cold Brew:
- Equipment: Glass jar or pitcher
- Water: Room temperature
- Time: 8-12 hours
- Ratio: 2x normal amount
- Serving: Over ice, pure or with milk
```

## Storage & Freshness

### Optimal Storage
```yaml
Storage Conditions:
- Airtight Container: Glass or ceramic with tight seal
- Cool Temperature: 50-70°F (10-21°C)
- Dark Place: Away from light exposure
- Dry Environment: Low humidity, no moisture
- Odor-Free: No strong odors nearby

Shelf Life:
- First Flush: 6-12 months (best when fresh)
- Second Flush: 12-18 months (good aging potential)
- Lower Grades: 18-24 months
- Vacuum Sealed: Extended shelf life possible
- Refrigeration: Not recommended (can damage delicate flavors)
```

## Common Mistakes

### Brewing Errors
```yaml
Temperature Issues:
- Too Cool: Under-extracts, weak flavor
- Too Hot: Can damage delicate flavors
- Solution: Use proper temperature for flush type
- Effect: Proper extraction of complex flavors

Steeping Time:
- Too Short: Weak, under-developed flavor
- Too Long: Bitter, harsh extraction
- Solution: Follow guidelines for specific flush
- Effect: Balance of strength and delicacy

Water Quality:
- Tap Water: Chlorine affects flavor
- Hard Water: Minerals interfere with extraction
- Solution: Use filtered water
- Effect: Cleaner, purer flavor expression
```

### Storage Issues
```yaml
Moisture Exposure:
- Mold Growth: Ruins tea completely
- Flavor Loss: Rapid quality degradation
- Solution: Airtight storage, dry environment
- Effect: Preserves delicate aromas

Light Exposure:
- UV Damage: Destroys flavor compounds
- Color Fading: Visual quality loss
- Solution: Dark storage containers
- Effect: Maintains freshness and quality

Temperature Fluctuations:
- Condensation: Moisture damage
- Quality Degradation: Accelerated aging
- Solution: Stable, cool storage
- Effect: Consistent quality maintenance
```
