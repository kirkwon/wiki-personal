# Coffee Bean Processing (咖啡豆处理)


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[Beverages/Coffee]]
### Type**: Processing Method
### Category**: Post-Harvest Preparation
### Skill Level**: Intermediate
### Time Required**: Days to weeks
### Best For**: Quality development, flavor preservation
### Key Variables**: Time, temperature, moisture control



## Summary

Coffee bean processing is the critical post-harvest journey that transforms the coffee cherry into the green coffee beans ready for roasting. This process dramatically influences the final flavor, aroma, and quality of the coffee. Different processing methods create distinct flavor profiles, from the bright, clean notes of washed coffees to the fruity, complex characteristics of natural processed coffees. Understanding processing is essential for appreciating the full spectrum of coffee flavors.

## Processing Overview

### Core Principles
- **Cherry Removal**: Separating beans from fruit pulp
- **Fermentation**: Controlled microbial activity for flavor development
- **Drying**: Reducing moisture to safe storage levels
- **Sorting**: Quality control and defect removal
- **Preservation**: Maintaining bean integrity for quality

### Processing Stages
```yaml
Harvest:
- Picking ripe coffee cherries
- Quality assessment at farm level
- Transportation to processing facility

Primary Processing:
- Cherry cleaning and sorting
- Removal of pulp and mucilage
- Fermentation (varies by method)
- Washing (varies by method)

Drying:
- Mechanical or sun drying
- Moisture reduction to 10-12%
- Quality monitoring during drying

Secondary Processing:
- Hulling to remove parchment
- Sorting by size and quality
- Final quality assessment
- Bagging and storage
```

## Processing Methods

### Washed Processing (水洗处理)
```yaml
Process Overview:
1. **Pulping**: Remove outer skin mechanically
2. **Fermentation**: Soak in water to remove mucilage
3. **Washing**: Rinse beans completely
4. **Drying**: Sun or mechanical drying

Time Requirements:
- Pulping: 1-2 hours
- Fermentation: 12-48 hours
- Washing: 1-2 hours
- Drying: 7-14 days

Flavor Profile:
- Clean, bright acidity
- Balanced, well-defined flavors
- High clarity and complexity
- Light to medium body
- Often showcases origin characteristics

Quality Control:
- Fermentation timing critical
- Water quality important
- Drying must be even
- Moisture monitoring essential

Best Origins:
- Ethiopia (Washed)
- Colombia
- Costa Rica
- Kenya
```

### Natural Processing (日晒处理)
```yaml
Process Overview:
1. **Sorting**: Select ripe cherries
2. **Drying**: Dry whole cherries in sun
3. **Turning**: Rotate cherries regularly
4. **Hulling**: Remove dried fruit later

Time Requirements:
- Sorting: 1-2 days
- Drying: 2-4 weeks
- Turning: Daily during drying
- Hulling: After drying complete

Flavor Profile:
- Fruit-forward, sweet
- Heavy body, low acidity
- Complex fermented notes
- Jammy, wine-like characteristics
- Often more rustic, earthy notes

Quality Control:
- Cherry selection critical
- Drying must be even
- Mold prevention essential
- Moisture control vital

Best Origins:
- Ethiopia (Natural)
- Brazil
- Yemen
- Indonesia
```

### Honey Processing (蜜处理)
```yaml
Process Overview:
1. **Pulping**: Remove outer skin, leave mucilage
2. **Drying**: Dry beans with sticky mucilage
3. **Monitoring**: Check moisture regularly
4. **Hulling**: Remove parchment when dry

Honey Levels:
- **Black Honey**: Most mucilage, longest drying
- **Red Honey**: Medium mucilage, moderate drying
- **Yellow Honey**: Least mucilage, shortest drying
- **White Honey**: Minimal mucilage, brief drying

Time Requirements:
- Pulping: 1-2 hours
- Drying: 8-15 days
- Monitoring: Daily during drying
- Hulling: After moisture reaches target

Flavor Profile:
- Balanced sweetness and acidity
- Full body, smooth mouthfeel
- Honey, stone fruit notes
- Balanced fermentation notes
- Good complexity without strong fruitiness

Quality Control:
- Mucilage consistency critical
- Drying environment important
- Temperature monitoring essential
- Moisture control throughout

Best Origins:
- Costa Rica
- Brazil
- Guatemala
- El Salvador
```

### Pulped Natural (湿法日晒)
```yaml
Process Overview:
1. **Pulping**: Remove outer skin only
2. **Limited Washing**: Remove some mucilage
3. **Drying**: Partial mucilage remaining
4. **Hulling**: Remove parchment when dry

Time Requirements:
- Pulping: 1-2 hours
- Washing: 30 minutes
- Drying: 7-12 days
- Monitoring: Daily during drying

Flavor Profile:
- Combination of washed and natural
- Good body with some brightness
- Balanced acidity and sweetness
- Complex fruit notes
- Smooth, clean finish

Quality Control:
- Mucilage level control critical
- Drying must be even
- Temperature management important
- Moisture monitoring essential

Best Origins:
- Brazil
- Colombia
- Peru
- Mexico
```

### Anaerobic Fermentation (厌氧发酵)
```yaml
Process Overview:
1. **Cherry Selection**: Perfect ripeness required
2. **Container Sealing**: Airtight containers
3. **Fermentation**: Controlled anaerobic environment
4. **Drying**: Traditional or mechanical drying

Fermentation Variables:
- Temperature: Controlled 75-85°F
- Time: 24-72 hours
- Pressure: Natural build-up
- Additives: Sometimes used for flavor

Time Requirements:
- Preparation: 1-2 hours
- Fermentation: 24-72 hours
- Drying: 7-14 days
- Monitoring: Continuous during fermentation

Flavor Profile:
- Intense, unique flavors
- Wine-like, fermented notes
- Heavy body, low acidity
- Complex fruit and floral notes
- Often very distinctive and memorable

Quality Control:
- Sterilization critical
- Temperature control essential
- Monitoring vital to avoid spoilage
- Small batch processing common

Best Origins:
- Costa Rica
- Colombia
- Kenya
- Guatemala
```

## Harvest Considerations

### Cherry Maturity
```yaml
Optimal Ripeness:
- Color: Deep red, fully colored
- Feel: Firm but yields slightly
- Size: Consistent within batch
- Stem: Detaches easily

Immature Cherries:
- Color: Green or pink
- Flavor: Sour, astringent
- Processing: Difficult to process
- Quality: Creates defects

Overripe Cherries:
- Color: Dark red, almost black
- Feel: Soft, shriveled
- Flavor: Overripe, fermented
- Processing: Difficult to handle
- Quality: Can create off-flavors
```

### Harvest Methods
```yaml
Selective Picking:
- Process: Pick only ripe cherries
- Frequency: Multiple passes (5-8 times)
- Quality: Highest quality coffee
- Cost: Most expensive method

Strip Picking:
- Process: Strip all cherries at once
- Frequency: Once or twice per tree
- Quality: Mixed maturity, lower quality
- Cost: Most economical method

Mechanical Harvesting:
- Process: Machines shake trees
- Frequency: Once per season
- Quality: Mixed maturity, lower grade
- Cost: Initial investment but efficient
```

## Quality Factors

### Processing Quality Indicators
```yaml
Visual Quality:
- Color: Uniform, consistent color
- Size: Consistent bean size
- Defects: Minimal defects present
- Damage: No physical damage
- Mold: No visible mold growth

Physical Quality:
- Moisture: 10-12% ideal
- Density: Good bean density
- Sound: Beans should rattle
- Weight: Consistent weight per volume
- Feel: Firm, not rubbery

Flavor Quality:
- Clean Cup: No off-flavors
- Acidity: Appropriate for origin
- Body: Consistent mouthfeel
- Balance: Harmonious flavors
- Aftertaste: Pleasant, lingering
```

### Common Defects
```yaml
Primary Defects:
- Sour: Fermentation gone wrong
- Moldy: Improper drying conditions
- Stinker: Fermented beyond recovery
- Black: Over-fermented, spoiled

Secondary Defects:
- Quakers: Underdeveloped beans
- Broken: Physical damage during processing
- Foreign Matter: Rocks, twigs, etc.
- Insect Damage: From pests during processing

Defect Grading:
- Grade 1: No primary defects
- Grade 2: Minor defects acceptable
- Grade 3: Significant defects
- Commercial: Mixed quality, defects present
```
