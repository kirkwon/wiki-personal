# Charles Duhigg
Charles Duhigg is an American investigative journalist and non-fiction author, known for his works exploring the science of habits, productivity, and communication. He is a Pulitzer Prize-winning reporter for *The New York Times*. His work synthesizes neuroscience, corporate case studies, organizational behavior, and communication research into accessible frameworks, explaining how habits form and how they can be changed.

## Key Works
- **The Power of Habit** — habit loops, keystone habits, organizational habit training
- **Smarter Faster Better** — The science of productivity and motivation
- **Supercommunicators** — communication frameworks (also in wiki as [[supercommunicators-charles-duhigg]])

## Notable Works
* **[[the-power-of-habit-concept|The Power of Habit: Why We Do What We Do in Life and Business]]** (2012): This book explores the science behind habit formation, introducing the concept of the [[habit-loop]] (cue, routine, reward) and the idea of [[keystone-habits]] as high-leverage behaviors that catalyze broader change. It draws on research into the basal ganglia, dopamine-driven craving, and organizational behavior, and demonstrates how habits influence individuals, organizations, and societies through case studies such as Alcoholics Anonymous, Starbucks employee training, Target's consumer habit prediction through data analytics, and social movements that spread through community habits.
* **Smarter Faster Better: The Secrets of Being Productive in Life and Business]]** (2016): Focuses on the science of productivity and how certain choices can make individuals and teams more effective. It explores habit science, willpower, organizational habit change, and group dynamics, examining how companies like Starbucks and Procter & Gamble embed productive habit loops into their cultures. It presents mental models for productivity, including motivation, teams, and focus.
* **[[supercommunicators-charles-duhigg-book-summary|Supercommunicators: How to Unlock the Secret Language of Connection]]** (2024): This work delves into the principles and practices of effective communication, arguing that it is a learnable skill rooted in psychological and neurological processes. It examines how understanding the "setting" or social context of communication—recognizing patterns and relationships in the social environment—improves message delivery and reception. It explores how habit loops and mental models operate in communication contexts. It emphasizes how understanding human psychology—habit loops, social motivation, emotional intelligence, and storytelling—can make people more effective communicators.

## Core Ideas
Duhigg's work centers on habit loops as the foundation of success. He examines how willpower functions as a trainable neurological resource rather than a fixed trait, and how group dynamics reinforce or undermine individual habits through shared routines and social norms. His approach relies heavily on narrative case studies—Alcoholics Anonymous for breaking habit chains, Starbucks for building habit loops—rather than controlled experimental research. This places his work in dialogue with more rigorously framed perspectives like [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]] on dual-process cognition and [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] on probabilistic decision-making. His research draws on the basal ganglia, dopamine-driven craving, and organizational behavior, providing a neurological and behavioral-science foundation for understanding automatic behavior and organizational culture. The mental model of "Settings"—understanding social context and the relationships within it—emerges as a key framework in his later work for navigating complex communication situations. His work connects to broader themes of [[effective-listening]], [[emotional-intelligence-in-communication]], and [[building-a-cohesive-team]].
