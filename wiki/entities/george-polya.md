# George Polya

George Polya was a Hungarian-American mathematician whose 1945 book *How to Solve It* became one of the most influential texts on mathematical problem-solving methodology. His most influential contribution reframed mathematical problem-solving as a learnable skill rather than an innate talent, and his four-step framework—Understand, Plan, Carry Out, Look Back—became foundational in mathematics education and has since influenced fields ranging from software engineering to decision science.

Polya's work bridges structured analytical thinking and practical heuristics. His "dictionary of heuristic" provides dozens of strategies for tackling unfamiliar problems, from analogy and decomposition to working backwards and the inventor's paradox. He emphasized the role of insight and acknowledged subconscious incubation in problem-solving. His distinction between pedantry and mastery and his emphasis on reflective practice ("looking back") directly parallel modern ideas about [[decision-tracking]] and separating decision quality from outcome quality.

Key contributions:
- Four-step problem-solving framework
- Comprehensive taxonomy of heuristics (the Heuristic Dictionary)
- Emphasis on reflective practice and Looking Back
- Recognition of the Inventor's Paradox
- Distinction between pedantry and mastery
- Acknowledgment of subconscious incubation in problem-solving

His ideas complement [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] (probabilistic framing), [[naturalistic-decision-making]] (intuitive recognition), and [[paul-elder-critical-thinking-framework]] (reflective analysis).
