# Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)

Rosemary is a robust, aromatic herb from the Lamiaceae (mint) family with a distinctive pine-like, camphoraceous flavor. Native to the Mediterranean, it is essential for roasting meats, grilling, and adding depth to hearty dishes. Its strong flavor means it should be used judiciously but adds tremendous character to food.

## Flavor Profile

- **Pine**: Coniferous, forest-like aroma
- **Woodsy**: Earthy, tree bark notes
- **Camphor**: Medicinal, cooling undertones
- **Mint**: Subtle mint family characteristics
- **Balsamic**: Sweet, resinous finish

## Varietal Differences

| Variety | Flavor Profile | Primary Uses | Appearance |
|---------|----------------|--------------|------------|
| **Tuscan Blue** | Classic pine, strong | Roasts, grilling | Upright, blue flowers |
| **Arp** | Milder, sweeter | Fried foods, desserts | Trailing habit, pale flowers |
| **Prostratus** | Very mild | Ground covers, mild dishes | Low-growing, trailing |
| **Majorica Pink** | Floral, mild | Garnishes, light dishes | Pink flowers, compact |

## Culinary Applications

### Mediterranean Cuisine
- Roasts: lamb, chicken, potatoes with rosemary
- Grilled foods: skewers, marinated meats
- Breads: rosemary bread, focaccia, scones
- Infused olive oils for dipping

### French Cuisine
- Herbes de Provence: traditional blend with rosemary
- Roasts: beef, pork, root vegetables
- Compound butters for steaks
- Pastries, stuffings, stocks

### Italian Cuisine
- Pizza: fresh rosemary on focaccia
- Roasts: pork, chicken, vegetables
- Grilled meats and vegetables
- Rosemary breadsticks, focaccia

### British/American
- Roast potatoes: classic Sunday roast
- Lamb: mint jelly alternative
- Infusions: butters, oils, vinegars
- BBQ rubs and marinades

## Pairing Guide

Rosemary pairs excellently with [[ingredient-pairings]] companions including lamb, chicken, pork, beef, potatoes, carrots, eggplant, mushrooms, garlic, thyme, lemon, and olive oil. It complements [[aromatics-the-holy-trinity]] herbs and works well with Herbes de Provence. Delicate fish and very delicate greens should be avoided as rosemary can overpower them.

## Storage Methods

- **Refrigerator (Fresh)**: Bundle, wrap in damp paper towel, 7-10 days
- **Drying**: Hang upside down in dark, dry place, 1-2 weeks, store airtight
- **Freezing**: Whole sprigs or chopped in ice cube trays with oil, 3-4 months
- **Infused Oils**: Steep sprigs in olive oil, 1-2 weeks, refrigerate for 2 months

## Key Cooking Techniques

- Strip leaves by running fingers down stem
- Use sparingly due to strength; fresh preferred over old/dried
- Add early as it handles long cooking times
- Briefly toast in pan to enhance aroma
- Remove sprigs before serving
