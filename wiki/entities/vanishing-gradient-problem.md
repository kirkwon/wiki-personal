# Vanishing Gradient Problem

The Vanishing Gradient Problem is an issue where the gradient of a neural network's loss function becomes exceedingly small during training, causing learning to slow down or stop entirely in earlier layers. This can lead to poor performance and difficulty in capturing complex patterns, similar to how over-seasoning early stages of cooking might ruin the final dish. In terms of photography, it's akin to losing detail in shadows due to improper exposure settings; for personal finance, it resembles diminishing returns on investment as interest compounds minimally.


In cognitive science, understanding this problem is crucial because neural networks are often used to model brain functions and learning processes, so addressing vanishing gradients can lead to more accurate simulations of human cognition.
