# Quit — The Power of Knowing When to Walk Away

Annie Duke's *Quit: The Power of Knowing When to Walk Away* explores the strategic value of disengagement. The book examines how accumulated experience informs the decision to stop pursuing a path, connecting to themes of [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]] and [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]].

This work references the [[experiences]] mental model, suggesting that knowing when to quit is itself an experiential skill rooted in pattern recognition. It complements Duke's earlier work on decision-making in [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] and [[how-to-decide---annie-duke]].

## Key Themes

- Strategic quitting as a decision-making skill
- Recognizing when to disengage from losing propositions
- When to walk away from commitments, projects, or paths
- Experience as a guide for disengagement
- The cost of sunk investment versus adaptive pivoting
- The cost of staying committed to the wrong path

## Connection to Ways and Experiences

Quit provides one of the two source references for the [[ways-mental-model]], connecting the idea of navigating complexity to knowing when to change direction. It also ties into the [[experiences]] mental model, framing quitting as a skill refined through accumulated pattern recognition.
