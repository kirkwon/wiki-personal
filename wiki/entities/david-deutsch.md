# David Deutsch

David Deutsch is a physicist and philosopher of science, best known for his work on the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics and for his book *The Beginning of Infinity*. He is a Visiting Professor of Physics at the University of Oxford and a Fellow of the Royal Society.

## Key Contributions
- **Many-worlds interpretation**: Deutsch was instrumental in developing and popularizing the many-worlds interpretation of quantum mechanics, which posits that all possible outcomes of quantum measurements are realized in branching parallel universes.
- **Theory of universal computation**: He proposed the concept of a universal quantum computer, laying groundwork for quantum computing.
- **Epistemology of knowledge growth**: In *The Beginning of Infinity*, Deutsch argues that knowledge grows through conjecture and criticism rather than induction, and that explanatory reach is the key criterion for evaluating explanations.

## Core Ideas in *The Beginning of Infinity*
- Explanations with [[explanatory-reach]] can account for phenomena beyond their origin cases.
- Knowledge advances through [[conjecture-and-criticism]], not through observation or induction alone.
- The [[principle-of-optimism]] holds that all evils stem from insufficient knowledge, making problems fundamentally soluble.
- Creativity is the generation of explanatory knowledge through variation, selection, and retention.
- The growth of knowledge is unbounded; problems are symptoms of knowledge gaps, not inherent limits.
