# Annie Duke

Annie Duke is a former professional poker player, author, and decision-making expert. She is best known for her work translating lessons from poker into frameworks for everyday decision-making under uncertainty. She won the World Series of Poker Main Event in 2004 and also won the 2004 WSOP Ladies Event, accumulating over $4 million in live tournament earnings. After transitioning from poker to writing and consulting, she became a leading voice on decision science, emphasizing the importance of evaluating decision processes rather than outcomes.

Her work synthesizes insights from behavioral science, her poker experience, and organizational case studies into practical frameworks for better decision-making. She bridges the gap between analytical traditions (like Kahneman's work on cognitive biases) and intuitive expertise models (like Gary Klein's naturalistic decision-making), arguing that both perspectives are essential for robust decisions.

## Key Contributions

- Developed the distinction between decision quality and outcome quality
- Developed the "Thinking in Bets" framework, reframing all decisions as probabilistic wagers
- Emphasized framing effects as a critical lever for better decisions
- Proposed strategic (long-term) thinking as superior to reactive (short-term) thinking
- Advocated for building decision cultures in organizations
- Integrated emotion and intuition as valuable inputs alongside rational analysis
- Advocates for tracking decisions and updating beliefs through Bayesian reasoning
- Discusses bet sizing strategies including the [[kelly-criterion]] and [[martingale-strategy]]
- Explores strategic bluffing as a decision-making tactic
- Addresses the decision to stop a process versus persist, connecting quitting as a process skill

## Relevance to Processes and Experiences

Duke's work on quitting as a process skill connects to the [[processes]] mental model by showing that process awareness includes recognizing when to disengage from a failing approach. Her broader framework connects to the [[experiences]] mental model through the lens of experiential learning and strategic disengagement — drawing on her extensive experience making repeated decisions under uncertainty with clear outcome feedback in poker, then generalizing those lessons into broader decision-making principles.

## Background

Duke's poker career provided extensive experience in making repeated decisions under uncertainty with clear outcome feedback, which she later generalized into broader decision-making principles. Her 2004 WSOP Main Event victory and over $4 million in live tournament earnings grounded her expertise in high-stakes, probabilistic environments.
