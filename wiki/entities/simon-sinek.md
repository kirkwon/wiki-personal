# Simon Sinek

Simon Sinek is an author, motivational speaker, and leadership consultant known for his work on leadership, purpose, and communication. Best known for popularizing the concept of "Start With Why" and for his work on organizational culture, purpose-driven leadership, and the concept of infinite games. His central framework connects to [[the-golden-circle]] and emphasizes that discovering one's deep purpose—the Why—forms the foundation for effective leadership, personal fulfillment, and organizational success. He uses Apple's journey under Steve Jobs as a primary case study to illustrate how starting with purpose rather than features creates lasting impact. He also draws on historical examples including Martin Luther King Jr. and the Wright Brothers to illustrate how infinite game thinking produces sustained success through trust, collaboration, and adaptability.

## Core Works

### *Start With Why*
Sinek's foundational work introduces the Golden Circle framework—Why → How → What—and argues that organizations and individuals should lead with their purpose (Why) rather than focusing on what they do or how they do it.

### *Leaders Eat Last*
This book explores how organizations succeed when leaders prioritize employee belonging, trust, and service over short-term profit. Sinek draws on case examples from Southwest Airlines, Costco Wholesale, and Starbucks to illustrate service-first leadership in practice. It introduces the concept of the Triple Bottom Line—measuring success by the impact on people, culture, and profit rather than profit alone.

### *Find Your Why*
Sinek shares his personal journey toward understanding his own purpose and offers frameworks for readers to explore theirs, including the three essential questions (What, How, Why) and the concept of finding one's personal legend. His approach relies heavily on narrative and anecdotal evidence, contrasting with more empirically grounded decision-making frameworks like those of Daniel Kahneman or Annie Duke. A deep-seated sense of purpose or calling—what Sinek calls a "Personal Legend"—drives lifelong action.

### *The Infinite Game*
This book reframes how organizations should approach competition and long-term strategy, arguing that success is not a finite contest to "win" but an ongoing game to keep playing. It connects to themes of [[infinite-game-mindset]] and [[zero-sum-vs-infinite-game]].

## Core Ideas

- The Golden Circle: Why → How → What
- Infinite game mindset versus finite game thinking
- Purpose (Why) is the innermost driver of behavior and influence
- Leading by articulating and living one's Why rather than by authority or features
- Organizations thrive when leaders put employees first
- Trust and belonging are foundational to team performance
- Shared purpose unites employees toward common goals
- Culture is shaped by deliberate choices leaders make
- Leaders who prioritize people and service over profit create more successful, resilient organizations
- The Triple Bottom Line—people, culture, profit—should guide organizational decisions

## Relevance to Wiki

Sinek's work on purpose-driven messaging connects to the [[message]] mental model, which treats clear communication and pattern recognition as tools for navigating complex situations. His Golden Circle framework is distinct from Angela Duckworth's [[grit-concept]]—Sinek frames sustained drive as purpose rather than perseverance.
