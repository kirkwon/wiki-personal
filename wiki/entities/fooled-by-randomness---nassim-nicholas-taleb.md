# Fooled by Randomness - Nassim Nicholas Taleb

*Fooled by Randomness* is Nassim Nicholas Taleb's exploration of how human cognition systematically fails to recognize randomness, leading to poor decision-making across finance, risk assessment, and forecasting. The book argues that people construct stories around chance events, confusing luck with skill and patterns with noise. It explores how human beings systematically misperceive randomness and chance, arguing that we are prone to seeing patterns where none exist, underestimating the role of stochastic processes, and overestimating our ability to predict outcomes.

## Key Themes

- Narrative fallacy: the tendency to impose coherent stories on random events
- Overconfidence in predicting outcomes from limited data
- Survivorship bias in interpreting success stories
- The role of chance in shaping perceived cause and effect
- Cognitive biases toward attributing outcomes to skill rather than luck
- The illusion of control in uncertain environments
- How success can be driven by randomness rather than ability
- Connections to [[antifragility]] and [[black-swan-events]] in Taleb's broader body of work
