# Predictably Irrational

*Predictably Irrational* is a book by behavioral economist [[dan-ariely]] that examines the systematic ways in which human decision-making deviates from rationality. Through a series of experiments, Ariely demonstrates that value perception is constructed through context—anchoring, decoy effects, and zero-price effects systematically distort how humans evaluate options. The book shows that people's choices are consistently influenced by cognitive biases, emotional factors, and contextual cues in predictable patterns.

## Key Concepts

- **Anchoring**: Initial reference points disproportionately influence subsequent valuations.
- **Decoy Effect**: The presence of a third, asymmetric option shifts preferences between two others.
- **Zero-Price Effect**: Free offerings carry disproportionate psychological weight beyond their economic value.
- **Social vs. Market Norms**: Different contexts activate different valuation frameworks.
- Humans are not rational actors; instead, we rely on mental shortcuts that lead to systematic errors.
- Behavioral economics provides tools to understand and anticipate these patterns.
- Decision-making is shaped by environment, framing, and social context.

These findings reinforce that value is not an objective property but a constructed perception shaped by environment and framing.
