# C Corporation

A C Corporation is a separate legal entity owned by shareholders, with limited liability and a double taxation structure. Profits are taxed at the corporate level, then dividends are taxed again at the shareholder level.

## Formation

- Articles of Incorporation with state
- Corporate Bylaws
- Organizational meeting minutes
- Stock ledger and shareholder records
- Annual meetings and minutes required
- State franchise taxes and filing fees

## Legal Characteristics

- Separate legal entity distinct from shareholders
- Limited liability protection for shareholders
- Double taxation: corporate profits taxed, then dividends taxed to shareholders
- Perpetual existence regardless of shareholder changes
- Formal governance requirements (board of directors, officers)

## Advantages

- Unlimited number of shareholders and types of stock
- Easier to raise capital through equity financing
- Ability to offer stock options to employees
- Potential for lower self-employment taxes
- Fringe benefits not taxable to shareholders (health insurance, etc.)
- International taxation potential for deferral on foreign earnings

## Disadvantages

- Double taxation of corporate profits and shareholder dividends
- Complex formation and ongoing compliance
- Formal corporate governance requirements
- Administrative burden and higher costs
- Potential accumulated earnings tax
- Difficulty converting to other entity types

## Tax Strategies

- Strategic retention of earnings for growth
- Optimize dividend payment timing
- Comprehensive fringe benefit packages for employees/shareholders
- Consider Form 8832 for tax elections

## Best For

- Large businesses
- Venture-backed startups
- Companies with significant retained earnings needs
- Businesses planning international operations
