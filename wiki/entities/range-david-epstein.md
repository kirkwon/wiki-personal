# Range - David Epstein

*Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World* is David Epstein's argument that breadth of experience and cross-domain thinking often lead to better outcomes than narrow specialization. Epstein challenges the assumption that early, deep focus is optimal, presenting evidence that versatile learners and those who cultivate range outperform specialists in many contexts. The book draws on examples from sports, science, music, and business to demonstrate that generalists thrive when problems are novel or require transfer of knowledge across domains.

## Key Arguments and Themes

- Breadth of experience over narrow specialization
- Cross-domain transfer of knowledge
- Narrow specialization can be a liability in rapidly changing or complex environments
- Diverse experience enables better pattern recognition and transfer of skills
- The ability to apply knowledge across domains is often more valuable than deep expertise in one area
- Range as a pattern-recognition tool for navigating complexity
- The role of range in adaptive decision-making
