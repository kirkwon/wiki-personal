# Viktor Frankl

Viktor Frankl was an Austrian psychiatrist, neurologist, and philosopher who founded logotherapy, a psychotherapeutic approach centered on meaning-finding. Born in 1905 in Vienna, Austria, he died in 1997. His experiences in Nazi concentration camps, particularly Auschwitz, profoundly shaped his understanding of human resilience and the will to meaning. These experiences became the foundation for *Man's Search for Meaning*, in which he argued that meaning — not pleasure or power — is the primary human motivation. Frankl emphasized that individuals can choose their attitude toward suffering and that purpose can be found even in extreme adversity.

## Key Facts
- Born: 1905, Vienna, Austria
- Died: 1997
- Profession: Psychiatrist, neurologist, philosopher
- Survived Auschwitz and other concentration camps
- Developed logotherapy as a successor to Freudian psychoanalysis and Adlerian individual psychology
- Author of *Man's Search for Meaning* (1946 original; 2024 edition referenced)

## Key Contributions

- **Logotherapy**: Developed as an alternative to Freudian pleasure-seeking and Adlerian power-seeking, logotherapy posits that the primary human drive is the search for meaning.
- **Tragic Optimism**: The capacity to maintain optimism despite unavoidable suffering, pain, guilt, and death.
- **Three Sources of Meaning**: Work/achievement, love/connection, and courage in suffering.
- **Noö-Dynamics**: The spiritual tension between one's current self and potential self.
- **Meaning-Finding Framework**: His core thesis that meaning — not pleasure or power — is the primary human motivation, and that individuals can choose their attitude toward suffering.

## Legacy

Frankl's work profoundly influenced later thinkers on meaning and well-being, including Mark Manson (*Will*) and the broader field of existential psychology. His emphasis on meaning over happiness provides a counterpoint to positive psychology approaches that focus primarily on affect.
