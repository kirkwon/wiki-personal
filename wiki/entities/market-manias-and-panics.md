# market-manias-and-panics

Market manias and panics refer to periods of excessive investor enthusiasm or fear that lead to significant asset price volatility in financial markets. These phenomena can be likened to a 'flash' in cooking when flavors unexpectedly intensify, or an overexposed photograph where details become distorted due to extreme light levels—both representing sudden and potentially unpredictable shifts. In cognitive science, they reflect rapid changes in collective beliefs that may not align with underlying economic fundamentals, akin to impulsive decision-making influenced by emotions rather than rational analysis.


In personal finance, understanding market manias and panics is crucial for risk management, as these events can dramatically affect investment portfolios' value, much like how an unexpected kitchen fire or equipment failure can impact a meal preparation process.
