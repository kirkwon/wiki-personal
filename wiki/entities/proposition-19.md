# Proposition 19

California ballot measure approved by voters in November 2020, effective February 16, 2021, that significantly modified property tax transfer rules under Proposition 13. Generates an estimated $750M–$1B over 10 years for wildfire prevention.

## Key Changes

### Parent-Child Transfer Rules
Before Prop 19, children inherited parent's property tax basis with no reassessment. After Prop 19, full protection is limited to:
- **Primary Residence**: Children inheriting parent's primary residence receive full tax basis protection
- **Family Farm**: Agricultural properties ≤$1 million receive full protection
- **Disabled Child**: Severely disabled children always receive full protection

Non-qualified transfers are reassessed at current market value, though the original purchase price basis is preserved for capital gains calculations.

### Inherited Property
Primary residences can transfer property tax basis if the inheriting child keeps it as primary residence (children under 22 must use as primary residence). Other properties are generally reassessed at market value. County transfer within the same county is required for full protection.

### Severely Disabled Individuals
Transfers to severely disabled individuals always receive full property tax basis protection regardless of property type, requiring medical certification of disability status.

### Senior Transfers (55+)
Seniors, disabled individuals, or wildfire victims can transfer property tax basis to replacement property of equal or lesser value within the same county, with no limit on the number of transfers.

### Wildfire Victims
Properties damaged or destroyed by wildfires can transfer basis to replacement property in the same county within 2 years of wildfire declaration, with proof of damage from fire authority.

## Tax Impact Example
A property with $100,000 base year value taxed at $1,000/year before Prop 19 could see reassessment to $1,000,000 market value after a non-qualified transfer, resulting in $10,000+/year in property taxes — a $9,000+ annual increase.

## Capital Gains vs. Property Tax Tradeoff
A core tension: inheriting at a lower property tax basis saves on annual taxes but may result in higher capital gains taxes at eventual sale. [[1031-exchange-strategy]] may be preferable to defer both taxes.

## Strategic Implications
- Longer holding periods mitigate tax increases
- Entity structure (LLC, trust) impacts transfer rules differently
- County-specific implementation varies
- [[real-estate-investment]] portfolio planning must account for reassessment risk
- Professional guidance from California real estate tax specialists is essential
