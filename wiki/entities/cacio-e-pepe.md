# Cacio e Pepe

Cacio e Pepe — "cheese and pepper" in Roman dialect — is one of Rome's most iconic pasta dishes. A classic Roman pasta dish consisting of only Pecorino Romano cheese, black pepper, and pasta water. Its elegance lies in its extreme simplicity, and the magic is in the emulsion: Pecorino Romano, black pepper, and starchy pasta water form a creamy emulsion that coats each strand in a silky, peppery cheese coating. Mastering this cheese emulsion is considered one of the true tests of Italian pasta technique.

## Key Ingredients

- **Pecorino Romano** — The only cheese; finely grated
- **Black pepper** — Abundant, freshly cracked (coarsely ground)
- **Pasta water** — The starchy liquid is essential for emulsification

## Technique

The starchy pasta water is essential — it is used to create an emulsion with the Pecorino Romano and black pepper, forming a creamy, cohesive sauce that coats the pasta. This technique demonstrates the importance of [[al-dente]] cooking and reserving starchy water, as outlined in [[pasta-shapes-and-sauces]].

1. Cook spaghetti or tonnarelli al dente, reserve starchy water
2. Toast black pepper in rendered guanciale fat (or olive oil)
3. Off heat, add small amounts of hot pasta water to create a loose paste with the pepper
4. Add finely grated Pecorino, tossing vigorously
5. Add more pasta water gradually until a creamy, cohesive sauce forms
6. Toss with pasta off heat

The emulsion is the entire dish — when done correctly, the sauce coats each strand in a silky, peppery cheese coating.
