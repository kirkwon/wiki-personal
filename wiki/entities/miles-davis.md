# Miles Davis

Miles Davis (May 26, 1926 – September 28, 1991) was a groundbreaking jazz trumpeter, bandleader, and composer from Alton, Illinois. He continually reinvented himself and the jazz genre across six distinct eras: bebop, cool jazz, hard bop, modal jazz, the second quintet, and fusion. His signature is economy of expression—"the notes you don't play."

## Key Facts
- **Born**: May 26, 1926, Alton, Illinois
- **Died**: September 28, 1991, Santa Monica, California
- **Instrument**: Trumpet
- **Primary Genres**: Bebop, Cool Jazz, Modal Jazz, Fusion
- **Signature Style**: Lyrical, economical, innovative

## Musical Eras

### Bebop Era (1945-1948)
Learned from [[charlie-parker]], developed his style through early recordings like "Now's the Time" and "Billie's Bounce." Characterized by fast tempos, complex harmonies, and virtuosity.

### Birth of the Cool (1949-1950)
Nonet recordings arranged by [[gil-evans]] featuring a cooler sound with French horn and tuba. Key tracks include "Boplicity" and "Moon Dreams." Influenced the cool jazz movement.

### Hard Bop Era (1951-1955)
Quintet with [[john-coltrane]], more aggressive and blues-influenced. Key recordings: "Walkin'," "Solar." Rhythm section: Red Garland, Paul Chambers, Philly Joe Jones.

### Modal Jazz Era (1957-1960)
First Great Quintet with [[john-coltrane]], Cannonball Adderley, Bill Evans, Paul Chambers, Jimmy Cobb. Landmark album [[kind-of-blue]] (1959) became the best-selling jazz album of all time. Used fewer chord changes for more melodic exploration.

### Second Great Quintet (1963-1968)
With Herbie Hancock, Wayne Shorter, Ron Carter, Tony Williams. More complex harmonies and free rhythmic interplay. Key albums: *E.S.P.*, *Miles Smiles*, [[nefertiti]]. Characterized by telepathic communication and collective improvisation.

### Electric/Fusion Era (1968-1975)
[[bitches-brew]] (1970) pioneered jazz-rock fusion with electric instruments, rock rhythms, and studio experimentation. Influential bands: Weather Report, Mahavishnu Orchestra, Return to Forever.

## Playing Style
- **Economical Phrasing**: Every note counts; used space and silence as musical elements. Famous quote: "It's not the notes you play, it's the notes you don't play."
- **Lyrical Melodic Sense**: Singing quality to his lines with emotional depth without excessive display. Notable ballads: "My Funny Valentine," "Blue in Green."
- **Harmonic Innovation**: Pioneered modal playing, complex chord progressions with subtle substitutions, pentatonic scales, Lydian mode, tritone substitutions for exotic sounds.
- **Sound Production**: Warm, breathy tone. Harmon mute for intimate sound. Later used wah-wah pedal for electric trumpet.
- **Leadership Style**: Cast the right musicians and let them shine; constantly evolving.

## Key Albums
- *Kind of Blue* (1959) - "So What," "All Blues," "Blue in Green"
- *Bitches Brew* (1970) - "Bitches Brew," "Spanish Key"
- *Sketches of Spain* (1960) - "Concierto de Aranjuez"
- *Milestones* (1958) - "Milestones," "Sid's Ahead"
- *Round About Midnight* (1957)
- *Nefertiti* (1967) - "Nefertiti," "Hand Jive"

## Influence
Influenced Wynton Marsalis, Chris Botti, Terence Blanchard, Herbie Hancock, Wayne Shorter, John Scofield, Prince, Radiohead, Madlib, Flying Lotus, and various electronic music artists.^[raw/articles/miles-davis.md]
