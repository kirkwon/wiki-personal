# Richard Thaler

Richard Thaler is a Nobel laureate behavioral economist and author, best known for his work on mental accounting, the endowment effect, and libertarian paternalism (nudging). His 2024 book *The Winner's Curse* synthesizes decades of behavioral research into a comprehensive account of how human economic behavior systematically deviates from rational choice in predictable ways.

Thaler's contributions span cognitive biases in judgment, mental accounting, framing effects, fairness norms, self-control failures, financial market anomalies, and policy design through choice architecture. He demonstrated that people treat money non-fungibly through mental accounting, overestimate their abilities and timelines (planning fallacy), and are heavily influenced by how choices are framed. He coined the term "nudge" and popularized the concept of libertarian paternalism—preserving choice freedom while designing default options that improve outcomes. He co-authored *Nudge* with Cass Sunstein.

His work connects to Daniel Kahneman's dual-process theory, Amos Tversky's prospect theory, and the broader behavioral economics movement that challenges standard rational choice assumptions.
