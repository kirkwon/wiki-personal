# Complications: A Surgeon's Notes on an Imperfect Science

*Complications: A Surgeon's Notes on an Imperfect Science* is a book by Atul Gawande that explores the realities of surgical practice, the limits of medical knowledge, and the nature of complications in medicine. Gawande uses the concept of "families" to describe patterns of cases or problems that share common characteristics, providing a framework for understanding complexity in medical decision-making.

## Key Themes
- The imperfect nature of medical science and surgery
- Patterns and families of complications in clinical practice
- Decision-making under uncertainty
- The role of experience and pattern recognition in navigating complex situations

## Connection to Mental Models

The book introduces the "Families" mental model, which Gawande uses to cluster cases into recognizable patterns for better understanding and decision-making. This connects to broader themes in Gawande's work on checklists, end-of-life care, and the limits of expertise.
