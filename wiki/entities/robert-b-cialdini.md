# Robert B. Cialdini

Robert B. Cialdini is a social psychologist and professor emeritus at Arizona State University, best known for his research on persuasion, compliance, and influence. He is the author of *Influence: The Psychology of Persuasion*, a seminal work that identifies psychological principles driving human behavior in social and commercial contexts. His follow-up book [[pre-suasion-a-revolutionary-way-to-influence-and-persuade]] explores the concept of priming and environmental framing before a persuasion attempt. His framework is widely applied in marketing, negotiation, and behavioral science, and his principles are recognized as a universal mental model for understanding patterns of influence across domains.

His research focuses on the mechanisms people use to influence others and how these principles shape decision-making. Cialdini's work draws on decades of experimental social psychology and real-world anecdotes, and connects closely to cognitive science, behavioral economics, and the study of cognitive biases.

## Key Contributions

- **Six Principles of Influence**: Cialdini identified six core principles — reciprocation, commitment and consistency, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity — that systematically drive human compliance and persuasion.
- **Pre-Suasion**: In his later work, Cialdini expanded the framework by arguing that what happens *before* a persuasion attempt matters as much as the attempt itself. Environmental cues, attention priming, and initial framing determine receptivity. The concept that the moments before persuasion shape its effectiveness, by guiding attention and priming responses. His book [[pre-suasion-a-revolutionary-way-to-influence-and-persuade]] is referenced as a source supporting the [[techniques]] mental model, highlighting how pre-suasion — the process of shaping perceptions before making a request — serves as a technique for navigating complex social situations.
- **Experimental and Applied Research**: His work draws on experimental social psychology and real-world anecdotes to demonstrate how these principles operate largely automatically in human decision-making. The book's later chapters address practical application strategies and ethical concerns around manipulation.
- His work informs understanding of how heuristic shortcuts in decision-making can be leveraged or exploited.

## Ethical Distinction

Cialdini draws a line between legitimate influence and manipulation, emphasizing that effective persuasion should respect autonomy and not exploit cognitive vulnerabilities.
