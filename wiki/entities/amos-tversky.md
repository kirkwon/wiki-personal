# Amos Tversky

## Overview

Amos Tversky (1937–1996) was an Israeli cognitive psychologist and a foundational figure in the behavioral decision-making movement. Together with [[daniel-kahneman]], he pioneered the study of heuristics and biases in human judgment, and co-created [[prospect-theory]] — one of the most influential theoretical models in the social sciences. Tversky was a professor at Stanford University and later at Harvard, and his work reshaped economics, psychology, law, and medical decision-making.

Tversky's intellectual signature was a rare combination of mathematical rigor and deep insight into human reasoning. He and [[daniel-kahneman]] began collaborating in the late 1960s, meeting regularly in Jerusalem and later across the Atlantic, producing a stream of papers that systematically dismantled the rational-actor model underlying classical economics. Where Kahneman was more intuitive and expansive, Tversky was precise, skeptical, and relentlessly focused on formal theoretical models — a complementarity that made their partnership extraordinarily productive.

Although he died before the Nobel Prize was awarded to [[daniel-kahneman]] in 2002, Tversky's contributions were fully recognized in the award statement. Had he lived, he would almost certainly have been a co-recipient. Richard Thaler, a later Nobel winner in behavioral economics, called the Kahneman-Tversky collaboration "the greatest intellectual partnership in the history of social science."

## Key Works

- *Judgment Under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases* (1974, with [[daniel-kahneman]]) — The seminal taxonomy of cognitive shortcuts and their systematic errors
- *Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision Under Risk* (1979, with [[daniel-kahneman]]) — The foundational text of behavioral economics (see [[prospect-theory]])
- *Advances in Prospect Theory: Cumulative Representation of Uncertainty* (1992, with [[daniel-kahneman]]) — The refined cumulative version of prospect theory
- *The Framing of Decisions and the Psychology of Choice* (1981, with [[daniel-kahneman]]) — Demonstrating how the same choice, framed differently, produces preference reversals

## Influence

- His and [[daniel-kahneman]]'s work on [[prospect-theory]] directly spawned the field of behavioral economics, influencing Richard Thaler's nudge theory and Cass Sunstein's choice architecture
- The concept of [[loss-aversion]] — which Tversky helped formalize mathematically — has become a core principle in marketing, negotiation, and policymaking
- His work on cognitive biases (collected in the [[cognitive-biases-library]]) is taught in psychology, business, medicine, and law schools worldwide
- The Tversky-Kahneman partnership is studied as a model of successful interdisciplinary collaboration in science
