# Daniel Kahneman

Daniel Kahneman is an Israeli-American psychologist and Nobel laureate, widely regarded as the father of behavioral economics. Born in 1934 in Tel Aviv, he spent his career at Princeton University and Hebrew University, fundamentally reshaping how we understand human judgment and decision-making. In 2002, he was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for his work integrating psychological insights into economic science — work done primarily in collaboration with [[amos-tversky]].

Kahneman is the author of *Thinking, Fast and Slow* and co-developed prospect theory with Amos Tversky. His central contribution is the empirical demonstration that human beings are not the rational actors assumed by classical economic theory. Through decades of experiments, he showed that people rely on cognitive shortcuts (heuristics) that produce systematic biases in judgment. His research uncovered the psychological mechanisms behind risk perception, probability assessment, and value attribution, creating a new field at the intersection of psychology and economics. His collaboration with Amos Tversky produced foundational research in behavioral economics and decision-making under uncertainty.

His work fundamentally changed economics, psychology, and public policy by demonstrating that human decision-making is far less rational than classical economics assumed. His research with Tversky laid the groundwork for what is now called behavioral economics.

His 2011 book *Thinking, Fast and Slow* synthesized a lifetime of research into a single accessible framework, introducing millions of readers to the [[dual-process-theory]] of System 1 (fast, intuitive) and System 2 (slow, deliberate) thinking. The book became a global bestseller and cemented Kahneman's influence well beyond academia, reaching practitioners in business, medicine, law, and public policy. His later book *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* examines inconsistency in human judgment and makes the case for systematic processes to reduce errors.

## Key Contributions

- **Dual-process theory** — the framework of System 1 (fast, intuitive) and System 2 (slow, deliberate) thinking (see [[dual-process-theory]] / [[system-1-vs-system-2-thinking]])
- **Prospect theory** — how people evaluate losses and gains asymmetrically, developed with [[amos-tversky]] (see [[prospect-theory]])
- **Heuristics and biases research** — identifying systematic errors in human judgment (see [[heuristics-and-biases]])
- **Loss aversion** — the principle that losses loom larger than equivalent gains (see [[loss-aversion]]); losses hurt roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good
- **Noise in judgment** — demonstrating that human decision-making is subject to random variation, making structured processes valuable for reducing errors (see [[noise-a-flaw-in-human-judgment]])
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — comprehensive synthesis of decades of research

## Key Works

- *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) — The definitive synthesis of Kahneman's research on judgment and decision-making, organized around the dual-process framework
- *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* — examines inconsistency in human judgment and the case for systematic processes to reduce errors
- *Judgment Under Uncertainty: Heuristics and Biases* (1974, with [[amos-tversky]]) — The landmark paper that launched the heuristics and biases research program
- *Prospect Theory: An Analysis of Decision Under Risk* (1979, with [[amos-tversky]]) — The paper that founded behavioral economics
- *Choices, Values, and Frames* (1984, with [[amos-tversky]]) — Extending prospect theory to framing effects and preference reversals

## Relevance to Processes

Kahneman's work on noise in judgment supports the Processes mental model by demonstrating that human decision-making is subject to random variation, making structured processes valuable for reducing errors.

## Influence

- Founded behavioral economics alongside [[amos-tversky]], directly inspiring later Nobel winners like Richard Thaler
- His work on [[loss-aversion]] has influenced everything from marketing to public policy (e.g., organ donation opt-out policies)
- The heuristics-and-biases tradition he pioneered shaped modern [[cognitive-biases-library]] frameworks used in critical thinking education
- His collaboration with Gary Klein (adversarial collaboration) demonstrated his commitment to scientific dialogue, directly engaging with [[naturalistic-decision-making]] to test the boundaries of his own theories
- His framework also connects to Annie Duke's [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] approach to probabilistic decision-making

## Relationship to Other Figures

Kahneman's work closely parallels and sometimes contrasts with [[gary-klein]]'s naturalistic decision-making research (see [[two-views-of-decision-kahneman-vs-klein]]). His research on cognitive biases, prospect theory, and the dual-process theory of human cognition has shaped how we understand decision-making under uncertainty.
