# Factor Crowding & Rotation

Factor crowding refers to the phenomenon where multiple factors or variables converge within a system, potentially leading to complex interactions; in cooking, this could mean combining various ingredients that affect flavor profiles. Factor rotation is an analytical technique used in multivariate analysis (such as Principal Component Analysis) to reorient data axes for improved interpretability and variance explanation, which can be likened to adjusting camera angles or lighting to enhance photographic composition. In personal finance, it involves strategically reallocating assets across different investment factors to optimize portfolio performance, while in cognitive science, factor rotation might relate to reorganizing mental constructs for clearer understanding of thought processes.
