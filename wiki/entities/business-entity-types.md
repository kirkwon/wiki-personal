# Business Entity Types

This page catalogs the primary business entity types available for structuring businesses and investments, from simple sole proprietorships to complex corporate and trust structures.

## Sole Proprietorship
Business owned and operated by one individual with no legal separation between owner and business. Minimal formation costs ($50–200 for DBA), pass-through taxation, but unlimited personal liability. Suitable for freelancers, consultants, and low-risk businesses. No ability to raise capital or offer equity.

## General Partnership (GP)
Owned by two or more individuals sharing management and unlimited liability. Joint and several liability means each partner is liable for all debts. Automatic formation when co-owners operate together. Partnership dissolves upon partner death or withdrawal.

## Limited Partnership (LP)
Combines a general partner (unlimited liability, management control) with limited partners (liability capped at investment amount, no management rights). Can attract passive investors while maintaining control. Pass-through taxation. Limited partners lose protection if they participate in management.

## Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)
All partners have limited liability from partnership debts, though each retains unlimited liability for their own wrongful acts. More complex formation than GP but less than corporation. Suitable for professional service partnerships (lawyers, accountants, doctors).

## LLC (Limited Liability Company)
Hybrid entity combining corporate limited liability with pass-through taxation. Flexible management (member-managed or manager-managed). Requires operating agreement, articles of organization, and EIN. Higher formation costs than sole proprietorship but superior liability protection. Ideal for small businesses, real estate investors, and family businesses.

## C Corporation
Separate legal entity with limited liability but double taxation — corporate profits taxed, then dividends taxed again to shareholders. Unlimited shareholders and stock classes. Complex compliance requirements (board meetings, annual filings, franchise taxes). Suitable for large businesses and venture-backed startups.

## S Corporation
Small business corporation with pass-through taxation and corporate formalities. Must file Form 2553 for election. Max 100 shareholders, one class of stock, domestic only. Must pay reasonable compensation to employee-shareholders. Reduces self-employment tax by taxing only salary at payroll rates, distributions as ordinary income.

## REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts)
SEC-regulated entities owning or operating income-producing real estate. Minimum 100 shareholders, 75% assets and income from real estate, 90% distribution requirement. Pass-through taxation at entity level. Publicly traded or non-traded structures.

## Family Limited Partnerships (FLPs)
LPs used for estate planning and wealth transfer among family members. General partners (usually parents) control the partnership; limited partners (usually children) have liability protection. Valuation discounts for gift tax purposes.
