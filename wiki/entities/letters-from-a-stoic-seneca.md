# Letters from a Stoic

*Letters from a Stoic* is a collection of 124 letters written by the Roman Stoic philosopher Seneca to his protégé Lucilius. The work spans topics including time management, reading practices, friendship, death, aging, anger, grief, wealth, and simple living — always emphasizing practical application of philosophy to daily life.

## Structure and Themes

- **Letter 1**: On Saving Time — time as life's most precious resource.
- **Letter 2**: On Discursiveness in Reading — depth over breadth.
- **Letter 3**: On True and False Friendship — evaluating relationships for authenticity.
- **Letter 4**: On the Terrors of Death — facing mortality calmly.
- **Letter 5**: On the Philosopher's Mean — moderation and avoiding extremes.
- **Letter 10**: On Living Simply — rejecting luxury and extravagance.
- **Letter 11**: On Blushing — handling shame and embarrassment.
- **Letter 12**: On Old Age — aging from a Stoic perspective.
- **Letter 13**: On Groundless Fears — not fearing unlikely or uncontrollable things.
- **Letter 16**: On Philosophy, the Guide of Life — philosophy as practical guide.

## Practical Tools

The letters provide frameworks for philosophical journaling, time management, simple living, friendship evaluation, emotional regulation, mortality reflection, and daily self-examination.

## When to Stop

Seneca warns against using Stoic principles to justify passivity, emotional numbing, asceticism, or intellectual detachment from life.
