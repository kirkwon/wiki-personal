# The Art of War - Sun Tzu

Ancient Chinese military treatise attributed to Sun Tzu, comprising thirteen chapters covering strategy, tactics, terrain, and espionage. The central thesis is that superior strategy—careful planning, terrain understanding, intelligence, adaptability, and positioning—allows victory without direct confrontation, and prolonged conflict is self-destructive.

## Key Themes
- **Victory without fighting** (Chapter 3): The highest form of generalship is to defeat the enemy without fighting through strategy and maneuvering.
- **Water analogy** (Chapter 5): Military forces should flow like water seeking low ground, adapting to circumstances.
- **Intelligence** (Chapter 13): Espionage and information gathering are critical force multipliers.
- **Deception** (Chapter 1, 3): Strategic misdirection and feints create advantage.
- **Adaptability** (Chapters 5, 6, 8): Tactics must vary with the specific situation rather than rigidly following rules.
- **Terrain analysis** (Chapters 9, 10): Understanding the operating landscape is essential for positioning and movement.

## Connections to Wiki

## When to Stop
The source explicitly warns against:
- When strategy becomes manipulation or unethical
- When overthinking prevents action
- When war metaphor is inappropriate for cooperative contexts
- When sacrificing ethics for victory
- When treating every situation as zero-sum conflict

8. Variation in Tactics – Adaptability

11. The Nine Situations – Strategic situation responses

13. The Use of Spies – Intelligence and espionage
