# Cass R. Sunstein

Cass R. Sunstein is an American legal scholar and behavioral economist best known as co-author of [[nudge]] (also *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness*) with [[richard-h-thaler]]. His work focuses on how law, regulation, and institutional design can incorporate behavioral insights to improve outcomes while preserving individual freedom. Sunstein has been influential in applying nudge theory to governance and public policy settings.

His work spans constitutional law, administrative law, and behavioral economics, focusing on how government and institutional design can influence decision-making without restricting freedom of choice. He has written extensively on regulatory policy, constitutional law, and behavioral public policy. His work bridges law, behavioral economics, and public policy, emphasizing choice architecture, libertarian paternalism, and how institutional design can influence decisions without restricting freedom of choice.

Sunstein's collaboration with Thaler on [[nudge]] connects to broader themes of [[choice-architecture]] and [[framing-effects]], showing how defaults and environmental cues shape outcomes in complex systems. His work also connects to [[nudges]] and [[decision-making-frameworks]].

## Key Contributions
- Co-authored *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness* with [[richard-h-thaler]], introducing choice architecture and the concept of "nudge" and libertarian paternalism to a broad audience.
- Applied behavioral economics to legal and regulatory frameworks; former administrator of the White House Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs.
- Extensive work on the ethical and legal implications of nudging.
- Explored how choice architecture can be used in public policy and written on constitutional law and behavioral public policy.
