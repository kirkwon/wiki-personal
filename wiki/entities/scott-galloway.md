# Scott Galloway

Scott Galloway is a business strategist, professor at NYU Stern, and author known for his sharp analysis of technology markets, corporate competition, and societal trends. He has written multiple books examining how large-scale forces shape economic and social outcomes. His work bridges [[platform-dominance]] and [[personal-finance]], emphasizing that strategic thinking applies equally to business and personal life.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]^[raw/articles/Society.md]

## Key Works

- [[the-four---scott-galloway]] — *The Four: The Hidden DNA of Amazon, Apple, Google, and Facebook* (2017) — Analysis of the four dominant technology platforms shaping the economy: Amazon, Apple, Facebook, and Google, identifying them as a tech oligopoly reshaping global business landscapes.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]
- [[the-algebra-of-happiness---scott-galloway]] — *The Algebra of Happiness* (2019/2024) — Framework for personal and professional satisfaction, applying competitive strategy frameworks to life decisions.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]
- [[Post Corona_ From Crisis to Opportunity - Scott Galloway.md]] — *Post Corona: From Crisis to Opportunity* (2024) — Post-pandemic business transformation and geopolitical shifts.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]
- [[Adrift - Scott Galloway]] — *Adrift* — Examines how social and economic systems drift without clear direction, providing a societal-level analysis that complements platform analysis.^[raw/articles/Society.md]

## Competitive Framework

Galloway's framework contrasts each US tech giant with a rival from China or another sector—Apple vs. Amazon, Google vs. Uber, Facebook vs. Baidu, Microsoft vs. Tencent—framing competition as asymmetric warfare. His analysis connects to broader questions about strategic adaptation under uncertainty and the role of agility in rapidly evolving marketplaces.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]

## Central Arguments in Post Corona

Galloway argues that COVID-19 is a decisive inflection point requiring fundamental rethinking of capitalism, work, retail, cities, and geopolitics. Companies that pivot swiftly will dominate. Key themes include tech titan power struggles, remote work normalization, urban resilience, and the tension between nationalism and globalization.

## Connection to Society Mental Model

Galloway's *Adrift* provides a societal-level analysis of economic and social drift, complementing his platform analysis in *The Four*. His work connects to how individuals navigate [[satisfaction-under-constraints]] within complex systems.^[raw/articles/Society.md]
