# Richard H. Thaler

Richard H. Thaler is an American behavioral economist and Nobel laureate known for challenging traditional economic assumptions of rational agent behavior. He is a central figure in the shift from traditional rational-agent models to behavioral economics, and his research demonstrates that humans are systematically biased—deviating from rationality through predictable cognitive biases. He co-authored [[nudge]] with Cass R. Sunstein, introducing the concept of choice architecture and libertarian paternalism to a broad audience. His work shows that small environmental changes—nudges—can improve decisions without restricting freedom of choice, exploring how subtle changes in how options are presented can significantly affect outcomes.

## Key Contributions
- Pioneered behavioral economics by challenging the rational-agent model of traditional economics
- Co-developed the concept of "nudging" and choice architecture — subtle environmental changes and the design of choices and defaults that influence decisions, including [[default-effects]]
- Demonstrated how small changes in how choices are presented can significantly affect outcomes
- Introduced mental accounting — how people categorize and evaluate financial decisions differently, compartmentalizing finances into separate mental accounts rather than making holistic financial decisions
- Advanced prospect theory's applications, demonstrating how loss aversion shapes risk behavior
- Worked on the endowment effect and other cognitive biases
- Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences (2017) for contributions to behavioral economics
- Information disclosure and privacy in decision contexts
- Demonstrated how cognitive biases systematically shape how people interpret data and make choices
- Research connects to [[choice-overload]] and [[decision-making-frameworks]], showing how environmental design influences behavior through nudges and choice architecture
- His research connects directly to [[framing-effects]], [[cognitive-biases-library]], and [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]]

## Core Works
- *Nudge: Improving Decisions About Health, Wealth, and Happiness* (with Cass R. Sunstein, 2008; 2024 edition referenced)
- *Misbehaving: The Making of Behavioral Economics* (2015, updated 2024 edition)

## Why It Matters
His frameworks provide practical tools for policy design, business strategy, and technology design by accounting for predictable human irrationality rather than assuming rational agents. His work bridges traditional economics with cognitive biases and prospect theory.
