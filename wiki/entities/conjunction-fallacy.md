# Conjunction fallacy

The conjunction fallacy occurs when it is assumed that the combination of two events is more likely than one single event. This error in probability reasoning often leads to overestimating the likelihood of specific scenarios (e.g., a chef using rare spices and taking professional-level photos) over simpler, broader ones (such as cooking with common ingredients or basic photography techniques).


In personal finance, it might manifest when someone believes that investing in multiple niche markets is more probable than diversifying across a few stable sectors. In cognitive science, this fallacy highlights the human tendency to perceive complex situations as more common due to their vividness or detail, despite statistical evidence suggesting otherwise.
