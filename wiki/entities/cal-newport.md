# Cal Newport

Cal Newport is a computer scientist, professor at Georgetown University, and author known for his work on deep work, digital minimalism, slow productivity, and career strategy. His work examines how societal norms, career market structures, and choice ecosystems shape individual behavior around work and career, exploring the impact of technology on cognitive function and personal wellbeing.^[raw/articles/Society.md] He advocates for intentional technology use and the development of rare and valuable skills through deliberate practice. His core philosophy centers on the value of deep, focused work in an age of constant distraction, providing actionable frameworks for building work habits that prioritize quality and depth over busyness and superficial output. His book *Deep Work* emphasizes the importance of environmental design for achieving focused, high-quality work.^[raw/articles/Environment.md]

He is referenced in the [[environment]] mental model as a source for understanding how surroundings and context shape performance outcomes.

## Key Works

- [[deep-work---cal-newport]] / *Deep Work* (2016) — Argues for focused, distraction-free work as a path to professional excellence and emphasizes environmental design for deep focus
- [[so-good-they-can-t-ignore-you]] / *So Good They Can't Ignore You* (2014) — Career capital, deliberate practice, and mission-driven work; argues career satisfaction comes from rare and valuable skills rather than following passion
- [[slow-productivity---cal-newport]] / *Slow Productivity* (2024) — Challenges hustle culture and advocates for doing fewer things at higher quality, proposing focused work sprints, strategic batching, solitude for creativity, and intentional technology selection
- *Digital Minimalism* (2019) — On intentional technology use driven by purpose rather than distraction

## Key Ideas
- **Digital Minimalism**: Intentional technology use driven by purpose rather than distraction
- **Purposeful Use Principle**: Evaluate tools by whether they serve important goals before adopting them
- **Slow Productivity**: Challenges the modern glorification of busyness, advocating for depth-first approaches to work, prioritizing deep focus, meaningful engagement, and boundaries over volume and constant activity
- **The Toll of Distraction**: Digital distractions drain mental energy and reduce capacity for deep thinking
- **Focus tools and technology-free zones**: Practical tactics for reducing digital interruptions, including environmental design for focused work
- "Follow your passion" is bad advice; develop rare skills first, then leverage them for meaningful work
- Deliberate practice is the foundation of expertise
- Skill capital (craft capital) matters more than general career credentials
- Passion is a result of skill development, not a starting point
- Modern knowledge work culture fetishizes busyness, producing low-quality output and poor well-being
- His work draws on neuroscience of attention and cultural critique of hustle culture
