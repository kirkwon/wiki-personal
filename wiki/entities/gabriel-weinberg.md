# Gabriel Weinberg

Founder of DuckDuckGo and author of *Super Thinking* (2024), a beginner-level book that presents mental models as a toolkit for better decision-making, problem-solving, and learning. The book's core thesis is that combining multiple mental models yields deeper insight and better outcomes than relying on any single framework.

Weinberg synthesized established concepts from cognitive science, economics, systems theory, psychology, decision science, game theory, creativity techniques, and learning science into an accessible toolkit. His "when to stop" guardrails — warning against analysis paralysis, model imperialism, and using complexity to sound smart — distinguish his approach from more prescriptive mental model libraries.

The book is organized into seven parts for improving decision-making across domains:

- **Part I: Core Concepts** — Map/Territory, Occam's Razor, First/Second-Order Thinking, Pareto Principle
- **Part II: Decision Making** — Bayes' Rule, Base Rate Fallacy, Sunk Cost, Opportunity Cost, Margin of Safety
- **Part III: Strategic Thinking** — Game Theory, Prisoner's Dilemma, Tragedy of the Commons, Competitive Advantage, Network Effects
- **Part IV: Systems Thinking** — Feedback Loops, Unintended Consequences, Dynamics, Emergence, Equilibrium
- **Part V: Creativity** — Lateral Thinking, Mental Sandbox, Inversion, First Principles, Thought Experiments
- **Part VI: Social/Emotional Intelligence** — Reciprocity, Authority Bias, Social Proof, In-Group Bias, Empathy
- **Part VII: Learning/Growth** — Growth Mindset, Feynman Technique, Spaced Repetition, Deliberate Practice, Dunning-Kruger Effect

His work connects closely to [[daniel-kahneman]] and [[gary-klein]]'s perspectives on decision-making, and extends the [[cognitive-biases-library]] with practical application frameworks. It is part of the broader mental model ecosystem including [[mental-model-library]].
