# Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)

Rosemary is a robust, aromatic herb from the Lamiaceae (mint) family, native to the Mediterranean. It has a distinctive pine-like, woodsy, camphoraceous flavor profile that stands up well to cooking heat, making it essential for roasting meats, grilling, and hearty dishes.

## Flavor Profile

### Primary Notes
- **Pine**: Coniferous, forest-like aroma
- **Woodsy**: Earthy, tree bark notes
- **Camphor**: Medicinal, cooling undertones
- **Mint**: Subtle mint family characteristics
- **Balsamic**: Sweet, resinous finish

### Varietal Differences
- **Tuscan Blue**: Classic pine, strong — best for roasts and grilling; upright, blue flowers
- **Arp**: Milder, sweeter — good for fried foods and desserts; trailing habit, pale flowers
- **Prostratus**: Very mild — suitable for ground covers and mild dishes; low-growing, trailing
- **Majorica Pink**: Floral, mild — ideal for garnishes and light dishes; pink flowers, compact

## Culinary Applications

### Mediterranean Cuisine
Rosemary is fundamental to Mediterranean cooking: roasts (lamb, chicken, potatoes), grilled skewers, breads, focaccia, rosemary bread, and infused olive oils.

### French Cuisine
Key component of [[herbes-de-provence]], used in roasts (beef, pork, root vegetables), compound butters for steaks, savory pastries, stuffings, and stocks.

### Italian Cuisine
Fresh rosemary on focaccia and pizza, roasts (pork, chicken, vegetables), grilled meats and vegetables, rosemary breadsticks.

### British/American
Classic Sunday roast potatoes, lamb preparations, infused butters, oils, vinegars, and BBQ rubs.

## Pairing Guide

### Proteins
- Excellent: Lamb, chicken, pork, beef

### Vegetables
- Excellent: Potatoes, carrots, eggplant, mushrooms

### Other Ingredients
- Perfect pairings: Garlic, thyme, lemon, olive oil
- Good with: Sage, oregano, rose wine
- Blend component: Herbes de Provence, bay leaves

## Storage & Handling

### Fresh
- Refrigerator: Bundle, wrap in damp paper towel — 7-10 days
- Change paper towel every 3 days
- Selection: Dark green, flexible stems, strong pine fragrance

### Dried
- Air dry: Hang upside down in dark, dry place — 1-2 weeks
- Store in airtight container
- Ratio: 1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried

### Frozen
- Whole sprigs or chopped in ice cube trays with oil — 3-4 months
- Best for roasts, stews, cooked dishes

### Infused Oils
- Steep sprigs in olive oil — 1-2 weeks
- Refrigerate for up to 2 months

## Preparation Methods

1. **Strip leaves**: Run fingers down stem
2. **Chiffonade**: Stack, roll, slice thin ribbons
3. **Chopped**: Fine chop for even distribution
4. **Whole sprigs**: Insert in roasts for flavor extraction, remove before serving
5. **Infused**: Add to oils, vinegars, butters

### Handling Tips
- Use sparingly — strong flavor, start with small amounts
- Remove whole sprigs before serving
- Add early — handles long cooking times
- Toast briefly in pan to enhance aroma
- Crush to release oils before adding

## Classic Recipes

### Rosemary Roast Potatoes
Toss 2 lbs cubed potatoes with 1/4 cup olive oil, salt, pepper, 2 rosemary sprigs (leaves stripped), and 4 smashed garlic cloves. Roast at 425°F (220°C) for 45-60 minutes. Remove rosemary sprigs before serving.

### Rosemary Garlic Butter
Mix 1 cup softened unsalted butter with 2 tbsp chopped rosemary, 3 minced garlic cloves, 1 tsp salt, 1/2 tsp pepper. Roll into log, chill 2 hours. Use on steaks, vegetables, bread.

### Mediterranean Skewers
Marinate 1 lb cubed lamb with zucchini, bell peppers, red onion in olive oil, lemon juice, salt, pepper. Thread onto rosemary sprigs (soaked in water). Grill 10-15 minutes. Remove sprigs before serving.

### Rosemary Infused Olive Oil
Heat extra virgin olive oil gently with 4-6 fresh rosemary sprigs until fragrant (do not boil). Steep 1-2 hours, strain through fine mesh, store in dark bottle refrigerated.

## Substitutions

- **Thyme**: Similar strength, 1:1 ratio — different aromatic profile, less piney
- **Sage**: Milder, 1:1 ratio
- **Thyme + Sage mix**: Approximation of earthiness, missing pine
- **Dried vs Fresh**: 1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried; dried is more concentrated but less aromatic

## Common Mistakes

- **Using too much**: Causes overpowering pine-tasting soapiness — less is more
- **Old/dried rosemary**: Loss of aroma, harsh flavor — use fresh or properly dried
- **Cooking whole sprigs**: Hard to remove, bitter when eaten — strip leaves or tie with twine for easy removal
- **Overwatering plants**: Root rot, poor flavor — let soil dry between waterings

## Health Benefits
- Antioxidants: Rich in rosmarinic acid
- Memory enhancement: Traditional cognitive aid
- Anti-inflammatory: Contains carnosic acid
- Digestive aid: Stimulates bile production
- Antimicrobial: Natural preservative properties

## Cultural Significance
Ancient Greece: Symbol of remembrance and fidelity. Middle Ages: Used in weddings and funerals. Traditional medicine for memory and circulation. Mediterranean culinary staple for thousands of years.

## Growing Tips
- Sun: Full sun (6-8 hours)
- Soil: Well-drained, alkaline preferred
- Water: Drought-tolerant, prefers dry
- Pruning: Regularly to maintain shape
- Harvest: Year-round, morning preferred
- Winter: Evergreen in mild climates
