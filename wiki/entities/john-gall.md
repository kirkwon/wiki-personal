# John Gall

Author of *The Systems Bible: The Beginner's Guide to Systems Large and Small*, a foundational text on systems thinking. Gall's work provides a practical, often humorous guide to understanding how systems behave, emphasizing that systems produce outcomes not intended by their designers. His central insight is that complex working systems must evolve from simple working systems—not be designed whole. Gall's perspective emphasizes that complex systems have inherent characteristics that resist simple prediction and reductionist analysis, and that understanding natural tendencies in complex systems is essential for effective navigation.

His ideas are foundational to the [[system-mental-model]] as one of its two canonical source references. His work contributes to the [[implications-mental-model]] by providing a framework for understanding what can be inferred about complex systems from their structure and observed behaviors. Gall's systems perspective emphasizes that complex systems often exhibit behaviors that are not predictable from their individual components, and that the implications of any change to a system are frequently non-obvious and far-reaching.

Gall is also referenced as the source for the [[theorem]] mental model, which frames systems thinking as a conceptual shortcut for navigating complexity.

## Key Contributions
- **Gall's Law** — The principle that complex systems evolve from simple ones
- **Fundamental Theorem of Systems** — "New systems generate new problems"
- **System Replacement vs. Repair** — Framework for deciding when to replace vs. fix
- **Unintended Consequence Analysis** — Methodology for anticipating side effects
- Authored *The Systems Bible*, which explores how systems behave, fail, and interact
- Provides a systems-level perspective on understanding complexity and navigating problems
