# Kevin Kelly

Kevin Kelly is an author, journalist, and founding executive editor of *Wired* magazine. He is known for his work on the intersection of technology, ecology, and complex systems, and for exploring how intelligence and order emerge from decentralized, self‑organizing systems without central control.^[raw/articles/Technology.md]

His work draws heavily on biology, complexity science, and network theory to propose design principles for distributed systems. He coined or popularized concepts such as the "technium" and has written extensively about the implications of networked, emergent systems for technology and society.^[raw/articles/Technology.md]

## Key Ideas

- Intelligence can emerge from complex systems in ways that are not centrally controlled.^[raw/articles/Intelligence.md]
- Distributed networks and bottom‑up processes can produce intelligent behavior.^[raw/articles/Intelligence.md]
- Out‑of‑control systems exhibit emergent properties that cannot be predicted from individual components alone.^[raw/articles/Intelligence.md]
- Order arises without central control, and the most important effects in complex systems are often unintended.^[raw/articles/Implications.md]
- Self‑organization, swarm intelligence, and the Nine Laws of God describe design principles for emergent systems.^[raw/articles/Technology.md]

## Key Works

- [[Out-of-Control---Kevin-Kelly]] (2024) – Central thesis on self‑organization, swarm intelligence, and the Nine Laws of God for designing emergent systems.^[raw/articles/Technology.md]
- *The Inevitable* – Explores trends shaping the future of technology.
- *What Technology Wants* – Frames technology as an evolving, self‑organizing system.

## Connection to Intelligence Model

Kelly’s perspective on emergent intelligence complements the broader intelligence mental model by showing that intelligence is not limited to individual cognition but can arise from collective and systemic processes. This connects to [[Out-of-Control---Kevin-Kelly]] and the broader themes of [[obstacles]] and [[reality-mental-model]].^[raw/articles/Intelligence.md]

His work on how order arises without central control and how the most important effects in complex systems are often unintended contributes to the [[implications-mental-model]] by highlighting the difficulty of predicting and managing downstream consequences in complex environments.^[raw/articles/Implications.md]

## Relevance

Kelly’s frameworks connect to [[self-organization-and-emergence]], [[swarm-intelligence]], and [[the-nine-laws-of-god]]. His emphasis on decentralized design contrasts with centrally managed approaches found in [[building-a-cohesive-team]] and raises questions about when control versus emergence is optimal.

He is referenced alongside Sönke Ahrens, Tiago Forte, and Rolf Dobelli as part of the cross‑source grounding for the [[Information]] mental model.^[Information.md] His work connects to information as a mental model by framing how patterns and relationships emerge from complex systems.
