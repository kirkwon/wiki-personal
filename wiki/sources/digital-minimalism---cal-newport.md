# Digital Minimalism - Cal Newport

Cal Newport's 2024 book argues that default digital habits are distraction-filled and undermine deep thinking, wellbeing, and fulfillment. A minimalist, purpose-driven approach to technology use restores cognitive capacity and life satisfaction. It extends the ideas in [[deep-work---cal-newport]] by emphasizing the value of solitude, leisure, and focused attention in an age of constant connectivity.

## Core Framework
- **Digital Minimalism**: Intentional, purpose-driven technology use vs. distraction-filled default habits
- **The Purposeful Use Principle**: Evaluate tech by whether it serves important goals, not by convenience or entertainment value
- **The Minimalist Mindset**: Intentionality over quantity in tech use

## Key Themes
- Intentional technology selection
- Solitude and leisure as essential human needs
- Reducing digital distractions to enable deeper work and presence
- Quality over quantity in digital engagement

## Key Chapters
1. The Case for a Purposeful Life Online
2. The Minimalist Mindset

10. Purposeful Content Consumption — curated feeds, quality sources, avoiding mindless scrolling
11. Purposeful Technology Investment — critically evaluating new tech before adopting
12. Purposeful Work in a Digital Age
13. Purposeful Learning in a Digital Age
14. Purposeful Parenting in a Digital World
15. Purposeful Leadership in a Digital World
16. Purposeful Digital Minimalism in Action — personal anecdotal evidence from Newport's implementation

## Connections
This framework directly contrasts with [[hooked---nir-eyal]]'s Hook Model, which describes how to build habit-forming products. Newport advocates stepping back from those very mechanisms. It also connects to [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]] on distraction and cognitive depletion, [[time-management-matrix]] on prioritizing important over urgent, and [[Hyperfocus - Chris Bailey]] on managing attention.

## Notes
The argument is largely philosophical and prescriptive rather than empirically rigorous—strong on reasoning, weaker on controlled evidence. Chapter 3 cites research on technology's negative cognitive impacts.
