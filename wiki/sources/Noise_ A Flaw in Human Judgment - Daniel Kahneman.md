# Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment - Daniel Kahneman

Daniel Kahneman's *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment* (2024) introduces noise as a pervasive problem in human decision-making — the inconsistency and variability in judgments across different decision-makers, distinct from the systematic errors captured by the concept of bias.

## Core Thesis

Noise refers to unwanted variability in judgments — the degree to which different people making similar decisions arrive at different outcomes, or the same person making similar decisions at different times. This variability degrades decision quality across medicine, finance, criminal justice, and other fields, often at significant cost.

## Key Concepts

- **Noise vs. Bias**: Noise is random variability in judgments; bias is systematic deviation from accuracy. Both degrade decision quality but require different remedies.
- **Measuring Noise**: Statistical methods can quantify judgment inconsistency across domains, revealing the true scale of the problem.
- **Costs of Variability**: Excessive noise leads to suboptimal decisions, unfair outcomes, and wasted resources in medicine, finance, and criminal justice.
- **Uncovering Noise**: Techniques for comparing expert judgments under controlled conditions reveal sources of inconsistency.
- **Organizational Reduction**: Collaborative culture, structured decision processes, and calibration can reduce noise.

## Domains Covered

- **Medicine**: Misdiagnosis, inconsistent treatment selection, variable clinical trial interpretation.
- **Finance**: Investor judgment variability contributing to market volatility and inconsistent wealth allocation.
- **Criminal Justice**: Inconsistent sentencing outcomes among judges, raising fairness and efficiency concerns.

## Practical Takeaways

Noise can be measured and reduced through structured processes, calibration exercises, and organizational culture that promotes shared decision-making and minimizes competitive behavior. The book emphasizes consistency among judgments as a means to improve overall outcomes.
