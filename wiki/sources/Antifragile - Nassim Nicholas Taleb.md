## Key Concepts

- **Antifragility** — Systems that gain from disorder and stress. Already in wiki as [[antifragility]] (central).
- **Fragility** — The opposite: systems that break under stress. Mentioned as counterpart.
- **Black Swan events** — Unexpected, high-consequence events. Already in wiki as [[black-swan-events]].
- **Tail risk / extreme outcomes** — Focus on rare, high-impact scenarios. Related to [[antifragility]] and [[black-swan-events]].
- **Epistemological fallacy** — Mistaking perceived certainty for truth, leading to fragility. New concept for wiki.
- **Measurement errors** — Statistical measures that reduce variability but increase fragility. Overlaps with [[limits-of-computation]] and [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]].
- **Embracing randomness** — Variability as a source of growth. Related to [[uncertainty]] and [[variability]].

## Main Arguments & Findings

- **Core claim**: Antifragility—systems that improve when stressed—is superior to robustness or fragility. Embracing uncertainty and variability leads to growth; avoiding them creates hidden fragility.
- **Evidence**: Conceptual arguments from Taleb's broader body of work; no empirical citations in this summary. Strength is theoretical and philosophical rather than empirical.
- **Supporting ideas**: Black Swan theory, measurement error problems, tail risk, epistemological fallacy, antifragile rules for individuals and societies.
