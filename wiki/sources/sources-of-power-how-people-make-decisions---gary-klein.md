# Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions - Gary Klein

Gary Klein's *Sources of Power* presents a landmark study of how experts make decisions under real-world conditions. Based on field research with fireground commanders and cross-domain cases in nursing, chess, military command, and business, Klein introduces the Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) model: experts recognize situations as familiar patterns, retrieve a typical response, and mentally simulate it rather than comparing options systematically.

Key concepts include the power of intuition rooted in extensive experience, mental simulation as a rapid evaluation tool, metaphor and analogy for transferring expertise across domains, and storytelling as a mechanism for knowledge transfer and shared mental model building. The book contrasts this naturalistic approach with classical decision theory that emphasizes rational option comparison and expected value maximization.

Practical implications span training (learning by doing with diverse scenarios), technology design (augmenting natural patterns rather than forcing rationalistic procedures), and organizational culture (valuing expertise and allowing narrative knowledge while avoiding excessive procedural constraints).

## Core Chapters

- **Chapter 1**: Chronicling fireground commanders — experienced leaders rarely compare options but rely on pattern recognition from past incidents.
- **Chapter 2**: The RPD Model — experts recognize situations as typical, retrieve a typical response, and mentally simulate it.
- **Chapter 3**: The Power of Intuition — rapid unconscious pattern recognition honed over years of practice.
- **Chapter 4**: The Power of Mental Simulation — running a course of action forward in time to check for flaws.
- **Chapter 5**: The Power of Metaphor and Analogy — mapping novel situations onto familiar experiences to transfer expertise.
- **Chapter 6**: The Power of Storytelling — narrative as a vehicle for sharing expertise and building shared mental models.
- **Chapter 7**: The Power of Conscious Thinking — balancing intuitive and analytical thinking depending on familiarity.
- **Chapter 8**: The Power of Experience — experience built through deliberate practice is irreplaceable by rules or algorithms.
- **Chapter 9**: Naturalistic Decision Making in the Wild — RPD confirmed across nursing, chess, military, and business.
- **Chapter 10**: A New View of Decision Making — decisions are about sizing up situations, not picking among options.
- **Chapter 11**: Implications for Training — simulations, case studies, and feedback over abstract principles.
- **Chapter 12**: Implications for Technology Design — decision support should augment natural patterns.
- **Chapter 13**: Implications for Organizations — cultures that value expertise and minimize unnecessary procedural constraints.
- **Chapter 14**: Final Reflections — respect for expertise and alignment of research/design with how people actually decide.
