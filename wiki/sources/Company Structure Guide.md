# Company Structure Guide

Comprehensive framework for selecting and structuring business entities to optimize liability protection, tax efficiency, and operational flexibility. Covers entity types from sole proprietorships to complex corporate structures, with specific focus on real estate investment applications and multi-entity strategies.

## Business Entity Types

### Sole Proprietorship
Business owned and operated by one individual with no legal separation between owner and business. Minimal formation costs, pass-through taxation, but unlimited personal liability.

### Partnership
- **General Partnership (GP)**: Two or more individuals with unlimited liability and joint and several liability.
- **Limited Partnership (LP)**: At least one general partner (unlimited liability) and one limited partner (liability limited to investment).
- **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP)**: All partners have limited liability protection from partnership debts.

### Limited Liability Company (LLC)
Flexible business entity combining limited liability of corporation with pass-through taxation. Members have limited liability, flexible management structure, and option to elect corporate taxation.

### Corporation
- **C Corporation**: Separate legal entity with double taxation structure.
- **S Corporation**: Small business corporation with pass-through taxation while maintaining corporate formalities. File Form 2553 for S Election.

### Special Purpose Entities
- **REITs**: Companies that own, operate, or finance income-producing real estate. Require SEC registration, minimum 100 shareholders.
- **FLPs**: Family Limited Partnerships where general partners are family members controlling the partnership.
- **Dynasty Trusts**: For long-term wealth preservation and asset protection.
- **International Business Companies (IBCs)**: Offshore business structures.

## Entity Selection Framework

Decision matrix based on liability assessment, income level, growth plans, and ownership structure guides entity selection. Key factors include:
- Liability risk
- Tax optimization
- Operational complexity
- Franchise tax differences across jurisdictions
- Reasonable compensation requirements
- Step-up in basis considerations
- Substance requirements
- Growth plans and ownership structure

## Multi-Entity Structures

Recommended structures:
- **Real Estate Investors**: Holding company (LLC or C Corp) → property-specific LLCs → operating company (LLC for property management). This layering optimizes risk isolation and liability protection.
- **Small Businesses**: Operating entity → IP holding entity → employee benefits entity.
- **Family Wealth Structures**: Multi-layer structures with holding companies, operating entities, and individual entities.

## Tax Planning Strategies

- **Pass-through entities** (LLC, S Corp, Partnership) avoid double taxation.
- **S Corp optimization**: Pay reasonable compensation to avoid self-employment tax on distributions.
- **C Corp strategies**: Retained earnings, dividend timing, fringe benefits.
- **Form 2553** (S Corp election) and **Form 8832** (C Corp election) are key IRS filings.

## Advanced Strategies

- **Charging order protection**: Legal mechanism protecting LLC interests from creditors.
- **Dynasty Trusts and Offshore Trusts**: For long-term wealth preservation and asset protection.
- **Asset Protection**: Multiple LLCs, separate identities, proper corporate formalities.

## Jurisdiction Considerations

- **Delaware**: Flexible corporate law, established case law.
- **Nevada**: Asset protection reputation, no corporate income tax.
- **Wyoming**: Low costs, privacy, favorable LLC laws.
- **California**: High franchise taxes (minimum $800 annually).
- **Texas**: No corporate income tax.

Regular review of entity structures as business conditions change is essential for maintaining optimal structure and compliance.

## Implementation

Step-by-step formation process: entity selection → name selection → document preparation → filing and registration. Post-formation requirements include annual compliance filings, board/shareholder meetings, financial record maintenance, and tax filings.
