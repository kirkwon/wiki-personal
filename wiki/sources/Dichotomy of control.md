# Dichotomy of control

## Summary

The source recontextualizes the Stoic concept of the dichotomy of control as a productivity skill. It focuses on auditing workflow, eliminating low-value tasks, and iterating on results to improve efficiency and output. The source draws primarily from Marcus Aurelius' *Meditations* but blends the philosophical principle with generic productivity optimization advice.

## Key Claims

- Dichotomy of control improves efficiency by focusing energy on controllable factors and eliminating waste.
- Application involves auditing workflow, restructuring approaches, and scaling effective methods.
- The skill connects to error-correction mechanisms, peer accountability systems, and scatterfocus.

## Connections

- Marcus Aurelius — *Meditations* as the philosophical origin.
- Related to existing productivity frameworks: [[deep-work---cal-newport]], [[getting-things-done---david-allen]], [[Hyperfocus - Chris Bailey]], [[scatterfocus]].
- Related mental models: [[what-is-the-unknown]], [[solutions-mental-model]], [[effort]].

## Tensions

The source conflates the Stoic principle (accepting what is uncontrollable) with productivity optimization (maximizing control over processes). This tension is not acknowledged in the source.
