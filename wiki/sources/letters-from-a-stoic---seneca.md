# Letters from a Stoic - Seneca

Seneca's *Letters from a Stoic* is a collection of 20 letters of practical philosophical advice addressed to Lucilius. The work covers topics ranging from the urgency of time and purposeful living, to the nature of true friendship, facing death without fear, moderation as a guiding principle, simple living over luxury, emotional regulation through reason, and the imperative to practice what one preaches.

## Key Themes

- **Time as precious resource**: Urges purposeful living and avoiding wasted time on trivial pursuits.
- **Depth over breadth in learning**: Advises against superficial reading; advocates for deep engagement with fewer texts.
- **True friendship vs. false friendship**: Distinguishes genuine relationships from superficial ones based on mutual virtue.
- **Memento mori**: Reflection on mortality to prioritize what truly matters.
- **Simple living**: Advocacy against luxury and extravagance.
- **Emotional regulation**: Managing anger, fear, shame, grief through rational analysis.
- **Philosophical seclusion vs. social engagement**: Balance between solitude for character development and meaningful social engagement.
- **Practical philosophy**: The demand to align actions with stated beliefs.
