# Source: Paul-Elder Critical Thinking Framework.md

## Key Entities
- **Richard Paul and Linda Elder** — Developers of the Paul-Elder Critical Thinking Framework. Central to the source. Likely not yet in wiki (index only references Paul-Elder via the comparison page title).
- **Paul-Elder Critical Thinking Framework** — Structured reasoning framework combining 8 elements and 9 intellectual standards. Central. Already exists as a page ([[paul-elder-critical-thinking-framework]]) per the index.
- **Critical Thinking: Tools for Taking Charge of Your Learning and Your Life** — Source book by Paul and Elder. Peripheral. Not in wiki index.

## Key Concepts
- **Eight Elements of Reasoning** — Purpose, Question, Information, Concepts, Assumptions, Implications, Point of View, Inferences. The structural backbone of the framework. Central. Not yet in wiki; related to [[decision-making-frameworks]] and [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]].
- **Nine Intellectual Standards** — Clarity, Accuracy, Precision, Relevance, Depth, Breadth, Logic, Significance, Fairness. Quality criteria for evaluating reasoning. Central. Not yet in wiki.
- **Critical Thinking (as a discipline)** — Systematic evaluation of reasoning quality. Central. Related to existing pages on decision-making and cognitive biases.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- The framework provides a comprehensive, teachable method for evaluating the quality of any reasoning process by mapping it against 8 structural elements and 9 quality standards.
- Evidence is largely pedagogical and theoretical — grounded in Paul and Elder's published work rather than empirical studies. The framework is widely used in education but its effectiveness depends on practice and metacognitive discipline.
- Strengths: comprehensive, domain-flexible, encourages self-reflection. Weaknesses: time-intensive, requires practice, potentially rigid, and standards like "fairness" are context-dependent.
