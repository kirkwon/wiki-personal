# Essentialism: The Disciplined Pursuit of Less — Source Summary

Greg McKeown's *Essentialism* presents a philosophy and practical framework for identifying and pursuing only what is truly important, rejecting non-essential commitments in favor of a more focused, fulfilling life. The book argues that reducing commitments leads to higher-quality experiences and greater fulfillment.

Core concepts include the idea that every "yes" to something is a "no" to something else, making opportunity cost central to essentialist thinking. Chapter 4 identifies societal pressure and personal insecurities as key obstacles to essentialist living. Chapter 5 introduces a methodology for prioritizing commitments based on alignment with values. Chapter 6 deepens the opportunity cost lens, emphasizing deliberate choice about time and resources.

The Essentialist Toolkit (Chapter 7) provides practical tools: habit tracking, time blocking, environment design, the 2-minute rule, Eisenhower Matrix, focus enhancement techniques including Deep Work and Flow states, goal setting, distraction elimination, and systematic review routines. The book also explicitly flags when to stop optimizing — when productivity systems create more work than they save, when relationships or health are sacrificed, or when the pursuit of productivity destroys joy.
