# Music Theory Fundamentals

## Overview

Music theory is the study of the practices and possibilities of music. It provides a vocabulary for understanding how music works and helps musicians communicate ideas, analyze compositions, and create new music. This source provides a comprehensive beginner-level introduction covering:

- Pitch (frequency, notes, intervals)
- Duration (rhythm, tempo, meter)
- Dynamics (pp to ff)
- Timbre (tone color)
- The musical alphabet
- Intervals
- Staff notation
- Key signatures
- Time signatures
- Note values
- Chords (triads and seventh chords)
- Practice routines
- Common mistakes

## Musical Sound

### Pitch
- **Frequency**: How high or low a sound is (measured in Hz)
- **Note**: A specific pitch with a name (A, B, C, etc.)
- **Interval**: Distance between two pitches

### Duration
- **Rhythm**: Pattern of sounds and silences in time
- **Tempo**: Speed of the music (beats per minute)
- **Meter**: Grouping of beats (2/4, 3/4, 4/4, etc.)

### Dynamics
- Volume of the sound (pp, p, mp, mf, f, ff)
- **pp**: pianissimo (very soft)
- **p**: piano (soft)
- **mp**: mezzo-piano (medium-soft)
- **mf**: mezzo-forte (medium-loud)
- **f**: forte (loud)
- **ff**: fortissimo (very loud)

### Timbre
- Quality or "color" of a sound
- What distinguishes instruments from each other

## The Musical Alphabet

Western music uses 12 pitches, arranged as:

A - A#/Bb - B - C - C#/Db - D - D#/Eb - E - F - F#/Gb - G - G#/Ab - A

- **Natural notes**: A, B, C, D, E, F, G (7 notes)
- **Sharps (♯)**: Raise pitch by one half-step
- **Flats (♭)**: Lower pitch by one half-step
- **Enharmonic**: Same pitch, different name (A# = Bb)

## Intervals

An interval is the distance between two pitches.

### Perfect Intervals
- **Unison**: Same note (0 half-steps)
- **Perfect 4th**: 5 half-steps (F to Bb)
- **Perfect 5th**: 7 half-steps (C to G)
- **Octave**: 12 half-steps (C to next C)

### Major Intervals
- **Major 2nd**: 2 half-steps (C to D)
- **Major 3rd**: 4 half-steps (C to E)
- **Major 6th**: 9 half-steps (C to A)
- **Major 7th**: 11 half-steps (C to B)

### Minor Intervals
- **Minor 2nd**: 1 half-step (C to Db)
- **Minor 3rd**: 3 half-steps (C to Eb)
- **Minor 6th**: 8 half-steps (C to Ab)
- **Minor 7th**: 10 half-steps (C to Bb)

## The Staff

The staff is the set of 5 horizontal lines where music is written. Clefs include the Treble Clef (G Clef) for higher pitches, Bass Clef (F Clef) for lower pitches, and the Grand Staff combining both for piano.

## Key Signatures

A key signature tells you which sharps or flats to play throughout the piece. The Circle of Fifths is the organizing principle for key signatures.

## Time Signatures

Time signatures tell you how many beats per measure and what note value gets one beat (4/4, 3/4, 2/4, 6/8, etc.).

## Note Values and Rests

Note values range from whole notes (4 beats) to sixteenth notes (1/4 beat). Each note value has a corresponding rest symbol indicating silence.

## Chords

A chord is three or more notes played together.

### Triads
- **Major Triad**: Root - Major 3rd - Perfect 5th (C-E-G)
- **Minor Triad**: Root - Minor 3rd - Perfect 5th (C-Eb-G)
- **Diminished Triad**: Root - Minor 3rd - Diminished 5th (C-Eb-Gb)
- **Augmented Triad**: Root - Major 3rd - Augmented 5th (C-E-G♯)

### Seventh Chords
- **Major 7th**: Major triad + Major 7th (C-E-G-B)
- **Dominant 7th**: Major triad + Minor 7th (C-E-G-Bb)
- **Minor 7th**: Minor triad + Minor 7th (C-Eb-G-Bb)
- **Minor Major 7th**: Minor triad + Major 7th (C-Eb-G-B)
- **Diminished 7th**: Diminished triad + Diminished 7th (C-Eb-Gb-Bbb)
- **Half-Diminished 7th**: Diminished triad + Minor 7th (C-Eb-Gb-Bb)

## Practice Tips

### Daily Practice Routine (30-60 minutes)
1. **Warm-up (5-10 min)**: Scales, arpeggios
2. **Technique (10-15 min)**: Exercises, fingerings
3. **Theory Application (10-15 min)**: Chord progressions, ear training
4. **Repertoire (15-20 min)**: Learning songs, improvising

### Key Practice Principles
- **Consistency over intensity**: 30 min every day > 2 hours once a week
- **Slow practice**: Practice slowly enough to play without mistakes
- **Focus on weaknesses**: Spend 70% of time on what you can't do yet
- **Use a metronome**: Develop steady rhythm

### Common Mistakes
- Skipping fundamentals
- Playing too fast
- Not listening
- Only playing what you know
- Ignoring theory
