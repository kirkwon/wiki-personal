## Key Concepts
- **Deep Work** — Sustained, distraction-free focus on cognitively demanding tasks. Central to the entire source.
- **The Age of Distraction** — How modern technology erodes capacity for focus. Provides the problem framing.
- **Expertise Economy** — Deep specialization commands premium value in the job market. Connects to professional development themes.
- **The Deep Worker Ethos** — Discipline-driven mindset prioritizing high-quality output over busywork. Not in wiki.
- **Overcoming the Noise** — Strategies for combating digital distractions (selective attention, productivity tools). Related to existing [[time-management-matrix]] and [[quadrant-2-focus]].
- **Building an Expertise Economy** — Parallel to but distinct from existing knowledge about specialization; emphasis on depth over breadth.

## Main Arguments & Findings
- Modern technology has created an environment of constant distraction that undermines productivity and expertise development.
- Deep, focused work is the primary driver of professional value; shallow, fragmented work does not build the kind of expertise that commands premium outcomes.
- Deep work requires deliberate practice: structured environments, scheduled blocks, and a disciplined ethos.
- Balancing deep work with personal life demands intentional prioritization—similar in spirit to Covey's quadrant framework but with sharper emphasis on cognitive intensity.
- Community and mentorship around deep work principles amplify individual practice.

Evidence is largely prescriptive and anecdotal—based on Newport's observations and case studies rather than controlled research. Moderate strength for framing; weaker for specific actionable methods (the source notes "actionable_methods" is empty).
