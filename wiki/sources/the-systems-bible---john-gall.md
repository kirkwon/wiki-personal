- **The Systems Bible** – 2024 book on systems theory; central source. New.
- **Gall's Law** – Core principle: "A complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked." Not in wiki; new concept.

- **Recognition-Primed Decision Model (RPD)** – Existing wiki page; relevant parallel to Gall's emphasis on how systems behave vs. how we expect them to.

## Key Concepts

1. **Gall's Law** – Complex working systems must evolve from simple working systems. Central organizing principle.
2. **Fundamental Theorem of Systems** – New systems generate new problems. Implication: every intervention creates new failure modes.
3. **Unintended Consequences** – Systems inevitably produce outcomes beyond design intent. Connects to decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality and cognitive biases.
4. **Systems Self-Awareness Absence** – Systems lack awareness of their own behavior. Relevant to illusion-of-control and overconfidence.
5. **Perpetual Maintenance (Systems Are Never Fixed)** – Systems degrade continuously; no permanent fix exists. Connects to technical debt and legacy systems.
6. **Replacement vs. Repair** – Sometimes only complete replacement resolves systemic dysfunction. Relevant to real-options-analysis and strategic-vs-reactive-thinking.
7. **Meta-Systems** – Systems of systems interact unpredictably. Connects to group-decision-polarization and organizational dynamics.
8. **Temporal Dysfunction** – Systems always run late. Connects to planning fallacy and decision-paralysis.
9. **Entropy/Decay** – Systems always get worse over time. Connects to antifragility (Taleb) as a counter-concept.
10. **No Point of No Return** – Systems can be abandoned; sunk cost fallacy is a risk. Connects to endowment-effect and prospect-theory.

## Main Arguments & Findings

- **Core claim**: Systems—whether technical, organizational, or social—behave in counterintuitive, frustrating ways that are nonetheless predictable.
- **Key theorem**: New systems generate new problems (every solution spawns new failure modes).
- **Key law**: Gall's Law—complexity must be built incrementally from working simple systems.
- **Key paradox**: The only way to truly fix a system is to replace it, yet replacement is rarely chosen over incremental repair.
- **Evidence**: Anecdotal and observational—drawn from decades of systems experience. Strength is conceptual/pragmatic rather than empirical.
- **Caveat**: The book warns against over-applying systems thinking to simple problems; it can become an excuse for inaction or buzzword compliance.
