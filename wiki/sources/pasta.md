# Italian Pasta

Pasta is the foundation of Italian cuisine—versatile, affordable, and beloved worldwide. From long strands to short shapes, fresh to dried, each pasta shape has its ideal sauce and regional tradition.

## Overview
This source covers Italian pasta comprehensively — types, shapes, cooking techniques (al dente, fresh vs. dried), classic dishes, sauce pairings, common mistakes, and fresh pasta making.

## Pasta Types
- **Fresh (pasta fresca)**: soft, tender, cooks quickly; for special occasions and stuffed pasta
- **Dried (pasta secca)**: firm, al dente, shelf-stable; for everyday cooking

## Pasta Shapes and Sauce Pairings
Each pasta shape has its ideal sauce for optimal adhesion and mouthfeel. Matching shape to sauce is a key technique.

## Classic Dishes
- [[carbonara]]: Guanciale, eggs, Pecorino Romano, black pepper—no cream
- [[cacio-e-pepe]]: Pecorino Romano, black pepper, pasta water emulsion
- [[amatriciana]]: Guanciale, tomato, onion, Pecorino Romano
- [[ragu-alla-bolognese]]: Ground beef and pork, tomato, onion, celery, carrot, wine, milk, slow-cooked 3-4 hours
- [[pesto-alla-genovese]]: Basil, pine nuts, Pecorino, Parmesan, garlic, olive oil
- [[pasta-alla-norma]]
- [[spaghetti-alle-vongole]]

## Key Techniques
- [[al-dente]]: Firm doneness standard; cook 1-2 minutes less than package time
- [[fresh-pasta-making]]: Flour, eggs, kneading, resting, rolling process
- Starch water as emulsifier
- Matching pasta shape to sauce for optimal adhesion and mouthfeel

## Common Mistakes
- Overcooking pasta
- Not salting water sufficiently (under-salting)
- Rinsing pasta (removes starch)
- Wrong sauce for shape
- Not tossing pasta in sauce

## Where to Learn More
Bologna for fresh pasta, Naples for dried pasta, Genoa for pesto.
