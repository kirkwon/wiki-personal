# Super Thinking - Gabriel Weinberg

## Overview
Super Thinking (2024) by Gabriel Weinberg is a beginner-level book that presents mental models as a toolkit for better decision-making, problem-solving, and learning. The book's core thesis is that combining multiple mental models yields "super thinking" — deeper insight and better outcomes than relying on any single framework.

## Key Chapters and Concepts

### Part I: Core Concepts
- **The Map Is Not the Territory** — Our understanding of reality is always incomplete; models are simplifications.
- **Occam's Razor** — Simpler explanations are usually better.
- **First-Order Thinking** — Thinking only about immediate consequences.
- **Second-Order Thinking** — Thinking about the consequences of consequences.
- **Pareto Principle** — The 80/20 rule and its applications.

### Part II: Decision Making
- **Bayes' Rule** — Updating beliefs based on new evidence.
- **Base Rate Fallacy** — Ignoring general information in favor of specific information.
- **Sunk Cost Fallacy** — Continuing an endeavor because of previously invested resources.
- **Opportunity Cost** — The cost of the next best alternative foregone.
- **Margin of Safety** — Building in buffers for uncertainty and error.

### Part III: Strategic Thinking
- **Game Theory** — Strategic decision-making where outcomes depend on others' choices.
- **Prisoner's Dilemma** — Cooperation vs. competition dynamics.
- **Tragedy of the Commons** — Individual rationality leading to collective irrationality.
- **Competitive Advantage** — Factors that give one entity an advantage.
- **Network Effects** — Products/services becoming more valuable as more people use them.

### Part IV: Systems Thinking
- **Feedback Loops** — Outputs becoming inputs, creating reinforcing or balancing effects.
- **Law of Unintended Consequences** — Actions having unforeseen effects.
- **Systems Are Dynamic** — Systems change over time and adapt.
- **Emergent Properties** — Properties arising from component interactions.
- **Equilibrium** — The state of balance in dynamic systems.

### Part V: Creativity and Innovation
- **Lateral Thinking** — Indirect, creative problem-solving approaches.
- **Mental Sandbox** — Simulating scenarios mentally to explore possibilities.
- **Inversion** — Thinking about problems backward or from opposite perspectives.
- **First Principles** — Breaking problems down to fundamental truths.
- **Thought Experiments** — Using hypothetical scenarios to test ideas.

### Part VI: Social and Emotional Intelligence
- **Reciprocity** — The tendency to return favors and build social bonds.
- **Authority Bias** — Excessive deference to authority figures.
- **Social Proof** — Looking to others to determine appropriate behavior.
- **In-Group/Out-Group Bias** — Favoring those similar to us.
- **Empathy** — Understanding and sharing others' feelings.

### Part VII: Learning and Growth
- **Growth Mindset** — Abilities can be developed through effort.
- **Feynman Technique** — Learning by teaching and simplifying concepts.
- **Spaced Repetition** — Reviewing information at increasing intervals.
- **Deliberate Practice** — Focused, goal-oriented practice.
- **Dunning-Kruger Effect** — Overestimating ability when incompetent, underestimating when competent.

## Key Insight
The power of the book lies not in any single model but in combining multiple models for deeper insight. The "when to stop" section adds important guardrails against analysis paralysis and model imperialism.
