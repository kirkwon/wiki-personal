# Options Trading Guide

This comprehensive guide provides a framework for understanding and implementing options trading strategies within a personal finance context. Options trading can be used for income generation, risk management, portfolio enhancement, and strategic wealth building. It covers LEAPS, straddles, butterflies, time decay (theta) management, ITM vs OTM strategies, and personal finance applications including retirement planning, education funding, and estate planning.

## Key Topics

- **LEAPS**: Long-dated options up to 3 years with reduced theta decay for strategic positioning, suitable for retirement, education funding, and estate planning.
- **Straddle Strategies**: Long straddles for volatility capture, short straddles for income generation, earnings plays, and M&A events.
- **Butterfly Spreads**: Multi-leg positions with limited risk/reward and theta advantages.
- **Theta Management**: Time decay dynamics, acceleration patterns, mitigation strategies; central to strategy selection and management.
- **ITM vs OTM Options**: ITM options are less theta-sensitive but more expensive; OTM options offer leverage but higher theta risk and demand larger price moves.
- **Personal Finance Applications**: Covered calls for income, cash-secured puts, protective puts for insurance, collars, credit spreads, iron condors, LEAPS for growth.
- **Position Sizing**: Theta-aware allocation (1-5% for long options, 5-15% for short options, 65% cash reserves).
- **Retirement, Education, and Estate Planning**: LEAPS for growth, covered calls for income, IRA/401(k) restrictions, estate planning integration.
- **Risk Management**: Greeks monitoring, stop-loss orders, portfolio theta exposure analysis, diversification, exit planning.

## Key Takeaways

- LEAPS minimize theta decay and enable long-term strategic positioning across retirement, education, and estate planning horizons.
- Straddles are powerful for volatility plays and income generation but require quick movement or careful theta management.
- Butterflies offer defined risk with theta advantages for range-bound strategies.
- Time decay (theta) is critical for all option strategies; ITM options reduce theta sensitivity at higher cost, while OTM options offer leverage but face faster time decay.
- Strategic alignment with market conditions, risk tolerance, and portfolio integration with overall financial planning is essential.
- Risk management is non-negotiable—start conservative and scale complexity with experience, using stop-losses, Greeks monitoring, and diversification.
