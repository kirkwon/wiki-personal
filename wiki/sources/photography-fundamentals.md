# Photography Fundamentals

## Overview

Photography is the art and science of capturing light to create images. Understanding the fundamentals — exposure, composition, and camera settings — is essential for creating compelling photographs.

## Exposure Triangle

The exposure triangle consists of three elements that control light: aperture, shutter speed, and ISO.

### Aperture

Aperture is the opening in the lens that controls how much light enters. Measured in f-stops (f/1.4, f/2, f/2.8, f/4, f/5.6, f/8, f/11, f/16, f/22):
- **Lower f-number (f/1.4)**: Larger opening, MORE light, SHALLOW depth of field (blurry background)
- **Higher f-number (f/22)**: Smaller opening, LESS light, DEEP depth of field (everything in focus)

Common uses:
- f/1.4 - f/2.8: Portraits, low light — blurry background (bokeh), maximum light
- f/4 - f/5.6: General photography — good balance of light and depth
- f/8 - f/11: Landscapes — deep depth of field, everything sharp
- f/16 - f/22: Bright scenes, maximum depth — deep depth, everything in focus

### Shutter Speed

Shutter speed is how long the camera's shutter stays open. Measured in seconds or fractions of a second (1/1000, 1/500, 1/250, 1/60, 1/30, 1/15, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1"):
- **Faster shutter (1/1000s)**: FREEZES motion, less light
- **Slower shutter (1/15s)**: MOTION BLUR, more light

Common uses:
- 1/1000 - 1/2000: Sports, wildlife — freezes fast motion
- 1/500 - 1/1000: Street, action — freezes most motion
- 1/125 - 1/250: General photography — good balance
- 1/60: General, handheld — minimum for handheld without shake
- 1/15 - 1/30: Low light, intentional blur — motion blur, need stabilization
- 1" - 30": Night, light painting — long exposures

### ISO

ISO is camera sensor sensitivity to light. Measured in numbers (100, 200, 400, 800, 1600, 3200, 6400, 12800, 25600):
- **Lower ISO (100)**: LESS sensitive, CLEAN images, needs more light
- **Higher ISO (6400)**: MORE sensitive, NOISY images, needs less light

Common uses:
- 100 - 200: Bright daylight, tripod — cleanest images
- 400 - 800: Overcast, indoor — good balance
- 1600 - 3200: Low light, indoor — acceptable noise
- 6400 - 12800: Very low light — visible noise
- 25600+: Extreme low light — heavy noise

### The Relationship

Proper Exposure = Aperture + Shutter Speed + ISO. If you change one, you must adjust another accordingly.

## Camera Modes

- **Automatic (Auto)**: Camera makes all decisions. Use for beginners, quick shots.
- **Program Mode (P)**: Camera selects aperture and shutter. Quick shooting, some control.
- **Aperture Priority (Av/A)**: You choose aperture, camera chooses shutter speed. Most used mode for portraits, landscapes, depth of field control.
- **Shutter Priority (Tv/S)**: You choose shutter speed, camera chooses aperture. For sports, action, intentional motion blur.
- **Manual Mode (M)**: You choose everything. Complete creative control, difficult lighting.

## Focus Modes

- **AF-S (Single Shot)**: One-time focus, good for stationary subjects
- **AF-C (Continuous)**: Continually adjusts, good for moving subjects
- **AF-A (Automatic)**: Camera chooses between single and continuous
- **Manual Focus (MF)**: For macro, low light, creative effects

## Metering

- **Evaluative/Matrix**: Entire scene, analyzes brightness distribution — most situations
- **Center-weighted**: Center of frame — portraits, backlit subjects
- **Spot**: Small area (usually center) — precise metering, high contrast

## Common Settings by Genre

### Portrait Photography
- Aperture: f/2.8 - f/4
- Shutter: 1/125 - 1/250
- ISO: 100 - 800
- Focus: AF-S
- Metering: Center-weighted or spot

### Landscape Photography
- Aperture: f/8 - f/16
- Shutter: 1/60 - 1/125 (or tripod, slower)
- ISO: 100 - 200
- Focus: Manual or AF-S
- Metering: Evaluative

### Street Photography
- Aperture: f/8 - f/11
- Shutter: 1/250 - 1/500
- ISO: Auto (up to 3200)
- Focus: Zone focus or AF-C
- Metering: Evaluative

### Low Light/Night Photography
- Aperture: f/2.8 - f/4
- Shutter: 1/60 - 30"
- ISO: 800 - 6400
- Focus: Manual
- Metering: Spot

## Composition Basics

- **Rule of Thirds**: Divide frame into 3x3 grid, place subject at intersections
- **Leading Lines**: Use lines to guide viewer's eye
- **Framing**: Use elements to frame your subject
- **Symmetry and Patterns**: Create balance and interest
- **Backgrounds**: Keep backgrounds simple and uncluttered

## Post-Processing Fundamentals

- Cropping: Improve composition
- Exposure adjustment: Brighten or darken
- Contrast: Enhance tonal range
- White balance: Correct color cast
- Clarity/Dehaze: Enhance details

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Camera shake | Use faster shutter or tripod |
| Blurry subject | Use faster shutter speed |
| Overexposed images | Lower ISO, faster shutter, smaller aperture |
| Underexposed images | Higher ISO, slower shutter, larger aperture |
| Noisy images | Use lower ISO, more light |
| Wrong focus mode | Choose AF-S for still subjects, AF-C for moving |
| Cluttered backgrounds | Move to cleaner background or use wider aperture |

## Equipment

### Camera Types
- **DSLR**: Good battery life, optical viewfinder, many lenses. Heavy, complex. Best for serious photographers.
- **Mirrorless**: Compact, electronic viewfinder, fast autofocus. Battery life, expensive. Best for most photographers.
- **Compact**: Portable, simple. Limited control. Best for beginners.
- **Phone**: Always with you, convenient. Limited optics, sensor size. Best for casual photography.

### Lens Types
- **Wide (14-24mm)**: Very wide field of view — landscapes, architecture
- **Standard (24-35mm)**: Normal view — street, travel
- **Normal (50mm)**: Similar to human eye — portraits, general
- **Telephoto (70-200mm)**: Narrow field — sports, wildlife, portraits
- **Super Telephoto (300mm+)**: Very narrow — wildlife, sports

### Essential Accessories
- Tripod, memory cards, extra batteries, cleaning kit, camera bag
