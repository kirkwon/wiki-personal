# Scatterfocus

Scatterfocus is presented as a systematic productivity skill for improving efficiency and output. It involves auditing current workflows, applying restructuring frameworks, iterating based on results, and scaling effective practices. The method is framed as a repeatable process: identify efficiency losses, restructure approaches around high-impact activities, measure impact of changes, and standardize what works.

The source connects scatterfocus to Chris Bailey's Hyperfocus concept, suggesting two complementary attention modes—deep concentration versus diffuse, systematic workflow optimization. Related skills mentioned include error-correction mechanisms, peer accountability systems, and a four-step problem-solving framework (similar to [[polya-four-steps]]). Applications span personal productivity, team coordination, and skill acquisition.

Confidence is noted as 0.7, with acknowledgment that the skill is primarily discussed in a single source.
