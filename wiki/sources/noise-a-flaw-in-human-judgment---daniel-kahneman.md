## Key Concepts

- **Noise vs. Bias** — Noise is variability in judgments; bias is systematic deviation. Distinction is central to the book and absent from current wiki content.
- **Measuring Noise** — Statistical methods for assessing judgment inconsistency across fields. New concept.
- **Cost of Variability** — How excessive judgment variability leads to suboptimal outcomes. Complements existing [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] but focuses on inter-rater variability rather than process-outcome separation.
- **Organizational Culture for Reducing Noise** — Collaborative decision-making, shared frameworks, minimizing competitive behavior. Connects to [[building-a-decision-culture]] and [[radical-transparency]].
- **Uncovering Noise in Judgments** — Controlled comparison of expert opinions to reveal inconsistency. New methodology.

## Main Arguments & Findings

- **Core claim**: Human judgment is not only biased but noisy — judgments vary widely across individuals and even across time for the same individual, and this variability is a significant and underappreciated source of error.
- **Distinction from bias**: Bias is a systematic error; noise is random (or semi-random) inconsistency. Both degrade decision quality, but they require different remedies.
- **Evidence**: Demonstrated through case studies in medicine (misdiagnosis, treatment variation), finance (market volatility from inconsistent investor judgments), and criminal justice (inconsistent sentencing). The evidence is primarily illustrative and conceptual rather than heavily empirical in this summary; the book itself draws on experimental and field data.
- **Solutions**: Structured decision processes, decision hygiene, reference class forecasting, organizational culture that encourages calibration and dialogue, and reducing unnecessary discretion.
