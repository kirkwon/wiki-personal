# Pour Over Coffee

## Quick Reference

**Field**: Beverages/Coffee
**Type**: Preparation Method
**Category**: Manual Drip Brewing
**Skill Level**: Intermediate
**Time Required**: 3-5 minutes
**Best For**: Single-origin specialty coffee, highlighting flavor complexity
**Key Variables**: Pour technique, water temperature, grind size

## Summary

Pour over coffee is a manual brewing method that involves pouring hot water over coffee grounds in a filter, allowing gravity to extract the coffee. This method provides exceptional control over extraction, making it ideal for highlighting the unique flavors of specialty coffee beans. The manual process emphasizes technique and attention to detail.

## Method Overview

### Core Principles
- **Controlled Extraction**: Manual pouring allows precise timing
- **Gravity Filtration**: Water passes through coffee and filter
- **Fresh Grounds**: Grind immediately before brewing
- **Water Quality**: Temperature and purity critical
- **Technique Focus**: Pour method affects extraction

### Equipment Required
**Essential**:
- Coffee Brewer: V60, Chemex, Kalita Wave, etc.
- Paper Filter: Compatible with brewer type
- Gooseneck Kettle: For controlled pouring
- Burr Grinder: Consistent grind size
- Scale: For precise measurements
- Timer: For timing control

**Optional**:
- Dripper Stand: Better pouring angle
- Digital Thermometer: Temperature control
- Server: Thermal carafe for serving
- Brush: For cleaning equipment

## Core Process

### 1. Preparation
- Measure coffee (e.g., 20g for 300g water)
- Standard ratio: 1:15 to 1:17 (coffee to water)
- Rinse filter with hot water
- Place coffee in dripper
- Pre-wet brewer if necessary

### 2. Heating Water
- Temperature: 195-205°F (90-96°C)
- Optimal: 200°F (93°C) for most coffees
- Lighter roasts: 195°F (90°C)
- Darker roasts: 205°F (96°C)

### 3. Grinding
- Grind Size: Medium-fine (table salt texture)
- Adjustment: Wetter coffee = coarser, drier = finer
- Equipment: Burr grinder recommended
- Timing: Grind immediately before brewing

### 4. Bloom Phase
- Add double coffee weight in water (e.g., 20g coffee → 40g water)
- Wait: 30-45 seconds for CO2 release
- Purpose: Release CO2, prepare grounds for extraction
- Should form even coffee bed, rise and settle, smell fragrant

### 5. Main Pour Phase
- Circular motion, keep water level consistent
- Pour in 2-3 stages for optimal extraction
- Total pour time: 2:00-2:30 minutes
- Maintain water level above coffee bed

### 6. Completion
- Wait for all water to drain through
- Should stop within 30 seconds of final pour
- Check for channeling (uneven extraction)
- Remove filter and coffee grounds, transfer to pre-warmed cup
- Serve immediately

## Brewer-Specific Techniques

### Hario V60
- Conical shape, single large hole, fast draining
- Highlights acidity and clarity
- Slow, controlled circular pours; bloom stage crucial
- Total time: 2:30-3:30 minutes

### Chemex
- Hourglass shape, thick glass, bonded paper filters
- Cleaner, smoother cup, slower extraction
- More aggressive pours; can use all water in one pour
- Total time: 4-5 minutes

### Kalita Wave
- Flat bottom, three small holes
- More forgiving, consistent, better for beginners
- Emphasizes body and sweetness
- Total time: 3-5 minutes

## Extraction Science

### Key Variables
- **Grind Size**: Affects surface area and extraction speed
- **Water Temperature**: Higher temp = faster extraction
- **Pour Rate**: Faster = more extraction, slower = less
- **Bloom Time**: Longer bloom = more CO2 release
- **Total Time**: Controls overall extraction percentage

### Extraction Target
- **Ideal Range**: 18-22% extraction
- **Under-extraction**: Sour, weak, missing complexity
- **Over-extraction**: Bitter, harsh, astringent
- **Measurement**: Requires refractometer for precision

### Flavor Effects
- Lighter pours: More acidity, cleaner cup, less body, more nuanced
- Heavier pours: More body, sweeter overall, less complexity, more balanced
- Variable pours: Complexity and balance, multiple flavor notes

## Common Issues & Solutions

### Channeling
- Problem: Water finds paths, uneven extraction
- Solutions: Proper grind size, adequate bloom, circular motion, even saturation

### Over-extraction
- Problem: Too much bitterness, harsh finish
- Solutions: Coarser grind, shorter total time, lower temperature, less aggressive pouring

### Under-extraction
- Problem: Sour, weak flavor
- Solutions: Finer grind, longer brew time, higher temperature, more thorough pouring

## Quality Factors

### Coffee Bean Selection
- Freshly roasted single-origin specialty coffee (light to medium roast, within 2-3 weeks of roasting)
- Single-origin works well for highlighting unique flavors

### Water Quality
- Filtered or spring water, 195-205°F, neutral to slightly alkaline pH
- Slight mineral content beneficial
- Temperature: 195-205°F (90-96°C)

## Brewing Ratios

### Starting Ratios
- 1:15 coffee to water (balanced, good for most coffees)
- 1:13 (more concentrated, better for milk drinks)
- 1:17 (more delicate, highlights acidity)

### Scaling
- Single Serve: 15g coffee → 240g water (8oz cup)
- Small Batch: 30g coffee → 480g water
- Large Batch: 60g coffee → 960g water

## Advanced Techniques
- Variable Ratio Pouring: First pour 50% quickly, pause 30 seconds, second pour remaining slowly
- Multi-stage Brewing: Bloom, first stage 60%, wait 30 seconds, second stage remaining 40%
- Temperature Adjustment: Light roasts 195°F, medium 200°F, dark 205°F
