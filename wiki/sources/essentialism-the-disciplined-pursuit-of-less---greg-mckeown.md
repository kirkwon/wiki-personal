# Essentialism: The Disciplined Pursuit of Less - Greg McKeown

## Author
[[greg-mckeown]] - Author, speaker, and thought leader on essentialism and personal effectiveness.

## Core Thesis
The most effective approach to navigating complexity is to identify the essential few and eliminate the rest, rather than trying to do everything. This requires making explicit trade-offs and being willing to say no to the non-essential. Traditional productivity culture leads to overcommitment and diluted quality of life; essentialism offers a better path by ruthlessly prioritizing.

## Key Concepts

- **Essentialism (framework)** – The disciplined pursuit of less; identifying and committing only to what truly matters. Core organizing concept of the book. Distinguishing the vital few from the trivial many.
- **Opportunity cost** – The cost of saying yes to one thing is saying no to everything else. Reinforces existing wiki concepts like [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] and [[quadrant-2-focus]].
- **Selective commitment** – Prioritizing based on alignment with values rather than defaulting to productivity. Connects to [[decision-making-frameworks]] and [[systematic-decision-making]].
- **Overcoming excess / societal pressure** – Identifying obstacles (social expectations, personal insecurities) that prevent essentialist living. Related to [[choice-overload]] and [[paradox-of-choice]].
- **The discipline of elimination** – Trade-offs and choice as central to essentialism.
- **Essentialism as a universal mental model** – For navigating complex situations.
