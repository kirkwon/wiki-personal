# Marinades and Mise en Place

Source document covering marinade techniques, mise en place workflow, and flavor-building practices in Asian and Western cooking.

## Summary
Marinades are liquid mixtures used to infuse flavor, tenderize, retain moisture, and prepare ingredient surfaces before cooking. They consist of five core components: acid, oil, seasoning, aromatics, and optionally cornstarch for Chinese velveting. Mise en place is the practice of preparing all ingredients before cooking begins, essential for fast-paced wok cooking where speed and heat retention are critical.

## Marinades

### Purpose
- **Flavor infusion** - Get seasonings inside the food
- **Tenderizing** - Enzymes break down proteins (meat)
- **Moisture retention** - Prevents drying during high heat
- **Surface preparation** - Creates slight crust when cooked

### Components
1. **Acid** - Rice vinegar, black vinegar (Chinese); mirin, sake (Japanese); lemon juice, wine (Western). Tenderizes, adds brightness, balances fat.
2. **Oil** - Neutral oils (vegetable, canola, grapeseed); Asian oils (toasted sesame, peanut). Coats, retains moisture, carries flavor.
3. **Seasoning** - Salty (soy sauce, fish sauce, salt); umami (oyster sauce, hoisin, fermented bean paste); sweet (sugar, honey, mirin). Base flavor profile.
4. **Aromatics** - Fresh (ginger, garlic, scallion); dried (five-spice, star anise, cinnamon). Complex flavor, aromatic depth.
5. **Cornstarch (Chinese Technique)** - Dissolved in marinade, coats protein. Creates velveting effect for silky, tender texture.

### Chinese Marinade Template
**Basic Marinade (for 1 lb protein)**:
- Soy sauce: 2 tbsp
- Shaoxing wine (or dry sherry): 1 tbsp
- Cornstarch: 1 tsp
- Sesame oil: 1 tsp
- White pepper: 1/4 tsp
- Ginger (grated): 1 tsp
- Garlic (minced): 1 clove

**Process**:
1. Mix marinade in bowl
2. Add protein, toss to coat
3. Marinate 15-30 minutes (don't over-marinate with soy - gets salty)
4. No need to rinse (unless extremely salty)

### Time Guidelines
| Protein | Minimum | Maximum | Notes |
|----------|----------|----------|-------|
| Chicken | 15 min | 2 hours | Longer = mushy |
| Pork | 30 min | 4 hours | Benefits from longer |
| Beef | 1 hour | 12 hours | Needs time for tenderizing |
| Seafood | 5-10 min | 30 min | Don't overcook! |
| Tofu | 15 min | 1 hour | Absorbs quickly |
| Vegetables | 5-10 min | 30 min | Don't over-marinate |

## Mise en Place

### Definition
French culinary term meaning "everything in its place" - preparing all ingredients before cooking begins. Essential for wok cooking where speed is critical.

### Why It Matters
- **For Wok Cooking**: Speed, heat retention, organization, flow.
- **For Cooking Quality**: Consistent results, no burning, better flavor, less stress.

### Mise en Place Checklist
- All proteins marinated (correct time)
- All vegetables washed, dried, cut
- All aromatics prepped (garlic minced, ginger sliced)
- Sauces measured and mixed
- Garnishes ready (scallions, herbs)
- Rice cooked (if serving with rice)
- Wok/skillet preheating

### Professional Workflow
1. **Prep First (30-45 min)**: Cut all vegetables, mince all garlic/ginger, measure all sauces, marinate proteins, cook rice.
2. **Setup (5 min)**: Arrange ingredients in cooking order, heat wok, have serving platter ready.
3. **Cook (5-15 min)**: Execute recipe, everything ready when needed.

### Common Mistakes
- Over-marinating seafood (texture becomes mushy)
- Not coating evenly
- Using too much salt (soy sauce is already salty)
- Marinating too long with acid (meat breaks down unpleasantly)
- Starting without prep (chaos, burned food)
- Forgetting ingredients
- Wrong sizes (inconsistent cooking)
- Not measuring (flavors off)

### Best Practices
- Use bowl large enough to toss food easily
- Cover and refrigerate (bacteria grow at room temp)
- Marinate at room temp only if cooking within 30 min
- Save some sauce - don't marinate and cook in same liquid (salty, burned bits)
- Cornstarch trick - add to marinade for silky texture
- Prep everything before turning on stove
- Small bowls are your friend (prep sauces)
- Clean as you go
- Label bowls if multiple marinades
- Time yourself to see how prep improves flow

## Advanced Techniques
- **Dry Rubs**: No liquid, just spices. Great for roasting, grilling. Example: Five-spice + salt + sugar.
- **Wet Brining**: Salt water bath (hours to days). Juicy, seasoned throughout. Example: Whole turkey, pork shoulder.
- **Combination**: Wet brine first (12-24 hours), dry rub before cooking. Best of both worlds.

## Recipes Reference
- **Quick Marinade (15 min)**: Soy sauce + sesame oil + garlic + ginger. Use: Stir-fry, pan-sear, grilling.
- **Overnight Marinade (8-12 hours)**: Soy + Shaoxing + five-spice + sugar. Use: Roast duck, pork belly, beef.
- **Velveting Marinade**: Soy + cornstarch + egg white (optional). Use: Chinese restaurant-style stir-fry, beef, chicken.
