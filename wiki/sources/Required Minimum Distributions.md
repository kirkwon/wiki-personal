# Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

Comprehensive guide to RMD rules, calculation methods, deadlines, penalties, and strategies for managing required minimum distributions from retirement accounts. Key topics include the RMD calculation formula (Account Balance ÷ Distribution Period), life expectancy tables for Single Life and Joint Life expectancy, account types subject to RMDs, age thresholds (70½ → 73 → 75), Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs), Roth conversion strategies, common mistakes to avoid, planning strategies by age, and integration with overall retirement planning including Social Security, tax optimization, and legacy considerations.

## Core Content

- RMD rules require minimum annual withdrawals from Traditional IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s, 457(b)s, SEP IRAs, and SIMPLE IRAs starting at age 73 (born 1950–1959) or 75 (born 1960+).
- Roth IRAs and Roth 401(k)s are exempt during the original owner's lifetime.
- Penalty for failing to take RMDs is 25% of the shortfall, reduced to 10% if corrected promptly.
- QCDs allow up to $105,000/year (2024) to count toward RMDs while reducing taxable income.
- Stretch IRA rules for non-spousal beneficiaries have been largely eliminated; most must deplete inherited accounts within 10 years.
- Five major strategies: Roth conversions, QCDs, aggressive withdrawal timing, asset location, and inherited IRA planning.
