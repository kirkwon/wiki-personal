# Blue Ocean Strategy - W. Chan Kim & Renee Mauborgne

Summary of the core framework, tools, and principles from Blue Ocean Strategy.

## Core Concept
Blue oceans are uncontested market spaces where competition is irrelevant. Companies can escape destructive competition by creating new market spaces rather than competing within existing industry boundaries (red oceans).

## Key Tools
- **Strategy Canvas** – Visual tool mapping how industries currently compete on key factors, revealing opportunity for divergence.
- **Four Actions Framework** – Eliminate, Reduce, Raise, Create to systematically rebuild the value curve.
- **Six Paths Framework** – Methods for reconstructing market boundaries across alternative industries, strategic groups, buyer chains, complementary offerings, functional/emotional appeal, and time.
- **Value Innovation** – Simultaneous pursuit of differentiation and low cost, breaking the value-cost tradeoff.
- **Non-Customer Analysis** – Three tiers: soon-to-be non-customers, refused non-customers, unexplored non-customers.
- **Strategic Sequence** – Buyer utility → Price → Cost → Adoption to ensure commercial viability.
- **Tipping Point Leadership** – Targeting key barriers (cognitive, resource, motivational, political) at the tipping point.
- **Fair Process** – Engagement, explanation, expectation clarity to build buy-in.

## Key Principles
1. Reconstruct market boundaries
2. Focus on the big picture
3. Reach beyond existing demand
4. Get the strategic sequence right
5. Overcome key organizational hurdles
6. Build execution into strategy

## Evidence
Case studies including Cirque du Soleil, Southwest Airlines, and Yellow Tail wine illustrate the framework.

## When to Use
- Industry is saturated with competition
- Margins are declining due to competition
- Need innovation beyond incremental improvements
- Want to create new markets rather than compete
- Facing commoditization pressures
- Growth is limited in current market

## When to Stop
- When blue ocean becomes red ocean (competition catches up)
- When differentiation becomes gimmick rather than value
- When ignoring customer needs for novelty
- When creating new markets that aren't actually valuable
- When strategic thinking becomes paralysis
- When overestimating ability to create new markets
- When ignoring competitive threats in emerging blue oceans
- When value innovation becomes cost-cutting in disguise
