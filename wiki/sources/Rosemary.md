# Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)

Rosemary is a robust, aromatic herb from the Lamiaceae (mint) family, native to the Mediterranean. It has a distinctive pine-like, woodsy, camphoraceous flavor profile that stands up well to cooking heat. It is essential for roasting meats, grilling, and adding depth to hearty dishes across Mediterranean, French, Italian, and British/American cuisines. Rosemary should be used sparingly due to its strength.

## Flavor Profile

- Primary notes: Pine, woodsy, camphor, subtle mint, balsamic finish
- Varieties:
  - **Tuscan Blue** – classic pine, strong
  - **Arp** – milder, sweeter
  - **Prostratus** – very mild
  - **Majorica Pink** – floral, mild

## Culinary Uses

- Roasts (lamb, chicken, potatoes), grilled meats, breads, focaccia, rosemary bread
- Herbes de Provence blend, compound butters, infused oils and vinegars
- Mediterranean skewers, pizza, roast potatoes, Sunday roast
- Key techniques: stripping leaves, chiffonade, infusing oils, using sprigs in roasts

## Storage & Handling

- **Fresh**: 7–10 days refrigerated, bundle with damp paper towel
- **Dried**: 1–2 weeks hanging, store airtight; 1 tbsp fresh = 1 tsp dried
- **Frozen**: 3–4 months in ice cube trays with oil
- **Infused oils**: 1–2 weeks steeping, refrigerate up to 2 months

## Pairings

- Excellent with: Lamb, chicken, pork, beef, potatoes, carrots, garlic, thyme, lemon, olive oil
- Avoid: Overuse (strong flavor causes soapiness), delicate fish

## Common Mistakes

- Using too much (start small, less is more)
- Using old/dried rosemary with lost aroma
- Cooking whole sprigs without removal before serving
- Overwatering plants (drought-tolerant)

## Health Benefits

Rich in rosmarinic acid (antioxidant), carnosic acid (anti-inflammatory), traditional cognitive aid, digestive stimulant, antimicrobial properties.

## Cultural Significance

Ancient Greece symbol of remembrance and fidelity; used in weddings and funerals in the Middle Ages; Mediterranean culinary staple for thousands of years.
