# Predictably Irrational - Dan Ariely

Behavioral economist Dan Ariely's core claim: human irrationality is systematic and predictable, not random. This enables design of better systems, institutions, and personal decision frameworks.

## Key Concepts

- **Relativity in decision-making** — We value options relative to comparisons, even arbitrary ones. Overlaps with anchoring and framing effects already in wiki via Kahneman and Dobelli.
- **Zero-cost bias** — "Free" has disproportionate psychological pull, causing irrational choices when zero is involved in pricing or offers.
- **Social vs. market norms** — Different decision rules apply depending on whether social or monetary context dominates.
- **Endowment effect** — Ownership inflates perceived value.
- **Expectation/placebo effects** — Price and expectations shape perceived quality.
- **Procrastination as predictable irrationality** — Self-control failures are systematic.
- **Context-dependence of ethics** — Character is situational, not fixed.

## Tools & Techniques

- Decoy effect analysis (adding irrelevant options to influence choice)
- Anchoring techniques (setting initial reference points)
- Default option design (leveraging inertia and status quo bias)
- Social proof mechanisms (using behavior of others to influence)
- Framing effects (presenting identical information differently)
- Commitment devices (pre-committing to counter procrastination)
- Expectation management (shaping how experiences are perceived)
- Endowment effect mitigation (factoring in ownership bias)
- Context analysis (examining environmental influences)
- A/B testing to measure irrational responses

## When to Stop

- When analysis paralysis sets in
- When countermeasures become more costly than the biases themselves
- When overcorrecting and missing opportunities due to excessive caution
- When social norms legitimately should dominate
- When emotional decisions have value
- When immediate action is required
- When market conditions overwhelm rational frameworks
