# Make It Stick - Peter C. Brown, Jeffrey D. Karpicke, and Henry L. Roediger III

## Book Summary

*Make It Stick* by Peter C. Brown, Jeffrey D. Karpicke, and Henry L. Roediger III presents evidence-based strategies for effective learning drawn from cognitive psychology research. The central thesis is that common study habits—re-reading, highlighting, and cramming—are largely ineffective for long-term retention, while strategies like retrieval practice, spaced repetition, and elaborative interrogation produce dramatically better results.

## Key Chapters

### Chapter 1: The Problem of Forgetting
Forgetting is not simply a failure but an integral part of learning. Retrieval difficulty signals that learning is occurring, and the act of struggling to recall information strengthens memory pathways.

### Chapter 2: Memorization Myths Busted
Traditional memorization methods are challenged in favor of techniques grounded in cognitive science research.

### Chapter 3: The Power of Retrieval Practice
Actively recalling information strengthens memory more than passive re-reading. Testing oneself is one of the most effective learning strategies.

### Chapter 4: Spaced Repetition vs Massed Study
Distributing study sessions over time outperforms cramming for long-term retention.

### Chapter 5: Elaborative Interrogation and Concrete Examples
Asking "why" questions about new information and using concrete examples deepen encoding and understanding.

### Chapter 6: The Feynman Technique Explained
Teaching material in simple terms exposes gaps in understanding and deepens mastery of complex topics.

### Chapter 7: Visual Learning - A Double-Edged Sword
Diagrams without explanatory context can be misleading; visual learning needs to be paired with meaningful explanation.

### Chapter 8: The Role of Motivation in Memory
Intrinsic motivation enhances retention and learning outcomes.

### Chapter 9: Practice Makes Perfect... or Does It?
Mere repetition does not lead to mastery; meaningful engagement with material is what produces true learning.

### Chapter 10: Written by Jake Hadley
Personal anecdotes illustrating how these strategies apply in real-life learning scenarios.
