## Key Concepts
- **Systematic Decision-Making Method** (General Decision Framework) – A 7-step model: define the decision → options → criteria → evaluate → uncertainty → reach a decision → follow-up.
- **10-10-10 Rule** – Evaluation of emotions in the short, medium, and long term.
- **Opportunity Cost Framework** – Comparison with the next best alternative.
- **Regret Minimization Framework** – A prospective framework (Bezos example).
- **First Principles Framework** – Breaking down assumptions into fundamental truths.
- **Bayesian Decision Framework** – Calculating probability and expected value.
- **Eisenhower Matrix** – Urgency/importance division.
- **Pros-Cons Framework** – Weighted advantages and disadvantages.
- **Pre-Mortem Analysis** – Predicting failure in advance.
- **Reversibility Test** – Two-way vs. one-way decisions.
- **Bounded Rationality**, **Prospect Theory**, **Behavioral Economics**, **Normative Decision Theory**, **Heuristics**, **Group Decision-Making** – Academic frameworks mentioned in the reference.

Most of these concepts already exist in the wiki (e.g., [[bayesian-thinking]], [[prospect-theory]], [[decision-making-frameworks]], [[pre-mortem-analysis]] – references are already extensive).

## Main Arguments and Results
- Systematic frameworks reduce cognitive biases and clarify trade-offs.
- The 7-step general model acts as a comprehensive foundation.
- Several specific types (10-10-10, regret minimization, pre-mortem, etc.) provide tools for different situations.
- Evidence is based mostly on methodical study and practice; there is little empirical evidence.
- The opportunity cost framework and the reversibility test are central to the internal logic of decision-making.
