Conflict Resolution Guide

Conflict When to Use First When to Use Second Resolution Strategy
Sunk Cost Recognition vs Grit When project has clear path & good match quality When evidence shows poor results or bad fit Use Match Quality as meta-decision framework
Strategic Quitting vs Grit When you're in a kind domain with clear path to mastery When in wicked domain or exploring fit Assess if domain is kind/wicked first
Specialization vs Generalization Kind problems: stable rules, clear feedback (golf, chess) Wicked problems: changing rules, ambiguity (business, strategy) Use Kind/Wicked Assessment first
Focus vs Exploration When deep work needed, clear priority, or executing known strategy When learning, career early stage, or solving novel problems Time-box: explore then focus; use Match Quality to decide
Speed vs Deliberate Practice When learning routine skills or kind-domain problems When developing expertise in complex domains Fast for breadth, slow for depth
Intuition vs Probabilistic Thinking In your circle of competence with extensive experience Outside circle of competence or high-stakes decisions Know your Circle of Competence boundaries
First Principles vs Analogy Novel problems, innovation, or when analogies aren't working Routine problems in stable domains with proven solutions Start with first principles, use analogies for communication

Decision Framework

  1. Assess context: What type of problem? (Kind/Wicked)
  2. Check domain: Is this in my Circle of Competence?
  3. Evaluate match quality: Am I in the right area?
  4. Consider timing: Early exploration vs. late execution?
  5. Apply appropriate model: Use conflict resolution above
