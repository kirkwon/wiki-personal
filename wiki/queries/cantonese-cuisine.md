## Overview

Cantonese cuisine (粤菜, Yuè cài) is known for its mild, fresh flavors and emphasis on ingredients' natural taste. Characterized by delicate cooking methods and fresh ingredients, it's one of China's most influential regional cuisines and the style most familiar to Western palates.

## Flavor Philosophy

### Emphasis on Freshness

**"Ingredients should taste like themselves"**

Cantonese cooking prioritizes:
- **Fresh ingredients** - Quality over complexity
- **Natural flavors** - Enhance, don't mask
- **Seasonal cooking** - What's in season, what's local
- **Mild seasonings** - Let ingredient shine

### Five Cooking Principles

| Principle | Description | Application |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **Fresh** | Use fresh, not preserved | Live fish, seasonal vegetables |
| **Light** | Delicate seasoning | Soy sauce, salt, oyster sauce |
| **Tender** | Quick cooking, preserve texture | Stir-fry, steam |
| **Smooth** | Velvety sauces, textures | Thickened sauces |
| **Aromatic** | Scallions, ginger, garlic | Aromatic base for dishes |

| Type | Use | Popular Dishes |
|------|-----|----------------|
| **Live fish** | Steamed with ginger, scallions | Steamed sea bass |

### Sauces & Condiments

| Sauce | Use | Flavor Profile |
|-------|-----|----------------|
| **Light soy sauce** - Coloring, umami | Light, salty |
| **Dark soy sauce** | Color, depth | Rich, caramel |
| **Oyster sauce** | Umami, sweetness | Seafood, vegetables |
| **Hoisin sauce** | Sweet, thick | Roast duck, char siu |

### Aromatics

- **Ginger** - Mild, aromatic, neutralizes fishiness
- **Scallions/Green onions** - Fresh, aromatic base
- **Garlic** - Aromatic, depth (used more sparingly than in other regions)

## Cooking Techniques

### Steaming (蒸, Zhēng)

**The quintessential Cantonese technique**

**Applications:**
- Steamed fish with ginger and scallions
- Dim sum (har gow, siu mai, char siu bao)
- Steamed vegetables
- Rice cakes

**Advantages:**
- Preserves natural flavors
- Delicate texture
- Healthy cooking method

### Stir-Frying (炒, Chǎo)

**Quick cooking over high heat**

**Characteristics:**
- Maintain crisp texture of vegetables
- Wok hei aroma
- Fast cooking (2-3 minutes)

### Roasting (烤, Kǎo)

**Cantonese roast meats**

**Famous dishes:**
- Cantonese roast duck (烧鸭, Shāo yā)
- Char siu (Chinese BBQ pork, 叉烧, Chā shāo)
- Roast pork belly (siu yuk, 烧肉)
- Roast goose

### Braising (焖, Mèn)

**Slow-cooked, rich flavors**

**Dishes:**
- Beef brisket with radish
- Chicken with ginger scallion
- Pork belly

## Classic Dishes

### Dim Sum (点心, Diǎnxīn)

**See [[Chinese Cuisine/Concepts/dim-sum]] for detailed coverage**

Cantonese brunch tradition featuring:
- Har gow (shrimp dumplings)
- Siu mai (pork dumplings)
- Char siu bao (BBQ pork buns)
- Cheung fun (rice noodle rolls)
- Egg tarts
- Many more varieties

### Steamed Fish (清蒸鱼, Qīngzhēng yú)

**Simple, elegant**

**Preparation:**
- Whole white fish (sea bass, snapper)
- Ginger, scallions, cilantro
---
- Soy sauce, sesame oil

**Key:** Don't overcook - fish should be just done

### Cantonese Roast Duck (烧鸭, Shāo yā)

**See [[Chinese Cuisine/Concepts/roast-duck]]**

- Crispy skin, juicy meat
- Served with plum sauce
- Cantonese roast duck shops are iconic

### Char Siu (叉烧, Chā shāo)

**Chinese BBQ pork**

**Characteristics:**
- Pork shoulder, belly, or loin
- Marinated in hoisin, soy sauce, honey
- Roasted until glazed
- Sweet, savory, red exterior

**Use:**
- As main dish
- In fried rice
- In bao buns (char siu bao)
- In noodle soups

### Black Bean Clams (豉椒蛤蜊, Chǐjiāo gélì)

**Classic Cantonese seafood**

**Flavors:**
- Fermented black beans
- Chilies, garlic
- Soy sauce
- Fresh clams

### Sweet and Sour Pork (咕噜肉, Gūlū ròu)

**Widely known Cantonese dish**

**Components:**
- Bite-sized pork pieces, battered and fried
- Pineapple, bell peppers, onions
- Sweet and sour sauce (vinegar, sugar, ketchup)

### Beef Chow Fun (干炒牛河, Gān chǎo niúhé)

**Wok-tossed rice noodles**

**Characteristics:**
- Wide, flat rice noodles
- Beef, bean sprouts, onions
- Wok hei essential
- Smooth, slightly charred noodles

## Menu Planning

### Traditional Cantonese Meal

**Lunch:**
- Soup (clear or creamy)
- 3-4 dishes (meat, seafood, vegetable)
- Rice
- Dim sum (if weekend brunch)

**Dinner:**
- Soup (usually bone broth)
- 4-6 dishes
- Variety of cooking techniques
- Rice

**Dim Sum Brunch:**
- Multiple dim sum baskets
- Tea (pu'er, jasmine, chrysanthemum)
- Congee (rice porridge)

### Flavor Balance

- **Soup** to start (cleanses palate)
- **Mix textures** (crisp, tender, soft)
- **Balance flavors** (mild, savory, sweet)
- **Vary cooking methods** (steam, stir-fry, braise)

## Eating Traditions

### "Yum Cha" (饮茶, Yǐn chá)

**"Drinking tea" - Dim sum brunch**

**Components:**
- Tea service (pot, cups)
- Multiple dim sum dishes
- Lazy Susan on round table
- Family-style sharing

**Etiquette:**
- Pour tea for others first
- Tap fingers to thank pourer
- Use chopsticks for food

### Soup Culture

**Cantonese soups are legendary**

**Types:**
- Clear soups (steamed fish with vegetables)
- Creamy soups (corn, pumpkin with chicken)
- Herbal soups (ginseng, angelica root)
- Double-boiled soups (slow-cooked in jars)

**Purpose:**
- Nutritional
- Medicinal
- Comfort

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | How to Fix |
|---------|------------|
| Over-seasoning | Start mild, taste, adjust |
| Overcooking seafood | Cook quickly, watch texture |
| Not using fresh ingredients | Quality over quantity |
| Not achieving wok hei | Preheat wok, work quickly |
| Skipping soup | Soup is essential part of meal |

## Practice

1. **Make dim sum** - Start with har gow or siu mai
2. **Steam fish** - Simple, elegant, perfect technique
3. **Try char siu** - Learn marinade and roasting
4. **Host yum cha** - Experience dim sum brunch

**Remember:** Cantonese cuisine is about quality ingredients and letting flavors shine. Less is more.
