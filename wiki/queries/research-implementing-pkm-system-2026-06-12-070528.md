# Research: Implementing PKM System

# Personal Knowledge Management System
A personal knowledge management (PKM) system is a structured approach to collecting, organizing, and retrieving knowledge. It combines methods and tools to ensure that past reading and learning inform current thinking. The goal is to capture, organize, and connect information in a way that makes it easy to retrieve and use.

## PKM Frameworks
Several PKM frameworks exist, including:
1. **PARA**: Projects, Areas, Resources, Archives. Organizes everything around actionability.
2. **Zettelkasten**: The slip-box method. Each note is atomic, densely linked to other notes, and assigned a unique ID.
3. **CODE**: Capture, Organize, Distill, Express. A workflow framework designed to turn raw information into creative output.
4. **GTD**: Getting Things Done. A productivity system that captures everything and processes it with clear next actions.

## PKM Tools
Various tools support PKM, including:
1. **Obsidian**: A local-first markdown editor with powerful bidirectional linking and a graph view.
2. **Notion**: An all-in-one workspace that combines notes, databases, and collaboration.
3. **Logseq**: An outliner with block-level referencing and a daily journaling approach.
4. **Atlas**: An AI-native knowledge workspace that treats notes, web clips, and uploaded documents as a queryable corpus.

## PKM Best Practices
To build an effective PKM system:
1. **Start small**: Begin with a simple structure and add complexity as needed.
2. **Choose the right tool**: Select a tool that fits your workflow and preferences.
3. **Develop a consistent habit**: Regularly capture, organize, and review your knowledge.
4. **Focus on connections**: Link notes to create a network of knowledge.
5. **Review and reflect**: Regularly review your knowledge to identify gaps and areas for improvement.

## PKM and AI
AI can enhance PKM by:
1. **Automating organization**: AI can help organize and connect notes.
2. **Providing insights**: AI can analyze notes and provide insights and recommendations.
3. **Enhancing search**: AI can improve search functionality and help retrieve relevant information.

## Common PKM Mistakes
Common mistakes to avoid:
1. **Over-engineering**: Creating a complex system that is difficult to maintain.
2. **Over-tagging**: Using too many tags, making it difficult to find relevant information.
3. **Capture without distill**: Capturing information without processing or summarizing it.
4. **Public-system bias**: Copying someone else's system without adapting it to your needs.

## PKM for Different Use Cases
PKM can be applied to various use cases, including:
1. **Research**: Organizing and connecting research notes and sources.
2. **Learning**: Creating a knowledge base for learning and personal development.
3. **Creative work**: Using PKM to generate ideas and organize creative projects.
4. **Professional development**: Applying PKM to improve productivity and career advancement.

## Conclusion
A well-designed PKM system can help individuals capture, organize, and connect knowledge, leading to improved productivity, creativity, and decision-making. By choosing the right framework, tool, and habits, anyone can build an effective PKM system that supports their goals and needs.
