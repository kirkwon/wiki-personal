# Oolong Tea (乌龙茶 - Wūlóng Chá)


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[Beverages/Tea]]
### Type**: Tea Category
### Oxidation Level**: 8-80% (partial oxidation)
### Processing**: Complex, multi-stage processing
### Caffeine Level**: Medium (30-50mg per 8oz)
### Color Profile**: Green to brown, amber to reddish liquor
### Best For**: Gongfu brewing, multiple infusions, flavor complexity



## Summary

Oolong tea represents the most complex and varied tea category, occupying the middle ground between green and black tea through partial oxidation. This intricate processing creates teas with incredible diversity, from light, floral oolongs to dark, roasted varieties. The craftsmanship involved in oolong production makes it one of the most prized tea categories, especially from China and Taiwan.

## Processing Method

### Traditional Oolong Processing
```yaml
Complex Multi-Stage Process:
1. **Plucking**: Harvest mature leaves and buds
2. **Withering**: Reduce moisture (4-12 hours)
3. **Shaking/Tossing**: Bruise leaves to start oxidation
4. **Oxidation**: Partial oxidation (8-80% complete)
5. **Fixation**: Heat to stop oxidation (parching)
6. **Rolling**: Shape the leaves (ball or strip)
7. **Drying**: Final moisture removal
8. **Roasting**: Optional final roast for complexity

Key Technique: Precise control of oxidation level
```

### Oxidation Control
```yaml
Light Oolong (8-30% oxidation):
- Process: Minimal bruising, short oxidation
- Temperature: Lower heat application
- Time: Shorter processing time
- Effect: More green tea characteristics

Medium Oolong (30-60% oxidation):
- Process: Moderate bruising, medium oxidation
- Temperature: Balanced heat application
- Time: Standard processing time
- Effect: Balanced green/black characteristics

Dark Oolong (60-80% oxidation):
- Process: Heavy bruising, long oxidation
- Temperature: Higher heat application
- Time: Longer processing time
- Effect: More black tea characteristics
```

## Major Varieties by Origin

### Chinese Oolongs
**Tieguanyin (Iron Goddess of Mercy)**
- **Origin**: Anhui Province, China
- **Processing**: Traditional green oolong processing
- **Flavor**: Floral, orchid, sweet, complex
- **Appearance**: Tightly rolled pellets, green-brown color
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), 20-30 seconds per infusion, multiple infusions

**Da Hong Pao (Big Red Robe)**
- **Origin**: Wuyi Mountains, Fujian Province
- **Processing**: Traditional rock oolong processing
- **Flavor**: Roasted, complex, mineral, long-lasting
- **Appearance**: Dark, twisted leaves, reddish liquor
- **Best Brew**: 205-212°F (96-100°C), longer infusions, developing complexity

**Bai Hao (Oriental Beauty)**
- **Origin**: New Taipei, Taiwan
- **Processing**: Heavy oxidation, insect bite influence
- **Flavor**: Honey, peach, woody, unique
- **Appearance**: Brown tips, distinctive appearance
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), shorter infusions, honey sweetness

**Phoenix Dancong**
- **Origin**: Phoenix Mountain, Guangdong Province
- **Processing**: Single bush varieties, traditional processing
- **Flavor**: Highly aromatic, specific cultivar characteristics
- **Appearance**: Long, twisted strips, various colors
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), multiple short infusions

### Taiwanese Oolongs
**Dong Ding (Frozen Summit)**
- **Origin**: Nantou County, Taiwan
- **Processing**: Medium oxidation, light roast
- **Flavor**: Balanced, floral, nutty, sweet
- **Appearance**: Tightly rolled, green-brown
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), multiple infusions, developing sweetness

**Ali Shan (Ali Mountain)**
- **Origin**: Chiayi County, Taiwan
- **Processing**: High altitude, careful processing
- **Flavor**: Elegant, floral, buttery, complex
- **Appearance: Green-brown, uniform appearance
- **Best Brew**: 190-200°F (87-93°C), gentle infusion, preserve delicate flavors

**Lishan (Pear Mountain)**
- **Origin**: Nantou County, Taiwan
- **Processing**: High elevation (2600m), traditional methods
- **Flavor**: Deep, complex, mineral, long-lasting
- **Appearance**: Dark green, tightly rolled
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), longer infusions, developing complexity

**Jinxuan (Golden Lily)**
- **Origin**: Various regions, Taiwan
- **Processing**: Specific cultivar, milk oolong type
- **Flavor**: Creamy, buttery, floral, sweet
- **Appearance**: Green-yellow, rolled strips
- **Best Brew**: 185-195°F (85-90°C), gentle extraction, creamy texture

## Brewing Recommendations

### Gongfu Style Brewing
```yaml
Essential Equipment:
- Gaiwan or small teapot
- Gongfu tray
- Small cups (3-4 oz)
- Kettle with temperature control
- Tea pitcher (for serving)

Parameters:
- Tea Amount: 4-6g per 100ml water
- Water Temperature: 190-210°F (87-99°C)
- Infusion Time: 15-30 seconds, increasing each time
- Multiple Infusions: 4-8 infusions possible
- Water Amount: Small portions, multiple additions

Process:
1. Rinse tea briefly with hot water
2. First infusion: 15-30 seconds
3. Subsequent infusions: Add 10-15 seconds each time
4. Pour all infusions into pitcher for serving
5. Enjoy the changing flavor profile over infusions
```

### Western Style Brewing
```yaml
Equipment:
- Teapot or mug
- Thermometer
- Timer
- Tea infuser (optional)

Parameters:
- Tea Amount: 1-2 tsp per 8 oz water
- Water Temperature: 195-210°F (90-99°C)
- Steeping Time: 3-5 minutes
- Multiple Infusions: Possible but less effective

Process:
1. Pre-warm vessel
2. Add tea leaves
3. Pour water at appropriate temperature
4. Steep for recommended time
5. Remove leaves or strain
6. Serve immediately
```

### Brewing by Tea Type
```yaml
Light Oolongs (Tieguanyin, Baozhong):
- Temperature: 195-200°F (90-93°C)
- Time: 30-45 seconds per infusion
- Multiple infusions: 6-8 times
- Focus: Preserve delicate floral notes

Medium Oolongs (Dong Ding, Ali Shan):
- Temperature: 200-205°F (93-96°C)
- Time: 30-60 seconds per infusion
- Multiple infusions: 4-6 times
- Focus: Balance and development of complexity

Dark Oolongs (Da Hong Pao, Lishan):
- Temperature: 205-212°F (96-100°C)
- Time: 60-90 seconds per infusion
- Multiple infusions: 3-5 times
- Focus: Extract roasted, complex flavors
```

## Storage & Freshness

### Optimal Storage Conditions
```yaml
Environment:
- Airtight container: Glass or ceramic with tight seal
- Cool temperature: 50-70°F (10-21°C)
- Dark place: Away from light exposure
- Dry conditions: Low humidity, no moisture

Shelf Life:
- Light oolongs: 6-12 months (preserve freshness)
- Medium oolongs: 1-2 years (some improvement with age)
- Dark oolongs: 2-3 years (benefit from aging)
- Vacuum sealed: Extended shelf life possible
```

### Freshness Indicators
```yaml
Visual:
- Color: Should be vibrant, not dull
- Leaves: Intact shape, not broken
- Texture: Flexible, not brittle

Olfactory:
- Aroma: Should be fragrant, characteristic
- Intensity: Should release aroma when handled
- Character: Should match variety description

Taste:
- First infusion: Should show characteristic flavors
- Development: Should evolve over multiple infusions
- Balance: Should not be stale or off
```

## Common Mistakes

### Brewing Errors
```yaml
Water Temperature:
- Too Hot: Destroys delicate flavors, creates bitterness
- Too Cool: Under-extracts, weak flavor
- Solution: Use thermometer, adjust for tea type

Steeping Time:
- Too Long: Extracts bitter compounds, loses balance
- Too Short: Under-extracts, weak flavor
- Solution: Start with recommended time, adjust

Tea Amount:
- Too Much: Over-concentrated, bitter
- Too Little: Weak, under-developed
- Solution: Use proper measuring, adjust to taste
```

### Storage Issues
```yaml
Moisture Exposure:
- Mold growth, flavor contamination
- Premature aging
- Solution: Airtight container, dry environment

Light Exposure:
- UV rays degrade compounds
- Color and aroma loss
- Solution: Dark storage, opaque containers

Temperature Fluctuations:
- Condensation damage
- Quality degradation
- Solution: Consistent, cool storage
```
