# Black Tea (红茶 - Hóng Chá)


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[Beverages/Tea]]
### Type**: Tea Category
### Oxidation Level**: 80-100% (full oxidation)
### Processing**: Complete oxidation of tea leaves
### Caffeine Level**: Medium-High (40-70mg per 8oz)
### Color Profile**: Dark leaves, amber to reddish liquor
### Best For**: Morning tea, milk additions, robust flavor



## Summary

Black tea is the most oxidized tea category, resulting in bold, robust flavors and dark color. This processing creates rich malty, chocolatey, and sometimes smoky notes that make black tea perfect for breakfast blends, milk teas, and iced tea. From Indian Assam to Chinese Keemun, black tea offers a wide range of flavor profiles within its fully oxidized character.

## Processing Method

### Traditional Process
```yaml
Steps:
1. **Plucking**: Harvest two leaves and a bud
2. **Withering**: Reduce moisture content (12-24 hours)
3. **Rolling**: Break cell walls, release enzymes
4. **Oxidation**: Expose to oxygen (1-4 hours)
5. **Firing**: Heat to stop oxidation (20-40 minutes)
6. **Sorting**: Grade by quality, size, and appearance

Key Technique: Oxidation timing and temperature control
```

### Oxidation Process
```yaml
Chemical Changes:
- Enzymes convert catechins to theaflavins and thearubigins
- Polyphenols polymerize into complex compounds
- Chlorophyll breaks down, creating darker color
- Essential oils develop characteristic aroma

Visual Stages:
- Initial: Green to olive color
- Mid-oxidation: Coppery, bronze color
- Complete: Dark brown, reddish tints
```

## Flavor Profile

### Primary Characteristics
- **Malty**: Grain-like, bread-like notes
- **Astringent**: Dry, mouth-drying sensation
- **Full-bodied**: Rich, heavy mouthfeel
- **Complex**: Layered flavors develop over time
- **Versatile**: Accommodates milk, sugar, lemon

### Taste Spectrum
```yaml
Malty & Sweet:
```

## Major Varieties by Origin

### Indian Black Teas
**Assam**
- **Origin**: Assam, Northeast India
- **Processing**: Orthodox or CTC
- **Flavor**: Malty, strong, full-bodied
- **Appearance**: Dark, twisted leaves, coppery liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 3-5 minutes

**Darjeeling**
- **Origin**: Darjeeling, West Bengal
- **Processing**: Orthodox, light oxidation
- **Flavor**: Muscatel, floral, delicate
- **Appearance**: Light, tippy leaves, amber liquor
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), 2-4 minutes

**Nilgiri**
- **Origin**: Nilgiri Hills, South India
- **Processing**: Orthodox processing
- **Flavor**: Smooth, citrusy, mild
- **Appearance**: Uniform black leaves, bright liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 3-4 minutes

### Sri Lankan (Ceylon) Teas
**Dimbula**
- **Origin**: Central Highlands, Sri Lanka
- **Processing**: Orthodox processing
- **Flavor**: Strong, coppery, brisk
- **Appearance**: Black, twisted leaves, bright liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 3-4 minutes

**Nuwara Eliya**
- **Origin**: High elevation, Sri Lanka
- **Processing**: Orthodox, careful processing
- **Flavor**: Delicate, floral, light
- **Appearance**: Thin, dark leaves, pale liquor
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), 2-3 minutes

**Uva**
- **Origin**: Eastern Highlands, Sri Lanka
- **Processing**: Orthodox processing
- **Flavor**: Strong, aromatic, unique character
- **Appearance**: Dark, wiry leaves, coppery liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 3-5 minutes

### Chinese Black Teas
**Keemun**
- **Origin**: Anhui Province, China
- **Processing**: Orthodox, careful oxidation
- **Flavor**: Fruity, wine-like, complex
- **Appearance**: Thin, black leaves, reddish liquor
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), 3-4 minutes

**Lapsang Souchong**
- **Origin**: Fujian Province, China
- **Processing**: Smoked over pine fires
- **Flavor**: Smoky, pine, intense
- **Appearance**: Black, twisted leaves, dark liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 2-4 minutes

**Yunnan Gold**
- **Origin**: Yunnan Province, China
- **Processing**: Orthodox, golden tips
- **Flavor**: Malty, sweet, chocolate
- **Appearance**: Golden-tipped leaves, coppery liquor
- **Best Brew**: 195-205°F (90-96°C), 3-4 minutes

### African Black Teas
**Kenyan**
- **Origin**: Kenya Highlands
- **Processing**: Primarily CTC method
- **Flavor**: Bold, astringent, brisk
- **Appearance**: Small granules, bright liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 2-3 minutes

**Malawi**
- **Origin**: Malawi Highlands
- **Processing**: Orthodox processing
- **Flavor**: Rich, full-bodied, smooth
- **Appearance**: Dark, wiry leaves, bright liquor
- **Best Brew**: 200-212°F (93-100°C), 3-4 minutes

## Brewing Recommendations

### Western Style Brewing
```yaml
Equipment:
- Teapot or mug (ceramic or glass)
- Teaspoon measuring spoon
- Kettle with temperature control
- Timer

Parameters:
- Water Temperature: 200-212°F (93-100°C)
- Tea Amount: 1 tsp per 8 oz water
- Steeping Time: 3-5 minutes
- Multiple Infusions: Limited, 2-3 max

Process:
1. Pre-warm vessel
2. Add tea leaves
3. Pour boiling water
4. Set timer (adjust for strength preference)
5. Remove leaves when time is up
6. Serve with milk/sugar if desired
```

### Traditional Milk Tea Method
```yaml
For Indian/Asian Style:
- Water Temperature: Rolling boil
- Tea Amount: 1-2 tsp per 6 oz water
- Steeping Time: 5-7 minutes (strong)
- Milk: Equal amount to tea
- Sweetener: Sugar or condensed milk

Process:
1. Brew tea very strong
2. Add hot milk (1:1 ratio)
3. Sweeten to taste
4. Serve hot, with spices (optional)
```

### Iced Tea Preparation
```yaml
Cold Brew Method:
- Water Temperature: Room temperature
- Tea Amount: 2x normal amount
- Steeping Time: 8-12 hours refrigerated
- Dilution: Serve over ice, may need less steeped tea

Hot Brew Method:
- Water Temperature: 200-212°F (93-100°C)
- Tea Amount: 1.5x normal amount
- Steeping Time: 3-4 minutes
- Cool: Refrigerate 1-2 hours before serving
- Serve: Over ice with lemon or mint
```

### CTC vs Orthodox Processing
```yaml
CTC (Crush, Tear, Curl):
- Process: Mechanical cutting into small particles
- Flavor: Strong, consistent, quick infusion
- Appearance: Small granules, uniform
- Use: Tea bags, commercial blends, strong tea

Orthodox:
- Process: Traditional rolling and oxidation
- Flavor: Complex, nuanced, varies by batch
- Appearance: Whole leaves, varied sizes
- Use: Specialty tea, loose leaf, connoisseur tea
```

## Storage & Freshness

### Optimal Storage Conditions
```yaml
Environment:
- Airtight container: Metal or ceramic with tight lid
- Cool temperature: 60-70°F (15-21°C)
- Dark place: Away from light exposure
- Dry conditions: Low humidity environment

Shelf Life:
- Loose leaf: 1-2 years (longer than green tea)
- Vacuum sealed: 2-3 years
- Tea bags: 6-12 months
- Proper storage maintains flavor and aroma
```

### Freshness Indicators
```yaml
Visual:
- Color: Deep, consistent color (not faded or dusty)
- Leaves: Intact, not broken beyond normal
- Texture: Flexible, not overly dry

Olfactory:
- Aroma: Strong, characteristic black tea aroma
- Should smell fresh, not musty or stale
- Intensity: Should release fragrance when handled

Taste:
- First infusion: Strong, flavorful, not weak
- Aftertaste: Clean, lingering malty notes
- Complexity: Should have characteristic depth
```

## Common Mistakes

### Brewing Errors
```yaml
Water Temperature:
- Too Cool: Under-extracts, weak flavor
- Solution: Use boiling water, proper kettle

Steeping Time:
- Too Short: Weak, under-developed flavor
- Too Long: Bitter, overly strong
- Solution: Adjust time based on desired strength

Water Quality:
- Hard Water: Minerals interfere with extraction
- Chlorinated Water: Affects flavor
- Solution: Use filtered water, fresh boiled water
```

### Serving Issues
```yaml
Milk Addition:
- Wrong Temperature: Curdling, poor mixing
- Wrong Ratio: Too much milk masks flavor
- Solution: Add hot milk, proper milk-to-tea ratio

Sweetening:
- Too Much Sugar: Overpowers tea flavor
- Wrong Timing: Adding too early affects brewing
- Solution: Add after brewing, adjust to taste

Serving Temperature:
- Too Hot: Burns mouth, affects perception
- Too Cold: Loss of aroma and complexity
- Solution: Allow to cool slightly, maintain warmth
```
