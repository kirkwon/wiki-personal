# Proposition 19 Overview

## Quick Reference

### Proposition**: California Property Tax Transfer Reform
### Effective Date**: February 16, 2021
### Purpose**: Update property tax transfer rules, fund fire prevention
### Key Changes**: Parent-child transfers, disability transfers, base year value adjustments
### Impact**: Estate planning, property transfers, tax calculations

## Overview
Proposition 19, approved by California voters in November 2020 and effective February 16, 2021, significantly modified California's property tax transfer rules under Proposition 13. The changes primarily affect inherited property, transfers between parents and children, and transfers involving severely disabled individuals, while also generating funds for wildfire prevention programs.

## Historical Context

### Proposition 13 (1978)
- **Established**: 1% property tax rate + 2% annual increase cap
- **Protected**: Long-term homeowners from dramatic tax increases
- **Transfer Protection**: Base year value preserved on transfers between parent-child, disability transfers, or inherited property
- **Limitation**: Could lead to significantly different property taxes for similar properties

### Proposition 19 Changes (2021)
- **Revenue Generation**: Estimated $750-1 billion over 10 years for fire prevention
- **Updated Transfer Rules**: Modified parent-child, disability, and inheritance protections
- **Market Value Transfers**: Many transfers now subject to reassessment at current market value
- **Senior Protections**: Enhanced protection for seniors moving within same county

## Before and After Comparison

### Parent-Child Transfers

| Aspect | Before Prop 19 | After Prop 19 |
|---|---|---|
| Full Protection | All parent-child transfers | Primary residence, family farm ≤$1M, disabled child only |
| Reassessment | None | Non-qualified properties reassessed at market value |
| Basis for Capital Gains | Original purchase price inherited | Original purchase price still inherited |
| Tax Impact | $1,000/year on $100K basis | $10,000+/year on $1M market value |

### Inherited Property

| Aspect | Before Prop 19 | After Prop 19 |
|---|---|---|
| Protection | Full for all inherited properties | Primary residence only (child must keep as primary) |
| Age Restriction | None | Under 22 must use as primary residence |
| County Requirement | None | Must transfer within same county |
| Other Properties | Full protection | Generally reassessed at market value |

### Senior Transfers

| Aspect | Before Prop 19 | After Prop 19 |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Proposition 60/90 limited transfers | Expanded to same county, unlimited transfers |
| Eligibility | 55+, disabled, wildfire victims | Same + any property type |
| Value Limit | Varied | Replacement ≤ sale price of original |
| County | Different county allowed | Same county only |

## Key Provisions of Proposition 19

### Full Protection (Qualified Transfers)
- Parent's primary residence to child
- Family farm ≤ $1 million
- Severely disabled child (any property type)
- Senior/disabled/wildfire victim same-county transfers
- Inherited primary residence (child keeps as primary, same county)

### Partial/No Protection (Non-Qualified Transfers)
- Investment properties transferred to children
- Vacation properties
- Agricultural properties > $1 million
- Inherited non-primary residences
- Cross-county transfers for seniors

### 1. Transfer Between Parents and Children

#### Before Proposition 19
- **Full Protection**: Children inherited parent's property tax basis
- **No Reassessment**: Property taxes remained at parent's base year value
- **Unlimited Transfers**: Any number of parent-child transfers preserved basis

#### After Proposition 19
**Parent-to-Child Transfers with Full Protection:**
- **Primary Residence**: Children inheriting parent's primary residence receive full tax basis protection
- **Family Farm**: Agricultural properties of $1 million or less receive full protection
- **Disabled Child**: Severely disabled children always receive full protection regardless of property type

**Parent-to-Child Transfers with Partial Protection:**
- **Non-Qualified Properties**: Properties not meeting above criteria reassessed at market value
- **Taxable Transfer**: Difference between old and new base year value subject to property tax
- **Basis Protection**: Still inherit parent's original purchase price for capital gains calculations

### 2. Inherited Property Transfers

#### Before Proposition 19
- **Full Reassessment Protection**: Inherited properties kept original property tax basis
- **No Limitations**: All inherited properties received this protection
- **Tax Advantage**: Significant property tax savings for heirs

#### After Proposition 19
**Inherited Property Protection:**
- **Primary Residence**: Can transfer property tax basis if inherited child keeps it as primary residence
- **Other Properties**: Generally reassessed at market value with limited exceptions
- **Age Restriction**: Children under 22 must use as primary residence to qualify
- **County Transfer**: Must transfer property within same county to maintain full protection

### 3. Severely Disabled Individuals

**Enhanced Protections:**
- **Full Protection Severely Disabled**: Property transfers to severely disabled individuals always receive full property tax basis protection
- **No Property Type Restrictions**: Any property type qualifies for disabled transfers
- **Documentation Requirement**: Requires certification of disability status
- **Permanence**: Protection applies regardless of future changes in disability status

### 4. Senior Citizens, Veterans, and Disabled Homeowners

**Expanded Transfer Rights:**
- **Same County Transfer**: Seniors (55+), disabled, or victims of wildfires can transfer property tax basis to replacement property of equal or lesser value within same county
- **Unlimited Transfers**: No limit on number of transfers (unlike previous Proposition 60/90)
- **Value Limit**: Replacement property value ≤ sale price of original property
- **Property Type**: Can transfer to any property type (single-family, condo, etc.)

### 5. Wildfire Victims

**Special Protection:**
- **Reassessment Relief**: Properties damaged or destroyed by wildfires eligible for property tax relief
- **Transfer Basis**: Can transfer property tax basis to replacement property in same county
- **Time Limit**: Must replace property within 2 years of wildfire declaration
- **Documentation**: Requires proof of property damage from fire authority

## Impact on Different Property Types

### Primary Residences
**High Impact Areas:**
- **Valuable Homes**: Properties with high equity benefit significantly from transfer protection
- **Family Homes**: Multi-generational living scenarios with inherited properties
- **Retirement Planning**: Seniors downsizing within same county maintain tax basis

**Strategic Considerations:**
- **Timing**: Critical for seniors planning to relocate within county
- **Documentation**: Proper estate planning documentation required
- **Family Communication**: Early family discussions about property intentions

### Investment Properties
**Significant Changes:**
- **Market Value Reassessment**: Most investment properties now subject to reassessment on transfer
- **Cash Flow Impact**: Higher property taxes reduce rental income
- **Portfolio Planning**: Affects estate planning for rental property portfolios
- **1031 Integration**: Combined with 1031 exchanges for tax planning

**Strategic Considerations:**
- **Hold Period**: Consider longer holding periods to minimize tax increases
- **Entity Structure**: LLC structure impacts transfer rules differently
- **Succession Planning**: Business entity transfers vs. individual transfers

### Vacation Properties
**Limited Protection:**
- **County Transfer Restrictions**: Most vacation properties don't qualify for senior transfer protections
- **Market Value Exposure**: Generally reassessed at current market value
- **Inheritance Rules**: Complex rules for inherited vacation properties
- **Usage Requirements**: Must meet primary residence criteria for protections

### Agricultural Properties
**Special Considerations:**
- **Family Farm Protection**: Properties ≤ $1 million receive full parent-child protection
- **Value Limitations**: Larger agricultural operations face reassessment
- **Agricultural Exemptions**: May qualify for additional agricultural property tax exemptions
- **Business Structure**: Corporate vs. individual ownership affects transfer rules

## Tax Calculation Changes

### Property Tax Impact Example

**Before Proposition 19:**
- Property purchased in 1980: $100,000 base year value
- Current market value: $1,000,000
- Annual property tax: $1,000 (1% of $100,000 + $200 annual increase)
- Transferred to child: $1,000 annual tax continues

**After Proposition 19 (Non-Qualified Transfer):**
- Original property: $1,000,000 market value
- Child inherits: $1,000,000 reassessed value
- Annual property tax: $10,000+ (1% of $1,000,000 + annual increases)
- **Tax Increase**: $9,000+ annually

### Capital Gains vs. Property Tax Tradeoffs

**Transfer Decisions:**
- **Proposition 19 Benefit**: Lower property taxes on inherited property
- **Proposition 19 Cost**: Potentially higher capital gains tax at sale
- **1031 Integration**: May be preferable to defer both taxes through exchange
- **Hold Period**: Longer holding periods mitigate tax increases

The tax basis vs. property tax tradeoff requires balancing lower ongoing taxes against potential higher capital gains at sale, connecting to [[1031-exchange-strategy]] for tax planning integration and [[trusts-guide]] for trust structure considerations.

## Common Pitfalls and Avoidance Strategies

### 1. Misunderstanding Transfer Eligibility
**Common Error**: Assuming all inherited properties receive tax basis protection
**Solution**: Understand specific qualifying criteria for property type and use
**Mitigation**: Consult with California real estate tax specialist before transfers

### 2. Missing Senior Transfer Deadlines
**Common Error**: Failing to act on senior transfer rights within timeframes
**Solution**: Create timeline for senior moves with appropriate buffers
**Mitigation**: Proactive planning and professional guidance

### 3. Inadequate Documentation
**Common Error**: Insufficient proof of primary residence or eligibility
**Solution**: Comprehensive documentation system for property usage and eligibility
**Mitigation**: Regular document audits and professional document management

### 4. Geographic Planning Errors
**Common Error**: Selecting replacement properties in wrong county
**Solution**: Detailed geographic analysis considering county rules
**Mitigation**: County-specific research and local expert consultation

### 5. Professional Team Gaps
**Common Error**: Working with professionals without California real estate tax expertise
**Solution**: Assemble team with specialized California real estate planning expertise
**Mitigation**: Verify credentials and California-specific experience
