# I Will Teach You to Be Rich

## Quick Reference

- **Field**: [[personal-finance]]
- **Type**: Six-Week Action Program
- **Purpose**: Practical, automated personal finance system
- **Key Principles**: Automation, conscious spending, guilt-free spending
- **Related Concepts**: [[the-index-card-rules]], [[the-psychology-of-money]], [[the-algebra-of-wealth]]

## Summary
I Will Teach You to Be Rich is a six‑week program that focuses on automating your finances so you can spend guilt‑free on what you love while automatically saving and investing for the future. The philosophy is that you don't need to be perfect with money—you just need to be consistent.

## Core Claim
You don't need to be perfect with money — you just need to be consistent. A six‑week automated system beats willpower‑based budgeting.

## Core Philosophy

### Conscious Spending
- **Not budgeting** – budgeting is restrictive and nobody sticks to it.
- **Conscious spending means**:
  - Spend extravagantly on what you love.
  - Cut costs mercilessly on what you don't.
  - No guilt about spending (it's planned).
  - Save/invest automatically (so you don't have to think about it).
- **The 4 Buckets**:
  1. **Fixed costs (50‑60%)**: Rent, utilities, debt payments, groceries.
  2. **Investments (10‑20%)**: Retirement, investing accounts.
  3. **Savings (5‑10%)**: Emergency fund, short‑term goals.
  4. **Guilt‑free spending (20‑35%)**: Dining out, hobbies, fun.
- **Key Insight**: If your fixed costs are too high, you're screwed. Optimize these first.

### Automation Over Willpower
- **Why**: Willpower fails, systems work.
- **Automate**: Bill payments, savings transfers, investment contributions, debt payments, everything possible.
- **Result**: You don't have to make decisions about money every month—the system handles it.

## The 6‑Week Program

### Week 1: Get Your Credit Cards Under Control
#### Step 1: Find Out Where You Stand
- Check all three credit reports (free at AnnualCreditReport.com).
- Check credit scores (free through credit cards/banks).
- List all credit cards with balances, rates, and limits.

#### Step 2: Clean Up Credit Report
- Dispute any errors.
- Remove old addresses/names.
- Check for fraudulent accounts.
- Fix negative items (if legitimate, wait 7 years or negotiate).

#### Step 3: Choose Your Credit Card Strategy
- **Option A: Rewards Points** – Travel hackers, big spenders; Chase Sapphire Preferred, Amex Gold; maximize sign‑up bonuses (often worth \$500‑1,000).
- **Option B: Cash Back** – Simple, straightforward; Citi Double Cash (2% on everything); no category tracking needed.
- **Option C: Low Interest** – Carrying balance (not recommended); Discover it, Citi Simplicity; 0% APR for 15‑21 months.

**Key Rule**:
- If carrying debt: Use low‑interest card, pay off aggressively.
- If no debt: Maximize rewards, pay in full monthly.

#### Step 4: Stop Getting Screwed by Credit Cards
- Call and ask for lower APR (works 50%+ of the time).
- Ask for annual fee waiver.
- Set up automatic minimum payment (never miss payment).
- Use credit cards like debit cards (only spend what you have).

### Week 2: Set Up Your Bank Accounts
#### The Account Structure
**You need 4 accounts**:
1. **Checking Account** – For daily spending; keep 1 month expenses here; use debit card for ATM withdrawals only.
2. **Savings Account (High‑Yield)** – Emergency fund + short‑term savings; keep 3‑6 months expenses; online banks pay 4‑5% (Marcus, Ally, etc.).
3. **Investing Account (Taxable)** – Long‑term investments (non‑retirement); contribute monthly automatically; buy index funds (VTSAX, etc.).
4. **Retirement Accounts (401k, IRA)** – Never touch until retirement; max out tax‑advantaged first; target‑date funds or index funds.

#### Step 1: Open High‑Yield Savings Account
- **Why**: Big banks pay 0.01%, online banks pay 4‑5%.
- **Options**: Marcus, Ally, Discover, Capital One 360.
- **Result**: \$10,000 earns \$400‑500/year vs. \$1.

#### Step 2: Set Up Automatic Transfers
**Payday workflow**:
1. Income hits checking account.
2. Automatic transfer to savings (20% of income).
3. Automatic transfer to investments (10% of income).
4. Automatic transfer to retirement (if not payroll deduction).
5. Pay bills from remaining checking balance.

#### Step 3: Create Sub‑Savings Accounts
- For specific goals: emergency fund, vacation fund, car repair fund, wedding/holiday fund, house down payment.
- **Psychology**: Named accounts prevent “stealing” from yourself.

### Week 3: Open a 401(k) and Roth IRA
#### Step 1: Get Your Employer 401(k) Match
- **This is FREE money – don’t miss it.**
- **Example**: Employer matches 50% up to 6% of salary; you contribute 6% = \$3,000 (on \$50k salary); employer adds \$1,500 → 50% return, guaranteed.
- **Action**: Contribute at least enough to get full match; contribute through payroll deduction (automatic); invest in target‑date fund (set and forget).

#### Step 2: Open a Roth IRA
- **Why**: Tax‑free growth forever.
- **Contribution limit**: \$7,000/year (2024).
- **Income limit**: Phaseout starts at \$138k (single), \$218k (married).

**Best Roth IRA providers**:
- **Vanguard**: Lowest costs, best target‑date funds.
- **Fidelity**: Great service, low costs.
- **Schwab**: Excellent customer service.

**Investment choice**:
- Target‑date fund (VTTHX for 2055, etc.) or VTSAX (Total Stock Market Index); set up automatic monthly contribution.

#### Step 3: Contribution Priority
1. **401(k) match** (FREE money – do this first).
2. **Roth IRA** (tax‑free growth).
3. **Remaining 401(k)** (up to limit).
4. **Taxable investing** (after maxing tax‑advantaged).

**Exception**: If you have high‑interest debt (>7%), pay that off first.

#### Step 4: Choose Your Investments
- **For most people**: Target‑date fund – pick fund for your retirement year; automatically adjusts risk as you age; zero maintenance; low fees (0.1‑0.2%).
- **Or build your own**: VTSAX (Total Stock Market Index, 0.04% fee), VFIAX (S&P 500 Index, 0.04% fee), VBTLX (Total Bond Market, 0.05% fee); simple: 80‑90% stocks, 10‑20% bonds.

### Week 4: Conscious Spending
#### Step 1: Track Your Spending (One Week)
- Use an app: Mint (free), YNAB (\$84/year), Personal Capital (free).
- Or manually check last 3 months of spending, categorize everything, calculate percentages by category.

#### Step 2: Create Your Conscious Spending Plan
**The goal is NOT to cut everything** – it's to cut the unimportant so you can spend on the important.

**Example: \$4,000/month take‑home pay**

- **Fixed Costs (60% = \$2,400)**: Rent \$1,200, utilities \$150, groceries \$400, minimum debt payments \$200, insurance \$150, phone/internet \$100, car payment \$200.
- **Investments (10% = \$400)**: 401(k) \$200, Roth IRA \$200.
- **Savings (5% = \$200)**: Emergency fund \$100, vacation fund \$50, new car fund \$50.
- **Guilt‑Free Spending (25% = \$1,000)**: Dining out \$300, entertainment \$200, shopping \$200, hobbies \$150, random stuff \$150.

**Total**: \$4,000 (100%).

#### Step 3: Optimize Your Fixed Costs
**Big wins > small wins**

**Big wins**:
- Refinance mortgage (save \$200‑500/month).
- Shop insurance (shop annually, save \$100‑300).
- Refinance car loan (lower rate).
- Negotiate rent (when renewing lease).
- Cut unused subscriptions (\$50‑150/month).

**Small wins (do these too)**:
- Call and negotiate cable/internet.
- Switch to MVNO phone carrier (Mint, Visible).
- Buy generic brands.
- Meal prep (saves \$200‑400/month).
- Library cards vs. buying books.

#### Step 4: Automate Your Plan
**On payday**:
1. Automatic transfer to checking (fixed costs).
2. Automatic transfer to savings account.
3. Automatic transfer to investment accounts.
4. Automatic bill payments.
5. Whatever remains = guilt‑free spending.

**Result**: You never “decide” to save. It happens automatically.

### Week 5: Pay Off Debt Smartly
#### The Debt Hierarchy
**Pay in this order**:
1. **Credit card debt** (18‑25%+): Pay minimums on all cards; throw all extra money at highest‑rate card; once paid off, move to next highest (debt avalanche).
2. **Personal loans** (8‑15%): Same strategy – highest rate first.
3. **Car loans** (4‑8%): You can invest instead of paying early (if <6%); peace of mind may be worth paying early.
4. **Student loans** (3‑7%): Don’t pay early unless you want peace of mind; invest instead (likely higher returns).
5. **Mortgage** (3‑7%): Don’t pay early (better to invest); exception: near retirement and risk‑averse.

#### Step 1: List All Your Debt
Create spreadsheet with: creditor name, balance owed, interest rate, minimum payment, due date.

#### Step 2: Choose Your Strategy
- **Option A: Debt Avalanche (recommended)**: Pay minimums on everything; throw all extra at highest interest rate; saves most money long‑term.
- **Option B: Debt Snowball**: Pay minimums on everything; throw all extra at smallest balance; quick wins build momentum; costs more in interest; better if you need psychological wins.

#### Step 3: Automate Debt Payments
- Set up automatic minimum payments on all cards.
- Set up automatic extra payment on target card.
- Don’t close cards after paying (helps credit score).
- Use cards for small purchases, pay in full monthly.

#### Step 4: Consider Balance Transfer
- **If**: Credit card debt > \$5,000 and rate >18%.
- **Then**: Transfer to 0% APR card.
- **Cost**: 3‑5% balance transfer fee.
- **Benefit**: 12‑21 months interest‑free; save \$500‑2,000 in interest.
- **Warning**: Don’t use card for new purchases; pay off before 0% period ends; check balance transfer fee (some cards waive it).

### Week 6: Why Investing Isn't Rocket Science
#### The Investment Philosophy
- Most investors fail because they try to time the market, pick individual stocks, panic in downturns, or pay high fees.
- Winning strategy: Buy index funds (diversified, low cost), invest automatically every month, never sell because of market drops, time in market > timing market.

#### Step 1: Buy the Market, Not a Stock
- Index funds own everything: VTSAX = 4,000+ companies; you own Apple, Google, Amazon, Microsoft; when one company fails, you're protected.
- Why this beats picking stocks: Even pros can’t consistently beat the market; index funds outperform 80‑90% of active funds; lower fees (0.04% vs 1%+); zero stress.

#### Step 2: Choose Your Asset Allocation
- **Rule of thumb**: 110 – your age = % in stocks.
- **Examples**: Age 25 → 85% stocks, 15% bonds; Age 35 → 75% stocks, 25% bonds; Age 45 → 65% stocks, 35% bonds; Age 55 → 55% stocks, 45% bonds.
- **Or use target‑date fund**: Pick fund for your retirement year (e.g., VTTHX); fund automatically adjusts allocation.

#### Step 3: Buy and Hold Forever
- **Don’t sell because**: Market dropped (that’s when you buy more), you found a “better” stock (you don’t know that), someone on TV said to (they’re often wrong), you’re “locking in gains” (missing out on future gains).
- **When to sell**: You need the money (retirement, big purchase), rebalancing, tax‑loss harvesting, you change your allocation (age‑based shift).

#### Step 4: Automate Your Investing
- Set up automatic monthly investment: \$500/month to Roth IRA, \$200/month to taxable account, \$500/month to 401(k) (payroll deduction).
- **Dollar‑cost averaging**: Invest same amount every month; buy more shares when prices are low; buy fewer shares when prices are high; eliminates market timing risk; removes emotion.

#### Step 5: Ignore the Noise
- **Turn off**: Financial news (designed to scare you), stock tips (unsolicited advice is usually bad), market predictions (nobody can predict the future), hot stocks (usually already crashed by time you hear).
- **Focus on**: Your goals, not market returns; your savings rate, not market volatility; your timeline, not daily headlines; your behavior, not your portfolio balance.

## The Big Wins

### The Big Wins That Actually Move the Needle
1. **Automate Everything** – Time: 2‑3 hours to set up; Impact: \$100,000+ over 30 years.
2. **Get Your Employer Match** – Time: 15 minutes to enroll; Impact: \$1,500+ per year (free money).
3. **Negotiate Your Salary** – Time: 1 hour preparation, 30‑minute meeting; Impact: \$5,000‑10,000+ per year.
4. **Refinance High‑Interest Debt** – Time: 2‑3 hours; Impact: \$200‑500/month savings.
5. **Shop Insurance Annually** – Time: 2 hours; Impact: \$100‑300/year savings.
6. **Subscriptions Audit** – Time: 30 minutes; Impact: \$50‑150/month savings.

### The Small Wins (Do These Too, But Don't Obsess)
- **Daily**: Make coffee at home sometimes, bring lunch to work (but enjoy meals out too), use library for books.
- **Weekly**: Meal prep for weekdays, review spending for week (5 minutes), plan free weekend activities.
- **Monthly**: Review subscriptions (cancel unused), check for fees on accounts, read one personal‑finance article.

## The Psychology

### Why This Works
1. **It's Automatic** – Willpower fails (you're human); systems work (they don't get tired); automation = success by default.
2. **It's Guilt‑Free** – You planned your spending; no guilt about enjoying life; you're saving and investing automatically.
3. **It's Realistic** – Not about deprivation; not about never spending money; about spending on what matters to YOU.
4. **It's Simple** – 6‑week program; step‑by‑step actions; no complex calculations; set it and forget it.

### Common Objections
- **"I don't have enough money to save"** – Start with 1%; increase by 1% each month; you'll be at 10‑20% within a year.
- **"Investing is too risky"** – Not investing is riskier (inflation eats your money); long timeline (20+ years) = stocks historically win; diversification reduces risk.
- **"I'll start when I make more money"** – Lifestyle inflation will eat your raise; start now with whatever you have; building the habit matters more than the amount.
- **"I don't have time to manage investments"** – That's why you automate; spend < 1 hour per year on investments; index funds require zero maintenance.

## The Finish Line

### What "Rich" Means
- **Not**: Being a millionaire.
- **Yes**: Having enough to live life on your terms.
- Rich means: no debt stress, freedom to quit a job you hate, ability to travel/pursue hobbies, not worrying about bills, helping family/friends, giving back to causes you care about.

### How Much Is Enough?
- **The 4% Rule**: Withdraw 4% annually in retirement; need 25× annual expenses saved.
  - Example: \$50k expenses = \$1.25 M needed.
- **Or use**: 80% of current income.
  - Example: \$100k income = need \$80k/year → \$80k × 25 = \$2 M needed.

### Freedom Number
1. Track annual expenses (be honest).
2. Multiply by 25.
3. That's your freedom number.

**Example**: Annual expenses \$60,000 → Freedom number \$1.5 M; at 7% return, saving \$1,000/month = 26 years.

## Key Takeaways
1. Automate everything – willpower fails, systems work.
2. Conscious spending – spend extravagantly on what you love, cut mercilessly on what you don't.
3. Invest early and often – time is your greatest asset.
4. Use index funds – don't try to beat the market, be the market.
5. Get the employer match – this is free money.
6. Negotiate – salary, fees, insurance – everything is negotiable.
7. Ignore the noise – financial news is designed to scare you, not help you.
8. Focus on big wins – a few big decisions > thousands of small ones.
9. Start today – the best time to start was yesterday, the second best time is today.
10. Live your life – money is a tool, not the goal. Spend on what matters to you.
