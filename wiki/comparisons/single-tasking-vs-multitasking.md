# Single-Tasking vs Multitasking

Chris Bailey's *Hyperfocus* provides a clear verdict: single-tasking substantially outperforms multitasking for both quality and speed of output.

## Single-Tasking
- Allows full engagement through [[hyperfocus]] states
- Eliminates [[attention-residue]] between tasks
- Produces higher quality work in less total time
- Enables deeper cognitive processing

## Multitasking
- Fragments attention across tasks
- Creates attention residue that degrades performance
- Increases errors and reduces creative output
- Feels productive but delivers lower quality results

## Verdict
Single-tasking is the superior approach for complex, high-value work. Multitasking may be acceptable for trivial or routine tasks, but for anything requiring deep thought, single-tasking wins decisively.

This finding aligns with the broader evidence from cognitive science on the costs of task-switching and reinforces the [[hyperfocus]] mode as the optimal state for demanding work.
