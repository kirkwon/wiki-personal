# Rates

Rates is a mental model that provides a shortcut for understanding patterns and relationships in complex systems, particularly economic ones. At its core, the concept centers on the idea that various rates—interest rates, credit growth rates, returns on investment, inflation rates, and money growth rates—serve as the fundamental drivers of how economies and financial systems behave over time.

In Ray Dalio's framework as described in [[how-the-economic-machine-works---ray-dalio]], rates are central to the credit-debt cycle. When borrowing costs are low and returns are high, credit expands, money growth accelerates, and the economy tends to expand. When the opposite occurs—high borrowing costs, low returns—credit contracts and the economy slows or contracts. Understanding these rates provides a mental shortcut for anticipating the direction of economic cycles and making sense of macro trends.

The model applies beyond pure economics as well. Rates of change, rates of adoption, rates of decay, and rates of learning all function as conceptual shortcuts for navigating complexity. Recognizing which rate matters most in a given situation helps cut through noise and identify the dominant force shaping outcomes.

This concept connects to broader mental models like [[markets]] and [[leverage]], where understanding the dynamics of rate-of-change is essential for making sense of how systems evolve.
