# Authority Bias

Authority bias is the tendency to attribute greater accuracy to the opinion of an authority figure and be more influenced by that opinion than it merits. Excessive deference to authority figures — giving too much weight to the opinions of those perceived as experts or in positions of power. Covered in Part VI of *Super Thinking*.

Already covered in [[cognitive-biases-library]] under social/emotional biases.

## Core Idea
People defer excessively to those with perceived expertise, titles, or status — even when the authority figure's opinion is outside their domain of expertise. This can lead to poor decisions and suppression of dissenting views.

## Applications
- **Decision-making**: Question whether expert consensus is warranted or if independent analysis is needed.
- **Organizations**: Flatten hierarchies to encourage input from all levels.
- **Learning**: Evaluate claims on merit, not source.

## Connection to Other Models
Authority bias connects to [[social-proof]] (conforming to perceived norms), [[reciprocity]] (deference to those who have helped us), and [[in-group-out-group-bias]] (trusting insiders). Weinberg includes it in the social and emotional intelligence section of *Super Thinking*.
