# Peril of Leverage

Financial leverage amplifies both gains and losses in unpredictable markets due to randomness and uncertainty. Taleb explains how leverage can devastate when random events turn against you.

## Definition

Leverage is the use of borrowed capital to increase the potential return of an investment. While it can magnify gains, it equally magnifies losses — and in random markets, the distribution of losses can be far more severe than the distribution of gains.

## Overview

Leverage — borrowing to invest, using derivatives, or otherwise amplifying exposure — magnifies the impact of random events. In good times, leverage produces spectacular returns. In bad times, it produces catastrophic losses. Because randomness ensures that bad times will eventually come, leverage creates a systematic vulnerability.

## Key Points

- Leverage amplifies the impact of random events
- Asymmetric payoff structures mean losses can exceed gains
- Connected to [[skin-in-the-game]] — those who benefit from leverage often don't bear the downside
- Relates to [[black-swan-events]] — extreme leverage makes tail events catastrophic

- Size positions so that worst-case scenarios are survivable
