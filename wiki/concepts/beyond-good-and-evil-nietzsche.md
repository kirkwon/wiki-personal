# Beyond Good and Evil

Nietzsche's 1886 philosophical work that challenges the foundations of traditional morality and philosophy. The central claim is that moral systems are human creations shaped by psychological motivations, historical circumstances, and power dynamics—not objective truths.

## Core Frameworks

- **Genealogical analysis of moral concepts**: Tracing how moral values originated from psychological states like resentment, pride, or the will to power rather than from rational or divine sources.
- **Master vs. slave morality**: A framework distinguishing noble values (strength, creativity, pride) from resentment-based values (meekness, equality, pity).
- **Will to Power**: The fundamental drive behind human action, encompassing self-overcoming and creation, not merely domination.
- **Free spirit**: Individuals who transcend conventional morality and create personal, life-affirming values.

## Key Insight

Moral systems are human creations with psychological origins, not divine or rational necessities. Nietzsche calls for a revaluation of all values and the creation of new, life-affirming ones by free spirits.

## Ethical Guardrails

Nietzsche provides "when to stop" conditions to prevent his critique from becoming nihilism or justification for cruelty. The philosophy is meant to enable self-overcoming and value creation, not destruction for its own sake.
