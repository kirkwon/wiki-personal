# Pour Over Coffee

Pour-over coffee is a manual drip brewing method that involves pouring hot water over coffee grounds in a filter, allowing gravity to extract the coffee. This method provides exceptional control over extraction, making it ideal for highlighting the unique flavors of specialty coffee beans. The manual process emphasizes technique and attention to detail.

## Quick Reference

- **Field**: [[Beverages/Coffee]]
- **Type**: Preparation Method
- **Category**: Manual Drip Brewing
- **Skill Level**: Intermediate
- **Time Required**: 3-5 minutes
- **Best For**: Single-origin specialty coffee, highlighting flavor complexity
- **Key Variables**: Pour technique, water temperature, grind size

## Summary

Pour-over coffee is a manual brewing method that involves pouring hot water over coffee grounds in a filter, allowing gravity to extract the coffee. This method provides exceptional control over extraction, making it ideal for highlighting the unique flavors of specialty coffee beans. The manual process emphasizes technique and attention to detail.

## Core Principles

- **Controlled Extraction**: Manual pouring allows precise timing via [[controlled-extraction]]
- **Gravity Filtration**: Water passes through coffee and filter
- **Fresh Grounds**: Grind immediately before brewing
- **Water Quality**: Temperature and purity critical
- **Technique Focus**: Pour method affects extraction
- **Precision Variables**: Grind size, water temperature, pour rate, bloom time, and total brew time are the five key variables managed through manual pouring

## Key Variables

- **Pour technique**: Circular motion, maintain water level above coffee bed
- **Water temperature**: 195-205°F (90-96°C); lighter roasts use 195°F, darker roasts use 205°F
- **Grind size**: Medium-fine (table salt texture); affects surface area and extraction speed; finer = faster extraction, coarser = slower
- **Brew ratio**: 1:15 to 1:17 coffee to water
- **Total brew time**: 3-5 minutes; target 3:00-3:30 minutes
- **Bloom time**: 30-45 seconds with double the coffee weight in water; releases CO2 for even extraction

## Step-by-Step Process

### 1. Preparation
```yaml
Measure Coffee:
- Standard ratio: 1:15 to 1:17 (coffee to water)
- Example: 20g coffee for 300g water
- Adjust based on taste preference

Prepare Equipment:
- Rinse filter with hot water
- Place coffee in dripper
- Pre-wet brewer if necessary
```

### 2. Heating Water
```yaml
Temperature: 195-205°F (90-96°C)
- Optimal: 200°F (93°C) for most coffees
- Lighter roasts: 195°F (90°C)
- Darker roasts: 205°F (96°C)
Method: Use thermometer for precision
```

### 3. Grinding
```yaml
Grind Size: Medium-fine (table salt texture)
- Reference: Granulated sugar consistency
- Adjustment: Wetter coffee = coarser, drier = finer
- Equipment: Burr grinder recommended
- Timing: Grind immediately before brewing
```

### 4. Bloom Phase
```yaml
Process:
- Add double coffee weight in water
- Example: 20g coffee → 40g water
- Wait: 30-45 seconds
- Purpose: Release CO2, prepare grounds for optimal extraction

Observations:
- Should form even coffee bed
- Should rise and settle
- Should smell fragrant
```

### 5. Main Pour Phase
```yaml
Pour Technique:
- Circular motion, keep water level consistent
- Pour in 2-3 stages for optimal extraction
- Maintain water level above coffee bed
- Avoid pouring directly onto filter paper

Timing:
- First pour: Add 60-70% of water total
- Second pour: Remaining water
- Total pour time: 2:00-2:30 minutes
- Total brew time: 3:00-3:30 minutes
```

### 6. Completion
```yaml
Draining:
- Wait for all water to drain through
- Should stop within 30 seconds of final pour
- Check for channeling (uneven extraction)

Serving:
- Remove filter and coffee grounds
- Transfer to pre-warmed cup if needed
- Enjoy immediately for best flavor
```

## Brewer-Specific Techniques

### Hario V60
```yaml
Characteristics:
- Conical shape, single large hole
- Fast draining, requires technique
- Highlights acidity and clarity

Technique:
- Slow, controlled circular pours
- Bloom stage crucial
- Maintain water level
- Total time: 2:30-3:30 minutes
```

### Chemex
```yaml
Characteristics:
- Hourglass shape, thick glass
- Bonded paper filters (thicker)
- Cleaner, smoother cup
- Slower extraction

Technique:
- More aggressive pours
- Bloom still important
- Can use all water in one pour
- Total time: 4-5 minutes
```

### Kalita Wave
```yaml
Characteristics:
- Flat bottom, three small holes
- More forgiving, consistent
- Better for beginners
- Emphasizes body and sweetness

Technique:
- Can be more aggressive
- Less precise pouring needed
- More forgiving of timing
- Total time: 3-5 minutes
```

## Extraction Science

### Extraction Target
- **Ideal Range**: 18-22% extraction
- **Under-extraction**: Sour, weak, missing complexity
- **Over-extraction**: Bitter, harsh, astringent
- **Measurement**: Requires refractometer for precision

### Key Variables
- **Grind Size**: Affects surface area and extraction speed
- **Water Temperature**: Higher temp = faster extraction
- **Pour Rate**: Faster = more extraction, slower = less
- **Bloom Time**: Longer bloom = more CO2 release
- **Total Time**: Controls overall extraction percentage

### Flavor Effects
```yaml
Lighter Pours:
- More acidity
- Cleaner cup
- Less body
- More nuanced

Heavier Pours:
- More body
- Sweeter overall
- Less complexity
- More balanced

Variable Pours:
- Complexity and balance
- Multiple flavor notes
- More sophisticated cup
```

## Flavor Development

### Acidity Enhancement
- **Lighter roasts**: Bright, fruity notes
- **Proper bloom**: Releases volatile compounds
- **Controlled extraction**: Preserves delicate acids
- **Clean equipment**: No off-flavors

### Body Development
- **Medium roasts**: Natural oils, fuller mouthfeel
- **Proper grind**: Consistent extraction
- **Brew time**: Longer extraction for body
- **Filter type**: Thicker filters remove body

### Sweetness Preservation
- **Fresh beans**: Natural sugars preserved
- **Correct temperature**: Avoids scorching
- **Even saturation**: Complete extraction
- **Clean equipment**: No bitterness sources

## Common Issues & Solutions

### Channeling
```yaml
Problem: Water finds paths, uneven extraction
Symptoms: Uneven coffee bed, fast draining
Solutions:
- Use proper grind size
- Bloom adequately
- Pour in circular motion
- Ensure even saturation
```

### Over-extraction
```yaml
Problem: Too much bitterness
Symptoms: Harsh finish, astringent mouthfeel
Solutions:
- Use coarser grind
- Shorter total time
- Lower water temperature
- Less aggressive pouring
```

### Under-extraction
```yaml
Problem: Sour, weak flavor
Symptoms: Light color, thin mouthfeel
Solutions:
- Use finer grind
- Longer brew time
- Higher water temperature
- More thorough pouring
```

## Advanced Techniques

### Variable Ratio Pouring
```yaml
Technique:
- First pour: 50% of water quickly
- Pause: 30 seconds
- Second pour: Remaining water slowly
Benefits:
- Better extraction control
- More complex flavors
- Balanced cup profile
```

### Multi-stage Brewing
```yaml
Technique:
- Bloom with water
- First stage: 60% of water
- Wait: 30 seconds
- Second stage: Remaining 40%
- Benefits: Enhanced complexity, balanced extraction
```

```yaml
By Brew Time:
- Faster brew: Higher temperature
- Slower brew: Lower temperature
```
