# Situational Ethics

The idea that ethical decisions are heavily shaped by situational factors—including the halo effect, peer pressure, authority, incentive structures, and cultural norms—sometimes overriding personal moral standards. Rather than being fixed traits, ethical behavior is influenced by context, and small changes in the decision environment can dramatically alter honesty. Context matters more than character in determining honest behavior.

## Key Factors

- **Halo effect**: Positive impressions of a person or institution lower vigilance against dishonesty; prior positive impressions can lower ethical vigilance.
- **Peer pressure**: Observing others cheat normalizes dishonest behavior, reduces personal guilt, and shifts what people consider acceptable.
- **Authority**: Commands from authority figures can override individual moral judgments, and hierarchical structures influence willingness to act unethically.
- **Environmental design**: Small changes in the decision environment can dramatically alter cheating rates.
- **Incentive structures**: Reward systems can promote or discourage dishonesty.
- **Cultural norms**: Different societies normalize different degrees of dishonesty.

## Connections to Design and Culture

Understanding situational ethics is crucial for [[ethical-design-manipulation-question]]—designing environments, organizations, and systems that encourage honest behavior rather than inadvertently enabling dishonesty. It also relates to [[building-a-decision-culture]], where organizational culture shapes ethical behavior.
