# Agent-Based Modeling

Agent-based modeling (ABM) is a computational simulation technique and modeling approach where individual agents—each following simple rules—interact within an environment. The aggregate behavior of these interactions produces emergent patterns that are difficult to predict from the rules alone, and complex collective behavior emerges from their interactions. It is central to understanding how [[emergent-complexity-from-simple-rules]] arises.

## Key Features

- Individual agents with defined rules.
- Interactions between agents produce system-level patterns.
- No central controller; order emerges from local interactions.
- Can capture heterogeneity among agents.
- Useful for modeling markets, epidemics, social dynamics.

## How It Works

- Define agents with simple behavioral rules.
- Place agents in a shared environment.
- Let agents interact according to their rules.
- Observe emergent patterns at the system level.

## Applications

- Simulating swarm behavior and [[swarm-intelligence]].
- Modeling market dynamics and [[platform-dominance]] effects.
- Exploring [[evolution-of-cooperation]] in competitive environments.
- Designing [[artificial-life-alife]] systems.
- Modeling epidemics and social dynamics.

## Connection to Other Concepts

ABM relates to [[cellular-automata]] (which is a specific type of agent-based model on a grid) and [[network-models]] (which defines the connection structure agents interact through).

## Connection to Kelly's Framework

ABM is a primary tool for exploring the principles in [[the-nine-laws-of-god]] and [[self-organization-and-emergence]]. It allows designers to test whether decentralized rules produce the desired emergent outcomes before deploying real systems.
