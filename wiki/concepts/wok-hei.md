# Wok Hei（镬气 / 鑒氣）

[[wok-hei]] (粤语：鑊氣 / 鑒氣，意为"锅的呼吸") is the distinctive smoky, charred flavor achieved in wok cooking through extremely high heat and rapid cooking. It is the smoky, complex flavor characteristic of stir-fry dishes cooked in a well-seasoned wok over very high heat, resulting from the combination of extreme temperatures, oil vaporization, and caramelization of food juices against the hot wok surface. It is also known as the "breath of the wok" (锅的呼吸 / breath of the wok).^[raw/articles/Wok Hei.md]

- **Field**: [[gastronomy]]

## Summary

Wok Hei is the smoky, complex flavor characteristic of stir-fry dishes cooked in a well-seasoned wok over very high heat. The flavor is primarily a product of the [[maillard-reaction]] occurring at the intense temperatures generated in a well-seasoned wok, combined with oil vaporization, smoke infusion, and rapid browning.

## The Science

Wok Hei depends on the following physical and chemical processes:

1. **Extreme Heat**: Wok temperatures reach 500-600°F (260-315°C)
2. **Oil Vaporization**: Oil partially vaporizes and recondenses on food
3. **Maillard Reaction**: The reaction between amino acids and reducing sugars at 285-330°F produces the complex roasted, nutty, and meaty flavor compounds characteristic of wok hei. Rapid browning at these temperatures creates complex flavor compounds.
4. **Smoke Infusion**: Brief smoke from oil/grease adds depth

High heat combined with small batches prevents temperature drops that would inhibit the Maillard reaction.

## Required Conditions

To achieve true Wok Hei, the following must be met:

- **Carbon steel or cast iron wok** (not non-stick)
- **High-output burner** (commercial-grade or powerful home burner, ideal: 15,000+ BTU)
- **Seasoned wok** (patina from repeated use)
- **Hot oil** (smoking point reached)
- **Small batches** (don't overcrowd)

## How To Achieve Wok Hei

### Preparation

1. Heat wok until smoking (10-20 seconds on high heat)
2. Add oil and swirl to coat sides
3. Wait for smoke (oil at smoking point)
4. Add ingredients immediately

### Technique

- Cook in small batches (maintain heat)
- Toss constantly (prevent burning)
- Don't crowd the wok (food should steam-fry, not stew)
- Finish with aromatics (garlic, ginger, scallions last 10 seconds)
- Rapid stirring to distribute heat evenly
- Proper seasoning of the wok surface

### Common Mistakes

- ❌ Using non-stick wok (can't achieve required temperature)
- ❌ Low heat setting
- ❌ Overcrowding (temperature drops too much)
- ❌ Adding ingredients to cold wok
- ❌ Not tossing enough food

## Equipment

### Woks for Wok Hei

- **Flat-bottom carbon steel** (works on gas/electric)
- **Round-bottom carbon steel** (traditional, requires wok ring)
- **Cast iron** (retains heat well, heavy)

### Burners

- **Professional gas range** (ideal: 15,000+ BTU)
- **Wok burner attachment** (home alternative)
- **Outdoor propane burner** (camping option)

## Best Practices

- Preheat thoroughly — Wok should be smoking hot
- Use neutral oil with high smoke point (peanut, avocado, grapeseed)
- Marinate proteins — Adds flavor and prevents sticking
- Cut uniformly — Even cooking
- Dry vegetables — Prevents steaming
- Work in batches — Never crowd the wok
- Finish with aromatics — Add garlic/ginger at the end

## Dishes Known for Wok Hei

- Beef Chow Fun（干炒牛肉粉）
- Cantonese-style Fried Rice（港式炒饭）
- Char Siu（叉烧）
- Salt and Pepper Shrimp（盐椒虾 / 椒盐虾）
- Stir-Fried Greens with Garlic（蒜炒时蔬）
