# Qualified Intermediary

A **qualified intermediary** (QI) is an independent third party who facilitates 1031 exchanges by holding and managing exchange funds, ensuring compliance with IRS regulations, and handling all necessary documentation.

## Requirements

- Must be an independent third party (cannot be a related party or agent of the taxpayer)
- Must hold funds in a separate account
- Must have experience with 1031 transactions
- Must carry errors and omissions insurance
- Fees typically range from 0.5-1.5% of the exchange value

## Key Services

1. **Documentation Preparation**: Exchange agreements and assignments
2. **Folding Control**: Receipt and disbursement of exchange funds
3. **Timeline Management**: Critical date tracking and compliance (45‑day identification, 180‑day exchange)
4. **Reporting**: Exchange completion documentation for tax purposes
5. **Post-Exchange Support**: Recordkeeping and IRS compliance

## Selection Criteria

When selecting a QI, investors should verify credentials, insurance coverage, track record, and experience with 1031 transactions. Interview and choose a QI with a good reputation, and ensure the QI controls all exchange proceeds and distributions. Due diligence is critical since QI failure (bankruptcy, incompetence) is a key risk in the exchange process.
