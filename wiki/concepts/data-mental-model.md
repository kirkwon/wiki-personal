# Data as a mental model

Data is a universal mental model that functions as a conceptual shortcut for navigating complex situations through pattern and relationship recognition. Rather than treating data as raw facts or information, this model elevates it to a framework for making sense of the world—identifying patterns, spotting relationships, and building understanding.

The model draws on cross-source perspectives from behavioral economics and risk analysis. Nassim Nicholas Taleb's *Fooled by Randomness* argues that people systematically misread randomness in data and narrative, cautioning against overconfidence in patterns that may not exist. Richard H. Thaler's *Misbehaving* demonstrates how humans deviate from rational-agent models, showing that our interpretation of data is shaped by cognitive biases.

This mental model connects to [[biases]], [[thinking-mental-model]], and [[superforecasting]], all of which address how humans misinterpret data and navigate uncertainty. It extends [[reality-mental-model]] by framing data as a navigational tool rather than mere information. The confidence level is set at 0.7, reflecting moderate uncertainty about the model's completeness and the open question of what distinguishes it from [[biases]] or [[thinking-mental-model]].

Key considerations:
- Data as pattern-recognition tool vs. raw information
- Risk of being fooled by apparent patterns in randomness
- Cognitive biases affecting data interpretation
- Operational definition needed to differentiate from adjacent mental models
