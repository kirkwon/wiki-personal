# Self-Image and Morality

The principle that people cheat just enough to maintain a positive self-image, preserving their moral identity while still benefiting from dishonesty. Moral identity acts as a constraint on the extent of dishonest behavior.

## Key Findings

- People are "honorably dishonest"—they cheat opportunistically but not maximally, staying within a zone that preserves self-regard.
- Moral identity functions as an internal regulator that limits dishonesty more effectively than external punishment alone.
- Self-deception about the extent of one's cheating is common; people construct narratives that justify their dishonest behavior.
- The cognitive effort of maintaining a positive self-image while acting dishonestly creates psychological tension that limits extreme cheating.
