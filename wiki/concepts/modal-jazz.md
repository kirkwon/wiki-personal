# Modal Jazz

Modal jazz is a style of jazz that uses modes and fewer chord changes to allow for greater melodic exploration. Miles Davis pioneered this approach, most famously on *Kind of Blue* (1959). It represents a fundamental shift from the harmonic complexity of bebop toward a more spacious, melodically focused approach.^[raw/articles/miles-davis.md]

## Origins and Development
Miles Davis pioneered modal jazz with the landmark album [[kind-of-blue]] (1959). The approach emerged from his desire to move beyond the rapid chord changes of bebop, instead focusing on scales, modes, and melodic development. Davis's modal approach represented a deliberate aesthetic choice toward economy of expression. Rather than filling every bar with notes, modal jazz allows for the "notes you don't play" to carry equal weight. This philosophy connects to his broader approach of using space and silence as musical elements.

## Key Characteristics
- Soloing over one or two chords rather than rapid chord changes or complex progressions
- Emphasis on melodic development, contour, and space
- Use of modes like the Lydian mode for dreamy, floating qualities
- Pentatonic scales for exotic, bluesy sounds
- Tritone substitutions for added tension

## Landmark Recordings
- **Kind of Blue** (1959) – Tracks like "So What," "All Blues," "Freddie Freeloader," and "Blue in Green" exemplify the modal approach
  - "So What" - Modal exploration
  - "All Blues" - Minor blues feeling
  - "Freddie Freeloader" - Blues with bebop elements
  - "Blue in Green" - Ballad modal approach
- **Milestones** (1958) – Transitional album bridging hard bop and modal jazz
