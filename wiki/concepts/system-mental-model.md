# System as a Mental Model

System is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

This mental model is applicable universally across all domains. It functions as a lens for recognizing how components interact within larger wholes, how feedback loops drive behavior, and how interventions in one part of a system can produce unintended consequences elsewhere.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

The System mental model draws on two major traditions:

- **John Gall** — *The Systems Bible*, which offers a wry, accessible guide to how systems actually behave, emphasizing that systems tend to produce results not intended by their designers.
- **Donella H. Meadows** — *Thinking in Systems*, which provides a more formal framework for understanding feedback loops, stocks and flows, and leverage points in complex systems.

## Relationship to Other Mental Models

System as a mental model connects to [[thinking-mental-model]], [[reality-mental-model]], [[analysis]], and [[problem-md]]. The key distinction is that "System" emphasizes the relational and structural nature of complex situations — looking at how parts interconnect rather than focusing solely on individual elements or linear cause-and-effect.
