# Conscious Spending Plan

A budgeting framework developed by Ramit Sethi that replaces restrictive traditional budgeting with a four-bucket allocation designed to allow guilt-free spending on what matters while automatically handling savings and investments.

## The Four Buckets
1. **Fixed Costs (50-60%)**: Rent, utilities, debt payments, groceries, insurance, phone/internet, car payments.
2. **Investments (10-20%)**: Retirement contributions (401k, Roth IRA), long-term investing accounts.
3. **Savings (5-10%)**: Emergency fund, short-term goals (vacation, car repair, wedding).
4. **Guilt-Free Spending (20-35%)**: Dining out, entertainment, hobbies, shopping—spending extravagantly on what you love with no guilt because it's planned.

## Key Principles
- **Not budgeting**: Budgeting is restrictive and rarely sustainable. Conscious spending is about optimizing, not depriving.
- **Fixed costs first**: If fixed costs are too high, the plan fails regardless of other buckets.
- **Automation**: Automatic transfers on payday distribute money into each bucket without requiring daily decisions.
- **Big wins over small wins**: Refinancing, negotiating rent, shopping insurance annually, and auditing subscriptions move the needle more than daily frugality like making coffee at home.

## Example
On a $4,000/month take-home pay:
- Fixed costs: $2,400 (60%)
- Investments: $400 (10%)
- Savings: $200 (5%)
- Guilt-free spending: $1,000 (25%)

## Connection to Automation
The conscious spending plan works hand-in-hand with [[automation-over-willpower]]. Once the four-bucket allocation is set, automatic transfers handle distribution on every payday, eliminating the need for willpower-based decisions each month.
