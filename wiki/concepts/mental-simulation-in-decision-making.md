# Mental Simulation in Decision-Making

Mental simulation is the cognitive process of running a course of action forward in time in one's mind to check for flaws before committing to it. Gary Klein identifies it as one of the core "sources of power" behind expert decision-making.

Experts use mental simulation to rapidly evaluate a single course of action rather than comparing multiple options. They imagine how the situation will unfold, anticipate potential problems, and adjust the plan on the fly. This process is fast, largely unconscious, and relies on the rich pattern library built through experience.

Klein distinguishes mental simulation from formal analysis: it does not require enumerating all options or calculating probabilities. Instead, it leverages the decision-maker's internal model of how the world works. The technique can be trained through deliberate practice—exposing people to diverse scenarios and providing feedback on their mental runs.

This concept connects to [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]] in that both involve projecting outcomes forward, but mental simulation is specifically framed as an intuitive, experience-driven evaluation tool rather than a structured reasoning method. It also complements [[recognition-primed-decision-model]], where simulation serves as the evaluation step after pattern recognition triggers a response.
