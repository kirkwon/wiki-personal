# Breaking Barriers to Thinking

Breaking barriers to thinking refers to the deliberate process of overcoming mental constraints that limit creative exploration. Edward de Bono identifies self-imposed barriers as one of the primary obstacles to lateral thinking. This involves techniques that interrupt habitual thought patterns to access new perspectives, including "zooming out" to see the broader context and considering alternative possibilities that feel counterintuitive.

## Types of Barriers

- **Established patterns**: Default assumptions and habitual ways of framing problems
- **Language constraints**: Words and categories that limit how we perceive situations
- **Sequential thinking**: The tendency to proceed step-by-step without exploring alternatives
- **Fear of absurdity**: Resistance to considering ideas that seem impractical or strange

## Techniques for Breaking Barriers

- Zooming out to broaden perspective
- Using lateral movements like "what if" and "undo what you did"
- Challenging the words used to describe the problem
- Employing metaphors and analogies to reframe situations
- Maintaining a [[creative-log]] to capture insights outside normal patterns
- Considering alternative possibilities that feel counterintuitive

## Connections to Related Concepts

De Bono's "zooming out" technique connects to the [[inventors-paradox]] — the counterintuitive finding that more ambitious problem formulations can sometimes be easier to solve than restricted ones.

This concept also connects to [[seeing-the-unseen]], which focuses on observing and interpreting hidden aspects of problems, and to [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]], which uses inverse reasoning from goal to given. Both approaches share the goal of escaping narrow, default framings to discover overlooked dimensions of a problem.
