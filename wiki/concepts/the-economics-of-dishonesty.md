# The Economics of Dishonesty

The economic perspective on dishonesty examines how financial incentives, self-interest, and fairness motivations interact to shape lying and cheating behavior in market transactions and organizational settings.

## Core Ideas

- People balance rational self-interest against fairness motivations when deciding whether to be dishonest.
- Financial incentives can significantly influence honesty—higher stakes change the calculus of cheating.
- Dishonesty is not purely a moral failing but is partly driven by economic conditions and incentive structures.
- Organizations can design reward systems that either promote or discourage dishonest behavior.

## Connection to Decision Quality

This concept extends [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by showing that the decision environment—particularly incentive structures—shapes whether people act honestly or dishonestly, independent of their moral character. It connects to [[prospect-theory]] through the idea that people evaluate dishonest gains and losses asymmetrically.
