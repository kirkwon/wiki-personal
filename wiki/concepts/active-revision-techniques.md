# Active Revision Techniques

Active revision techniques are study methods that require the learner to engage deeply with material rather than passively consuming it. Research summarized in *Make It Stick* and other cognitive science sources demonstrates that strategies involving effortful retrieval, spaced practice, and elaboration produce significantly better long-term retention than re-reading, highlighting, or cramming.

## Evidence-Based Methods
- **Retrieval practice**: Actively recalling information from memory strengthens memory more than passive review. This is the single most effective evidence-based learning strategy.
- **Spaced repetition**: Distributing study sessions over time outperforms massed study (cramming) for long-term retention.
- **Elaborative interrogation**: Asking "why" questions about new information deepens encoding and integration with existing knowledge.
- **Feynman Technique**: Teaching material in simple terms exposes gaps in understanding and builds mastery.
- **Interleaving**: Mixing different topics or types of problems during study sessions improves the ability to discriminate between concepts and apply knowledge flexibly.
