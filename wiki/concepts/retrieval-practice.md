# Retrieval Practice

Retrieval practice is the act of actively recalling information from memory rather than passively re-reading or reviewing it. Research by Karpicke, Roediger, and others has consistently shown that the effort of retrieving information strengthens memory pathways and improves long-term retention more effectively than any passive study method.

## Key Principles
- **Active recall over passive review**: Testing yourself or trying to recall information without looking at notes produces stronger memory traces than re-reading highlights or summaries.
- **Difficulty is productive**: Struggling to retrieve information is not a sign of failure but a signal that learning is occurring. The effort itself strengthens encoding.
- **Retrieval as a learning event**: Each successful retrieval reinforces the memory and makes future retrieval easier.

## Practical Applications
- Use flashcards or self-quizzing instead of re-reading notes.
- Close the book and try to recall the main points before reviewing.
- Apply retrieval practice across spaced intervals rather than in single cramming sessions.
