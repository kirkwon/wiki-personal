# Validated Learning

Validated learning is the core epistemology of the lean startup methodology. It refers to learning that comes from structured experiments—testing hypotheses with real customers—rather than from intuition, experience, or vanity metrics.

In the lean startup framework, every product feature, marketing campaign, or business decision is treated as a hypothesis. The team builds an MVP to test the hypothesis, measures the result, and learns whether the hypothesis was correct. This cycle produces validated learning: knowledge that is grounded in evidence from real customer behavior.

Validated learning differs from traditional metrics in that it focuses on whether the team is making progress toward its goals, not just whether it is producing output. Innovation accounting provides the measurement framework for tracking validated learning progress.

This concept closely parallels probabilistic thinking approaches. Just as superforecasters update their beliefs based on new evidence [[superforecasting-methodology]], and as Annie Duke advocates treating decisions as bets to be updated [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]], validated learning treats startup hypotheses as bets that must be tested and revised.

Key principles:
- Learning must be grounded in evidence from real customers
- Vanity metrics (page views, downloads, etc.) are not validated learning
- Actionable metrics reveal whether the team is making real progress
- The goal is not to build things but to learn what to build
