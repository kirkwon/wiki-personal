# Randomness as a mental model

Randomness is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Definition

As a mental model, randomness serves as a universal framework for recognizing when outcomes are driven by chance rather than causation. It prompts us to question apparent patterns, resist narrative fallacies, and account for stochastic effects in our reasoning about complex systems.

## Key Insights

- We tend to see patterns where none exist and underestimate the role of randomness in outcomes
- Success can often be attributed to luck rather than skill, and failure to bad luck rather than poor decisions
- This model connects to [[antifragility]] and [[black-swan-events]] — Taleb's broader exploration of how we misunderstand uncertainty
- It relates to [[prospect-theory]] and [[biases]], since our cognitive biases reinforce the illusion of control and narrative coherence
- The model has applications in [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]] and contrasts with approaches like [[superforecasting]] that emphasize probabilistic thinking
