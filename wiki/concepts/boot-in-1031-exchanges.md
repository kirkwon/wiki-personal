# Boot in 1031 Exchanges

**Boot** refers to cash or mortgage relief received during a 1031 exchange that triggers a taxable event. Boot is subject to capital gains taxation at standard rates.

## Cash Boot
**Definition**: Cash received by taxpayer in exchange
**Tax Implications**: Boot is taxable at capital gains rates
**Examples**:
- Net proceeds after acquisition
- Cash received in addition to replacement property
- Mortgage relief not fully replaced

## Mortgage Boot
**Definition**: Debt relief that exceeds new debt assumption
**Tax Implications**: Subject to capital gains taxation
**Calculation**:
```
Mortgage Boot = Old Mortgage - New Mortgage
Taxable Amount = Mortgage Boot × Capital Gain Percentage
```

## Avoiding Boot
**Strategies**:
- Replace 100% of debt with new debt
- Use all equity to acquire higher value properties
- Take minimal cash distributions
- Structure exchanges as reverse exchanges if needed
