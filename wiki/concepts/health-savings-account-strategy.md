# Health Savings Account Strategy

The HSA is the most tax-efficient vehicle for managing healthcare costs in retirement, offering a triple tax advantage.

## Triple Tax Advantage
1. **Contributions**: Tax-deductible (or pre-tax if payroll)
2. **Growth**: Tax-free
3. **Withdrawals**: Tax-free for qualified medical expenses

## HSA Eligibility and Limits (2024)
- Must have High Deductible Health Plan (HDHP)
- **Individual**: $4,150 | **Family**: $8,300
- **Age 55+ catch-up**: $1,000
- **HDHP deductible**: Individual ≥$1,600 | Family ≥$3,200
- **Max out-of-pocket**: Individual $8,050 | Family $16,100

## HSA in Retirement
- **After 65**: Can withdraw for any reason (non-medical taxed as income)
- **Before 65**: 20% penalty + income tax for non-medical withdrawals
- **No Required Minimum Distributions** (unlike traditional IRA/401k)
- **Can pay Medicare premiums** (Parts B, D, Medicare Advantage) tax-free
- **Cannot pay Medigap premiums** tax-free

## HSA Investment Strategy
1. **Build cash reserve**: Keep 1-2 years of estimated out-of-pocket costs
2. **Invest long-term**: Growth portion in low-cost index funds
3. **Reimburse yourself**: Pay medical expenses out-of-pocket, save receipts, reimburse from HSA years later
4. **Designate beneficiaries**: Spouse can treat as their own HSA; non-spouse gets taxable distribution

## HSA vs. FSA Comparison
| Feature | HSA | FSA |
|---------|-----|-----|
| Ownership | Individual | Employer |
| Rollover | Yes (year to year) | No (use-it-or-lose-it) |
| Investment | Yes | No |
| Contribution Change | Anytime | Open enrollment only |
| Portability | Yes | No |
| HDHP Required | Yes | No |
