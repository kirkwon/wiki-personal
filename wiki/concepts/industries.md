# Industries

Industries is a mental model that functions as a conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern and relationship recognition. It provides a cognitive shortcut for making sense of patterns, power dynamics, cycles, and relationships across economic, organizational, and societal domains.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

## Core Idea

Rather than analyzing individual companies or events in isolation, framing situations through the lens of "industries" reveals structural patterns that recur across very different contexts. This includes recognizing how industries rise and fall, how power shifts between players, and how systematic approaches can improve execution within them.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

## Cross-Source Perspectives

### Post-COVID Reshuffling (Scott Galloway)
Galloway argues that crises like COVID fundamentally reshuffle industry power. Industries that were disrupted must be understood not just as collections of companies but as ecosystems where competitive positions are redefined by external shocks.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

### Long-Cycle Macro Dynamics (Ray Dalio)
Dalio's framework for understanding how rising and emerging powers reshape industries over long cycles. This perspective emphasizes that industry structures are not static—they follow predictable patterns of ascent, peak, and decline tied to broader economic and geopolitical forces.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

### Innovation and New Industry Creation (Peter Thiel & Blake Masters)
From *Zero to One*, the insight that truly valuable startups must create new industries rather than compete within existing ones. This reframes "industry" not just as a market category but as something that can be invented.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

### Systematic Execution (Atul Gawande)
Gawande's checklist approach demonstrates that within any industry, standardizing complex operations through checklists dramatically improves outcomes. This operational lens shows that industries are also domains where systematic thinking reduces error.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

## Relationship to Other Mental Models

Industries connects to [[markets]] as a mental model, which focuses on economic patterns and relationships. It also relates to [[business]] as a mental model, though Industries emphasizes the structural and cyclical nature of industry-level analysis over individual business decisions.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

The model is distinct from [[decision-making-frameworks]] in that it provides an external lens for pattern recognition rather than a process for making decisions. However, it complements frameworks like [[building-a-cohesive-team]] by showing how systematic approaches improve execution within complex industry environments.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]

## Open Questions

Is "Industries" distinct enough from [[markets]] or [[business]] as mental models, or is it redundant? The common thread across these four sources is using industry-level analysis as a cognitive shortcut, but the breadth of the model—from macro cycles to operational checklists—raises questions about whether it is a coherent abstraction or a forced one.^[raw/articles/Industries.md]
