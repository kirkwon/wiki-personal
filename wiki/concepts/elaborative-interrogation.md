# Elaborative Interrogation

Elaborative interrogation is a learning strategy where you ask yourself "why" questions about new material to deepen your understanding and strengthen memory encoding. Instead of passively absorbing information, you actively connect it to what you already know, creating richer associative networks. Rather than passively absorbing material, the learner actively seeks explanations and connections.

## How It Works

When you encounter new information, pause and ask: Why is this true? Why does this matter? How does this relate to what I already know? These questions force you to process the material more deeply, transforming surface-level facts into meaningful connections.

### Key Principles
- **Ask "why"**: For each new fact or concept, ask why it is true or how it relates to what you already know.
- **Connect to prior knowledge**: Linking new information to existing understanding creates richer memory representations.
- **Use concrete examples**: Anchoring abstract concepts with specific examples makes them easier to recall and apply.

## Evidence

[[make-it-stick---peter-c-brown-et-al.md]] identifies elaborative interrogation as one of the most effective study techniques, supported by cognitive psychology research. It outperforms passive review and even some forms of active practice because it engages the learner in constructing explanations.

## Practical Application

- After reading a paragraph, ask yourself why the author makes this claim
- While reading, pause and ask yourself why a particular claim is true
- When learning a new concept, relate it to something you already understand
- Use concrete examples to anchor abstract ideas
- Generate your own examples for abstract principles
- Teach the concept to someone else by explaining the "why" behind it
- Discuss material with others, explaining the reasoning behind key ideas
