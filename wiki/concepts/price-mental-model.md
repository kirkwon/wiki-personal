# Price as a mental model

Price is a universal mental model that functions as a conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern recognition. It helps interpret how value, cost, and perception interact to shape decisions and judgments.

## Definition

Price provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world. It is applicable across all domains and helps identify how pricing cues distort thinking.

## Core Insights from Dan Ariely

Dan Ariely's research in Predictably Irrational demonstrates several key phenomena:

- **Anchoring**: Initial price exposures set reference points that influence subsequent valuations, even when the anchor is arbitrary.
- **Zero-price effect**: The word "free" triggers disproportionate behavioral responses, overriding rational cost-benefit analysis.
- **Decoy pricing**: Introducing a third, inferior option can shift choices between two target options in predictable ways.
- **Arbitrary coherence**: Once an initial price is established, people adjust their valuations to be consistent with it, even when the initial price had no rational basis.

## Why It Matters

Price as a mental model highlights the gap between perceived value and actual cost. Recognizing these patterns helps navigate situations where pricing cues are being used—deliberately or not—to influence decisions. It connects to broader decision-making concepts like [[framing-effects]], [[prospect-theory]], and [[cognitive-biases-library]].
