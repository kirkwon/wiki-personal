# Decisions

Decisions is a universal mental model — a conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern recognition and mental shortcuts. Unlike specific decision frameworks such as [[prospect-theory]] or the [[kelly-criterion]], Decisions functions as an umbrella principle that encompasses the broader habit of treating decisions as a lens for understanding relationships and patterns in the world.

The act of selecting among available options. Decisions are central to human agency but become psychologically costly when option sets are too large.

## Definition

Decisions provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex situations. It is applicable across all domains — from personal finance to cooking to photography to team dynamics — serving as a meta-level cognitive tool.

## Key Insight

The quality of a decision is not determined by the number of options considered, but by the alignment of the chosen option with one's values and the acceptance of trade-offs.^[The Paradox of Choice - Barry Schwartz.md]

## Practical Framework

- Define what matters before evaluating options.
- Set a stopping rule for information gathering.
- Accept the cost of forgone alternatives as inevitable.
- Track outcomes to improve future decision processes.^[The Paradox of Choice - Barry Schwartz.md]

## Relationship to Existing Decision-Making Concepts

This model differs from [[decision-making-frameworks]] and [[systematic-decision-making]] in that it is not a procedural method but rather a conceptual orientation. Where [[thinking-in-bets]] focuses on probabilistic thinking and [[superforecasting]] emphasizes intellectual humility and diverse perspectives, Decisions frames the entire domain of choice as a universal mental model.

## Open Questions

The definition remains somewhat vague — "mental shortcut or framework" could encompass almost any mental model. A key distinction from general [[problem-solving]] or systematic approaches needs sharpening. Additionally, the model draws predominantly from Western behavioral economics authors, raising questions about universality.
