# Checklists

Checklists is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Definition

Checklists function as a universal mental model—applicable across domains—for managing complexity and reducing error. Rather than relying solely on memory or intuition, structured checklists offer systematic, simple tools that handle multi-variable situations more effectively.

## Core Claim

Systematic, simple tools can handle complexity better than reliance on memory or intuition alone. This is supported by evidence from Atul Gawande's research on surgical checklists, which demonstrated measurable reductions in complications and deaths in medical settings.

## Applicability

The model is tagged as universal, applicable across general domains. It functions analogously to other mental models in the wiki such as [[recipes-mental-model]], [[Solutions]], and [[planning]]—all serve as universal conceptual tools for navigating complexity through pattern and relationship recognition.

## Connection to Atul Gawande

This concept is grounded in [[the-checklist-manifesto---atul-gawande]], where Gawande argues that checklists improve performance in medicine, aviation, and other high-stakes fields. This connects to his broader work on end-of-life care documented in [[being-mortal]].
