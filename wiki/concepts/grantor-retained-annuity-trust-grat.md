# Grantor Retained Annuity Trust (GRAT)

An irrevocable trust where the settlor receives annuity payments for a fixed term, then remaining assets pass to beneficiaries at discounted value for estate tax purposes.

## Structure

- Settlor retains right to annuity payments
- Fixed term (usually 2-10 years)
- Annuity amount based on IRS Section 7520 rate
- Remainder interest passes to beneficiaries at term end
- Zeroed-Out GRAT has no remainder value for maximum tax benefit

## Tax Benefits

- Appreciation passes tax-free to beneficiaries
- Assets removed from taxable estate
- Uses annual gift tax exemption
- Small gift enables large wealth transfer
- Beneficiaries bear investment risk

## Implementation Considerations

- Term selection based on appreciation expectations
- High-growth potential assets preferred
- IRS 7520 rate considerations
- Proper funding timing to maximize benefit
- Compliance with GRAT documentation requirements
