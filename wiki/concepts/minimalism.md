# Minimalism

Minimalism is a universal conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern recognition. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world, applicable across domains rather than limited to any single area such as digital life. In photography, minimalism is the practice of simplifying composition to essential elements, removing distractions, and letting negative space and clean lines create impact.

## Definition

Minimalism functions as a mental model — a principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations by identifying the essential patterns and stripping away noise. It is closely related to other mental models in the wiki such as [[planning]], [[Solutions]], [[stillness]], [[care]], [[environment]], and [[settings]], all of which share the framing of navigating complexity through pattern and relationship recognition.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

Cal Newport's [[Digital-Minimalism---Cal-Newport]] grounds this model in a specific, practical application: deliberately choosing technologies and practices that align with personal values. However, the core principle of minimalism as a mental model extends beyond digital life to any domain where reducing unnecessary complexity improves clarity and decision-making.

## Techniques in Photography

1. Remove distractions: Keep only what is necessary
2. Use simple backgrounds: Plain walls, sky, water
3. Focus on one main subject: Clear focal point
4. Employ negative space: Lots of empty space
5. Use clean lines: Straight, simple shapes

## Examples

- Person against a white wall: Minimal portrait
- Single leaf on a table: Simple still life
- Horizon line alone: Minimal landscape
