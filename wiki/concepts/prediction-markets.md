# Prediction Markets

Prediction markets are mechanisms that allow participants to bet on outcomes, producing aggregated probability estimates that reflect the collective knowledge of participants. They are forecasting mechanisms that aggregate diverse opinions into probability estimates for future events. Unlike traditional expert opinion polls, they incentivize participants to reveal their true beliefs through betting mechanisms, which can produce more accurate forecasts. They are one of the primary tools for harnessing crowd wisdom.

## How They Work

Prediction markets function similarly to financial markets but with contracts based on event outcomes rather than asset prices. Participants trade contracts tied to future events (elections, sales figures, project completion dates). When many participants with diverse information and perspectives trade these contracts, the resulting market price converges on an accurate probability estimate. Prices emerge from supply and demand, encoding the market's aggregated probability estimate. Because participants have financial incentives to trade accurately, prediction markets tend to produce well-calibrated forecasts.

## Advantages Over Traditional Forecasting

- **Diversity of opinion**: Markets aggregate insights from participants with different backgrounds and expertise.
- **Financial incentives**: Traders are motivated to provide accurate assessments to profit from correct predictions, incentivizing honest information sharing.
- **Continuous updating**: Market prices adjust in real time as new information emerges, making the mechanism self-correcting.
- **Resistance to groupthink**: Unlike expert panels, markets do not suffer from social pressure or authority bias, offering a structured way to aggregate diverse perspectives without falling into groupthink.
- **Probability estimates**: Produce probability estimates rather than binary predictions.
- **Efficient aggregation**: Aggregate dispersed knowledge efficiently.

## Relationship to Superforecasting

Tetlock and Gardner explore prediction markets in Chapter 5 of *Superforecasting* as a method that demonstrates efficiency in generating accurate predictions by combining many independent judgments. They highlight prediction markets as an alternative forecasting method that demonstrates efficiency in generating accurate predictions by aggregating diverse opinions. This approach aligns with the superforecaster emphasis on perspective diversity and probabilistic thinking. The chapter contrasts this approach with traditional expert polling and shows how market-based aggregation can outperform both.

## Strengths

- Incentivize honest information sharing
- Aggregate dispersed knowledge efficiently
- Produce probability estimates rather than binary predictions
- Self-correcting as new information arrives
- Offer a structured way to aggregate diverse perspectives without falling into groupthink

## Limitations

- Require sufficient participation to avoid illiquidity
- Susceptible to manipulation and speculation
- May reflect crowd biases if diversity and independence conditions are not met
- Not suitable for problems with no objective outcome
