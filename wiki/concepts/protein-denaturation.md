# Protein Denaturation

Protein denaturation is the process by which proteins lose their native structure due to heat, acid, or mechanical action. This transformation is fundamental to cooking, as it affects texture, solubility, color, flavor, and the ability of proteins to form new structures. When proteins denature, their folded three-dimensional structures unfold, exposing amino acid chains. This allows enzymes and heat to break peptide bonds, changing texture, color, and flavor.

## Role in Cooking
- **Heat application** (searing, braising, boiling) causes proteins to denature and coagulate
- **Acid** (lemon juice, vinegar) can denature proteins without heat, as in ceviche
- **Mechanical action** (kneading, mixing) can partially denature proteins, affecting gluten development in bread and dough

## Connection to Other Concepts
Protein denaturation underlies many cooking techniques, including [[searing]] (where surface proteins coagulate creating a crust), [[braising]] (where long cooking breaks down tough proteins), and [[maillard-reaction]] (which occurs on denatured protein surfaces). Denaturation occurs during searing, braising, and other heat-based techniques, and is closely related to the [[maillard-reaction]] which develops flavor on denatured protein surfaces.
