# The Economics of Happiness

Thaler's chapter on the economics of happiness challenges the assumption that more money always equals more happiness. Drawing on hedonic adaptation, relative comparison theory, and the Easterlin paradox, the chapter shows that income above basic needs has diminishing returns on well-being.

## Core Findings

- After basic needs are met, additional income has minimal effect on happiness
- Social comparison and relative income matter more than absolute levels
- People adapt to both increases and decreases in income
- Non-financial factors (relationships, purpose, health) drive sustained happiness

This connects to [[hedonic-adaptation]] and the [[easterlin-paradox]], and challenges the pursuit of wealth as a primary life strategy.
