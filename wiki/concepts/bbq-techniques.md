# BBQ Techniques

Core BBQ techniques center on low-and-slow smoking, dry rub application, and regional variations in finishing methods.

## Low-and-Slow Smoking

The foundation of BBQ: smoking meat at 225°F (107°C) for 12-18 hours. This slow process breaks down connective tissue and develops deep smoke flavor. Common mistakes include rushing the cook time and not letting meat rest after cooking (30-60 minutes for juice redistribution).

## Dry Rub

A dry rub is a mixture of salt, pepper, and spices applied to meat before smoking. Texas and Memphis BBQ rely heavily on dry rubs:
- Salt, black pepper, garlic powder, cayenne (Texas brisket)
- Salt, brown sugar, paprika, black pepper, garlic powder, onion powder, chili powder (Memphis ribs)

Rub is typically applied 12-24 hours before cooking.

## Burnt Ends

A Kansas City specialty: the fatty point of the brisket is cubed, resmoked with sauce, and caramelized until crispy. This technique transforms the less uniform portion of the brisket into a sought-after dish.

## Common Mistakes

- Not letting meat rest (rest 30-60 minutes)
- Rushing low-and-slow cooking (plan 12-18 hours)
- Using too much sauce (let meat shine; sauce is optional)
- Not trimming brisket fat cap (trim to 1/4 inch for proper rendering)
