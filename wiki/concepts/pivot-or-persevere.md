# Pivot or Persevere

Pivot or Persevere is the decision framework at the heart of the lean startup methodology. After running Build-Measure-Learn cycles and accumulating validated learning, a team must decide: should we change direction (pivot) or stay the course (persevere)?

A **pivot** is a structured change in strategy—keeping one foot grounded in what has been learned while making a fundamental change in the other areas of the business. It is not a failure; it is a recognition that initial hypotheses were wrong and that a new direction is warranted by the evidence.

A **persevere** decision means the evidence supports continuing with the current strategy. The team should double down on what is working.

Key principles:
- Base the decision on validated learning, not intuition or sunk cost
- A pivot without learning is just chaos; persevere without learning is just stubbornness
- Distinguish between "we haven't learned enough yet" and "the evidence says change direction"
- Experimentation should not become procrastination—there is a point where more data does not reduce uncertainty

This framework connects to broader decision-making under uncertainty. It is essentially a bet-sizing and evidence-update problem: how much evidence is needed to change course, and how do you avoid confirmation bias in interpreting results? This mirrors the decision-tracking and probabilistic thinking approaches in [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] and the superforecasting methodology [[superforecasting-methodology]].
