# Deception Tactics

Strategic misdirection as a tool for creating advantage. From *The Art of War*, deception is a core element of stratagem—making yourself seem weak when strong, and strong when weak, to confuse and mislead opponents.

## Key Ideas
- **Appear weak when strong**: Lure the enemy into overconfidence.
- **Appear strong when weak**: Deter premature attack.
- **All warfare is based on deception** (Chapter 1): This is one of the most quoted lines from the text.
- **Feints and misdirection**: Create false movements to draw enemy attention away from the real attack.

## Connections
Connects to [[strategic-bluffing]] (bluffing in competitive contexts), [[intelligence-gathering]] (deception is most effective against poorly informed opponents), and [[enemy]] (understanding what the opponent expects). Also relates to [[framing-effects]]—how situations are framed shapes perception and action.

## Tension
Deception assumes adversarial framing. The wiki's ethical orientation (via [[skin-in-the-game]] and [[the-infinite-game---simon-sinek]]) emphasizes transparency and trust. The source's "when to stop" guidance explicitly warns against strategy becoming manipulation or unethical.
