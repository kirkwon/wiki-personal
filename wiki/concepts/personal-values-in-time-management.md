# Personal Values in Time Management

Covey argues that time management choices must be grounded in individual values, principles, and beliefs. Without this foundation, productivity becomes hollow—efficient but misdirected. The question is not merely "how do I do more?" but "what am I doing this for?"

This values-driven approach informs the [[first-things-first-principle]]: prioritization should reflect what matters most, not what pressures are loudest. It also shapes the [[time-management-matrix]] by determining which activities qualify as "important" in the first place. When values are clear, Quadrant 2 investment becomes obvious; when they are not, everything feels urgent and nothing feels meaningful.

This concept connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by emphasizing that the quality of a decision process includes alignment with personal values, not just logical consistency. It also raises an open question for the wiki: how do values-based prioritization and probabilistic approaches (like [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]]) integrate?
