# Luck and Risk Duality

Success equals hard work plus luck. Failure equals poor decisions plus risk. These two sides are mirror images of the same underlying reality — chance plays a larger role in outcomes than most people acknowledge.

## Examples

Bill Gates was extremely smart AND lucky — born in 1955 with access to a computer at a critical time.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md] The Vietnam War draft demonstrates how birth month alone determined life outcomes.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Implications

- Be cautious about judging others' financial decisions, since luck heavily influences outcomes.
- Recognize luck in your own success.
- Focus on decisions you control, not outcomes.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Connection to Survivorship Bias

Focusing only on successful outcomes while ignoring failures (survivorship bias) makes it easy to attribute success entirely to skill and failure entirely to poor decisions.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md] Looking at the whole population reveals the role of chance.

## Connection to Decision Quality

This duality reinforces the principle of separating decision quality from outcome quality [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]]. Good process plus bad luck does not equal a bad decision, and bad process plus good luck does not equal a good decision.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]
