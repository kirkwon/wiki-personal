# Process Praise vs. Person Praise

A distinction central to Dweck's mindset research. The type of praise given to individuals — particularly children — shapes their implicit theory of ability.

## Process Praise
- Praising effort, strategies, focus, and persistence
- Attributes success to effort and strategy ("You worked really hard on that problem")
- Emphasizes what the person did and how they approached the task
- Encourages a growth mindset by linking success to controllable actions
- Reinforces that outcomes result from controllable effort and strategy
- Encourages persistence, learning from failure, and willingness to take on challenges

## Person Praise
- Praising innate intelligence, talent, or being "smart" ("You're so smart")
- Attributes success to innate trait
- Emphasizes fixed traits rather than effort
- Can foster a fixed mindset, leading to avoidance of challenges and fragility in the face of failure
- Implies fixed traits, making individuals avoid challenges to protect their self-image
- Can lead to avoidance of difficulty and fragility when facing setbacks

## How It Works
- Research shows children receiving process praise are more willing to take on difficult tasks and persist through failure.

## Implications
- **Parenting** — Parents who praise effort and strategy help children develop growth mindsets and resilience.
- **Education** — Teachers who provide process-oriented feedback promote deeper learning and collaboration, and can shift feedback language to emphasize process.
- **Organizations** — Feedback cultures that recognize effort and improvement over innate talent support continuous development; managers can praise team behaviors and strategies rather than individual "genius."
- **Personal relationships** — Acknowledging effort and approach strengthens growth-oriented dynamics.

## Connection to Growth Mindset
Effective praise is a key mechanism through which growth mindset is nurtured. By consistently reinforcing effort and strategy, individuals learn to value the process of learning over the appearance of ability. This connects to [[effective-listening]] and [[communication-skills]] — the way we give feedback shapes the recipient's relationship with challenge and learning.
