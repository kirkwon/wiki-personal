# Social Security Taxation

Social Security benefits can be partially taxable depending on total income. Understanding how benefits are taxed is essential for avoiding the "Social Security tax torpedo," where increased income from other sources triggers taxation of benefits that would otherwise be tax-free.

## Taxation Rules

- Up to **50%** of benefits taxable if provisional income exceeds $25,000 (single) / $32,000 (joint).
- Up to **85%** of benefits taxable if provisional income exceeds $34,000 (single) / $44,000 (joint).

## The Provisional Income Formula

```text
Provisional Income = Adjusted Gross Income + Tax-Free Interest + 50% of Social Security Benefits
```

## The Torpedo Effect

When provisional income crosses thresholds:
- **50% torpedo**: 50 cents of each dollar above the threshold is taxed.
- **85% torpedo**: 85 cents of each dollar above the threshold is taxed.
- This creates effective tax rates up to 46.25%.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Interaction with Other Income

Social Security taxation is particularly dangerous because it interacts with other income sources:
- Roth conversions increase AGI, which increases provisional income.
- RMDs add to AGI, potentially triggering Social Security taxation.
- Investment income (including tax-free interest) contributes to provisional income.
- The cascade effect means a single decision (e.g., a large Roth conversion) can trigger multiple tax increases simultaneously.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Avoidance Strategies

- Keep Roth conversions below provisional income thresholds.
- Time capital gains strategically to avoid threshold crossings.
- Use tax-advantaged investments to reduce AGI.
- Manage Required Minimum Distributions carefully.
