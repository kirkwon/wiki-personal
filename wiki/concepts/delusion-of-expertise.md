# Delusion of Expertise

The delusion of expertise describes how experts are prone to believing in their own infallibility, often ignoring randomness as an essential factor in shaping outcomes. Taleb argues that domain knowledge creates an illusion of control that masks the role of chance in determining results. It is the tendency for experts to overconfidently believe in their predictive ability while ignoring the role of randomness in shaping outcomes.

## Definition

Experts develop deep knowledge in their domains but can become overconfident, believing their expertise grants them the ability to predict outcomes. In reality, randomness plays a larger role than most experts acknowledge.

## Overview

Experts — in finance, forecasting, medicine, and other fields — systematically overestimate their ability to predict future events. They attribute outcomes to their skill while attributing failures to bad luck, creating an asymmetric view of their own competence.

## Practical Implications

- Be skeptical of expert predictions, especially in domains dominated by randomness.
- Distinguish between domains where expertise is reliable and domains where randomness dominates.
- [[decision-tracking]] helps calibrate expert judgment over time by examining actual track records.
