# Credit Cycle

The credit cycle describes the recurring pattern of credit expansion and contraction that drives economic activity. In Ray Dalio's economic machine framework, credit cycles are one of the fundamental forces shaping economies, alongside market forces, central bank actions, and government policy.

Credit expansion occurs when lenders are willing to lend and borrowers are willing to borrow, increasing spending and driving economic growth. When debts grow faster than income, the cycle reverses—credit tightens, spending slows, and the economy contracts. Central banks manage these cycles through interest rate adjustments and monetary policy.

Understanding credit cycles is essential for making informed investment and wealth management decisions, as they create predictable patterns in economic outcomes.
