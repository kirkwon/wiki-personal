# Anchoring

The cognitive bias where initial reference points (anchors) disproportionately influence subsequent judgments and valuations. Ariely demonstrates that our perception of value is shaped by initial prices and anchors, challenging the traditional economic view of market-driven price formation.

## How It Works

Once an anchor is established — whether through a suggested price, a first offer in negotiation, or an initial data point — subsequent evaluations are pulled toward that anchor. The anchor need not be rational or relevant; its mere presence distorts judgment.

## Applications

- Recognizing when initial pricing shapes your perception of value
- Negotiating by setting the first number
- Designing pricing strategies that account for anchoring effects
- Auditing past decisions for anchoring bias by removing context from value assessments

## Connection to Relativity

Anchoring is a specific mechanism through which Ariely's broader principle of relativity operates — we value options relative to comparisons, and the anchor is often the most powerful comparison we encounter.
