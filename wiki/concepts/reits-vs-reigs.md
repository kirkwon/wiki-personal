# REITs vs REIGs

REITs (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and REIGs (Real Estate Investment Groups) are two different structures for investing in real estate, each with distinct characteristics.

## REITs

**Definition**: Companies that own or finance income-producing real estate, traded like stocks on public exchanges.

**Types**:
- **Equity REITs**: Own properties (office, retail, residential)
- **Mortgage REITs**: Finance mortgages (riskier)
- **Hybrid REITs**: Both ownership and financing

**Pros**:
- Liquid (trade daily like stocks)
- Diversified across many properties
- Regular dividends (required to distribute 90% of taxable income)
- Professional management

**Cons**:
- Interest rate sensitivity
- Leverage on balance sheet
- Management fees
- Limited control over individual properties

## REIGs

**Definition**: Group of investors pooling money for specific real estate deals, typically private.

**Pros**:
- Access to larger deals than individual investors
- Professional sponsor management
- Passive investment option

**Cons**:
- Illiquid (locked up 3-7 years)
- Limited liquidity
- Sponsor risk
- Fees

## Comparison Table

| Dimension | REITs | REIGs |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Liquidity | High (daily trading) | Low (3-7 year lockup) |
| Diversification | Broad (many properties) | Narrow (specific deals) |
| Minimum Investment | Low (stock purchase) | Higher ($1k-$100k+) |
| Management | Professional public management | Sponsor-dependent |
| Dividends | Regular (90% distribution required) | Deal-dependent |
| Risk Profile | Market and interest rate risk | Illiquidity and sponsor risk |
