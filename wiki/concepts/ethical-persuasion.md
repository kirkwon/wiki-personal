# Ethics of Persuasion

The ethics of persuasion addresses the distinction between legitimate influence and manipulative exploitation. Cialdini draws a line between helpful pre-suasion — using environmental cues and framing to make communication more effective — and manipulative practices that exploit cognitive vulnerabilities without the audience's informed consent.

## Core Tension

Effective persuasion often works by bypassing deliberate evaluation, leveraging heuristics and biases. The ethical question is whether the persuader is respecting the audience's autonomy or covertly exploiting their cognitive limitations.

## Cialdini's Position

Cialdini argues that ethical pre-suasion should:
- Not deceive or mislead about the nature of the request
- Respect the audience's ability to make informed choices
- Distinguish between helping someone see a beneficial option and pressuring them into a harmful one
