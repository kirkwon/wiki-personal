# Pre-Suasion

Pre-suasion is a concept introduced by Robert B. Cialdini in his book [[pre-suasion-a-revolutionary-way-to-influence-and-persuade]]. It refers to the process of priming and setting up an audience's mindset before delivering a persuasion message. Rather than focusing solely on the content of a persuasion attempt, pre-suasion targets the moment preceding it — framing, priming, and attention management — to make the subsequent message more effective.

The moment before a message matters as much as the message itself. By strategically allocating attention, creating favorable associations, and managing cognitive load, a persuader can dramatically increase the likelihood that their audience will be receptive to what comes next.

## Key Mechanisms

- **Attention Priming**: Directing or framing what a person attends to before a request, which biases subsequent judgments and decisions. This includes directing focus toward a particular concept or value before making a request.
- **Environmental Framing**: Using context, physical space, timing, or conceptual context to shape receptivity and prime favorable interpretations of the upcoming message.
- **Temporal Placement**: Choosing the right moment to introduce a message based on the audience's cognitive state.
- **Cognitive Economy**: Minimizing cognitive load so the audience is not overwhelmed, keeping mental resources available for processing the persuasion.
- **Rapport and Trust**: Building connection and likability before making a request, creating a favorable interpersonal foundation.

## Connection to Other Concepts

Pre-suasion extends beyond the six principles of influence by focusing on what happens before the persuasion attempt itself. It relates closely to [[dual-process-theory]] — the idea that automatic, heuristic-driven processing can be primed before deliberate evaluation occurs. It also connects to [[attention-budget]] and [[cognitive-economy]], as managing mental resources before persuasion is central. It leverages unconscious cognitive processes to shape decisions before deliberate evaluation, connecting to [[framing-effects]] and [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]]. The distinction between legitimate pre-suasion and manipulation maps onto [[ethical-design-manipulation-question]].
