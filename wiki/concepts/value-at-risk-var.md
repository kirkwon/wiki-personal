# Value at Risk (VaR)

A quantitative risk modeling technique that estimates the maximum expected loss on a portfolio over a specified time horizon at a given confidence level.

## How VaR Works

- **Time Horizon**: Typically 1 year or 5 years
- **Confidence Level**: Commonly 95% or 99%
- **Calculation**: Maximum loss that will not be exceeded within the given confidence level

**Example**: A 95% VaR of $500,000 means that 95% of the time, losses will not exceed $500,000 in one year. A 99% VaR of $1,000,000 means 99% of the time, losses will not exceed $1,000,000 in one year.

## Applications

- **Capital Allocation**: Determine appropriate capital reserves
- **Position Sizing**: Limit exposure to individual properties
- **Risk Budgeting**: Allocate risk across portfolio

VaR provides a single-number summary of downside risk that supports data-driven investment decisions. It complements [[monte-carlo-simulation]] by providing a single summary metric, while Monte Carlo produces full probability distributions of outcomes.
