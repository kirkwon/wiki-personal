# Superforecasting

Superforecasting refers to the ability to make consistently accurate probabilistic predictions across uncertain domains. Based on research from the Good Judgment Project and central to Philip E. Tetlock and Dan Gardner's book *Superforecasting*, superforecasters consistently outperform traditional experts, average forecasters, and even sophisticated algorithms by combining probabilistic thinking, open-mindedness, intellectual humility, and diverse perspectives. The key insight is that this skill is learnable — it stems from specific mental habits and techniques rather than innate genius.

## Core Traits of Superforecasters

- **Open-mindedness**: Willingness to consider alternative explanations, change their minds, and update beliefs when new evidence emerges.
- **Intellectual humility**: Recognition of the limits of one's own knowledge and the uncertainty inherent in complex systems.
- **Probabilistic thinking**: Comfort with expressing uncertainty probabilistically, framing predictions in terms of probabilities rather than certainties, enabling calibration and continuous refinement.
- **Perspective diversity**: Seeking input from people with different backgrounds, viewpoints, and areas of expertise to counteract groupthink and blind spots.
- **Habit of considering multiple competing hypotheses**: Systematically generating and evaluating several possible explanations for an event rather than anchoring on a single narrative.
- **Active effort to seek disconfirming evidence**: Challenging one's own assumptions and avoiding confirmation bias.

## Key Techniques

- **Multiple hypotheses**: Systematically generating and evaluating several possible explanations for an event rather than anchoring on a single narrative.
- **Avoiding confirmation bias**: Actively seeking disconfirming evidence and challenging one's own assumptions.
- **Seeking diverse perspectives**: Drawing on a wide pool of experts and laypeople to aggregate insights.
- **Base rate reasoning**: Using historical frequencies and statistical priors as a starting point before incorporating specific evidence.
- **Updating beliefs incrementally**: Revising probability estimates as new information becomes available, rather than waiting for definitive outcomes.

## Prediction Markets

Prediction markets represent an alternative forecasting method that aggregates diverse opinions into a single outcome. Research shows they can generate more accurate predictions than traditional expert opinion polls by leveraging the wisdom of crowds and financial incentives for accuracy.

## Relationship to Other Decision Frameworks

Superforecasting connects to [[bayesian-thinking]] through its emphasis on updating probabilities with new evidence. It provides a concrete methodology for managing uncertainty that complements the broader framework in [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] and the bias-awareness discussed in [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]]. It also relates to [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]], [[systematic-decision-making]] by providing specific cognitive habits for reducing uncertainty, and [[naturalistic-decision-making]], though superforecasting tends to be more structured and analytically rigorous than the intuitive pattern-recognition approaches favored in naturalistic decision-making.
