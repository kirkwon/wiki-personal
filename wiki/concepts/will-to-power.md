# Will to Power

Nietzsche's concept of the fundamental drive behind all human action. It is not merely the desire for domination or control but encompasses self-overcoming, creativity, and the affirmation of life.

## Core Ideas

- The Will to Power is the basic motivational force behind all human action, including the pursuit of truth, creativity, and self-overcoming.
- **Self-overcoming**: The drive to surpass one's current state, not settle into fixed identities.
- **Creation**: Value creation and the affirmation of life rather than mere destruction.
- It manifests in various forms: physical strength, intellectual ambition, artistic creation, political influence, and even the desire for knowledge itself.
- Truth-seeking (Will to Truth) may itself be a form of Will to Power — a drive to impose order and meaning on chaos.
- The Will to Power is not inherently destructive; it is the fundamental energy of life affirming itself.
- **Beyond domination**: Not reducible to power-over-others; includes power-over-self.
- **Fundamental drive**: Underlies all human motivation, not just political or social ambitions.

## Connection to Will to Truth

Nietzsche questions whether the desire for truth is itself a form of Will to Power — whether philosophers and scientists are driven by a need to assert control through knowledge rather than by pure curiosity. This connects to [[will-to-truth]] and to broader questions about [[overconfidence-and-certainty]] in the wiki.

## Connection to Master Morality

Master morality is an expression of the Will to Power — noble values affirm life and self-overcoming. Slave morality, by contrast, is reactive and life-denying. Understanding the Will to Power helps explain why moral systems arise from power dynamics rather than objective foundations, and why [[master-vs-slave-morality]] reflects different expressions of this fundamental drive.
