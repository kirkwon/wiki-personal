# Systems Thinking Frameworks

General methodologies for understanding, designing, and managing complex systems. John Gall's *The Systems Bible* provides several practical frameworks that complement formal systems theory.

## Key Frameworks from Gall
- **Gall's Law** — Complex systems must evolve from simple systems
- **Fundamental Theorem of Systems** — New systems generate new problems
- **System Replacement vs. Repair** — When to abandon vs. fix a system
- **Unintended Consequence Analysis** — Anticipating side effects
- **System Lifecycle Mapping** — Understanding how systems age and degrade
- **Complexity Management** — Treating complexity as a first-order concern
- **Incremental Evolution Strategies** — Evolving systems step by step
- **System Boundary Analysis** — Defining what is inside vs. outside a system
- **Feedback Loop Identification** — Finding loops that drive system behavior

## When to Apply
- Designing or managing complex systems
- Troubleshooting persistent system problems
- Planning new systems or major system changes
- When systems seem to develop new problems
- When feeling frustrated by organizational or technical systems

## When to Stop
Gall warns against over-applying systems thinking: when it becomes an excuse for inaction, when simple problems are over-analyzed, or when it becomes a buzzword rather than a practical discipline.

## Connection to Other Concepts
This connects to [[antifragility]] (Taleb), [[infinite-game-mindset]] (Sinek), and [[systematic-decision-making]]. Gall's frameworks provide the practical "how" for the broader theoretical insights of other systems thinkers.
