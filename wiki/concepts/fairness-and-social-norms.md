# Fairness and Social Norms in Economic Behavior

Fairness and social norms play a significant role in economic behavior, contradicting the standard assumption of pure self-interest. People reject unfair offers, punish free-riders, cooperate in Prisoner's Dilemma scenarios, and forego profit to maintain fairness norms. These social norms operate alongside market norms and can dominate in many contexts.

Key evidence includes the Ultimatum Game, where proposers offer roughly 50% of a sum and responders reject offers below about 20%, even though rejecting means both parties receive nothing. This demonstrates that people care about fair distribution, not just maximizing their own payoff.

Thaler's work connects this to [[group-decision-polarization]] and [[behavioral-game-theory]], showing that standard rational choice models fail to predict actual human behavior in strategic situations. Fairness concerns also manifest in labor markets, where workers care about relative wages and reference points.
