# First Principles Thinking

First principles thinking involves breaking down problems to their fundamental truths and reasoning up from there, rather than reasoning by analogy or relying on assumptions.

## Core Idea
Strip away all assumptions, conventions, and received wisdom to identify the basic building blocks of a problem. Then reconstruct solutions from those fundamentals. This approach, famously used by Elon Musk for rocket cost analysis, helps avoid inherited constraints.

## Applications
- **Product design**: What are the essential requirements? What can be simplified?
- **Business strategy**: What fundamental value does the customer need? What assumptions are we carrying?
- **Learning**: Rather than memorizing formulas, understand the underlying principles.

## Relationship to Other Models
First principles thinking connects to [[decomposing-and-recombining]] (breaking down and rebuilding), [[inversion-thinking]] (reversing assumptions), and [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]] (starting from desired outcomes). Weinberg places it alongside lateral thinking and thought experiments as a creativity tool.

## Caveat
Weinberg notes that first principles thinking can ignore accumulated wisdom and practical experience. The balance between fundamental reasoning and practical knowledge is important — see the "when to stop" guardrails in [[super-thinking-gabriel-weinberg]].
