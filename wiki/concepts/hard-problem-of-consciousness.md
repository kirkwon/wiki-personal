# Hard Problem of Consciousness

The Hard Problem, coined by philosopher David Chalmers, refers to the fundamental mystery of explaining how physical processes in the brain give rise to subjective experience. While "easy" problems involve mapping correlations (like linking neural activity to specific behaviors), the hard problem asks why these physical states must be accompanied by *qualia*—the raw, qualitative feel of experience (e.g., the redness of red). It challenges purely functionalist views by suggesting that a complete physical description does not explain phenomenal consciousness itself.
