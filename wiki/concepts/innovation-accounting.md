# Innovation Accounting

Innovation accounting is the startup-specific metrics framework proposed in *The Lean Startup*. It provides a way to measure progress in conditions of extreme uncertainty, where traditional accounting metrics (revenue, profit) may not apply because the business model itself is unproven.

Innovation accounting focuses on actionable metrics that reveal whether the team is making validated learning progress. Examples include customer retention rates, engagement metrics, and conversion rates from MVP experiments—metrics that directly test the hypotheses being investigated.

Key principles:
- Vanity metrics (total signups, page views, press mentions) do not constitute progress
- Actionable metrics must be tied to specific hypotheses and learning goals
- The framework creates a "report card" for the startup, showing whether it is moving toward sustainable business metrics
- Early-stage startups may track learning milestones rather than financial performance

Innovation accounting connects to the broader theme of separating decision process quality from outcome quality [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]]—the metrics should reflect whether the right questions are being asked and answered, not whether the business has yet succeeded.
