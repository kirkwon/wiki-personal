# Fooled by Randomness

*Fooled by Randomness* by Nassim Nicholas Taleb explores how humans systematically misjudge the role of chance and randomness in their lives. The central thesis is that people overestimate their ability to predict outcomes and attribute success to skill when luck is often the dominant factor. The book argues that focusing on process rather than outcome is the only rational approach to navigating uncertainty.

## Key Ideas

- **Narrative Fallacy**: Humans construct stories after events to explain why things happened, creating false causal narratives that obscure the role of randomness.
- **Ludic Fallacy**: Mistaking the structured randomness of games for the unstructured randomness of real life, leading to flawed probability judgments.
- **Survivorship Bias**: Focusing on winners and successful outcomes while ignoring the many failures and silent losers, distorting our understanding of risk and success.
- **Probability Blindness**: Most people have poor intuition for probability, leading to overconfidence in predictions and underestimation of tail risks.
- **Skewness and Asymmetry**: Real-world outcomes are often skewed rather than symmetric, meaning rare events have outsized impacts that are poorly captured by averages.
- Randomness plays a larger role in success and failure than skill or strategy
- Outcome-focused thinking leads to poor decision-making
- Process-focused thinking provides a more defensible framework for action
- This directly supports the Processes mental model

## Relevance

The book provides a foundational framework for understanding why prediction markets, expert forecasts, and even sophisticated models often fail — because they ignore the fundamental role of randomness and the asymmetries in real-world outcomes.
