# Unintended Consequence Analysis

A systematic approach to anticipating and managing the side effects of system changes, rooted in John Gall's observation that systems inevitably produce outcomes beyond their intended purpose.

## Core Idea
Every system change generates unintended consequences. This is not a failure of planning—it is a fundamental property of complex systems. Unintended consequence analysis is the practice of identifying, assessing, and preparing for these emergent outcomes before they materialize.

## Techniques
- Before implementing a change, ask what new problems it will create
- Map feedback loops that could amplify or redirect the system's behavior
- Consider second-order and third-order effects
- Monitor for emergent problems after implementation
- Track the ratio of intended vs. unintended outcomes over time

## Connection to Other Concepts
This concept is central to [[fundamental-theorem-of-systems]] ("New systems generate new problems") and connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]]—a good decision process accounts for the likelihood of unintended consequences. It also relates to [[prospect-theory]] and [[cognitive-biases-library]], since humans systematically underestimate the probability and severity of side effects.

## Practical Application
When planning any system change—software, organizational, process, or policy—dedicate explicit time to identifying unintended consequences. This single practice can prevent many of the most costly system failures.
