# Enemy

The concept of understanding and analyzing opponents or competitors as a core element of strategic thinking. From *The Art of War*, knowing the enemy is foundational—Sun Tzu states that unless you know both the enemy and yourself, you cannot guarantee success.

## Key Ideas
- **Understanding the opponent** is a prerequisite for effective strategy. This extends beyond knowing their strength to understanding their motivations, patterns, and weaknesses.
- **Self-knowledge complements enemy knowledge**: Sun Tzu emphasizes that knowing yourself is equally important—comparing both yields the best outcome.
- **The enemy is not always a person**: In business, sports, or negotiations, the "enemy" can be a market condition, a deadline, a resource constraint, or an internal obstacle.

## Connections
This concept connects to [[superforecasting]] (understanding competitors through information), [[strategic-bluffing]] (misleading opponents), and [[the-art-of-war-sun-tzu]] (Chapter 1's five factors and Chapter 3's stratagem). It contrasts with [[the-infinite-game---simon-sinek]]'s cooperative framing, where the goal is not to defeat opponents but to sustain long-term relationships.

## Tension with Cooperative Frameworks
Sun Tzu's framework assumes adversarial competition. The wiki's broader philosophy emphasizes [[satisfaction-under-constraints]] and non-zero-sum outcomes. The "when to stop" guidance in the source explicitly warns against misapplying war thinking to cooperative contexts.
