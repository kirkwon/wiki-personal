# Polya's Four-Step Problem-Solving Framework

George Polya's central contribution is a simple yet powerful four-step process for solving problems of any kind:

1. **Understand the Problem** — What is given? What is unknown? What are the conditions? This phase involves clarifying the question, identifying constraints, and ensuring you truly grasp what is being asked.
2. **Devising a Plan** — Find a connection between the given data and the unknown. This is where heuristics come into play: analogy, working backwards, decomposition, specialization, and others. Polya asks: "Do you know a related problem?" and "Have you seen this problem before?"
3. **Carrying Out the Plan** — Execute the plan step by step, checking each step as you go. Be careful and verify your work.
4. **Looking Back** — Examine the solution. Can you check the result? Can you derive it differently? Can you use the result for other problems? This reflective phase is where learning happens.

This framework directly connects to [[decision-tracking]]—Polya's "Looking Back" phase is essentially a precursor to Annie Duke's practice of recording and reviewing decisions. It also relates to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]], since Polya explicitly asks you to evaluate the process independent of the result.
