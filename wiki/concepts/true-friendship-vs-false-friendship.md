# True Friendship vs. False Friendship

In *Letters from a Stoic* Chapter 3, Seneca distinguishes genuine friendship from superficial relationships. True friendship is based on mutual virtue and honesty, while false friendship is driven by self-interest or social obligation.

## Criteria for True Friendship

- Mutual trust and honesty without pretense.
- Friends who challenge you to improve, not merely flatter.
- Relationships sustained by shared virtue rather than mutual advantage.
- Connects to [[trust-fundamentals]] and [[effective-listening]].

## Connection to Team Dynamics

Seneca's analysis complements Lencioni's [[the-five-dysfunctions-of-a-team]], particularly the foundational dysfunction of [[absence-of-trust]]. Both frameworks recognize that genuine relationships require vulnerability and honesty, though Seneca frames this in philosophical rather than organizational terms.
