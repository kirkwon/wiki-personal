# Fresh Herbs Guide

A practical guide to selecting, storing, and preparing fresh herbs for cooking.

## Selection

When selecting fresh herbs, look for vibrant color, firm stems, and a strong fresh scent. Avoid wilted leaves, yellowing, slimy stems, or musty odors. Herbs should snap when bent rather than fold.

## Storage Methods

### Refrigerator
Place stems in water, cover loosely with a plastic bag. Change water daily. Most herbs last 3-5 days this way.

### Freezing
Chop herbs and freeze in ice cube trays with oil or water. Retains flavor for up to 6 months. Best for soups, sauces, and stews.

### Drying
Air dry or dehydrate quickly to preserve color. Note that dried herbs lose significant flavor compared to fresh—most dried herbs are best used within 1-2 weeks.

## Preparation Techniques

- **Chiffonade**: Roll leaves and slice into thin ribbons
- **Torn**: Hand-tear for salads to prevent bruising
- **Minced**: Fine chop for sauces and mixtures
- **Pounded**: Use mortar and pestle to release essential oils

## Heat Sensitivity

Fresh herbs are generally heat-sensitive. Add late in cooking—last 1-2 minutes for medium heat—or use raw. Infusing herbs in warm (not hot) oil preserves better flavor than prolonged cooking.

## Seasonal Variation

Spring herbs tend to be milder and more delicate, while summer herbs have full flavor and robust essential oils. Late summer herbs may be stronger and can flower—pinch flowers for better leaf quality.

## Common Substitutions

When fresh herbs are unavailable, substitutions include parsley (milder), spinach (for green color), Thai basil, or cilantro, though each alters the flavor profile.
