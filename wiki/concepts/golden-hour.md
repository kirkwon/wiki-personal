# Golden Hour

Golden hour is the period shortly after sunrise and before sunset when the sun is low in the sky, creating warm, soft, golden light prized by photographers for its beautiful, flattering quality.

## Definition and Characteristics

Golden hour typically refers to the first hour after sunrise and the last hour before sunset. It is the period when the sun is just above the horizon. Key characteristics include:

- **Warm, golden light**: Low sun angle creates warm color temperature
- **Soft, diffused light**: Light travels through more atmosphere, becoming softer
- **Long shadows**: Low angle creates dramatic shadows
- **Directional light**: Light comes from the side or near the horizon
- **Beautiful colors**: Orange, red, yellow tones

## Why Photographers Love It

Golden hour provides flattering portraits with warm skin tones, dramatic landscapes with long shadows, saturated colors, and soft light without harsh shadows. It creates depth and dimension through directional illumination. Warm light is kind to skin tones, offers no harsh shadows, and produces gentle directional light.

- **Flattering portraits**: Warm light is kind to skin tones
- **Dramatic landscapes**: Long shadows, golden light
- **Beautiful colors**: Warm tones, saturated colors
- **Soft light**: No harsh shadows, gentle on subjects
- **Directional light**: Creates depth and dimension

## Timing

Timing varies by season, latitude, and elevation. Typical durations:
- Summer: 45-60 minutes
- Winter: 30-45 minutes
- Near equator: Up to 2 hours
- Near poles: Can be very short or non-existent

### Sunrise Golden Hour

- **When**: First hour after sunrise
- **Characteristics**: Cool to warm transition, fresh morning air, rising sun angles, sometimes fog
- **Best for**: Landscapes, flowers, cityscapes before crowds, portraits

### Sunset Golden Hour

- **When**: Last hour before sunset
- **Characteristics**: Warm to orange to red, more saturated and dramatic, lower sun angles, atmosphere settles
- **Best for**: Portraits with glowing skin, dramatic landscapes, silhouettes against the sun, beach photography with ocean reflections

## Planning

### Apps for Calculation

Key apps include [[photopills]], [[the-photographer-s-ephemeris]], [[sun-surveyor]], [[golden-hour-one]], and [[sol]]. Use them to set location, date, and check golden hour times and sun position.

### Weather Considerations

- Clear skies provide the best golden hour with saturated colors
- Partly cloudy conditions add drama
- Overcast eliminates golden hour but still provides soft diffused light
- Rain may or may not produce golden hour but creates interesting reflections

### Location Scouting

Scout locations during the day to find compositions, check sun position, identify subjects, and verify access. Arrive at least 30 minutes before golden hour and bring a headlamp for after sunset.

## Equipment

### Camera Settings

- Mode: Manual or Aperture Priority for creative control
- Aperture: f/5.6 - f/8 for landscapes, wider for portraits
- ISO: Low (100-400) for landscapes, higher if handheld
- White Balance: Auto or Daylight preset
- Lens: Wide to normal

### Essential Accessories

- Tripod for slow shutter speeds
- Lens hood to reduce flare from low sun
- Polarizing filter to enhance colors and reduce reflections
- Graduated ND filter to balance bright sky and dark foreground
- Remote shutter release to prevent camera shake
- Headlamp/flashlight for after sunset

## Golden Hour Techniques

### Backlighting

Placing the subject between the camera and the sun creates rim light on hair and edges, a warm glow around the subject, and potential silhouettes. Ideal for portraits, landscapes, and dramatic silhouette shots.

### Side Lighting

Sun to the side of the subject creates dramatic shadows, dimension, and textural detail. Best for portraits with shadow on one side, landscapes where shadows create depth, and architecture emphasizing texture.

### Front Lighting

Sun behind the photographer with the subject fully lit produces flat, even lighting with saturated colors and minimal shadows. Suitable for landscapes, flowers, and some portraits.

### Silhouettes

Expose for the bright background (sky) rather than the subject to create dark silhouettes against the light. Look for recognizable shapes like trees, people, or buildings.

## Subjects for Golden Hour

### Portraits

Warm, flattering light with soft shadows and natural catchlights in eyes. Use side-lighting, front-lighting, backlighting, or reflectors.

### Landscapes

Warm golden light, long dramatic shadows, saturated colors, atmospheric effects. Include foreground elements for depth and use polarizing filters.

### Cityscapes and Architecture

Warm light on buildings, long shadows creating dimension, golden light on windows, and people going about their day. Great for [[street-photography]].

### Beach and Water Photography

Golden reflections on water, long shadows on sand, dramatic colors. Look for reflections, include people, and use long exposures with a tripod.

## Mistakes to Avoid

- Missed golden hour: Plan ahead, arrive early
- Sun too high: Shoot at proper times (first/last hour)
- Overexposed sky: Use graduated ND filter or bracket exposures
- Camera shake: Use tripod, faster shutter, increase ISO
- Flare in lens: Use lens hood, shade lens
- Wrong white balance: Adjust for desired warmth

## Practice Exercises

**Daily**: Check golden hour times, plan shoot, shoot during golden hour.
**Weekly**: Shoot at both sunrise and sunset, different subjects, experiment with angles.
**Monthly**: Document same location over multiple golden hours, compare seasons, curate portfolio.
