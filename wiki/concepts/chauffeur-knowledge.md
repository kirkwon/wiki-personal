# Chauffeur Knowledge

Superficial knowledge that mimics understanding — the difference between genuine understanding and knowledge that can fool others but not ourselves. Named after the chauffeurs who could recite Einstein's words without truly understanding the physics.

## Key Points
- Distinguish between knowing the name of something and understanding it
- Experts can possess chauffeur knowledge — familiarity with vocabulary without deep comprehension
- Connects to [[expert-illusion]] — experts overestimating their knowledge
- Connects to [[deformation-professionnelle]] — professional training creating blind spots

## Practical Guardrail
Test your understanding by explaining concepts in your own words, teaching others, or applying knowledge to novel situations. If you can't do these, you may have chauffeur knowledge.

## Connection to Thinking
The book emphasizes simple, clear thinking over complex reasoning patterns. Genuine understanding is simpler than imitation.
