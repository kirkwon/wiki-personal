# World

The World as a mental model captures Taleb's view of reality as fundamentally uncertain and dominated by rare events. Our models of the world—statistical, economic, historical—systematically fail because they assume normal distributions and ignore the fat tails where black swans live.

## Core Claim
The world is not the orderly place our models suggest. It is skewed, unpredictable, and shaped by extreme events that our frameworks cannot capture.

## Implications
- Relying on averages and standard deviations is dangerous.
- Robustness and optionality matter more than precision.
- Epistemic humility—accepting what we cannot know—is a practical virtue.
