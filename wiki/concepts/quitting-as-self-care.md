# Quitting as Self-Care

Quitting can sometimes be an act of self-care that ultimately leads to better long-term outcomes by preventing burnout, maintaining mental health, and preserving relationships. Duke illustrates how continuing in situations that drain energy or damage wellbeing can be more harmful than stepping away.

This concept connects to [[balancing-ambition-with-well-being]], which addresses the tension between pursuing goals and maintaining health. It also relates to [[decision-paralysis]] — sometimes the inability to quit is itself a form of self-harm, as it traps individuals in unproductive patterns.

The practical implication is that quitting decisions should weigh not just external outcomes but internal states: energy levels, mental health, relational costs. This aligns with Duke's broader framework in [[quit-the-power-of-knowing-when-to-walk-away---annie-duke]].
