# Role

Role is a universal mental model — a conceptual tool for navigating complex situations by identifying the position or function someone or something occupies within a system. It provides a shortcut for making sense of patterns and relationships.

## Definition

Role functions as a mental shortcut: it helps you understand how the position, identity, or assigned function of a person or entity shapes perception, behavior, and outcomes. When you recognize what "role" someone is playing — whether in a team, a negotiation, a family, or a market — you gain leverage for clearer thinking.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

### Perspective-Taking (Edward de Bono)
In [[Six Thinking Hats - Edward de Bono]], de Bono advocates wearing different hats — each a distinct role — to examine a problem from multiple angles. This is role-based perspective-shifting: by temporarily adopting different roles, you broaden your understanding and reduce blind spots.

### Ego and Identity Attachment (Ryan Holiday)
Ryan Holiday argues in [[ego-is-the-enemy]] that clinging to a role or self-image distorts judgment. When you over-identify with being "the expert," "the leader," or "the victim," you lose the flexibility to see situations clearly. Role-awareness means recognizing when ego is anchoring you to a role that no longer serves reality.

### Life-Role Optimization (Scott Galloway)
Scott Galloway's [[the-algebra-of-happiness]] emphasizes that the roles you inhabit — parent, professional, partner, friend — are the primary drivers of life satisfaction. Designing and balancing these roles deliberately, rather than drifting into them, is a form of strategic life planning.

### Social Influence Through Roles (Robert B. Cialdini)
Cialdini's [[influence-the-psychology-of-persuasion]] shows that authority, reciprocity, and social proof are amplified by role-based expectations. People comply more readily when a request comes from someone in a recognized role (doctor, teacher, expert), even independent of the actual content of the request.

### Strategic Role Exit (Annie Duke)
Annie Duke's [[quit-the-power-of-knowing-when-to-walk-away]] frames quitting as role redefinition — recognizing when a role (employee, investor, partner) is no longer serving you and having the courage to exit. This is not failure; it is role management.

## Practical Applications

- **In decision-making**: Ask "what role am I playing right now?" to check for bias or blind spots.
- **In teams**: Use role-switching (de Bono's hats) to surface assumptions.
- **In influence**: Recognize when role-based authority is being used — by others or by yourself.
- **In personal life**: Audit your life roles and evaluate whether each one aligns with your values and goals.

## Boundaries and Tensions

Role overlaps with related concepts like [[identity]], [[ego]], and [[perspective]]. The distinction is that "role" emphasizes the functional position within a system, while "identity" is more about self-concept and "ego" is about self-protection. Role is most useful when it points to how systemic position shapes behavior, rather than how self-image does.
