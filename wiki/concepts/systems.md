# Systems

Systems is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Definition

Systems thinking is the practice of understanding complex situations through the lens of patterns, relationships, and interconnections rather than isolated components. It serves as a universal mental model applicable across all domains—from personal productivity to organizational management to ecological understanding.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

This mental model is referenced across multiple systems thinkers:

- **John Gall** — In *The Systems Bible*, Gall explores how systems behave in ways that surprise their creators, emphasizing that systems are inherently more complex than their individual parts.
- **Peter M. Senge** — *The Fifth Discipline* and *The Fifth Discipline Fieldbook* introduce systems thinking as a core discipline for learning organizations, focusing on feedback loops, mental models, and shared vision.
- **Donella H. Meadows** — *Thinking in Systems* provides a foundational framework for understanding system dynamics, including stocks, flows, feedback loops, and leverage points.
- **Kevin Kelly** — *Out of Control* examines how decentralized, self-organizing systems emerge and function, with implications for understanding complex adaptive behavior.
- **David Allen** — *Getting Things Done* implicitly applies systems thinking to personal productivity, treating workflows as interconnected systems that need to be externally organized to reduce cognitive load.
- **Brian Christian / Tom Griffiths** — *The Alignment Problem* connects systems thinking to AI alignment, examining how complex systems can produce misaligned outcomes when design and intention diverge.

## Applicability

Systems thinking functions as a universal mental model for navigating complexity. It provides a mental shortcut for recognizing patterns and relationships across domains, connecting to existing mental models like [[thinking-mental-model]], [[planning]], [[overview]], and [[Solutions]].

- Systems provides a cross-domain framework for sense-making
- It emphasizes patterns and relationships over isolated components
