# Default Options and Inertia

Default options and inertia describe how established patterns, past habits, and pre-selected defaults significantly shape human behavior, often leading to seemingly irrational decisions that persist regardless of whether they remain optimal.

## Core Idea

Ariely analyzes how default options and a sense of inertia impact human behavior, resulting in seemingly irrational decisions influenced by established patterns or past habits.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]

## Key Mechanisms

- People tend to stick with default settings even when changing would be beneficial
- Past habits create momentum that resists rational reassessment
- Inertia reduces the perceived cost of continuing a behavior
- Defaults effectively make choices for people who don't actively opt out

## Connection to Existing Concepts

This concept extends [[decision-tracking]] and [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]] by highlighting how passive decision-making (not choosing) is itself a choice with consequences.
