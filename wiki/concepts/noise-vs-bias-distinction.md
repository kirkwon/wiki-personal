# Noise vs. Bias Distinction

One of the central contributions of Kahneman's *Noise* is distinguishing noise from bias as two separate sources of judgment error.

## Noise

Random variability in judgments. Different people (or the same person at different times) reach different conclusions when the situation is essentially the same. Noise has no consistent direction — it is scattered around the truth in both directions.

## Bias

Systematic deviation from accuracy. A consistent directional error — always too high or always too low, always optimistic or always pessimistic. Bias has a consistent pattern.

## Why the Distinction Matters

Because noise and bias require different remedies:
- **Bias** is addressed by correcting the systematic error — through awareness, debiasing techniques, or calibration.
- **Noise** is addressed by increasing consistency — through structured processes, shared criteria, and organizational practices that align judgments.

A system can be noisy without being biased (inconsistent but roughly centered on truth), or biased without being noisy (consistent but wrong), or both.

## Practical Implications

Understanding this distinction prevents the mistake of assuming that because judgments are inconsistent (noisy), they must also be systematically wrong (biased). Both problems need to be measured and addressed separately.
