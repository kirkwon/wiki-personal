# Decision-Making Frameworks

Structured methodologies for rational decision-making include:

- **SWOT Analysis**: Evaluating Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, and Threats
- **Cost-Benefit Analysis**: Quantifying pros and cons of options
- **Prospect Theory**: How framing affects risk perception (e.g., loss aversion)
- **Decision Trees**: Visualizing probabilistic outcomes and their consequences
- **Monte Carlo Simulations**: Modeling uncertainty through statistical sampling

These frameworks aim to reduce the influence of cognitive biases by introducing systematic analysis, transparency, and quantitative rigor. They are widely applied in finance, strategy, and personal decision-making.
