# Kind of Blue

**Kind of Blue** (1959) is the best-selling jazz album of all time and the defining work of modal jazz. Recorded by Miles Davis's First Great Quintet, it represents a landmark shift in jazz from complex chord changes to melodic exploration over sustained harmonies.

## Background
Recorded in August 1959, the album features Miles Davis on trumpet, John Coltrane on tenor saxophone, Cannonball Adderley on alto saxophone, Bill Evans on piano, Paul Chambers on bass, and Jimmy Cobb on drums.

## Track Listing and Significance
- **"So What"** – Opens with one of jazz's most iconic bass lines; modal exploration over a Dorian mode
- **"Freddie Freeloader"** – Blues with bebop elements, showcasing the bridge between styles
- **"Blue in Green"** – Lyrical ballad with emotional depth and Davis's signature use of space
- **"All Blues"** – Minor blues feeling with pentatonic scale soloing
- **"Flamenco Sketches"** – Open-ended modal improvisation

## Why It Matters
Kind of Blue demonstrated that jazz could thrive with fewer chord changes, placing melodic invention and harmonic color at the center. It remains essential listening for understanding [[modal-jazz]] and Miles Davis's approach to [[jazz-harmony]].

## Listening Recommendation
For newcomers, start with "So What" and "Blue in Green" before exploring the full album in sequence.
