# Healthcare Costs in Retirement — California Edition

## Overview

Healthcare is the largest unpredictable expense in retirement. For a 65-year-old couple retiring in 2025, Fidelity estimates **$165,000 in after-tax savings needed** for medical expenses throughout retirement (Medicare premiums, deductibles, copays, dental, vision, hearing — **excluding** long-term care). In California, costs are slightly above national average due to higher provider rates.

## Medicare — The Foundation

### Medicare Part B (Outpatient/Doctor)
- **Standard premium (2025):** ~$185/month per person
- **Deductible:** ~$257/year per person
- **Coinsurance:** 20% of most services after deductible
- **IRMAA surcharges** (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount):
  - Modified AGI >$106,000 single / $212,000 couple → $70–$450/month extra per person
  - Updates annually based on tax returns from 2 years prior
  - **Key lever:** Manage MAGI in years 63+ to control Part B costs in retirement

### Medicare Part D (Prescription Drugs)
- **Average premium:** ~$35–$55/month per person
- **Deductible:** Up to $545/year (2025)
- **Coverage gap (donut hole):** Closed by Inflation Reduction Act — 25% max out-of-pocket
- **Out-of-pocket cap (2025):** $2,000/year under IRA — **major improvement**

### Medicare Part A (Hospital)
- **Premium:** $0 (if you paid Medicare taxes for 10+ years — most workers qualify)
- **Deductible:** ~$1,676 per benefit period
- **Coinsurance:** Days 61–90: $419/day; Days 91+: $838/day (60 lifetime reserve days)

## The Gap: What Medicare Does NOT Cover

| Service | Medicare Coverage | Out-of-Pocket Exposure |
|---------|------------------|----------------------|
| Dental | None | Full cost |
| Vision (routine) | None | Full cost |
| Hearing aids | None | Full cost ($2K–$7K per pair) |
| Long-term care | Limited (100 days skilled nursing only) | Full cost beyond |
| Foreign travel | None | Full cost |
| Annual out-of-pocket max | None | Potentially unlimited (unless Medigap) |

## Medigap vs Medicare Advantage — California Decision

### Medigap (Supplemental Insurance)
- **Plan G (most popular):** Covers Part B 20% coinsurance, deductible only
- **California premium:** ~$160–$280/month (age 65), varies by location and gender
- **Key advantage:** No networks, no referrals, any doctor who accepts Medicare
- **Guaranteed issue:** During initial 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment (when you turn 65 and have Part B). After that, insurers can medically underwrite in most states — **but CA is an exception.**

### California's Birthday Rule
California has a **unique Medigap guarantee**: Within 30 days of your birthday each year, you can switch to any Medigap Plan G with *guaranteed issue* (no medical underwriting). This is a significant protection — you're not locked into a bad choice.

### Medicare Advantage (Part C)
- **Premium:** Often $0–$50/month
- **Out-of-pocket max:** ~$4,000–$8,000/year
- **Trade-off:** HMO/PPO networks, prior auths, narrower choice
- **In California:** Kaiser is the dominant player — good quality but you must use Kaiser facilities
- **Best for:** People who want a single plan, predictable costs, and don't mind networks

**Recommendation for Kirk:** Medigap Plan G from age 65. California's Birthday Rule means you can switch later if needed. The premium is worth the predictability and freedom.

## California-Specific Considerations

### Medi-Cal (Medicaid for CA)
- If income is low enough in retirement, Medi-Cal can cover Medicare premiums (Medicare Savings Programs)
- **2025 income limits:** ~$1,732/month for single QMB (pays Part B premium + cost-sharing)
- Kirk is unlikely to qualify given assets — but worth knowing as a backstop

### Covered California (ACA Marketplace)
- Available for early retirees (pre-65) who need bridge coverage
- **CA subsidy structure:** Stronger than federal base — CA added state subsidies on top of ACA
- **Premium range:** $500–$1,200/month for gold-level coverage at age 60–64 (before subsidies)
- **Out-of-pocket max:** ~$9,000/year individual
- **Key tip:** Manage MAGI in early retirement to maximize premium tax credits

### California Provider Rates
- California has higher healthcare costs than national average (~15–20% above)
- Particularly true in Bay Area and Los Angeles
- Medigap premiums reflect this — expect $180–$300/month for Plan G in SF Bay Area

## Long-Term Care — The Biggest Risk

### California Costs (2025 estimates)

| Care Type | Annual Cost (CA) | National Average |
|-----------|----------------:|-----------------:|
| Skilled nursing facility (private room) | $140,000–$180,000 | $108,000 |
| Assisted living | $60,000–$90,000 | $54,000 |
| Home health aide (44 hrs/week) | $65,000–$85,000 | $60,000 |
| Adult day care | $25,000–$35,000 | $22,000 |

### Statistically
- **~70% of 65-year-olds will need some form of long-term care**
- **~20% will need care for 5+ years**
- Women live longer → higher probability of needing care
- Inflation on long-term care services has been running 3–5% annually

### Funding Options
1. **Self-fund** — Viable if portfolio >$3M+ and willing to earmark $150K–$500K for LTC
2. **Traditional LTC insurance** — Expensive ($3K–$7K/year if purchased at 55–60); may not be available at 65+
3. **Hybrid LTC (life insurance + LTC rider)** — More popular; returns premium if you don't need care
4. **Home equity** — Reverse mortgage or sale to fund care
5. **Medi-Cal** — Pays for nursing home if you spend down assets (~$160K+ equity exempt)

**Recommendation:** For Kirk (near retirement with significant assets), self-funding is likely the most efficient path. A hybrid policy could make sense if there's a legacy motive.

### Age Escalation
Healthcare costs tend to rise with age:
- **Age 65–74:** ~$5K–$8K/year (premiums only)
- **Age 75–84:** ~$8K–$12K/year (more services, higher Part D)
- **Age 85+:** ~$12K–$20K/year (increased utilization, potential LTC)

## Strategies for Kirk

### 1. IRMAA Management
- IRMAA surcharges can double Part B premiums
- **Leverage:** Roth conversions in years before Medicare (age 63–64) must be done carefully — MAGI in year 63 determines Part B premium at age 65
- **Strategy:** Front-load Roth conversions before 63, or spread them to stay under IRMAA thresholds
- **2025 IRMAA brackets (Part B, per person/month):**
  - Standard: $185.00 (AGI <$106K single / $212K couple)
  - Tier 1: +$70.90 (AGI <$134K / $268K)
  - Tier 2: +$177.30 (AGI <$167K / $334K)
  - Tier 3: +$283.80 (AGI <$200K / $400K)
  - Tier 4: +$390.40 (AGI <$500K+)

### 2. HSA Maximization
- If Kirk has an HDHP between now and Medicare enrollment, max the HSA
- **2025 limit:** $4,300 single / $8,550 family (+$1,000 catch-up for 55+)
- HSA is **triple tax-advantaged**: deductible contributions, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses
- Can pay Medicare premiums (Part B, D, Medigap) tax-free from HSA — **except** Medigap premiums
- **Cannot contribute to HSA after enrolling in Medicare** — so front-load before age 65

### 3. Roth Conversion Planning
- Healthcare costs in retirement are after-tax — but tax bracket management affects Medicare premiums
- **Sweet spot:** Enough Roth to avoid IRMAA, enough traditional to fill lower brackets
- **Healthcare deduction:** Medical expenses exceeding 7.5% of AGI are deductible (if itemizing) — could justify higher traditional withdrawals in high-healthcare-cost years

### 4. Long-Term Care Reserve
- Earmark $200K–$300K of portfolio as LTC contingency
- If never needed, it's a legacy; if needed, it avoids forced selling
- Consider a Health Savings Account (HSA) as the primary vehicle for LTC funds

### 5. California Tax Note
- California does **not** tax HSA contributions or distributions (follows federal rules)
- California does **not** tax Social Security benefits (unique among states)
- Medigap premiums are **not** deductible for state taxes (medical expenses >7.5% AGI threshold still applies)
- California's high state income tax makes tax-efficient healthcare funding more valuable
