# Behavior as a Mental Model

Behavior is a universal conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern recognition and relationship understanding. It serves as a mental shortcut for making sense of how people act, decide, and are influenced in the world.

## Core Idea

Rather than treating behavior as random or chaotic, this model frames it as a lens for identifying systematic patterns. People consistently deviate from rational choice in predictable ways — through biases, social pressure, dishonesty, and environmental nudges. Recognizing these patterns allows for better decision-making, communication, and design of choice environments.

## Key Perspectives

### Predictable Irrationality (Dan Ariely)
People are not rational actors. Ariely's research in *The Honest Truth About Dishonesty* and *The Upside of Irrationality* demonstrates that systematic deviations from rational choice are both common and predictable. This connects to [[cognitive-biases-library]] and [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]].

### Choice Architecture (Richard Thaler)
Environments shape decisions. Thaler's work in *Misbehaving* shows how choice architecture and nudges can guide behavior without restricting freedom, linking to [[choice-overload]] and [[decision-making-frameworks]].

### Persuasion and Influence (Robert Cialdini)
Social dynamics drive compliance. Cialdini's six principles of influence — reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, and scarcity — explain how persuasion works, connecting to [[communication-skills]] and [[supercommunicators-charles-duhigg]].
