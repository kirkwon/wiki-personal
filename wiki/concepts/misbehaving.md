# Misbehaving by Richard H. Thaler

*Misbehaving* is Richard Thaler's account of behavioral economics—the study of how people systematically deviate from the predictions of rational-agent models. The book documents how mental accounting, loss aversion, overconfidence, and other biases shape economic decisions in ways classical economics fails to predict.

## Key Themes

- People are not rational actors; they use heuristics that lead to predictable errors.
- Economic models must account for psychological realities.
- Small changes in how choices are framed can dramatically alter outcomes.

Thaler's work in *Misbehaving* underpins the [[economics-mental-model]] by showing that economics as a framework must incorporate behavioral insights to be useful for navigating real-world complexity.
