# Decision-Making Frameworks

Decision-Making Frameworks are systematic methods that help people and organizations make better decisions under uncertainty. These frameworks provide a structure for evaluating alternatives, considering various factors, and selecting the best course of action. They reduce cognitive biases, clarify trade-offs, and increase the likelihood of good outcomes.

## Overview

Decision-Making Frameworks offer a structured approach to complex decisions and help update beliefs based on evidence. They can be applied to daily choices as well as strategic organizational decisions.

## Major Frameworks

### Probabilistic and Expected Value Approaches

#### Annie Duke: Thinking in Bets
Framing all decisions as probabilistic bets. Emphasizes expected value, bet sizing, strategic bluffing, and decision tracking. Connects to [[expected-value-ev]] and [[kelly-criterion]].

#### Bayesian Decision Framework
Update beliefs based on evidence. Calculate expected value: EV = (P × Gain) + ((1-P) × -Loss). For example, a side business: P=30%, Gain=$50,000, Loss=$5,000 → EV=$11,500.

#### General Decision Framework
A six-step process: define the decision, identify options, define criteria, evaluate options, account for uncertainty, make the decision and track outcomes. Each criterion can be weighted (1-5) and options scored systematically.

### Structured Problem-Solving

#### George Polya: Four-Step Problem-Solving
A structured process: understand the problem, devise a plan, carry out the plan, look back. Provides a dictionary of heuristics—analogy, decomposition, working backwards, specialization, auxiliary elements. The "looking back" phase parallels [[decision-tracking]].

#### First Principles Framework
Identify your assumptions, break them down to fundamental truths, and build your decision on those. Ask: "What do I know to be absolutely true?"

### Intuitive and Pattern Recognition

#### Gary Klein: Naturalistic Decision Making
Experts rely on pattern recognition from experience rather than deliberate analysis. The [[recognition-primed-decision-model]] describes how decisions are made under time pressure.

### Time-Based and Reflection Frameworks

#### 10-10-10 Rule
Ask yourself: "How will I feel about this decision in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years?" This helps distinguish short-term impulse from long-term impact.

#### Regret Minimization Framework
Imagine yourself at age 80 looking back—what choice causes the least regret? Jeff Bezos used this method when deciding to start Amazon.

#### Pre-Mortem Analysis
Imagine your decision has catastrophically failed. Why did it fail? This allows proactive response.

#### Reversibility Test
Two-door decisions (reversible) → act quickly and gather data. One-door decisions (irreversible) → think deeply and seek more information.

### Prioritization and Urgency

#### Eisenhower Matrix
Categorize decisions by urgency and importance:
- Urgent + Important: Do now
- Urgent + Not important: Delegate
- Not urgent + Important: Plan
- Not urgent + Not important: Eliminate

#### Opportunity Cost Framework
What do I lose with this choice? Compare against the next best alternative and choose the higher net present value.

### Comparison Frameworks

#### Pros-Cons Framework
List pros and cons, weight each (1-5), compare weighted sums.

### Cognitive Foundations

#### Daniel Kahneman: Dual Process Theory
System 1 (fast, intuitive) vs System 2 (slow, deliberate). Emphasizes cognitive biases like [[confirmation-bias]], [[overconfidence-and-certainty]], and [[framing-effects-in-decision-making]].

## Decision Quality Checklist

Before finalizing an important decision, check:
- Decision clearly defined
- 3+ alternatives generated
- Criteria identified
- Systematic evaluation completed
- Uncertainty accounted for
- Biases checked
- External perspective obtained
- Wait 24 hours
- Rationale documented
- Review date set

## Common Decision Biases

- **Analysis Paralysis**: Overthinking, never reaching a decision
- **False Dilemma**: Seeing only two options when many exist
- **Sunk Cost**: Continuing due to prior investments
- **Emotional Decision**: Deciding based on feelings without data
- **Groupthink**: Conforming to group pressure
- **Framing Effect**: Presentation format influences the decision
- **Confirmation Bias**: [[confirmation-bias]]
- **Overconfidence and Certainty**: [[overconfidence-and-certainty]]
- **Framing Effects in Decision-Making**: [[framing-effects-in-decision-making]]

## Practices

- **Daily decisions**: Apply one framework to each decision
- **Weekly review**: Evaluate last week's decisions and identify biases
- **Decision Journal**: Document important decisions, criteria, and rationale; track outcomes monthly

## Polya as a Third Perspective

Polya's systematic heuristics offer a middle ground: more structured than Klein's intuitive recognition but less probabilistic than Duke's betting framework. His emphasis on reflection and verification connects to [[paul-elder-critical-thinking-framework]].
