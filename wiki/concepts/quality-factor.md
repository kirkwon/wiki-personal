# Quality Factor

The quality factor is based on the premise that high-quality companies—those that are profitable, have stable earnings, and carry low debt—tend to outperform the market over time.

## Metrics

- **Return on Equity (ROE)** – Profitability measure
- **Return on Assets (ROA)** – Efficiency of asset use
- **Profit margins** – Gross, operating, and net profit margins
- **Debt-to-equity ratio** – Lower is better
- **Earnings stability** – Low variance in earnings over time

## Implementation

1. Screen for companies with ROE > 15%
2. Debt-to-equity < 50%
3. Positive cash flow for 5+ years
4. Rebalance annually

## Risks

- Quality stocks can be expensive (low value), potentially limiting upside
- May underperform in speculative or hype-driven markets

## Connection to Factor Investing

The quality factor is one of five primary factors in [[factor-investing]]. Quality-focused ETFs include QUAL (0.15% expense ratio). Quality tends to outperform in bull markets alongside momentum.
