# Strategies

Strategies is a universal mental model — a conceptual tool that provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex situations. It serves as an overarching lens for navigating uncertainty, leveraging influence, building productive habits, and making better decisions across domains.

## Definition

A strategy is a structured approach to understanding and acting within complex environments. Unlike isolated tactics, strategies involve pattern recognition, anticipation of outcomes, and alignment of actions with broader goals. This mental model functions as a meta-framework that connects productivity systems, influence techniques, habit formation, and decision-making under uncertainty.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

### Cal Newport — Slow Productivity
[[slow-productivity---cal-newport]] advocates for doing fewer things better, rejecting the cult of busyness. This reframes strategy as a commitment to depth over breadth — choosing high-value pursuits and protecting time for meaningful work.

### Scott Galloway — Post Corona & The Four
[[Post Corona_ From Crisis to Opportunity - Scott Galloway]] and [[the-four---scott-galloway]] emphasize strategic thinking during disruption. Galloway identifies platform dominance and the four corporate titans (Apple, Google, Amazon, Meta) as forces shaping opportunity. Strategy here means recognizing structural shifts and positioning accordingly.

### Robert B. Cialdini — Pre-Suasion
[[Pre-Suasion_ A Revolutionary Way to Influence and Persuade - Robert B Cialdini]] shows that influence begins before the pitch — through environmental priming and framing. This extends strategy into the domain of persuasion, where setting the context determines outcomes.

### Timothy Ferriss — Tools of Titans
[[tools-of-titans---timothy-ferriss]] distills strategies from high performers across domains: morning routines, decision frameworks, learning techniques. The pattern: effective strategies are often counterintuitive and require systematic experimentation.

### James Clear — Atomic Habits
[[atomic-habits-james-clear]] reframes habit change as a strategy of identity and environment design. Small systems compound into transformative results — a core insight connecting habit formation to strategic thinking.

### Annie Duke — Thinking in Bets
[[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] introduces a betting framework for decision-making under uncertainty. Strategy involves separating decisions from outcomes, tracking decisions, and thinking in probabilities rather than certainties.

### Mark Manson — Everything Is F*cked
[[Everything Is F_cked_ A Book About Hope - Mark Manson]] offers a strategy of strategic acceptance — acknowledging suffering and hopelessness as foundations for authentic action. This connects to [[will---mark-manson]] and the broader theme of embracing difficulty.

### David Allen — Getting Things Done
[[getting-things-done---david-allen]] provides the operational infrastructure for strategy execution: capturing, clarifying, organizing, reviewing, and engaging. Without this system, strategic intentions dissolve into overwhelm.

## How Strategies Differs from Related Models

- **vs. Planning**: Planning is a specific mode of strategy — mapping steps toward a defined goal. Strategies are broader, encompassing how to choose goals, adapt to change, and leverage influence.
- **vs. Problem-solving**: Problem-solving is reactive — addressing a defined challenge. Strategy is proactive — shaping environments and anticipating challenges before they arise.
- **vs. Recipes**: Recipes are fixed procedures. Strategies are adaptive frameworks that respond to context.
