# Caramelization

## Overview
Caramelization is the process of heating sugar to create a golden-brown color and rich flavor. This chemical reaction occurs when sugar is heated to around 170°C (338°F), causing it to break down into caramelized sugars that develop complex flavors and aromas. Caramelization is fundamental in cooking and baking, used to enhance the taste of foods like onions, fruits, and desserts.

## Key Concepts
- **Maillard Reaction**: A separate chemical reaction that occurs alongside caramelization, contributing to browning and flavor development.
- **Temperature Control**: Critical for achieving the desired color and flavor without burning the sugar.
- **Applications**: Used in making caramel sauces, glazed foods, and enhancing the flavor of vegetables and fruits.

## Techniques
- **Stirring**: Constant stirring prevents burning and ensures even heating.
- **Sugar Syrup**: Creating a sugar syrup by dissolving sugar in water and heating it to the desired temperature.
- **Caramel Sauce**: A common application where caramelized sugar is used as a topping or ingredient in desserts.
