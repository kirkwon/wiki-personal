# Diversification

Diversification is the basic risk management strategy — don't put all your eggs in one basket. It involves distributing investments across asset classes (stocks, bonds, real estate, commodities), geographies (US, international, emerging markets), sectors (technology, healthcare, energy), and company size (large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap).

The core insight is that different assets perform differently under varying economic conditions, so a portfolio with varied exposures is less likely to suffer catastrophic losses from any single event.

## Types of Diversification
- **Asset class diversification**: Stocks, bonds, real estate, commodities, alternatives
- **Geographic diversification**: US, international, emerging markets
- **Sector diversification**: Technology, healthcare, energy, utilities
- **Factor diversification**: Value, momentum, quality, size
- **Company size diversification**: Large-cap, mid-cap, small-cap

## Benefits
- Reduces unsystematic (non-systematic) risk
- Smoother returns over time
- Protection against sector downturns

## Connection to Alternatives
Alternative investments expand the diversification toolkit beyond traditional stocks and bonds. As described in [[alternative-investments]], real estate, private equity, commodities, collectibles, cryptocurrency, farmland/timber, and infrastructure each offer different return patterns and correlations with traditional markets. However, alternatives also introduce illiquidity, higher fees, and concentration risks that must be weighed against diversification benefits.

## Practical Framework
The source recommends starting with traditional low-cost index funds as the core (VT, VTI, VXUS) and treating alternatives as a satellite allocation (5-20%). This approach prioritizes liquidity and transparency while adding alternative exposure for inflation protection and non-correlation benefits.
