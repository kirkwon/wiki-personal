# Problems (mental model)

Problems is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

Unlike [[problem-solving]], which focuses on method and process, the Problems mental model treats "problems" as a universal lens for recognizing patterns and relationships across domains. This framing draws from multiple intellectual traditions:

- **John Gall's systems thinking** from *The Systems Bible* emphasizes awareness of how systems behave and interact.
- **James Surowiecki's collective intelligence** from *The Wisdom of Crowds* highlights how groups can recognize patterns individuals miss.
- **Peter M. Senge's organizational learning** from *The Fifth Discipline Fieldbook* stresses systemic pattern recognition within learning organizations.

The model is applicable universally and functions as a way to see complexity not as isolated obstacles but as interconnected patterns worth understanding. It complements related mental models like [[solutions-mental-model]], [[planning]], and [[group]].
