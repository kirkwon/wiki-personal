# Marinades and Mise en Place


## Quick Reference

### Field**: [[gastronomy]]
### Type**: Cooking Technique
### Purpose**: Infuse flavor, tenderize, prepare ingredients
### Key Elements**: Aromatics, soy sauce, timing
### Related Concepts**: [[aromatics]], [[wok-hei]], [[stir-fry]], [[flavor-building]]



## Summary
Marinades are liquid mixtures used to infuse flavor and sometimes tenderize ingredients before cooking. Mise en place (French: "everything in its place") is the practice of preparing all ingredients before cooking begins. Together, they form the foundation of efficient, flavorful cooking, especially in Asian cuisine.

## Marinades

### Purpose
1. **Flavor infusion** - Get seasonings inside the food
2. **Tenderizing** - Enzymes break down proteins (meat)
3. **Moisture retention** - Prevents drying during high heat
4. **Surface preparation** - Creates slight crust when cooked

### Components

#### 1. Acid
- **Chinese**: Rice vinegar, black vinegar
- **Japanese**: Mirin, sake, rice vinegar
- **Western**: Lemon juice, lime, wine
- **Purpose**: Tenderizes (enzymes), adds brightness, balances fat

#### 2. Oil
- **Neutral oils**: Vegetable, canola, grapeseed
- **Asian oils**: Toasted sesame (finish), peanut
- **Purpose**: Coats, retains moisture, carries flavor

#### 3. Seasoning
- **Salty**: Soy sauce, fish sauce, salt
- **Umami**: Oyster sauce, hoisin, fermented bean paste
- **Sweet**: Sugar, honey, mirin
- **Purpose**: Base flavor profile

#### 4. Aromatics
- **Fresh**: Ginger, garlic, scallion
- **Dried**: Five-spice, star anise, cinnamon
- **Purpose**: Complex flavor, aromatic depth

#### 5. Cornstarch (Chinese Technique)
- **Purpose**: Creates velveting effect
- **How**: Dissolved in marinade, coats protein
- **Result**: Silky, tender texture when cooked

### Chinese Marinade Template

**Basic Marinade (for 1 lb protein):**
- Soy sauce: 2 tbsp
- Shaoxing wine (or dry sherry): 1 tbsp
- Cornstarch: 1 tsp
- Sesame oil: 1 tsp
- White pepper: 1/4 tsp
- Ginger (grated): 1 tsp
- Garlic (minced): 1 clove

**Process:**
1. Mix marinade in bowl
2. Add protein, toss to coat
3. Marinate 15-30 minutes (don't over-marinate with soy - gets salty)
4. No need to rinse (unless extremely salty)

### Time Guidelines

| Protein | Minimum | Maximum | Notes |
|---------|----------|----------|--------|
| Chicken | 15 min | 2 hours | Longer = mushy |
| Pork | 30 min | 4 hours | Benefits from longer |
| Beef | 1 hour | 12 hours | Needs time for tenderizing |
| Seafood | 5-10 min | 30 min | Don't overcook! |
| Tofu | 15 min | 1 hour | Absorbs quickly |
| Vegetables | 5-10 min | 30 min | Don't over-marinate |

## Mise en Place

### Definition
French culinary term meaning "everything in its place" - preparing all ingredients before cooking begins. Essential for wok cooking where speed is critical.

### Why It Matters

#### For Wok Cooking
- **Speed** - Wok moves fast, no time to prep during cooking
- **Heat retention** - Stopping to prep drops wok temperature
- **Organization** - Know where everything is
- **Flow** - Rhythmic cooking, not chaotic

#### For Cooking Quality
- **Consistent results** - Same prep every time
- **No burning** - Ingredients ready when needed
- **Better flavor** - Proper marinade times
- **Less stress** - You're in control

### Mise en Place Checklist

#### Before You Start Cooking
- [ ] All proteins marinated (correct time)
- [ ] All vegetables washed, dried, cut
- [ ] All aromatics prepped (garlic minced, ginger sliced)
- [ ] Sauces measured and mixed
- [ ] Garnishes ready (scallions, herbs)
- [ ] Rice cooked (if serving with rice)
- [ ] Wok/skillet preheating

#### Setup for Efficiency
- **Small bowls** - Pre-measure sauces, aromatics
- **Group ingredients** - Proteins together, veg together
- **Plate organization** - Left to right cooking order
- **Waste bowl** - For trimmings, shells, bones

### Professional Workflow

#### 1. Prep First (30-45 min)
- Cut all vegetables
- Mince all garlic/ginger
- Measure all sauces
- Marinate proteins
- Cook rice

#### 2. Setup (5 min)
- Arrange ingredients in cooking order
- Heat wok
- Have serving platter ready

#### 3. Cook (5-15 min)
- Execute recipe
- Everything ready when needed
- No stopping, no hunting

## Common Mistakes

### Marinade Mistakes
- ❌ **Over-marinating seafood** - Texture becomes mushy (10 min max)
- ❌ **Not coating evenly** - Some parts flavored, some bland
- ❌ **Using too much salt** - Soy sauce is already salty
- ❌ **Marinating too long with acid** - Meat breaks down unpleasantly

### Mise en Place Mistakes
- ❌ **Starting without prep** - Chaos, burned food
- ❌ **Forgetting ingredients** - Recipe incomplete
- ❌ **Wrong sizes** - Inconsistent cooking
- ❌ **Not measuring** - Flavors off

## Best Practices

### Marinade Tips
- **Use bowl** large enough to toss food easily
- **Cover** and refrigerate (bacteria grow at room temp)
- **Marinate at room temp** only if cooking within 30 min
- **Save some sauce** - Don't marinate and cook in same liquid (salty, burned bits)
- **Cornstarch trick** - Add to marinade for silky texture

### Mise en Place Tips
- **Prep everything** before turning on stove
- **Small bowls** are your friend (prep sauces)
- **Clean as you go** - Minimize mess
- **Label bowls** if multiple marinades
- **Time yourself** - See how prep improves your flow

## Advanced Techniques

### Dry Rubs
- No liquid, just spices
- Great for roasting, grilling
- Example: Five-spice + salt + sugar
---

### Wet Brining
- Salt water bath (hours to days)
- Juicy, seasoned throughout
- Example: Whole turkey, pork shoulder

### Combination
- Wet brine first (12-24 hours)
- Dry rub before cooking
- Best of both worlds

## Recipes Reference

### Quick Marinade (15 min)
> Soy sauce + sesame oil + garlic + ginger
> Use: Stir-fry, pan-sear, grilling

### Overnight Marinade (8-12 hours)
> Soy + Shaoxing + five-spice + sugar
> Use: Roast duck, pork belly, beef

### Velveting Marinade
> Soy + cornstarch + egg white (optional)
> Use: Chinese restaurant-style stir-fry, beef, chicken
