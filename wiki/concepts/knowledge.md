# Knowledge

Knowledge is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. In David Deutsch's framework, it is not merely justified true belief but explanatory information that has [[explanatory-reach]]—the ability to account for phenomena beyond its origin cases. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Deutsch's Definition

Knowledge is an explanatory framework that survives critical scrutiny and can be applied to new situations. It grows through the cycle of [[conjecture-and-criticism]], not through passive accumulation of data.

## Key Properties

- **Explanatory**: Knowledge explains *why* things happen, not just *what* happens.
- **Reach**: Good knowledge extends beyond its original domain of application.
- **Falsifiable**: Genuine knowledge can be tested and potentially refuted.
- **Growing**: The total stock of knowledge is unbounded; it never ceases to grow.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

This mental model draws from three distinct sources:

- **Sönke Ahrens** — Knowledge management through systematic note-taking and world disclosure, making knowledge visible and actionable.
- **David Epstein** — The value of diverse knowledge and breadth of experience; range improves problem-solving across domains.
- **David Deutsch** — Knowledge as the fundamental creative capacity; knowledge can be created and grows without limit, forming the basis of progress through constructor theory.

## Tension with Justified True Belief

Traditional epistemology defines knowledge as justified true belief. Deutsch rejects this because justification often relies on induction, which he argues is logically invalid. Instead, knowledge is conjectural but can be objectively better or worse based on its explanatory power and ability to withstand criticism.

## Synthesis

These three perspectives converge on a shared framing: knowledge functions as a universal mental shortcut for pattern and relationship recognition in complex situations. Ahrens emphasizes the *practice* of making knowledge actionable, Epstein emphasizes the *breadth* of knowledge as an advantage, and Deutsch emphasizes knowledge as a *fundamental capacity* that drives progress.

## Connection to Mental Models

- [[ideas-mental-model]]: Ideas are the raw material; knowledge is ideas that have survived criticism.
- [[thinking-mental-model]]: Critical thinking is the mechanism for evaluating knowledge claims.
- [[problem-solving]]: Problems are reframed as knowledge gaps.
- [[effort]]: Sustained effort is needed to generate and test conjectures.

## Practical Implications

When building knowledge in any domain:

1. Generate multiple explanations (conjectures) for the phenomenon in question.
2. Critically test each explanation for errors.
3. Retain explanations that survive criticism and possess explanatory reach.
4. Use surviving explanations to identify new problems and new conjectures.
