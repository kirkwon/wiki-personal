
# Experience vs. Algorithms in Expertise

A central argument in Klein's *Sources of Power* is that expertise built through years of deliberate practice is irreplaceable by rules, checklists, or algorithms. This challenges the assumption that formalizing decision processes can substitute for human judgment.

Key points:
- **Expertise is pattern-based**: Experts store vast repertoires of situations and responses as patterns in memory. These patterns enable rapid recognition and intuitive evaluation that algorithms struggle to replicate.
- **Rules miss context**: Formal procedures are brittle—they cannot capture the rich contextual cues that experts use to size up situations. A checklist may tell you what to do, but it cannot tell you what is happening.
- **Experience requires time**: Building the pattern library that underpins expert intuition cannot be accelerated by abstract training alone. It requires exposure to diverse, real (or realistic) scenarios with feedback.
- **Algorithms have a role but not a replacement**: Decision support systems can augment expert judgment by providing data or flagging anomalies, but they should not force rationalistic procedures on people who think naturally through patterns and simulation.

This concept relates to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by emphasizing that the quality of a decision depends on the richness of the decision-maker's experience, not just the formality of the process. It also creates productive tension with [[systematic-decision-making]] and the value of structured processes.
