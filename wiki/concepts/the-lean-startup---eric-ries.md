# The Lean Startup - Eric Ries

*The Lean Startup* (2024) by Eric Ries introduces a methodology for building products and businesses under conditions of extreme uncertainty. Its core claim is that traditional business planning fails when the future is unknowable, so startups should instead run rapid experiments to validate assumptions.

The book is organized into three parts:
- **Vision** (chapters 1-8): Introduces the philosophy, defines the startup, explains validated learning, experimentation, the Build-Measure-Learn loop, measurement, and the pivot-or-persevere decision framework.
- **Steer** (chapters 9-13): Covers small batch production, sustainable growth, adaptation, innovation, and growth engines.
- **Accelerate** (chapters 14-20): Addresses speed, workflow optimization, quality, team building, and culture.

Key concepts include the Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop, the Minimum Viable Product (MVP), validated learning, innovation accounting, pivot-or-persevere decisions, and small batch production. The methodology treats all startup activities as experiments—each feature, campaign, or initiative is a hypothesis to be tested.

The book connects to broader themes of decision-making under uncertainty, probabilistic thinking, and real-options analysis. It shares philosophical roots with Annie Duke's [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] (treating decisions as bets to be updated) and Philip Tetlock's [[superforecasting-methodology]] (updating beliefs based on evidence). It also parallels real-options thinking, where decisions are treated as flexible options rather than irreversible commitments.
