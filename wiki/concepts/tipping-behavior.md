# Tipping Behavior

Tipping behavior is a specific manifestation of irrational decision-making where economic choices are heavily influenced by social cues, emotions, and the desire to appear generous or socially accepted, rather than by rational cost-benefit analysis.

## Core Idea

Dan Ariely demonstrates that tipping—seemingly a rational economic transaction—is driven by social cues, emotions, and irrational factors like the desire for social acceptance. People tip more or less based on factors unrelated to service quality, including mood, social pressure, and perceptions of fairness.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]

## Key Findings

- Tipping is influenced more by social norms and emotional states than by service quality
- The desire to appear generous or socially accepted overrides rational economic calculation
- Contextual cues and first impressions significantly affect tip amounts
- Willingness to tip can be shaped by feelings of guilt or obligation

## Connection to Broader Concepts

This behavior exemplifies how [[irrational-decision-making]] operates in everyday contexts and connects to [[fairness-perception]] and [[social-pressure]] as underlying drivers.
