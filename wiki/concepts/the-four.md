
# The Four

The Four is Scott Galloway's framework identifying Amazon, Apple, Google (Alphabet), and Facebook (Meta) as a new class of market titans that function as a de facto oligopoly reshaping entire industries. Rather than treating these companies as separate competitors, Galloway argues they collectively exert outsized influence over markets, consumer behavior, and business strategy.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]

The framework is structured around four rivalries that illustrate different dimensions of platform power:
- Amazon vs. Apple: retail and technology disruption
- Uber vs. Google: transportation and search dominance
- Facebook vs. Baidu: social media and Chinese tech ecosystems
- Microsoft vs. Tencent: cloud computing, AI, and global tech expansion

Galloway's central claim is that traditional businesses must fundamentally rethink strategy to survive against these companies. The "Battleground" chapter prescribes agility, talent recruitment, innovation focus, and adaptive leadership as key responses.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]

This concept connects to [[strategic-vs-reactive-thinking]] and [[scenario-planning]]—both of which emphasize proactive orientation toward competitive threats. It also raises questions about whether platform dominance is sustainable or whether regulatory and market forces will eventually disrupt the current landscape.
