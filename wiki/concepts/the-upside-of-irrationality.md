# The Upside of Irrationality

Dan Ariely's *The Upside of Irrationality* explores how systematic irrationalities in human decision-making are not merely bugs but can have adaptive or creative functions. The book extends his earlier work on predictable irrationality into the domain of motivation, honesty, and long-term thinking.

## Key Ideas

- **Irrationality is predictable**: Biases are not random errors but consistent patterns.
- **Motivation and meaning**: Irrational attachment to narratives and goals can drive persistence.
- **Honesty dynamics**: People cheat in small ways but maintain self-concept as honest.
- **Decision distortion**: Cognitive biases systematically affect how options are evaluated.

## Connection to Choices

Ariely's work contributes to [[choices]] by showing that irrational patterns are not just obstacles to good decisions but are woven into the fabric of how people choose.^[raw/articles/Choices.md]
