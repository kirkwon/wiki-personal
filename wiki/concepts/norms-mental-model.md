# Norms (mental model)

Norms is a universal mental model that functions as a conceptual tool for understanding and navigating complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world, applicable across domains from personal finance to cooking to photography.

The model is closely connected to behavioral economics and cognitive science. Dan Ariely's [[predictably-irrational-dan-ariely]] examines how social norms and irrational behaviors shape decision-making, providing the primary cross-source perspective for this mental model. This connects to broader work on [[cognitive-biases-library]], [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]], and [[decision-making-frameworks]].

## Relationship to other mental models
- [[biases]] – Norms overlap with bias as a cognitive shortcut, but norms specifically emphasize social conventions and pattern recognition in complex situations.
- [[tendency]] – Both serve as universal tools for pattern recognition and sense-making.
- [[reality-mental-model]] – Norms help navigate reality by recognizing recurring patterns.
- [[effort]] – Norms can inform how effort is allocated based on observed patterns.
