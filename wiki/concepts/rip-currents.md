# Rip Currents

Rip currents are strong, narrow currents moving away from shore that can pull swimmers and waders out to sea. They are a major safety concern at Ocean Beach, capable of pulling people far beyond their ability to swim back.

## Key Characteristics

- **Strong shore currents** that can pull people beyond their ability to swim back
- **Not always visible** — can occur in seemingly calm water
- **Present year-round** but stronger during storm seasons and larger surf
- **Especially strong near shore** and vary by section — Sloat Boulevard is more exposed with stronger currents than Kelly's Cove

## Safety Guidance

- **Do not swim** at Ocean Beach; it is not recommended for swimming
- **Never turn your back on the ocean**
- **Stay back from the water's edge**
- **Check conditions before visiting**
- **If caught in a rip current**: Swim parallel to shore rather than fighting the current
- **Swim only in designated areas** where lifeguards are present (none at Ocean Beach)

Rip currents are listed alongside [[sneaker-waves]] as the two primary water hazards at Ocean Beach. See also [[sneaker-waves]] and [[ocean-beach]] for the full safety context.^[ocean-beach.md]
