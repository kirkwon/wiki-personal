# Skin in the Game

The principle that decision-makers must bear the consequences of their decisions. Taleb argues this is fundamental to fairness, moral integrity, and system resilience. Without skin in the game, agents can externalize risk onto others while capturing upside gains, creating moral hazard and systemic fragility.

## Core Principle

Those who make decisions should experience the consequences—both positive and negative. This accountability ensures that decision-makers internalize risks, reducing reckless behavior and improving outcomes for everyone involved.

- Actors who benefit from outcomes without sharing in the downside create moral hazard.
- Effective systems require symmetric risk exposure between those who make decisions and those affected by them.

## Applications

- **Investment**: Investors who risk their own capital make better decisions than those managing others' money
- **Governance**: Representatives without personal stakes enable special-interest capture
- **Organizations**: Leaders who suffer from poor decisions are incentivized to make better ones
- **Ethics under uncertainty**: Accepting accountability even when outcomes are ambiguous preserves moral integrity

## Connection to Antifragility

Systems with skin in the game are antifragile—they adapt and strengthen under stress because participants have direct stakes in outcomes. This contrasts with fragile systems where decision-makers are insulated from consequences. The principle connects directly to antifragility: systems with skin in the game are more likely to adapt and improve under stress.
