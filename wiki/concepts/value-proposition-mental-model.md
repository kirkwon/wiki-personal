# Value Proposition Mental Model

## Definition
A mental model for evaluating decisions based on their potential impact on life goals, priorities, and values. It helps filter choices through the lens of what truly matters.

## Key Points
- Decisions should be evaluated not just on financial metrics but on their alignment with personal values and life priorities
- The value proposition considers both quantitative (expected value) and qualitative (meaning, purpose, fulfillment) dimensions
- This model helps prevent optimizing for metrics that don't reflect true life satisfaction
- It connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by separating the quality of the decision process from the quality of the outcome
- Related to [[expected-value-ev]] but extends beyond pure probabilistic calculation to include value alignment
- Reframes decision-making from optimization to alignment
- Helps filter opportunities against life goals and evaluate trade-offs between competing priorities
- Avoids decisions that are technically sound but misaligned with values

## Explanation
Rather than evaluating decisions purely on expected outcomes, immediate benefits, or financial returns, the value proposition model asks: does this decision align with my core values and long-term priorities?

## Application
Before making significant decisions—career moves, investments, time allocations—asking "What is the value proposition?" forces explicit consideration of whether the choice serves long-term goals and values. This means filtering opportunities against life goals, evaluating trade-offs between competing priorities, and avoiding decisions that are technically sound but misaligned with values.
