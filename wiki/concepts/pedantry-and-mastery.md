# Pedantry and Mastery

Pedantry and Mastery refers to the distinction between knowing rules and truly understanding them. Polya draws attention to the difference between rigid rule-following (pedantry) and deep comprehension that enables flexible application of principles. True mastery involves internalizing heuristics so they become intuitive tools rather than mechanical procedures.

This concept connects to the broader theme in *How to Solve It* that problem-solving is a learnable skill—though learning goes beyond memorizing steps. It requires developing judgment about when and how to apply different strategies, and the wisdom to know when a framework is helping versus when it is becoming a crutch.

Pedantry can manifest as rigidly applying frameworks without thinking, which Polya explicitly flags as a reason to stop systematic problem-solving. Mastery, by contrast, involves creative intuition working alongside systematic approaches.
