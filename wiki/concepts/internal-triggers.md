
# Internal Triggers

Internal triggers are emotional states, thoughts, or sensations that cue a behavior or action. In Nir Eyal's Hooked model, internal triggers are the first step in the engagement loop—they are the reasons a user thinks about taking action. Unlike external triggers (notifications, ads, cues in the environment), internal triggers originate from within the user — they feel like their own idea rather than a response to an external cue.

## How They Form

Internal triggers develop when a product becomes reliably associated with a specific emotional state. Common internal triggers include:
- **Boredom** — reaching for social media or entertainment
- **Loneliness** — checking messaging apps or social feeds
- **Curiosity** — opening news apps or search engines
- **Anxiety** — scrolling for distraction
- **FOMO** — fear of missing out driving app checks

## Significance

Internal triggers are the ultimate goal of habit-forming design. When a product can trigger itself through emotional association, it no longer needs external prompts to drive engagement. This is what distinguishes a habit from mere repeated use.

## Connection to Cognitive Biases

Internal triggers exploit predictable psychological patterns. The anticipation created by [[variable-reward-design]] leverages the same dopaminergic mechanisms that underlie [[cognitive-biases-library]] phenomena like anticipation bias and [[dread-aversion]]. Users often confuse the emotional relief of using the product with genuine satisfaction, similar to how [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] can be conflated.

## Connection to Internal Trigger Mapping

Internal trigger mapping extends this concept into a systematic productivity practice—identifying which internal triggers lead to unproductive behavior, then restructuring workflows to address them. The mapping process involves auditing current workflows, applying frameworks to focus on high-impact activities, and iterating based on results.
