# Social Norms and Economic Behavior

Social norms are the unwritten rules and expectations that shape economic behavior through conformity, peer influence, and societal expectations. Richard Thaler's behavioral economics framework emphasizes that these norms significantly influence decisions in ways traditional economic models ignore.

## Definition
Social norms are behavioral standards that are perceived as appropriate or expected within a group or society. They exert pressure on individual choices through social approval/disapproval, without formal enforcement.

## Types of Social Norms
- **Descriptive norms**: What people actually do ("most people save for retirement").
- **Injunctive norms**: What people think they should do ("you should tip 20%").
- **Personal norms**: Internalized standards of behavior.

## Economic Impact
- Tax compliance increases when people believe others pay their taxes.
- Energy consumption drops when neighbors reduce usage (revealed by comparison data).
- Charitable giving increases when social visibility is high.
- Savings behavior is influenced by what peers earn and spend.

## Connection to Other Concepts
Social norms interact with [[framing-effect]] (norms frame what is "normal") and [[choice-architecture]] (showing social norms in choice environments can nudge behavior). Understanding these dynamics improves [[decision-making-frameworks]] and connects to [[communication-skills]].

## Key Insight
Economic outcomes are not determined solely by individual rational calculation—they are shaped by the social environment in which decisions are made.
