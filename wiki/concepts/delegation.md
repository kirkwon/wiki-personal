# Delegation

Delegation is a universal mental model that serves as a conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern and relationship recognition. Rather than a simple task-assignment technique, it functions as a framework shortcut for making sense of how responsibilities, authority, and outcomes interconnect in organizational and personal contexts. In the context of *Making It All Work*, delegation is the practice of entrusting tasks and responsibilities to others as a core productivity strategy — not a sign of weakness but essential for sustainable workload management.

## Key Ideas

- Effective delegation requires trust in others' capabilities and clear communication of expectations.
- Delegation is a key tool for managing workload: allowing others to handle certain responsibilities frees capacity for higher-value work.
- Poor delegation leads to bottlenecks, burnout, and an inability to focus on strategic priorities.
- The 2-minute rule and other GTD techniques support delegation by ensuring captured tasks are appropriately routed—either to action, delegation, or elimination.
- Knowing what to delegate — and what to keep — requires clarity on personal strengths, values, and purpose.

## Mental Model Perspective

As a mental model, delegation helps distinguish between decisions that must be made personally and those that can be entrusted to others. This distinction is central to [[management]] and [[effort]] — knowing what to delegate amplifies [[leverage]] by multiplying the capacity to act without proportionally increasing personal workload. David Allen's work in [[making-it-all-work---david-allen]] frames delegation within a broader productivity system where clarity of commitments and next actions determines whether delegation succeeds or fails.

Delegation connects to [[recognition-primed-decision-model]] in that experienced delegators often rely on pattern recognition — knowing which people or teams can handle which types of decisions based on past outcomes. It also relates to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]], since the quality of a delegated decision depends on the quality of the person receiving it, not just the act of delegation itself. In team contexts, delegation supports [[building-a-cohesive-team]] by distributing ownership and fostering trust.

## When to Delegate

When to delegate versus decide personally remains a core tension. The [[decision-making-frameworks]] cluster and [[systematic-decision-making]] approaches provide guidance on evaluating decision importance, reversibility, and required expertise before choosing to delegate. The [[checklists]] mental model complements delegation by ensuring that handoff processes include clear inputs, expected outputs, and accountability structures.

## Delegation Is Not Abdication

Delegation is not abdication — it requires ongoing awareness of results and the willingness to intervene when patterns deviate from expectations. This aligns with the broader principle that mental models should be tools for sense-making rather than rigid rules.
