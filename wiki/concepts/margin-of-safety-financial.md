# Margin of Safety (Financial)

In financial planning, margin of safety means leaving room for error in plans, assuming things will go wrong, and building in buffers to protect against uncertainty.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Core Principles

- Plan for things to take longer than expected.
- Use conservative assumptions rather than optimistic ones.
- Build financial buffers for rare but devastating events.
- Assume average outcomes will not materialize—prepare for tail events.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Practical Application

- Maintain emergency funds beyond minimum recommendations.
- Use lower return assumptions when planning retirement.
- Avoid concentration risk in investments.
- Build in buffers for adverse outcomes.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Connection to Staying Wealthy

This concept is essential for staying wealthy. Paranoid risk management and pessimism about outcomes help preserve capital over time.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Connection to Risk Management

This connects to [[risk-assessment-framework]] and [[real-options-analysis]] by emphasizing that building buffers and flexibility is more valuable than precise prediction. It also relates to [[reasonable-vs-rational-decisions]]—a margin of safety makes financial decisions psychologically sustainable even when things go wrong.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

Practicing "tail event" thinking — assuming rare events will happen and preparing for financial shocks — is an extension of the margin of safety principle.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]
