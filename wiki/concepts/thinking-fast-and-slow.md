# Thinking, Fast and Slow - Daniel Kahneman

_by Daniel Kahneman_

## Chapters

### Chapter 1: The Tale of Two Systems
Brief description of how humans think using two systems: System 1 (fast, intuitive) and System 2 (slow, deliberate). Introduces the foundational concepts of dual-process theory.

### Chapter 2: Heuristics and Biases
Explores common shortcuts used by our intuition (heuristics), their benefits, but also how they can lead to systematic errors in judgment (biases).

### Chapter 3: Overconfidence and Certainty
Discusses overconfidence in judgments and decisions, the illusion of control, and how certainty often misguides us despite uncertainty.

### Chapter 4: Loss Aversion
Explains the phenomenon where losses loom larger than gains, influencing decision-making and risk assessment.

### Chapter 5: The Endowment Effect and Ascription Bias
Details how we assign higher value to things merely because we own them (endowment effect), and our tendency to attribute qualities to ourselves more so than others (ascription bias).

### Chapter 6: Framing
Illustrates how the way a problem or choice is presented can significantly alter decision-making, despite no change in outcome.

### Chapter 7: The Role of Money and Dread Aversion
Investigates the impact of money on our preferences and choices, as well as the influence of dread aversion towards negative outcomes.

### Chapter 8: The Hindsight Bias
Examines how we often believe after an event has occurred that we would have predicted or expected it to happen (hindsight bias).

### Chapter 9: Probability and Judgment Under Uncertainty
Introduces the concept of probability, challenges in judging probabilities accurately, and insights from judgment under uncertainty.

### Chapter 10: The Power of Focus Groups and Group Decision-Making
Explores how group settings can lead to conformity and polarization, affecting individual and collective decision-making.
