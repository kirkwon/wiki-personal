# Strategic Distraction

Strategic distraction is the intentional use of distractions as a tool rather than treating all distraction as the enemy. Instead of pursuing total digital abstinence, Bailey advocates using distractions deliberately to manage attention, energy, and creative needs.

## Rationale
- Total elimination of distractions is often unsustainable
- Controlled exposure to distraction can prevent binge behavior
- Strategic breaks with chosen distractions restore attention capacity
- The goal is conscious control, not deprivation

## Practice
- Choose specific distraction activities rather than consuming whatever is available
- Use distractions as planned restorative breaks, not reactive escapes
- Time-box distraction to prevent bleed into focused work
- Pair with [[attention-budget]] principles to stay within allocated limits

This contrasts with [[digital-minimalism]] which emphasizes reduction, while strategic distraction emphasizes intentional management.
