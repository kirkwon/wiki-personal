# Pasta Shapes and Sauces

Pasta shape dictates the ideal sauce pairing — this is a fundamental principle of Italian gastronomy. The shape influences sauce adhesion, mouthfeel, and the overall eating experience. Each shape has an ideal sauce based on its form, texture, and regional tradition.

## Long Pasta — Oil, Tomato, Seafood

Long shapes trap thin, flowing sauces and pair naturally with oil-based, tomato, and seafood preparations.

- **Spaghetti**: Round, thin — Tomato, aglio e olio, clam sauce
- **Linguine**: Flat, thin — Seafood, pesto, cream
- **Fettuccine**: Flat, wide — Creamy sauces
- **Tagliatelle**: Flat, wider — Bolognese ragù, cream
- **Pappardelle**: Very wide — Game ragù
- **Bucatini**: Thick, hollow — Amatriciana, carbonara

## Short Pasta — Chunky, Baked

Short shapes hold chunky sauces and work well in baked preparations.

- **Penne**: Angled cut, hollow — Chunky tomato, vodka sauce
- **Rigatoni**: Wide, ridged — Meat ragù, baked
- **Ziti**: Smooth, straight — Baked ziti
- **Fusilli**: Corkscrew — Pesto, creamy sauces
- **Orecchiette**: "Little ears" — Broccoli rabe, sausage
- **Conchiglie**: Shells — Chunky sauces, baked

## Stuffed Pasta — Light Sauces

Stuffed pasta carries its own flavor, so it pairs best with light, simple sauces.

- **Ravioli**: Square, various fillings — Brown butter sage, light tomato
- **Tortellini**: Ring-shaped — Broth (in brodo), light cream
- **Agnolotti**: Half-moon — Meat, herbs; Piedmont tradition
- **Cappelletti**: Hat-shaped — Meat, cheese; Emilia-Romagna

## The Role of Pasta Water

Reserved starchy pasta water acts as an emulsifier, binding sauce to pasta. This is essential in dishes like cacio e pepe and carbonara, where the emulsion is created by combining cheese, fat, and starchy water.

## Key Principles

- Long pasta works well with oil-based and ragù sauces that coat the strands, while short and stuffed shapes pair better with chunky or light sauces.
- Always finish cooking the pasta in the sauce rather than serving them separately.
