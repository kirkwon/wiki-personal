# Compounding Time

Wealth grows exponentially through time, not just through returns. Compounding time is arguably the greatest asset in building wealth, and its value is often underestimated. Time is the greatest asset in building wealth.

## Key Examples

- $1 invested at 10% for 30 years = $17.45.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]
- Warren Buffett's wealth: 90% accumulated after age 65.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]
- Time in market > timing the market.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Behavioral Implications

The main behavioral challenge is **not interrupting compounding.** People who start early—even with small amounts—benefit enormously from decades of uninterrupted growth. The temptation to withdraw during downturns, chase returns, or spend windfalls directly undermines this advantage. The seduction of optimism — believing you can beat the market or time entries and exits — leads to interrupting compounding through overtrading and excessive risk-taking.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

## Practical Application

- Start early, even with small amounts.
- Focus on not interrupting compounding.
- Match investments to timeline—don't need money soon? Take more risk; need money soon? Be conservative.
- Practice "tail event" thinking: assume rare events will happen and prepare for financial shocks.
- Time in market > timing the market.^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]
