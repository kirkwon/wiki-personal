# System 1 vs System 2 Thinking

System 1 and System 2 are the two modes of thinking described by Daniel Kahneman in *Thinking, Fast and Slow*. System 1 is fast, intuitive, and automatic — it handles routine judgments, pattern recognition, and emotional reactions with minimal effort. System 2 is slow, deliberate, and effortful — it engages in logical analysis, complex computations, and careful evaluation of evidence.

**System 1 characteristics**:
- Fast, automatic, effortless
- Generates impressions, feelings, and intuitions
- Prone to [[heuristics-problem-solving]] and [[cognitive-biases]]
- Handles everyday tasks like reading, recognizing faces, driving on empty roads

**System 2 characteristics**:
- Slow, deliberate, effortful
- Engages in logical reasoning and statistical calculations
- Capable of overriding System 1 errors but often lazy
- Activated by complex problems, novel situations, or when System 1 detects conflict

The interaction between these systems explains much of human irrationality. System 1 provides quick answers that are often good enough, but it systematically errs in areas like [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]], [[framing-effects]], and [[overconfidence-and-certainty]]. System 2 can correct these errors but requires motivation and cognitive resources.

This framework connects to [[dual-process-theory]] and underpins the entire [[cognitive-biases-library]].
