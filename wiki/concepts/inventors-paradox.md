# Inventor's Paradox

The Inventor's Paradox is the counterintuitive observation that more ambitious, general problems can sometimes be easier to solve than restricted or specific ones. By broadening the scope of the problem, you may gain access to more powerful techniques, symmetries, or structural insights that were hidden in the narrow case.

Polya presents this as a heuristic worth keeping in mind: if a problem resists solution, consider whether generalizing it might make it tractable. This principle appears in mathematics and engineering, where elegant general solutions often subsume messy special cases.

It connects to the broader heuristic toolkit in [[heuristic-problem-solving]] and Polya's emphasis on [[specialization-and-generalization]] as complementary strategies.
