# Critical Rationalism

Critical rationalism is the epistemological tradition, originating with Karl Popper and championed by David Deutsch, which rejects inductivism and holds that knowledge grows through conjecture and criticism — the generation and testing of explanatory hypotheses. David Deutsch extends Popper's ideas to encompass all forms of knowledge, not just scientific theories, and adds the concept of explanatory reach as a criterion for evaluating explanations.

## Core Tenets

- **No induction**: Observations cannot logically justify general theories. All theories remain conjectural. Knowledge does not grow by accumulating observations and forming generalizations.
- **Falsification over confirmation**: Scientific theories must be testable and refutable; unfalsifiable claims are not scientific. Theories cannot be proven true but can be proven false; surviving criticism is the criterion for knowledge.
- **Conjecture and criticism**: The cycle of proposing explanations and refuting errors is the engine of knowledge growth.
- **Explanations over predictions**: The value of a theory lies in its explanatory power, not its predictive accuracy alone.
- **Objective knowledge**: Knowledge can be objective despite being conjectural, because criticism is a rational, truth-tracking process. Knowledge can be objective even though it is created by human minds.
- **Error-correction**: The primary mechanism of progress is identifying and eliminating errors.
- **Explanatory reach**: Deutsch's addition to the Popperian framework — how far an explanation reaches in accounting for phenomena.

## Relationship to Karl Popper

Deutsch builds on Popper's rejection of inductivism and his emphasis on falsification. In *The Beginning of Infinity*, Deutsch extends Popper's ideas to encompass all forms of knowledge, not just scientific theories, and adds explanatory reach as a criterion for evaluating explanations.

## Contrast with Other Epistemologies

- **Against inductivism**: Rejects the idea that observations can justify theories (connects to [[the-problem-of-induction-revisited]])
- **Against Bayesianism**: While [[bayesian-thinking]] uses probabilistic updating, critical rationalism holds that probabilities cannot capture the explanatory content of theories. This creates a genuine tension: Bayesian thinking is practically powerful for prediction and decision-making, but critical rationalism offers a deeper philosophical account of how explanatory knowledge grows. Both frameworks can be useful in different contexts.
- **Against relativism**: Knowledge is objective and can be improved through criticism, not merely a matter of perspective
- **Against empiricism**: Experience alone cannot generate knowledge; conjecture is necessary

## Tension with Bayesian Epistemology

The wiki's [[bayesian-thinking]] framework updates beliefs based on evidence using probability theory. Critical rationalism rejects this as a form of induction. This creates a genuine tension in the wiki's epistemological coverage:
- Bayesian thinking is practically powerful for prediction and decision-making.
- Critical rationalism offers a deeper philosophical account of how explanatory knowledge grows.
- Both frameworks can be useful in different contexts, and the wiki should present this tension rather than resolve it prematurely.

## Connection to Decision-Making

Critical rationalism connects to [[decision-making-frameworks]] by providing an alternative to both Bayesian decision theory and heuristic approaches. It emphasizes:

- Generating multiple conjectures rather than selecting from known options
- Critically testing assumptions rather than relying on probability estimates
- Evaluating the explanatory reach of solutions rather than their expected value
