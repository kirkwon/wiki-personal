# Coordination

Coordination is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Definition

Coordination functions as a universal mental model applicable across all domains. Rather than being a simple aggregation of inputs, coordination emphasizes the ability to align, structure, and synthesize distributed information and actions to produce better outcomes than any individual decision-making process alone.

## Connection to Collective Intelligence

James Surowiecki's [[the-wisdom-of-crowds]] provides the foundational cross-source perspective on coordination through crowd wisdom. Surowiecki argues that diverse groups of people can produce better collective decisions than individual experts when certain conditions are met: independence of opinion, decentralization of knowledge, and aggregation of results. Coordination as a mental model builds on this insight by asking not just "how do we collect inputs" but "how do we coordinate those inputs effectively."

## Relationship to Other Concepts

Coordination differs from the [[group]] mental model in that it specifically emphasizes the process of aligning distributed elements—whether people, information, or resources—rather than simply recognizing group dynamics. It connects to:

- [[group-decision-polarization]] — Coordination can counteract polarization by structuring how diverse viewpoints are integrated
- [[communication-skills]] — Effective coordination requires clear communication channels
- [[building-a-cohesive-team]] — Team cohesion is one manifestation of successful coordination
- [[decision-making-frameworks]] — Coordination provides a meta-framework for selecting and combining decision approaches

## Open Questions

How does coordination as a mental model differ from or complement the existing [[group]] mental model? Coordination may be best understood as the active process dimension of group dynamics, while the group model captures the structural and relational patterns.
