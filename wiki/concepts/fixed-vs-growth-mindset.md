# Fixed vs. Growth Mindset

The fixed vs. growth mindset framework contrasts two fundamental beliefs about human abilities. A **fixed mindset** holds that intelligence, talent, and character are static traits determined at birth. A **growth mindset**, by contrast, holds that these qualities can be developed through effort, strategy, and learning from feedback.

## Comparison

| Dimension | Fixed Mindset | Growth Mindset |
|---|---|---|
| **Core Belief** | Abilities are innate and unchangeable | Abilities can be developed through effort and learning |
| **Response to Challenge** | Avoids challenges to protect self-image | Embraces challenges as opportunities to grow |
| **Response to Failure** | Interprets failure as evidence of permanent deficiency | Views failure as information for improvement |
| **Effort** | Sees effort as a sign of inadequacy | Sees effort as a path to mastery |
| **Feedback** | Resists criticism | Learns from criticism and feedback |
| **Inspiration from Others** | Feels threatened by others' success | Finds inspiration in others' success |
| **Impact on Motivation** | Reduced persistence after setbacks | Sustained motivation and resilience |

## Impact on Behavior

- **Fixed mindset**: Avoids challenges, gives up easily, sees effort as pointless, ignores useful criticism, and feels threatened by others' success.
- **Growth mindset**: Embraces challenges, persists through setbacks, sees effort as the path to mastery, learns from criticism, and finds inspiration in others' achievement.

## Connection to Grit

This distinction is central to [[grit-concept]] and [[growth-mindset]]. Duckworth argues that a growth mindset enables the sustained effort required for grit—without it, perseverance collapses at the first major setback because failure is interpreted as a permanent deficit rather than a temporary condition.

## Broader Implications

The mindset one adopts affects goal-setting, response to failure, willingness to take risks, and ultimately the trajectory of long-term achievement. It connects to [[embracing-failure-for-personal-growth]] and [[dual-process-theory]] through its influence on how people process feedback and regulate effort.

## Key Insight

The shift from a fixed to a growth mindset is not merely an abstract belief change — it has measurable effects on performance, learning, and well‑being across education, workplace, and personal development contexts.
