# Planetary Boundaries

Planetary boundaries define the safe operating space for humanity within the Earth's biophysical systems. This concept, closely related to the framework introduced in *Limits to Growth*, identifies key environmental thresholds—such as climate change, biodiversity loss, nitrogen and phosphorus cycles, freshwater use, and land system change—beyond which abrupt or irreversible environmental changes become likely.

The 30-Year Update emphasizes that continued growth in resource extraction and pollution risks breaching these boundaries. Sustainable futures require operating within these limits while meeting human needs, a tension central to [[satisfaction-under-constraints]].

## Key Boundaries
- Climate change
- Biodiversity loss
- Nitrogen and phosphorus cycles
- Stratospheric ozone depletion
- Ocean acidification
- Freshwater use
- Land use change
- Atmospheric aerosol loading
- Chemical pollution

Understanding these boundaries is essential for integrating ecological constraints into economic models and political governance structures.
