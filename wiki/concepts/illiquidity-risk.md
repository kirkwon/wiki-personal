# Illiquidity Risk

Illiquidity risk is the inability or danger that alternative investments cannot be sold quickly or at a fair price. It is a central concern for alternative investments, which often lack the daily pricing and active markets available for traditional stocks and bonds, and is one of the five major risk categories for alternative investments.

## Examples by Asset Class

- **Real estate**: 6-12 months to sell a property
- **Private equity**: 5-10 year lockup periods
- **Collectibles**: Finding a buyer, negotiating, and verifying authenticity can take considerable time
- **Farmland and timber**: Years to sell due to specialized nature
- **Infrastructure**: Long investment horizons with limited secondary markets

## Mitigation Strategies

- Only invest money you don't need for 5+ years
- Maintain an adequate emergency fund before allocating to alternatives
- Keep alternatives in long-term portfolio allocations
- Diversify across multiple alternative assets to reduce concentration of illiquid positions
- Understand liquidity constraints before investing

## Connection to Decision Quality and Portfolio Allocation

Illiquidity risk connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] because being forced to sell at a bad time due to liquidity needs can produce poor outcomes even from sound decisions. This reinforces the importance of matching investment horizons to financial goals.

The illiquidity of alternatives is a key reason for limiting alternative allocation to 5-20% of total portfolio — investors need sufficient liquid reserves for near-term needs.
