# Savings Rate vs Returns

The central insight of this framework is that building wealth has little to do with your income or investment returns and lots to do with your savings rate. Savings is defined as income minus expectations, not income minus spending.

## Why Savings Rate Dominates

Investment returns are uncertain and uncontrollable. Savings rate is a behavioral choice you can influence directly. Even modest savings rates sustained over long periods with compounding time produce significant wealth.

**Formula**: Savings = Income − Expectations (not Income − Spending)

This reframes the conversation: instead of trying to earn more or invest better, focus on defining what "enough" means and adjusting expectations downward.

## Lifestyle Inflation

As income increases, spending tends to increase correspondingly, leaving savings rate unchanged. The key is to consciously prevent lifestyle inflation by:
- Defining "enough" to avoid endless consumption
- Automating savings before discretionary spending
- Using mental accounting to separate savings from spending budgets

## Connection to Compounding

Savings rate matters because it determines how much capital is available for compounding. [[compounding-time]] shows that time in market matters more than timing the market—so consistent savings contributions over decades amplify this effect significantly.
