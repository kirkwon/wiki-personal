# Clustering Illusion

The tendency to see patterns in random data, leading to superstitions and false causal beliefs. Humans naturally seek meaning and structure, so we perceive clusters, streaks, and coincidences in data that is actually random.

## Key Points
- Random events will produce clusters by chance alone
- We mistake these clusters for meaningful patterns
- Leads to superstitions, false causal beliefs, and poor investment decisions
- Connects to [[probabilistic-illusion]] — poor intuitive understanding of probability

## Example
Seeing a "hot streak" in coin flips or stock performance and believing the pattern will continue, when in reality the streak is purely random.

## Guardrail
Ask whether the pattern could reasonably occur by chance alone, and compare to what random data would look like.
