# Inversion Thinking

Inversion is a problem-solving technique that involves thinking about problems backward or from opposite perspectives. Instead of asking "how do I achieve success?" you ask "how would I guarantee failure?" and then avoid those things.

## Core Idea
By reversing the problem, you can often identify obstacles, risks, and failure modes that forward-thinking misses. This is particularly useful for risk management and avoiding common pitfalls.

## Applications
- **Risk avoidance**: Instead of optimizing for success, identify what would cause failure and eliminate those factors.
- **Strategic planning**: Consider what competitors would do to destroy your business, then build defenses.
- **Personal improvement**: Rather than listing habits that lead to success, list habits that guarantee failure and remove them.

## Relationship to Other Models
Inversion connects to [[first-principles-thinking]] (breaking down to fundamentals), [[lateral-thinking]] (indirect approaches), and [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]] (starting from desired end states). Weinberg includes inversion as a key creativity and innovation tool in *Super Thinking*.

## Guardrail
Inversion is powerful but can become contrarianism for its own sake. The "when to stop" criteria from [[super-thinking-gabriel-weinberg]] warn against using complexity to sound smart rather than add value.
