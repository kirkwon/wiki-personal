# Big Cycle Framework

The Big Cycle Framework, developed by Ray Dalio, describes the long-term cyclical patterns through which empires and dominant powers rise, peak, and decline. It provides a structured way to analyze how global power shifts occur over decades and centuries.

The framework identifies several key determinants of national success and decline:
1. Education and innovation
2. Competitiveness
3. The value of the currency
4. Debt levels and credit availability
5. Internal stability and social cohesion
6. External stability and geopolitical position
7. The role of reserve currency status
8. The balance of power between internal and external forces

Historical cycles—Dutch, British, American—follow similar trajectories: a nation accumulates wealth and power, reaches a peak of dominance, then gradually loses relative standing as debt grows, internal conflicts emerge, and competing powers rise. Dalio argues the current transition from American to Chinese dominance follows this same pattern.

The framework emphasizes that these cycles are not deterministic but can be navigated with better decision-making. Understanding where a nation sits in the cycle helps identify investment opportunities, policy risks, and strategic pivots.
