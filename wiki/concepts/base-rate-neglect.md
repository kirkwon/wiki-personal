# Base Rate Neglect

Base rate neglect is the tendency to ignore general prevalence information (prior probabilities) in favor of specific, individuating information. People focus on vivid, specific details while overlooking the statistical base rates that should anchor their judgments. This is a failure of Bayesian thinking where people overweight specific evidence and underweight general statistical patterns.

## Key Ideas

- Start with prior probabilities before updating with new evidence
- Specific cases are more vivid but less reliable than base rates
- Avoiding base rate neglect is a core Bayesian discipline
- Related to [[bayesian-thinking]] and [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]]

## Example

- Fearing shark attacks (1 in 3.7 million) more than heart disease (1 in 6)
- Ignoring overall cancer rates when evaluating a specific symptom
- When predicting rare events, people often ignore how rare the event is, leading to overconfident predictions

## Impact

Misallocation of attention, resources, and fear. This bias causes people to overreact to rare but dramatic events while underweighting common but less dramatic risks.

## Mitigation

Always consider base rates first. Before evaluating specific information, establish the general prevalence and use that as your starting point. Use Bayesian updating to combine base rates with new evidence.
