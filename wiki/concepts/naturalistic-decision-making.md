# Naturalistic Decision Making

## Overview

Naturalistic Decision Making (NDM) is a research framework developed by [[gary-klein]] that studies how people make decisions in real-world, time-pressured, high-stakes environments under ambiguity and limited information. It emerged as a reaction to classical decision theory's laboratory-based models that assume people have the time and resources to evaluate all options rationally. Unlike traditional decision research conducted in laboratories with undergraduates making hypothetical choices, NDM researchers observe experts in their natural settings — firefighters commanding structure fires, emergency room triage nurses, tank commanders in simulated combat, nuclear plant operators managing simulated emergencies, as well as chess players and business strategists.

The core finding of NDM is that expert decision-makers rarely engage in formal comparison of options. They don't generate lists of alternatives, assign probabilities, weigh pros and cons, and calculate expected values. Instead, they rely on experience-driven pattern recognition to size up situations, recognize typical scenarios, and implement previously successful courses of action. [[gary-klein]] formalized this as the [[recognition-primed-decision-model]] (RPD), which describes how experts match current situations to prior experience through rapid, intuitive judgment.

NDM represents a fundamentally different lens on decision-making from the heuristics-and-biases tradition of [[daniel-kahneman]]. Where Kahneman's work emphasizes the failures of intuition and the need for deliberative correction, NDM emphasizes the power of intuition in skilled practitioners and seeks to understand how it works so it can be trained. This tension led to a famous adversarial collaboration between Kahneman and Klein, who ultimately agreed that intuitive expertise is real but domain-specific — it develops only in "high-validity" environments with clear feedback and ample practice opportunities.

## Key Points

- Studies experts in natural settings rather than novices in laboratories
- Experts rely on pattern recognition from extensive experience rather than option comparison
- The [[recognition-primed-decision-model]] is the core theoretical framework
- Mental simulation allows rapid evaluation of a single course of action
- Metaphor and analogy enable expertise transfer across domains
- Storytelling is a primary mechanism for knowledge sharing and organizational learning
- NDM emphasizes situational awareness, sense-making, and experience over computation
- Intuitive expertise is genuine but limited to high-validity environments with good feedback
- Validated across domains including firefighting, nursing, military command, chess, and business strategy
- The adversarial collaboration between [[gary-klein]] and [[daniel-kahneman]] helped delineate when intuition works and when it fails
- Provides an empirically grounded alternative to purely rational models
- Connects to [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]] and [[decision-making-frameworks]]
