# Diversity in Systems

Diversity is identified by Meadows as a fundamental source of adaptability and resilience in complex adaptive systems. Systems with greater diversity of agents, strategies, and responses are better equipped to handle change and disturbance.

In social systems, this means cultural diversity, varied perspectives, and multiple approaches to problem-solving strengthen the system as a whole. In ecological systems, biodiversity provides resilience against shocks. Meadows argues that homogeneity — even when efficient in the short term — reduces a system's capacity to respond to novel challenges.

This concept connects to [[grit-concept]] and [[growth-mindset]] by suggesting that diversity of approach and tolerance for variation are systemic qualities, not just individual traits.
