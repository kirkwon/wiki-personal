# Platform Dominance

Platform dominance refers to the outsized market power wielded by technology companies that control critical infrastructure, data flows, and ecosystem lock-in. Scott Galloway's *The Four* frames Amazon, Apple, Google, and Facebook as the primary exemplars of this phenomenon—companies whose scale, data advantages, and network effects make them extraordinarily difficult for traditional businesses to compete against.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-gallowry.md]

Key mechanisms of platform dominance include:
- **Scale advantages**: Economies of scale that smaller competitors cannot match
- **Data accumulation**: User data that improves products and creates switching costs
- **Ecosystem lock-in**: Interconnected services that make it costly for users to leave
- **Winner-take-most dynamics**: Markets that reward the dominant player disproportionately

Galloway argues that competing against platform titans requires fundamentally different strategies than traditional competition—emphasizing agility, niche focus, and innovation rather than head-to-head rivalry.^[raw/articles/the-four---scott-galloway.md]

This concept relates to [[real-options-analysis]] by highlighting how businesses need flexible strategies that can adapt as platform dynamics shift, and to [[strategic-vs-reactive-thinking]] by underscoring the importance of proactive positioning rather than reactive responses to dominant players.
