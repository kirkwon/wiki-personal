# Decoy Effect

A cognitive bias where the introduction of an irrelevant third option influences choice between two other options. By adding a decoy that makes one preferred option look more attractive relative to the other, people's selections shift in predictable ways.

## How It Works

The decoy effect operates through relative comparison rather than absolute evaluation. When a decoy option is positioned to make one target option seem superior in comparison, individuals disproportionately choose that target — even when the decoy itself would never be selected.

## Applications

- Pricing strategies where a third tier makes the middle option look like the best deal
- Marketing campaigns that structure choices to steer preferences
- User experience design that presents default or highlighted options alongside less appealing alternatives
- Financial decisions where framing investment options relative to each other shifts allocation

## Connection to Relativity

The decoy effect is a direct manifestation of Ariely's principle that we value options relative to comparisons, even when those comparisons are arbitrary. It pairs with [[anchoring]] and [[framing-effects]] as a tool for understanding and designing better choice environments.
