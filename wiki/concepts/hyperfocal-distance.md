# Hyperfocal Distance

Hyperfocal distance is the closest distance at which a lens can be focused while keeping objects at infinity acceptably sharp. It is a key technique for maximizing depth of field in street photography.

## What It Is

The hyperfocal distance is the point at which the lens focuses where everything from half that distance to infinity remains in focus. This provides the maximum possible depth of field for a given aperture.

## How to Use It

1. **Calculate or use an app**: Many smartphone apps can compute hyperfocal distance for your lens and aperture
2. **Set focus**: At the hyperfocal distance
3. **Shoot freely**: Everything from half that distance to infinity will be sharp

## Why Use It

- **Maximum depth of field**: Ensures the widest possible range of sharpness
- **Pre-focus**: Allows shooting without refocusing, enabling faster capture
- **Versatility**: Useful for street scenes with subjects at varying distances

## Connection to Street Photography

Hyperfocal distance complements [[zone-focusing]] as a pre-focusing strategy. Both techniques prioritize speed and discretion, enabling photographers to capture the [[decisive-moment]] without fumbling with focus.
