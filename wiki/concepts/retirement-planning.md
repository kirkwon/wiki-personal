# Retirement Planning

## Quick Reference

- **Field**: [[personal-clients]]
- **Type**: Financial Planning
- **Purpose**: Build wealth to support lifestyle after work
- **Key Principles**: Start early, maximize tax advantages, automate
- **Related Concepts**: [[risk-management]], [[options-trading]], [[alternative-investments]]

## Summary

Retirement planning involves saving and investing during working years to build a portfolio that generates income in retirement. Key factors include time horizon, required income, inflation, healthcare costs, and Social Security benefits. The earlier you start, the more powerful compounding becomes. Tax optimization, withdrawal sequencing, and risk management—including awareness of Tax Torpedoes—are critical dimensions of the plan.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Key Principles

- **Start early** — Time is more valuable than money. A table shows $500/month from age 25 grows to $1.2M by 65, outperforming $5,000/month from age 55.
- **25x expenses rule** — Portfolio target = annual expenses × 25, assuming a 4% withdrawal rate.
- **Maximize tax advantages** — Capture employer 401(k) matches, contribute to IRAs and HSAs.
- **Automate** — Set up automatic contributions.

## Retirement Numbers

### 1. How Much to Save?

#### Rule of Thumb: 25x Expenses
- If annual expenses = $60k → Need $1.5M portfolio
- Includes Social Security (adjust accordingly)
- Assumes 4% withdrawal rate

#### More Precise Method:
```
Annual retirement income needed = Current annual expenses × (1 - Savings rate)
Portfolio needed = Annual income ÷ Withdrawal rate
```

**Example**:
- Current expenses: $80k/year
- Savings rate in retirement: 20% (mortgage paid off)
- Income needed: $80k × 0.8 = $64k
- Social Security (estimate): $25k/year
- Portfolio income needed: $64k - $25k = $39k
- Portfolio at 4%: $39k ÷ 0.04 = $975k

### 2. How Much to Save Monthly?

#### Time Value Example (7% annual return):

| Age Started | Monthly | Total by Age 65 | Total Contributed | Earnings |
|-------------|--------|------------------|------------------|----------|
| 25 | $500 | $1,204,000 | $240,000 | $964,000 |
| 35 | $1,000 | $1,228,000 | $360,000 | $868,000 |
| 45 | $2,000 | $1,025,000 | $480,000 | $545,000 |
| 55 | $5,000 | $832,000 | $600,000 | $232,000 |

**Lesson**: Starting early beats starting later with higher amounts

#### Rule: Save 15-20% of Income
- 15% for early starters (age 20-30)
- 20% for later starters (age 30-40)
- 25%+ for late starters (age 40+)

## Phased Approach

- **10+ years before retirement**: Start Roth conversions, manage investment location, consider geographic arbitrage, optimize Social Security timing.
- **5-10 years before**: Finalize Roth conversion plan, build taxable accounts, plan healthcare costs.
- **1-5 years before**: Execute Roth ladder, finalize withdrawal strategy, plan for RMDs.
- **Retirement and beyond**: Implement withdrawal plan, monitor tax torpedo triggers, adjust strategy.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Account Types

### Tax-Advantaged Accounts

#### 1. 401(k)/403(b)
**Contribution Limits (2024)**:
- Under 50: $23,000
- 50+: $30,500 (catch-up)

**Pros**:
- Pre-tax or Roth option
- Employer match (FREE MONEY)
- Tax-deferred growth
- High contribution limits

**Employer Match**:
- Always contribute enough to get full match
- Match = 100% return (instant doubling)
- Don't leave money on table

#### 2. IRA (Individual Retirement Account)
**Contribution Limits (2024)**:
- Under 50: $7,000
- 50+: $8,000 (catch-up)

**Types**:
- **Traditional IRA**: Pre-tax, tax-deferred growth, taxed in retirement
- **Roth IRA**: After-tax, tax-free growth, tax-free withdrawals

**Roth vs Traditional Decision**:
- Roth if: Younger, expect higher income later, expect higher tax rates
- Traditional if: Higher income now, expect lower income later, lower tax bracket

#### 3. HSA (Health Savings Account)
**Contribution Limits (2024)**:
- Individual: $4,150
- Family: $8,300
- 55+: +$1,000 catch-up

**Triple Tax Advantage**:
1. Pre-tax contribution
2. Tax-free growth
3. Tax-free withdrawals for medical expenses

**Retirement Use**:
- After 65, can withdraw for any reason (like 401k)
- No penalty, no medical requirement

### Taxable Accounts
**When to use**:
- Maxed tax-advantaged accounts
- Need flexibility (early access)
- Alternative investments (real estate, private equity)

**Tax considerations**:
- Long-term capital gains (hold >1 year): 0%, 15%, 20%
- Short-term capital gains (hold <1 year): Ordinary income rates
- Qualified dividends: 0%, 15%, 20%

**Strategy**:
- Use for tax-loss harvesting
- Hold assets >1 year for long-term gains
- Asset location (tax-efficient in taxable)

**Allocation**:
- Stocks: 80-90%

  - US mid/small-cap: 20-30%
  - International: 20-30%
- Bonds: 0-10%
- Cash: 0-10%

**Focus**: Growth, time to recover from volatility
**Risk**: High, but long horizon

**Focus**: Still aggressive, some stability
**Risk**: Moderate-high

**Focus**: Balance growth with risk reduction

**Focus**: Preservation with some growth

- Cash: 10-20%

**Focus**: Income and preservation
**Risk**: Low-moderate

## The 4% Rule

### Rule of Thumb
---
**Withdraw 4% of portfolio annually, adjust for inflation**

**Example**:
- Portfolio: $1M
- Year 1 withdrawal: $40,000
- Year 2 (3% inflation): $41,200
- Year 3 (3% inflation): $42,436
- ...and so on

### Success Rate
Historical studies (30-year periods):
- 4% withdrawal: 95% success rate
- 5% withdrawal: 85% success rate
- 3% withdrawal: 99% success rate

### Criticisms and Adjustments
- **Lower bond yields today** → 3% might be safer
- **Longer lifespans** → May need 3-3.5%
- **Flexible spending** → Can withdraw less in bad years

**Dynamic approach**:
- Base withdrawal: 3.5%
- Extra in good years: 0.5-1%
- Cut in bad years: -0.5-1%

### Dynamic Withdrawal Strategy
Use dynamic withdrawal strategies that adjust based on market conditions and tax torpedo thresholds.^[Tax Torpedoes.md] Consider Monte Carlo analysis to stress-test income streams.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Tax Torpedoes and Tax Optimization

The Tax Torpedoes framework adds a critical dimension to retirement planning by identifying unexpected tax consequences from interactions between income sources, tax rules, and government programs.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

### Key Considerations
- Monitor IRMAA thresholds and Medicare premium costs.
- Manage provisional income to avoid Social Security taxation.
- Use dynamic withdrawal strategies that adjust based on market conditions and tax torpedo thresholds.
- Employ Monte Carlo analysis to stress-test income streams.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Use Roth ladder strategy to manage taxable withdrawals.

### Roth Conversions and Ladders
- Start Roth conversions 10+ years before retirement to spread tax impact.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Execute Roth ladder in the 1-5 years before retirement for tax-efficient access to funds.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Roth conversions reduce future RMDs and provide tax-free growth.

### Withdrawal Sequencing
- Plan the order in which you draw from accounts (taxable, tax-advantaged, Roth) to minimize tax burden.
- Avoid triggering unexpected tax events such as provisional income thresholds that increase Social Security taxation.

### IRMAA and Medicare Premiums
- IRMAA (Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount) can significantly increase Medicare Part B and Part D premiums based on income.
- Plan around these thresholds to avoid higher premiums in retirement.

## Social Security

### When to Claim
**Factors**:
- **Full retirement age (FRA)**: 66-67 (depends on birth year)
- **Early claim (62)**: Reduced benefits (up to 30% less)
- **Delayed claim (70)**: Increased benefits (up to 32% more)

### Breakeven Analysis
- Claim at 62: Get 8 years of checks before FRA
- Claim at 70: 3 years less of checks, but 32% higher

**Rule of thumb**:
- Poor health/short life expectancy: Claim early
- Good health/long family history: Delay to 70
- Need money now: Claim early
- Can afford to wait: Delay (highest lifetime total)

### Benefits Estimation
- Create account at ssa.gov
- Check your estimate
- Plan around this guaranteed income

### Social Security Taxation
- Manage provisional income to avoid Social Security taxation.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Provisional income includes half of Social Security benefits plus tax-exempt interest and 85% of other income.

## Healthcare in Retirement

### Medicare Eligibility
- **Age 65**: Eligible for Medicare Part A (hospital)
- **Part B**: Medical insurance (premium + Part B)
- **Part D**: Prescription drugs (premium)

### What Medicare Doesn't Cover
- Long-term care (nursing home)
- Most dental/vision/hearing
- Prescription drugs fully
- Deductibles and co-pays

### Planning for Costs
- **Fidelity estimate (for couple, retiring at 65)**:
  - Healthcare costs: $315,000 (lifetime)
  - Medicare premiums: $140,000
  - Out-of-pocket: $175,000

**Strategies**:
- HSA contributions (triple tax advantage)
- Long-term care insurance
- Keep working for employer coverage
- Geographic arbitrage (retire overseas, lower costs)

### IRMAA Considerations
- Monitor IRMAA thresholds and Medicare premium costs.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Higher income in retirement can trigger higher Medicare premiums through IRMAA.

## Inflation Protection

### The Problem
- $1M at 65 buys less at 85
- At 3% inflation: $1M buys $550k purchasing power at 85

### Solutions
1. **Growth assets** - Equities historically outpace inflation
2. **TIPS** - Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities
3. **I Bonds** - Inflation-adjusted savings bonds
4. **Real assets** - Real estate, commodities
5. **Annuities with COLA** - Cost-of-living adjustments

### Withdrawal Strategy
- Start conservative (3%) and increase if portfolio outperforms
- Don't use fixed dollar amount (adjust for inflation)
- Consider RMDs (Required Minimum Distributions) at 72

## Common Mistakes

### ❌ Starting Late
- Compound interest is your friend
- Every 10 years of delay doubles the required savings

### ❌ Not Getting Employer Match
- 401(k) match = 100% return
- Leaving free money on table

### ❌ Cashing Out Early
- 10% penalty + ordinary income tax
- Lost compounding
- Better: Rollover to IRA

### ❌ Underestimating Expenses
- Healthcare, travel, hobbies cost money
- Rule of thumb: 80-100% of current expenses

### ❌ Too Conservative Allocation
- Inflation erodes purchasing power
- Bonds alone don't grow enough
- Need equity exposure

### ❌ No Plan
- How much to save?
- When can I retire?
- What's my withdrawal strategy?

### ❌ Ignoring Tax Torpedoes
- Unexpected tax consequences from interactions between income sources, tax rules, and government programs can erode retirement income.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- Failure to plan for IRMAA, Social Security taxation, and Roth conversion timing can lead to higher taxes than anticipated.

## Best Practices

1. **Start early** - Time is more valuable than money
2. **Automate** - Set up automatic contributions
3. **Maximize tax advantages** - 401k, IRA, HSA
4. **Get employer match** - Never leave money on table
5. **Diversify** - Don't concentrate risk
6. **Increase contributions** - Raise savings rate when income increases
7. **Pay off high-interest debt** - Before aggressive investing
8. **Review annually** - Adjust allocation as you age
9. **Understand Social Security** - When to claim
10. **Plan for healthcare** - Biggest retirement expense
11. **Monitor tax torpedo triggers** - Adjust strategy as income and market conditions change.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
12. **Use Monte Carlo analysis** - Stress-test income streams.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
13. **Plan Roth conversions** - Start 10+ years before retirement.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
14. **Execute Roth ladder** - In the 1-5 years before retirement for tax-efficient access.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
