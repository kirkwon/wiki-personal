# Intelligence Gathering

The systematic collection and analysis of information about opponents, environments, and circumstances as a strategic advantage. From *The Art of War* Chapter 13, Sun Tzu identifies five types of spies and emphasizes that ignorance of the enemy guarantees failure.

## Key Ideas
- **Five types of spies**: Local spies (inhabitants), inward spies (enemy officials), converted spies (enemy agents turned), doomed spies (disinformation), surviving spies (trusted intelligence officers).
- **Intelligence as force multiplier**: The side with better information can achieve victory at lower cost.
- **Continuous gathering**: Intelligence is not a one-time activity but an ongoing process of understanding the competition and environment.

## Connections
Connects to [[superforecasting]] (probabilistic thinking about future states), [[prediction-markets]] (aggregating diverse information), and [[decision-making-frameworks]] (information quality affects decision quality). Also relates to [[strategic-bluffing]]—deception works best when the other side lacks intelligence.

## Tension
Intelligence gathering in *The Art of War* assumes adversarial competition. The wiki's broader philosophy emphasizes [[satisfaction-under-constraints]] and cooperative information sharing. The "when to stop" guidance warns against paranoia about opponents.
