# Lifestyle Design

Lifestyle Design is a framework for deliberately constructing one's ideal life around four core elements: time, money, people, and place. Rather than conforming to societal expectations or traditional career paths, the approach encourages defining success on personal terms and then engineering life to match those definitions.

Timothy Ferriss presents Lifestyle Design as a structured process in *The 4-Hour Workweek*, including a [[lifestyle-design-formula]] and a step-by-step [[lifestyle-design-protocol]] with measurable outcomes. The framework emphasizes outsourcing non-essential tasks, building portable income streams, and embracing uncertainty as a path to adaptability and continuous learning.

This concept connects to broader decision-making literature. Annie Duke's [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] similarly encourages treating life decisions probabilistically, while Covey's [[time-management-matrix]] provides a contrasting disciplined prioritization framework. The anti-routine stance in "The Gym Sucks" pushes against rules-based optimization found in habit-formation models like [[hooked---nir-eyal]].
