# Dishonesty

Dishonesty is a mental model and conceptual tool for understanding how people self-deceive, rationalize dishonest behavior, and maintain a gap between their stated values and actual actions. It provides a framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex social and moral situations.

## Core Idea

People routinely cheat in small ways while preserving an honest self-concept. This dishonesty continuum—where individuals engage in minor dishonesty without fully reclassifying themselves as dishonest—is a universal mental shortcut for navigating the tension between self-image and behavior.

## Key Findings

- **Self-concept preservation**: People act dishonestly while keeping their self-image intact, a phenomenon related to [[moral-licensing]] and [[overconfidence-and-certainty]].
- **Small-scale cheating**: Dishonesty tends to occur in incremental, justifiable amounts rather than as dramatic betrayals.
- **Moral licensing**: Prior good behavior can create psychological permission for subsequent dishonesty.
- **Rationalization**: Individuals construct narratives that justify their dishonest actions to themselves.
