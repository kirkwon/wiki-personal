# Alternative Investments

Alternative investments are asset classes beyond traditional stocks, bonds, and cash. They are used for [[diversification]], inflation protection, and potential return enhancement. These investments are typically less liquid, carry higher fees, and are more complex than traditional assets, but they can be uncorrelated with traditional markets.

## Core Idea
The central rationale for alternatives is [[diversification]] — spreading exposure across asset classes that may behave differently than stocks and bonds during various economic conditions. Alternatives also serve as an [[inflation-protection|inflation hedge]], since many real assets (real estate, commodities, farmland) tend to rise in price when currency loses value.

## Asset Classes

### Real Estate
Includes [[direct-ownership]] of rental properties, vacation rentals, and commercial buildings. Also encompasses [[reits]] (Real Estate Investment Trusts) and [[reigs]] (Real Estate Investment Groups). Real estate offers tangible assets, leverage through mortgages, tax benefits like depreciation, and potential cash flow, but comes with illiquidity and active management requirements.

### Private Equity
Investments in private companies including venture capital, growth equity, and buyouts. Offers illiquidity premium potential but requires long lockups (5-10 years), has high fees (2% management + 20% performance), and is limited to accredited investors.

### Commodities
Physical commodities like precious metals, energy, agriculture, and base metals, plus commodity ETFs like [[gld]], [[uso]], and [[dba]]. Provide real asset inflation hedging but lack cash flow and earnings growth.

### Collectibles
Art, watches, coins, wine, and sports memorabilia. Offer enjoyment value and potential appreciation but are highly illiquid with no intrinsic value and significant authenticity risks.

### Cryptocurrency
Bitcoin, Ethereum, and stablecoins. Provide diversification from traditional markets with high return potential but are extremely volatile with regulatory uncertainty and security risks.

### Farmland and Timber
Agricultural land and forest land providing income, appreciation, and inflation protection through biological growth and commodity exposure.

### Infrastructure
Toll roads, utilities, ports, and communication networks offering stable cash flows, inflation-protected regulated rates, and long-term contracts.

## Allocation Guidelines

Alternative investments should represent 5-20% of a portfolio depending on risk tolerance:

- **Conservative**: 5-10% alternatives, 90-95% traditional
- **Moderate**: 10-20% alternatives, 80-90% traditional
- **Aggressive**: 15-25% alternatives, 75-85% traditional

Within alternatives, diversify across asset classes: 40-60% real estate, 20-40% private equity, 10-20% commodities, and 0-10% collectibles/crypto as the speculative portion.

## Key Risks

Five major risk categories apply across alternative investments:

- [[illiquidity-risk]]: Cannot sell quickly; real estate takes 6-12 months, private equity locks up 5-10 years
- [[valuation-risk]]: Hard to determine fair value in opaque private markets
- [[concentration-risk]]: Single property or fund creates concentrated exposure
- [[manager-risk]]: Dependence on manager skill in private equity and real estate
- [[regulatory-risk]]: Changing laws and regulations affect value

## Best Practices

1. Start small (5-10% maximum)
2. Diversify within and across asset classes
3. Conduct due diligence on managers and platforms
4. Understand liquidity constraints
5. Monitor fees carefully (2% management + 20% performance fees compound over time)
6. Plan for tax consequences
7. Maintain long-term horizon (5+ years)
8. Never chase past performance
9. Keep emergency fund intact before investing
