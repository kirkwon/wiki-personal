# Attention Residue

Attention residue is the cognitive carryover from a previous task that contaminates performance on the next task. When you switch tasks, a portion of your mental focus remains occupied by the previous task, reducing the quality and speed of work on the new task.

## How It Works
Task-switching does not produce a clean reset of attention. Residual mental activity from the prior task lingers, creating a partial attention state where you are neither fully present on the current task nor fully disengaged from the previous one.

## Impact
- Reduces performance quality on the new task
- Increases errors and time required to re-engage
- Accumulates across multiple switches throughout the day
- Particularly harmful for tasks requiring deep [[hyperfocus]]

## Mitigation
- Minimize unnecessary task switches
- Use buffers between tasks to clear residue
- Batch similar tasks together
- Recognize that single-tasking [[hyperfocus]] periods reduce residue buildup

This concept connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] because attention residue can degrade the quality of decisions made during contaminated cognitive states.
