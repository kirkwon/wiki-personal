# The Gift of Time

The Gift of Time is Gawande's reframing of what medical success means at the end of life. Rather than measuring success by how long a patient survives, he argues we should measure by what patients are able to do with the time they have — whether they can think clearly, connect with loved ones, experience comfort, and maintain a sense of meaning.

This reframing challenges the default assumption that extending every possible minute through medical intervention is always beneficial. Gawande documents cases where patients chose to stop treatment and spend their remaining time at home with family, gaining weeks or months of clarity and connection that aggressive treatment would have destroyed.

The concept connects to [[quality-vs-quantity-of-life]]: time is only a gift if it is time well spent. It also connects to [[the-good-death]] because autonomy, comfort, dignity, and family involvement are precisely the things that make time meaningful.
