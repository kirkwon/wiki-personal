# Financial Crises

Financial crises are periods of severe economic distress caused by the breakdown of financial systems, asset bubbles, excessive debt, or systemic failures. In Dalio's framework, crises are a natural part of the economic machine—inevitable consequences of credit cycles, market forces, and policy failures.

Key aspects include:
- Causes of financial crises such as credit expansion beyond sustainable levels
- Historical examples and patterns of crisis and recovery
- Recovery mechanisms including monetary policy interventions and fiscal measures
- Policy measures used to stabilize economies during turbulent times

Understanding the mechanics of financial crises enables better preparation and decision-making during periods of economic turbulence.
