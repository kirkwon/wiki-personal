# The PARA Method

The PARA Method is a digital organization framework developed by Tiago Forte that categorizes all information and actions into four buckets: [[projects]], [[areas]], [[resources]], and [[archives]].

## Four Categories

- **Projects** — Active efforts with a defined outcome and deadline. These are the things you are working on right now that will result in a completed deliverable.
- **Areas** — Ongoing responsibilities that require maintenance but have no specific deadline. Examples include health, finances, relationships, and home maintenance.
- **Resources** — Topics of interest or reference material that may be useful in the future. This includes notes, articles, inspiration, and knowledge that doesn't belong to an active project or area.
- **Archives** — Items that are no longer active but worth keeping for reference. Completed projects, inactive areas, and outdated resources go here.

## Core Principles

The method emphasizes gaining perspective before organizing, prioritizing what truly matters, analyzing recurring patterns and habits, and regularly reassessing systems for efficiency. It advocates for adaptive learning—adjusting strategies based on what works—rather than rigid routines. Customization of digital tools and environments is encouraged, along with automation and outsourcing of repetitive tasks. The PARA Method addresses digital life chaos by providing a simple, scalable categorization system.

## Measurement and Self-Assessment

Effectiveness can be measured through output per hour, habit consistency, focus session quality, task completion rates, energy levels, and decision quality. The framework also includes clear signals for when to stop optimizing: when it causes stress, destroys joy, sacrifices relationships, or when the cost exceeds the benefit.

## Relationship to Other Frameworks

PARA shares conceptual ground with Sönke Ahrens' approach in [[how-to-take-smart-notes---snke-ahrens]], particularly around categorizing information for long-term utility. It also connects to [[categorizing-information]] and [[active-revision-techniques]] through its emphasis on organizing notes and revisiting material. The system reassessment component relates to [[systematic-decision-making]] and [[decision-tracking]]. PARA complements [[getting-things-done]] and [[how-to-take-smart-notes]] by providing a specific organizational taxonomy. While GTD emphasizes capture and processing, and the Zettelkasten method emphasizes interconnected notes, PARA focuses on categorizing information by the type of action required.

## Practical Application

The framework is designed to work across any digital tool—note-taking apps, task managers, file systems—and provides a top-level structure that helps users quickly locate information and decide where new content belongs. By distinguishing between active work (Projects), ongoing responsibilities (Areas), reusable knowledge (Resources), and historical records (Archives), PARA reduces the cognitive burden of digital information management.
