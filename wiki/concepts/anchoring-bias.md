# Anchoring Bias

Anchoring bias is the tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information encountered when making decisions or estimates.

## Definition

**Anchoring Bias**: Relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered, which then skews subsequent judgments and negotiations.

## Example

A $100,000 starting salary negotiation makes $90,000 seem like a deal, even if $90,000 is still above market rate. The initial number anchors your perception of what is reasonable.

## Impact

Poor negotiation outcomes, inaccurate estimates, and distorted decision-making. Anchoring can affect pricing, valuations, risk assessments, and virtually any domain where numbers are involved.

## Mitigation

Start negotiations and estimates with objective data rather than arbitrary numbers. Be aware of where initial numbers come from and whether they are relevant to the actual value being assessed.
