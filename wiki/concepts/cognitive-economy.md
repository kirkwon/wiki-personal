# Cognitive Economy

Cognitive economy refers to the practice of minimizing cognitive load and optimizing mental resources for effective decision-making and persuasion. In the context of pre-suasion, it means designing the pre-persuasion environment so that the audience's mental resources are neither overwhelmed nor depleted, leaving capacity available for processing the subsequent message.

## Key Ideas

- People have limited mental bandwidth; managing that bandwidth before a request ensures the audience is in an optimal state to receive it.
- Overwhelming an audience with too much information or too many demands reduces persuasiveness — simplicity and focus are key.
- Cognitive economy connects to the broader principle of [[attention-budget]]: treating attention as a finite resource that must be allocated deliberately.

## Connection to Dual Process Theory

Cognitive economy aligns with [[dual-process-theory]] — when cognitive load is managed, people can engage more effectively with both fast, heuristic processing and slower, deliberate reasoning. Pre-suasion leverages this by setting up the heuristic stage favorably before the deliberate evaluation stage begins.
