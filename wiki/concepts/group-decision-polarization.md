# Group Decision Polarization

Group decision polarization refers to the phenomenon where group discussions lead members to adopt more extreme positions than they individually held before the discussion. Groups tend to converge on positions that are more extreme than the average of initial individual views, driven by conformity pressures, social comparison, and the amplification of shared biases. In groups, people conform to perceived group norms, which can amplify initial tendencies — whether toward risk-seeking or risk-averse behavior.

Kahneman identifies this as a phenomenon where collective decision-making can lead to conformity and polarization, affecting both individual and collective outcomes. This connects to [[ascription-bias]] and [[overconfidence-and-certainty]], as group members may overestimate the certainty of shared beliefs. Kahneman's Chapter 10 describes how focus groups and collective settings amplify conformity and polarization, connecting to broader research on groupthink, social influence, and the dynamics of [[decisions-in-teams]].

## Key Dynamics

- **Conformity pressure** — individuals adjust their stated positions to match the group
- **Polarization** — the group's final position is more extreme than the average of individual starting positions
- **Shared responsibility** — diffusion of accountability can reduce individual caution
- **Reinforcement of shared biases** — shared biases are reinforced rather than challenged
- **Suppression of dissent** — individual dissent is suppressed through social pressure
