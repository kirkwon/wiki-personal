# Sunk Cost Fallacy

The sunk cost fallacy is the tendency to continue an endeavor because of previously invested resources — time, money, effort — rather than evaluating future costs and benefits. Sunk costs are irrecoverable and should not influence future decisions. Covered in Part II of *Super Thinking*.

## Definition

**Sunk Cost Fallacy**: Continuing an endeavor because of previously invested resources (time, money, effort) rather than evaluating future costs and benefits. The past investment is irrelevant to the decision going forward.

## Examples

- Staying in a bad movie because you paid for the ticket
- Continuing ineffective medical treatments because you already started them
- Holding losing investments because of money already put in
- Continuing to fund a failing project because of the money already spent
- Holding a losing stock because you've owned it for years

## Impact

Wasted resources, poor resource allocation, and missed opportunities. The sunk cost fallacy keeps people trapped in suboptimal situations.

## Mitigation

Evaluate decisions based on future costs and benefits only, not past investment. Ask: "If I were starting fresh today, would I choose this option?"
