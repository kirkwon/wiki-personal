# Speed and Timing

The principle that strategic success depends on acting at the right moment with appropriate speed. From *The Art of War* Chapters 2, 5, and 12, rapid victory is preferable to prolonged conflict, and timing the application of force—like fire or water—is critical.

## Key Ideas
- **Rapid victory**: Prolonged warfare destroys the state; swift resolution preserves resources.
- **Timing over haste**: Speed is valuable, but acting at the wrong moment is worse than waiting.
- **Fire analogy** (Chapter 12): Fire attacks depend entirely on timing—too early or too late diminishes effect.
- **Adaptive tempo**: Speed should match circumstances, not be applied uniformly.
