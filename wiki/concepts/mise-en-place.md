# Mise en Place

Mise en place (French: "everything in its place") is the practice of pre-preparing and organizing all ingredients before beginning to cook. Timothy Ferriss identifies it as a foundational time-efficiency technique in *The 4-Hour Chef*. It is essential for wok cooking where speed and heat retention are critical.

## Why It Matters

### For Wok Cooking
- **Speed** — Wok moves fast, no time to prep during cooking
- **Heat retention** — Stopping to prep drops wok temperature
- **Organization** — Know where everything is
- **Flow** — Rhythmic cooking, not chaotic

### For Cooking Quality
- Consistent results — same prep every time
- No burning — ingredients ready when needed
- Better flavor — proper marinade times
- Less stress — you're in control

## Key Aspects
- **Ingredient preparation**: Washing, chopping, measuring, and organizing all components before heat is applied
- **Workflow optimization**: Reduces hesitation and searching during cooking, creating a smoother process
- **Time savings**: Eliminates downtime between steps and prevents ingredients from overcooking while waiting

## Mise en Place Checklist

### Before You Start Cooking
- [ ] All proteins marinated (correct time)
- [ ] All vegetables washed, dried, cut
- [ ] All aromatics prepped (garlic minced, ginger sliced)
- [ ] Sauces measured and mixed
- [ ] Garnishes ready (scallions, herbs)
- [ ] Rice cooked (if serving with rice)
- [ ] Wok/skillet preheating

### Setup for Efficiency
- **Small bowls** — Pre-measure sauces, aromatics
- **Group ingredients** — Proteins together, veg together
- **Plate organization** — Left to right cooking order
- **Waste bowl** — For trimmings, shells, bones

## Professional Workflow

### 1. Prep First (30–45 min)
- Cut all vegetables
- Mince all garlic/ginger
- Measure all sauces
- Marinate proteins
- Cook rice

### 2. Setup (5 min)
- Arrange ingredients in cooking order
- Heat wok
- Have serving platter ready

### 3. Cook (5–15 min)
- Execute recipe
- Everything ready when needed
- No stopping, no hunting

## Common Mistakes
- ❌ Starting without prep — chaos, burned food
- ❌ Forgetting ingredients — recipe incomplete
- ❌ Wrong sizes — inconsistent cooking
- ❌ Not measuring — flavors off

## Best Practices
- Prep everything before turning on stove
- Small bowls are your friend (prep sauces)
- Clean as you go — minimize mess
- Label bowls if multiple marinades
- Time yourself — see how prep improves your flow
- Group ingredients (proteins together, veg together)
- Plate organization — left to right cooking order
- Waste bowl for trimmings, shells, bones

## Connection to Marinades
Proper mise en place ensures marinades are timed correctly and proteins are coated evenly before cooking begins. This discipline is especially important for Chinese restaurant-style stir-fry where the velveting technique requires precise timing.
