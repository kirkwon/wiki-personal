# American BBQ Philosophy

American BBQ is defined by regional identity rather than a single standard. The four main styles—Texas, Kansas City, Carolina, and Memphis—differ fundamentally in meat choice, sauce philosophy, smoking wood, and cooking approach.

## Core Principles

- **Low-and-slow cooking** — 12-18 hours of smoking at 225°F (107°C) is the universal foundation
- **Dry rub vs. wet sauce** — Texas and Memphis emphasize dry rub with minimal sauce; KC and Carolina emphasize sauce as essential
- **Regional pride** — Each style reflects local identity, ingredient availability, and tradition
- **Patience** — Rushing the process is the most common mistake; plan for long cook times

## Meat and Sauce Philosophy

| Region | Meat Focus | Sauce Approach |
|--------|-----------|----------------|
| Texas | Beef brisket | Dry rub, no sauce |
| Kansas City | Beef and pork | Sweet tomato-based sauce |
| Carolina | Pork shoulder/whole hog | Vinegar-based (Eastern: vinegar-only; Western: tomato-vinegar) |
| Memphis | Pork ribs | Dry rub, optional sweet sauce |

## Smoking Woods

- **Hickory** — Strong, bacon-like flavor (KC, Memphis)
- **Oak** — Medium, clean flavor (Texas)
- **Mesquite** — Strong, earthy flavor (Texas)
- **Apple** — Mild, sweet (Carolina)
- **Cherry** — Mild, sweet (various)

## Equipment

- Offset smoker — Long, low smoking
- Pellet grill — Easy temperature control
- Charcoal grill — For smoking
- Meat thermometer — Doneness monitoring
- Spray bottle — Moisture control during cooking
