# Mental Models in Innovation

This concept examines how mental models shape approaches to innovation, highlighting their impact on both incremental improvements and breakthrough ideas. The frameworks and assumptions we carry determine what we can see as possible.

## Key Points
- Existing mental models can both enable and constrain innovation
- Incremental innovation often works within existing mental models, while breakthrough innovation requires breaking or replacing them
- The [[inventors-paradox]] illustrates how more ambitious problem formulations can sometimes be easier than restricted ones, suggesting that mental model constraints can make problems harder
- [[bright-idea-insight]] moments often occur when someone applies an existing model from one domain to a new domain
- Sönke Ahrens' work on [[experiment-and-exploration-forms-of-world-disclosure---snke-ahrens]] complements this by emphasizing how different frameworks of thinking open different possibilities

## Implication
To foster innovation, it is not enough to gather more information or work harder—the mental models through which information is processed must also be examined and potentially updated.
