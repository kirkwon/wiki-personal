# Water (mental model)

Water is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.^[raw/articles/Water.md]

The model draws from Sun Tzu's *The Art of War*, where water metaphorizes strategic adaptability—flowing around obstacles, seeking low ground, and conforming to terrain. Like water, the practitioner should move with the situation rather than against it, finding paths of least resistance and adjusting form to context.^[raw/articles/Water.md]

## Core Idea
Water functions as a universal mental model applicable across domains. It encourages thinking in terms of flow, adaptability, and recognizing the underlying patterns that govern complex systems. Rather than forcing rigid plans, the Water model suggests reading the environment and responding in kind.^[raw/articles/Water.md]

## Relationship to Other Mental Models
- **Solutions-mental-model**: Both emphasize navigating complexity through pattern recognition rather than fixed approaches.
- **Minimalism**: Water's emphasis on simplicity and finding the least resistant path aligns with minimalist principles.
- **Stillness**: Complementary—stillness provides the observational foundation; water provides the responsive framework.
- **Thinking-mental-model**: Water is one lens within the broader thinking framework for recognizing relationships in complex situations.
- **World-disclosure**: Water relates to how we reveal and interpret patterns in the world around us.

## Limitations
The model is conceptual and relies heavily on metaphor rather than empirical grounding. Its broad applicability claim (universal) is supported by only a single source reference (Sun Tzu), and the specific patterns and relationships it helps recognize remain abstract without further elaboration. Confidence is rated at 0.7.^[raw/articles/Water.md]
