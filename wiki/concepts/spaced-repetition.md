# Spaced Repetition

Spaced repetition is a learning technique where you review information at increasing intervals over time rather than concentrating study in a single session (massed study or cramming). The spacing effect—one of the most robust findings in cognitive psychology—demonstrates that information studied across multiple sessions with intervals in between is retained significantly better than information studied in one massed session. Decades of research show that spacing out retrieval attempts produces significantly stronger long-term retention than cramming, even though cramming may feel more productive in the short term. Covered in Part VII of *Super Thinking*.

## Why It Works

Each time you retrieve a memory after a delay, the act of reconstruction strengthens the memory trace. The spacing introduces a desirable difficulty: the information is partially forgotten, and the effort of recalling it deepens encoding. Cramming creates an illusion of mastery because the material is fresh in working memory, but it fades quickly without consolidation.

## Evidence

[[make-it-stick---peter-c-brown-et-al.md]] cites research comparing spaced study versus massed study, showing that spaced sessions produce superior retention over weeks and months. This finding holds across age groups, subjects, and learning formats.

## Core Idea

Rather than cramming, you revisit material at strategically spaced intervals (e.g., 1 day, 3 days, 7 days, 21 days). Each review strengthens memory traces and makes recall more durable.

## Key Principles
- **Distribute study sessions**: Instead of cramming before an exam, spread review across days or weeks rather than one long session.
- **Increasing intervals**: As material becomes more familiar, the intervals between review sessions can grow longer.
- **Combining with retrieval**: Spaced repetition is most powerful when combined with active retrieval rather than passive re-reading.

## Practical Application
- Schedule multiple short study sessions over days or weeks rather than one long session
- Use flashcard systems that automatically schedule reviews based on how well you remember each card
- Review notes at increasing intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month
- Plan review sessions at increasing intervals after initial learning
- Use flashcards or self-testing at spaced intervals rather than rereading notes repeatedly
- Integrate spaced review into a broader note-taking and revision system
- Combine with [[retrieval-practice]] by testing yourself during each session rather than re-reading
- **Language learning**: Review vocabulary at spaced intervals
- **Professional knowledge**: Revisit key concepts from books and courses on a schedule
- **Mental model retention**: Regularly revisit models from [[mental-model-library]] to keep them accessible
