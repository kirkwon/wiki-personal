# Comfort Food

Comfort food refers to dishes that provide emotional satisfaction and a sense of home, often characterized by hearty portions, rich flavors, and simple preparation. It emphasizes emotional warmth, nostalgia, physical satisfaction, and communal eating.

## Core Principles
- **Comfort over complexity** — Simple, satisfying recipes
- **Hearty portions** — Feed everyone generously
- **Slow cooking** — Flavor develops over time
- **Community** — Food brings people together
- **Resourcefulness** — Use every part, waste nothing
- **Community and family tradition**

## Cultural Context
Comfort food traditions exist across many cuisines but are particularly prominent in American Southern and soul food, where they blend African, European, and Native American influences. Southern cuisine embodies the comfort food philosophy through dishes like [[fried-chicken]], [[mac-and-cheese]], [[collard-greens]], [[cornbread]], [[shrimp-and-grits]], and [[hoppin-john]]. Soul food, a subset rooted in African American tradition, adds braising techniques and offal cuts to the regional Southern foundation.

The distinction between Southern (regional) and Soul Food (African American heritage) adds important cultural nuance to the comfort food tradition. This concept connects to [[southern-soul-food]] as a specific regional expression.
