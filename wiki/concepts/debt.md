# Debt

Debt is a universal mental model—a conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern and relationship recognition. It provides a mental shortcut for making sense of how obligations, leverage, and cycles operate across economic, financial, and even metaphorical domains.

## Core Idea

At its most fundamental, debt represents a structured obligation: one party assumes a claim on future resources or actions from another. This framing extends beyond monetary debt to include social capital, technical debt, psychological obligations, and moral debts. As a mental model, debt helps identify hidden leverage points, predict cyclical patterns, and anticipate how imbalances resolve.

## Debt Cycles

Ray Dalio's framework for understanding big debt crises provides the primary analytical lens. Key dynamics include:

- **Accumulation phase**: Borrowing increases, asset prices rise, and the economy appears healthy. Short-term debt is plentiful and cheap.
- **Peak phase**: Debt levels become unsustainable relative to income and asset values. The gap between debt and capacity to service it widens.
- **Deleveraging phase**: When debt cannot be serviced, painful adjustments occur. Options include austerity (reducing spending), debt redistribution (defaults, restructurings), and money printing (monetary policy easing). Each carries trade-offs between inflation, deflation, and social unrest.
- **Long-term debt vs. short-term debt**: Short-term debt cycles are more frequent and volatile; long-term debt cycles are slower but deeper, often requiring fundamental restructuring.

## Metaphorical Applications

The debt lens extends beyond finance:
- **Technical debt**: Accumulated shortcuts in code or systems that create future maintenance burdens.
- **Social debt**: Unreciprocated favors or obligations that build relational pressure.
- **Psychological debt**: Guilt or obligation from past actions that influences present decision-making.
