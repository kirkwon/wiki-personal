# White Hat - Facts and Data

The White Hat in Edward de Bono's Six Thinking Hats framework represents the mode of thinking focused on gathering and analyzing objective information. It provides an unbiased view of the situation by emphasizing facts, data, and what is known or unknown. When wearing the White Hat, participants focus exclusively on collecting and analyzing relevant facts, data, and known information about a situation without interpretation or judgment.

## Key Practices

- Identify what information is available and what is missing
- Gather relevant data before forming judgments
- Ask what can be verified versus what is assumed
- Distinguish between facts and opinions
- Recognize gaps that need to be filled before proceeding
- Recognize the limits of current knowledge

## Role in Decision-Making

The White Hat ensures that decisions are grounded in reality rather than assumptions. It serves as the foundation upon which other perspectives (emotional, critical, optimistic, creative) can be layered. This hat directly supports [[systematic-decision-making]] by ensuring decisions are grounded in reality rather than assumptions. It parallels the data-driven orientation of [[bayesian-thinking]], where updating beliefs requires clear input about what is actually known.

The White Hat prevents premature conclusions by separating information from interpretation, making it a foundational step before any analysis or emotional evaluation.
