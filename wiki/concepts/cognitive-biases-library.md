## Overview

Cognitive biases are systematic patterns of deviation from rationality in judgment. They occur when people make decisions or judgments based on subjective social realities rather than objective information. Understanding these biases is the first step to making better decisions.

## Categories of Biases

### Memory Biases

**Memory biases** affect how we recall information.

#### Availability Heuristic
- **Definition**: Overestimating the likelihood of events based on how easily examples come to mind
- **Example**: After seeing a plane crash on the news, you think flying is more dangerous than driving
- **Mitigation**: Look at actual data, not vivid examples
- **Impact**: Causes fear disproportionate to risk

#### Hindsight Bias
- **Definition**: "I knew it all along" - believing you predicted an event after it happened
- **Example**: After a stock crashes, claiming you "knew it would"
- **Mitigation**: Write down predictions before events occur
- **Impact**: Overconfidence, poor learning

#### Confirmation Bias
- **Definition**: Seeking information that confirms existing beliefs while ignoring contradictory evidence
- **Example**: Only reading news sources that align with your political views
- **Mitigation**: Actively seek disconfirming evidence
- **Impact**: Polarization, closed-mindedness

#### Sunk Cost Fallacy
- **Definition**: Continuing an endeavor because of previously invested resources
- **Example**: Staying in a bad movie because you paid for the ticket
- **Mitigation**: Evaluate based on future costs/benefits, not past investment
- **Impact**: Wasted resources, poor resource allocation

### Social Biases

**Social biases** affect how we perceive and interact with others.

#### Anchoring Bias
- **Definition**: Relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered
- **Example**: A $100,000 starting salary negotiation makes $90,000 seem like a deal
- **Mitigation**: Start negotiations with objective data, not arbitrary numbers
- **Impact**: Poor negotiation, inaccurate estimates

#### Halo Effect
- **Definition**: Positive impression of one aspect influences overall opinion
- **Example**: Attractive people are perceived as more competent
- **Mitigation**: Evaluate each trait independently
- **Impact**: Hiring mistakes, unfair judgments

#### In-Group Bias
- **Definition**: Favoring one's own social group
- **Example**: Hiring someone from the same college
- **Mitigation**: Blind evaluation, diverse panels
- **Impact**: Lack of diversity, reduced innovation

#### Fundamental Attribution Error
- **Definition**: Overemphasizing personality traits and underemphasizing situational factors
- **Example**: "He's lazy" vs "He might be exhausted from caregiving"
- **Mitigation**: Consider context and circumstances
- **Impact**: Misjudgments, interpersonal conflict

### Statistical and Probability Biases

**Probability biases** affect how we assess risk and likelihood.

#### Gambler's Fallacy
- **Definition**: Believing past random events affect future independent events
- **Example**: "Red has come up 5 times, so black is due"
- **Mitigation**: Understand that independent events have no memory
- **Impact**: Poor gambling/investment decisions

#### Conjunction Fallacy
- **Definition**: Assuming specific conditions are more probable than general ones
- **Example**: "Linda is a bank teller AND feminist" > "Linda is a bank teller"
- **Mitigation**: Remember that specific is always ≤ general
- **Impact**: Poor probability judgments

#### Base Rate Neglect
- **Definition**: Ignoring general prevalence in favor of specific information
- **Example**: Fearing shark attacks (1 in 3.7M) more than heart disease (1 in 6)
- **Mitigation**: Always consider base rates first
- **Impact**: Misallocation of attention and resources

### Decision-Making Biases

**Decision biases** affect how we make choices.

#### Loss Aversion
- **Definition**: The pain of losing is psychologically about twice as powerful as the pleasure of gaining
- **Example**: Selling winning stocks too early, holding losers too long
- **Mitigation**: Frame decisions symmetrically
- **Impact**: Risk aversion, missed opportunities

#### Status Quo Bias
- **Definition**: Preferring the current state of affairs
- **Example**: Not switching banks even when a competitor offers better rates
- **Mitigation**: Actively evaluate alternatives regularly
- **Impact**: Suboptimal choices, lack of progress

#### Overconfidence Bias
- **Definition**: Overestimating one's own abilities
- **Example**: 93% of drivers think they're above average
- **Mitigation**: Seek objective feedback, use calibration exercises
- **Impact**: Poor planning, failure to prepare

#### Planning Fallacy
- **Definition**: Underestimating time, costs, and risks of future actions
- **Example**: "This project will take 2 weeks" → takes 6 weeks
- **Mitigation**: Use reference class forecasting (how did similar projects go?)
- **Impact**: Missed deadlines, budget overruns

## Decision-Making Heuristics

Heuristics are mental shortcuts that simplify decision-making.

### Recognition Heuristic
- **Rule**: If one of two objects is recognized and the other is not, infer that the recognized object has higher value
- **Useful**: Quick decisions under uncertainty
- **Limitation**: Fails with false recognition (branding)

### Representativeness Heuristic
- **Rule**: Judging probability by similarity to a prototype
- **Example**: A quiet person is assumed to be a librarian, not salesperson
- **Useful**: Quick pattern recognition
- **Limitation**: Ignores base rates and statistical information

### Affect Heuristic
- **Rule**: Making decisions based on emotional response
- **Example**: I like this company, so it's a good investment
- **Useful**: Intuition in familiar domains
- **Limitation**: Can lead to poor decisions in unfamiliar domains

## Bias Detection Checklist

When making an important decision, check for these biases:

- [ ] Am I considering all relevant data, or just what's readily available? (**Availability**)
- [ ] Am I seeking information that confirms my view? (**Confirmation**)
- [ ] Am I continuing because of past investment? (**Sunk Cost**)
- [ ] Am I being influenced by the first information I heard? (**Anchoring**)
- [ ] Am I assuming I knew this all along? (**Hindsight**)
- [ ] Am I overestimating my abilities? (**Overconfidence**)
- [ ] Am I underestimating time and costs? (**Planning Fallacy**)
- [ ] Am I being influenced by fear of loss? (**Loss Aversion**)
- [ ] Am I favoring the status quo? (**Status Quo**)
- [ ] Am I overemphasizing personality over situation? (**Fundamental Attribution**)

## Mitigation Strategies

### Individual Level

1. **Slow Down**
   - Take time before important decisions
   - Sleep on big choices

2. **Seek Disconfirming Evidence**
   - Ask "What would prove me wrong?"
   - Play devil's advocate with yourself

3. **Use Checklists**
   - Standardize decision-making processes
   - Prevent skipping important considerations

4. **Get Outside Perspective**
   - Ask trusted advisors
   - Consider others' viewpoints

5. **Write It Down**
   - Document predictions and rationale
   - Compare predictions to outcomes

### Organizational Level

1. **Red Teaming**
   - Assign team to challenge plans
   - Identify potential failures

2. **Pre-Mortem Analysis**
   - Imagine project failed, ask why
   - Identify risks before they happen

3. **Blind Evaluation**
   - Remove names/demographics from applications
   - Evaluate on merit only

4. **Diverse Teams**
   - Different backgrounds reduce groupthink
   - Broader perspectives

5. **After-Action Reviews**
   - What went well? What didn't?
   - Document lessons learned

## Common Biases by Domain

### Investing
- **Loss Aversion**: Hold losers, sell winners
- **Recency Bias**: Overweight recent performance
- **Confirmation Bias**: Research only bullish news on owned stocks
- **Overconfidence**: Believe you can beat the market

### Management
- **Planning Fallacy**: Underestimate project timelines
- **Sunk Cost**: Continue failing projects
- **Halo Effect**: Assume charismatic leader is competent
- **Survivorship Bias**: Copy only successful companies

### Relationships
- **Fundamental Attribution Error**: Blame personality, not circumstances
- **Halo Effect**: Physical attractiveness = good person
- **Confirmation Bias**: Notice only behaviors that confirm beliefs about partner

### Health
- **Availability Heuristic**: Overweight vivid risks (sharks) over common ones (heart disease)
- **Optimism Bias**: Think you're less likely than average to get sick
- **Sunk Cost**: Continue ineffective treatments because you started them

## Practice Exercises

### Daily Reflection
1. **What decision did I make today?**
2. **What biases might have influenced it?**
3. **How could I improve next time?**

### Prediction Journal
1. **Write down predictions** (market, sports, politics, personal)
2. **Record confidence level**
3. **Track outcomes**
4. **Review monthly**

### Bias Bingo
- Create a bingo card with common biases
- Try to identify one each day
- Reflect on how it affected decisions
