# Regime shifts in prediction

Rapid changes that invalidate historical patterns, rendering past data unreliable for future forecasting. Silver identifies regime shifts as a key reason prediction models fail.

## Key Ideas

- Historical patterns assume stable conditions that may not hold
- Regime shifts can be economic, political, technological, or environmental
- Models calibrated on past data may perform poorly after a regime change
- Knowing when to stop predicting is as important as making predictions
- Related to [[overfitting]] and the limits of data-driven forecasting

## When to Stop

When rapid change renders historical patterns invalid, prediction costs exceed forecast value, or overconfidence leads to excessive risk-taking.
