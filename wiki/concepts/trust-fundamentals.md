# Trust Fundamentals

A trust is a legal arrangement where one party (trustee) holds legal title to assets for the benefit of another party (beneficiary), managed according to the terms set by the trust creator (settlor/grantor). Trusts are powerful instruments for estate planning, asset protection, wealth transfer, and tax optimization.

## Essential Components

- **Settlor/Grantor**: Creates the trust and transfers assets
- **Trustee**: Manages and administers trust assets
- **Beneficiary**: Receives benefits from the trust
- **Trust Property**: Assets held in trust
- **Trust Terms**: Rules governing trust administration
- **Trust Document**: Written document establishing the trust

## Legal Characteristics

- Separate legal entity from trustee and beneficiaries
- Must have definite beneficiaries and proper purposes
- Requires valid transfer of legal title to assets
- Governed by state law (typically where trust is created)
- Can be created during lifetime (living trust) or at death (testamentary)

## Fiduciary Duties

- **Duty of Loyalty**: Act in beneficiaries' best interests
- **Duty of Care**: Prudent management of trust assets
- **Duty of Impartiality**: Balance needs of all beneficiaries
- **Duty to Comply**: Follow trust terms and applicable laws
- **Duty to Account**: Maintain proper records and reporting

## Trust Creation Process

1. **Trust Planning**: Define objectives, identify beneficiaries, select trust type
2. **Trust Document Drafting**: Draft trust agreement with specific terms
3. **Funding the Trust**: Transfer assets, change titles, update beneficiary designations
4. **Trust Administration**: Install trustee, maintain records, file tax returns

## Key Distinction: Revocable vs. Irrevocable

- **Revocable**: Can be modified or terminated during settlor's lifetime; provides probate avoidance and privacy but no asset protection or tax benefits
- **Irrevocable**: Cannot be modified after creation; provides asset protection, tax advantages, and Medicaid planning but sacrifices settlor control

Proper funding is essential—unfunded trusts provide no benefit. Trust administration requires ongoing record-keeping, tax compliance (Form 1041, 706, 709), and beneficiary communication.
