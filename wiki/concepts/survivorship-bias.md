# Survivorship Bias

The tendency to focus on winners and ignore losers, leading to a distorted understanding of success and flawed decision-making. This bias causes us to study only those who have succeeded while overlooking the vast number who failed using the same strategies, producing misleading conclusions about what leads to success.

## Key Points
- We focus on successful survivors while ignoring those who didn't make it
- This distorts our understanding of what factors actually cause success
- Historical examples: studying World War II plane damage based only on returning aircraft, analyzing successful entrepreneurs while ignoring failed ones
- Related to base-rate neglect — failing to consider the general prevalence of outcomes

## Connection to Other Biases
Links to [[hindsight-bias]] (overestimating predictability after the fact), [[base-rate-neglect]] (ignoring general probabilities), and [[reference-class-forecasting]] (comparing to similar past cases rather than unique narratives).

## Practical Guardrail
When analyzing success stories, actively ask: "How many people tried this and failed?" and "What do the losers have in common?"
