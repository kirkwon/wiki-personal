# Willingness to Pay for Good Deeds

Willingness to pay for good deeds describes the phenomenon where people are willing to pay more for products associated with good deeds, suggesting irrationality in decision-making due to emotionally charged factors like guilt or social responsibility.

## Core Idea

Ariely presents research showing that people are often willing to pay more for products associated with good deeds, suggesting irrationality in our decision-making processes due to emotionally charged factors like guilt or social responsibility.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]

## Key Insight

This behavior demonstrates that moral and emotional considerations can override price optimization, leading consumers to pay premium prices for products that align with their ethical self-image—even when objectively equivalent alternatives exist.

## Connection to Existing Concepts

This extends [[fairness-perception]] and [[tipping-behavior]] as examples of guilt-driven and socially-motivated economic decisions that deviate from rational self-interest.
