# Economic Cycles

Economic cycles, also known as business cycles, are recurring patterns of expansion and contraction in economic activity. Ray Dalio emphasizes that these cycles are central to understanding how the economic machine works and are identifiable through macroeconomic analysis, including the use of macroeconomic graphs, historical data analysis, and predictive modeling techniques.

Cycles are driven by the interplay of credit conditions, monetary policy, fiscal policy, market forces, and technological change. Identifying where an economy sits within a cycle—through macrographs, historical data analysis, and predictive modeling—enables better decision-making for investment and wealth management.

## Key Aspects

- Business cycles follow patterns of growth, peak, contraction, and trough
- Cycles can be analyzed using indicators such as GDP, employment, inflation, and credit conditions
- Understanding cycle phases helps inform investment and policy decisions
- Historical patterns provide templates for anticipating future movements
- Understanding economic cycles helps anticipate shifts in growth, inflation, and market conditions
