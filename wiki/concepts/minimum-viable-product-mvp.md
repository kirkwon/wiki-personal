# Minimum Viable Product (MVP)

The Minimum Viable Product (MVP) is the smallest version of a product that enables a team to conduct validated learning experiments with real customers. It is not a prototype, a beta, or a stripped-down version of a final product—it is specifically designed to test the most critical hypotheses about what customers want.

The MVP serves the Build-Measure-Learn loop by providing just enough functionality to gather meaningful feedback. Building an MVP prevents the waste of investing heavily in a product that may not meet customer needs.

Key principles:
- Build the smallest thing that can teach you something
- Focus on testing hypotheses, not delivering features
- Accept that the MVP will be imperfect—it exists to learn, not to impress
- Use customer development interviews and real-world interaction to gather data

The MVP concept connects to broader experimentation thinking: just as scientists design experiments to test specific predictions, startup teams design MVPs to test specific business hypotheses. It also relates to the principle of small batch production—iterating in small, fast cycles rather than large, slow ones.
