# Confirmation Bias

**Origin:** Francis Bacon (1620), formalized by Nickerson (1998)  
**Category:** Cognitive bias

The tendency to search for, interpret, favor, and recall information that confirms one's pre-existing beliefs or hypotheses, while giving disproportionately less attention to information that contradicts them.

## Mechanisms
- **Selective search:** Looking for evidence that supports your view
- **Biased interpretation:** Twisting ambiguous evidence to fit your narrative
- **Selective memory:** Remembering confirmatory evidence better than disconfirming

## Examples
- An investor who only reads bullish analysis on a stock they hold
- A trader who attributes wins to skill and losses to bad luck
- Sticking to a market thesis despite mounting contradictory data

## Counter
Actively seek disconfirming evidence. Pre-mortems: assume your view is wrong and explain why. Track predictions with explicit falsification criteria.
