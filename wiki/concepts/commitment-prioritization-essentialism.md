# Commitment Prioritization in Essentialism

Chapter 5 of McKeown's *Essentialism* introduces a methodology for prioritizing commitments based on their alignment with personal values. The core idea is that not all commitments are equal — only those that truly matter should receive time and energy.

This prioritization methodology connects to existing frameworks like the [[time-management-matrix]] and [[quadrant-2-focus]], but introduces a distinct values-alignment lens. Rather than simply categorizing tasks by urgency and importance, essentialism asks: does this commitment align with what I truly value? If not, it should be eliminated or minimized.

The framework also ties into [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] — making high-quality decisions about commitments (based on values) leads to better life outcomes, even if individual results vary.
