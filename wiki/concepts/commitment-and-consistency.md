# Commitment and Consistency

One of Cialdini's six principles of influence. People are driven to act consistently with their prior commitments and stated positions, even when circumstances change. Once a commitment is made, internal and social pressures make it difficult to reverse course.

## How It Works
Commitments — whether public or private, small or large — create psychological momentum. People invest in their earlier decisions and seek to justify them, leading to escalating commitment even when the original rationale no longer applies.

## Applications
- Getting small initial agreements that lead to larger compliance
- Leveraging written or public commitments for behavioral change
- Understanding why people persist in decisions that no longer serve them

## Connection to Decision Quality
This principle connects to the distinction between decision quality and outcome quality — people may commit to a poor decision and stick with it to maintain consistency, regardless of outcomes.
