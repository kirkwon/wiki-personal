# Hyperfocus and Scatterfocus

Chris Bailey's two-mode attention framework posits that human attention operates in two complementary states: [[hyperfocus]] and [[scatterfocus]]. Mastering both—and knowing when to deploy each—is essential for sustained productivity and creativity.

## Hyperfocus
Intense, sustained concentration on a single task. Characterized by deep immersion, high output quality, and flow-like states. Best applied to complex, demanding work that requires undivided attention.

## Scatterfocus
Deliberate mind-wandering mode. Not laziness or distraction, but an active cognitive state where the mind wanders freely to make novel connections, rest, and recover. Scatterfocus is the source of many creative insights and prevents the burnout that pure hyperfocus causes.

## When to Use Each
- [[hyperfocus]]: Complex analysis, deep creative work, writing, problem-solving
- [[scatterfocus]]: Idea generation, recovery, creative incubation, stepping back from a problem

The key insight is that neither mode is superior—both are necessary, and the skill lies in switching intentionally between them rather than defaulting to reactive attention patterns.
