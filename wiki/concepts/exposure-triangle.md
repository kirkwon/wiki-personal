# Exposure Triangle

The exposure triangle is the foundational framework for controlling light in photography. It consists of three interdependent elements: [[aperture]], [[shutter-speed]], and [[iso]]. Proper exposure is achieved when these three elements are balanced appropriately for the lighting conditions and creative intent.

**Proper Exposure = Aperture + Shutter Speed + ISO**

Changing one element requires adjusting at least one other to maintain the same exposure.

## The Three Elements

### Aperture
Controls the size of the lens opening and thus how much light enters. Measured in f-stops, where lower numbers (f/1.4) mean larger openings and shallower depth of field, while higher numbers (f/22) mean smaller openings and deeper depth of field. Lower f-numbers give shallow depth with blurry background; higher f-numbers give deep depth with everything in focus.

### Shutter Speed
Controls how long the sensor is exposed to light. Faster speeds (1/1000s) freeze motion but let in less light; slower speeds (1/15s or longer) allow motion blur and let in more light. Faster speeds freeze motion; slower speeds create motion blur.

### ISO
Controls the sensor's sensitivity to light. Lower ISO (100) produces cleaner images but requires more light; higher ISO (6400+) increases sensitivity but introduces noise.

## Balancing the Triangle

When one element changes, another must compensate:
- Decreasing aperture (larger opening, more light) → increase shutter speed (faster) or decrease ISO
- Increasing aperture (smaller opening, less light) → decrease shutter speed (slower) or increase ISO
- Increasing shutter speed (faster, less light) → decrease aperture (larger) or increase ISO
- Decreasing shutter speed (slower, more light) → increase aperture (smaller) or decrease ISO
- Increasing ISO (more sensitive) → decrease aperture or decrease shutter speed
- Decreasing ISO (less sensitive) → increase aperture or increase shutter speed

Wider aperture → faster shutter speed or lower ISO
Faster shutter → wider aperture or higher ISO
Higher ISO → wider aperture or slower shutter

## Balancing for Genre

Different genres require different balances:
- **Portraits**: wide aperture (f/2.8–f/4), moderate shutter (1/125–1/250), low ISO (100–800)
- **Landscapes**: narrow aperture (f/8–f/16), moderate shutter, lowest ISO (100–200)
- **Street**: moderate aperture (f/8–f/11), fast shutter (1/250–1/500), auto ISO up to 3200
- **Low light**: wide aperture (f/2.8–f/4), slow shutter (1/60–30"), high ISO (800–6400)

Mastering this balance is essential for [[camera-modes]] like Manual Mode and is the foundation for genre-specific settings in [[street-photography]], [[golden-hour]], and other scenarios.
