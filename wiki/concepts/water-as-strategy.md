# Water as Strategy

Sun Tzu's central metaphor for adaptability and energy management: military forces should flow like water seeking the low ground, adapting their shape and direction to the terrain and circumstances. This appears in Chapter 5 (Energy) and recurs throughout the text.

## Key Ideas
- **Flow to low ground**: Like water, strategy should move toward the path of least resistance and greatest advantage.
- **Adaptability over rigidity**: Water changes shape to fit its container; tactics must change to fit the situation.
- **Momentum and energy**: Water accumulates power through flow—strategic momentum builds through continuous, adaptive action.
- **Exploit weakness**: Water naturally flows toward and erodes weak points; strategy should identify and attack vulnerability.

## Connections
Connects to [[energy-and-momentum]] as a mental model, [[adaptability]] concepts, [[terrain-analysis]] (understanding where to flow), and [[effort]] (channeling energy effectively). The metaphor also connects to [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]]—understanding the landscape before deciding direction.

## Application
In any domain—business, personal projects, negotiation—thinking like water means staying flexible, reading the landscape, flowing toward opportunity, and avoiding rigidity.
