# Representativeness Heuristic

The representativeness heuristic involves judging probability by similarity to a prototype or stereotype, rather than by actual statistical likelihood. It provides quick pattern recognition but ignores base rates and statistical information.

## Definition

**Representativeness Heuristic**: Judging probability by similarity to a prototype. If something seems similar to a category, people assume it belongs to that category.

## Example

A quiet person is assumed to be a librarian rather than a salesperson, because the quiet demeanor matches the stereotype of a librarian more than a salesperson — even if there are far more salespeople than librarians. Judging someone's profession based on appearance matching a stereotype.

## Impact and Limitations

Useful for quick pattern recognition, but ignores base rates and statistical information. This heuristic leads to stereotyping and poor probability judgments. Can lead to systematic errors when the prototype is misleading or when base rates strongly favor a different category.
