# Predictably Irrational - Dan Ariely

Dan Ariely's central argument: human irrationality is systematic and predictable, not random. This predictability enables the design of better systems, institutions, and personal decision-making frameworks.

## Core Framework

- **Relativity** — We value options relative to comparisons, even arbitrary ones
- **Anchoring** — Initial prices and reference points shape perceived value
- **Zero-cost bias** — "Free" has disproportionate psychological pull
- **Social vs. market norms** — Different decision rules apply in social vs. monetary contexts
- **Arousal effects** — Emotional and physiological states distort risk assessment
- **Procrastination** — Self-control failures follow predictable patterns
- **Endowment effect** — Ownership inflates perceived value
- **Expectations** — Expectations shape experiences (placebo effects)
- **Price and perception** — Price influences perceived quality
- **Context of character** — Situational factors override fixed character traits
- **Beauty perception** — Cultural and social expectations shape value judgments

## Practical Tools

- Decoy effect analysis
- Anchoring techniques
- Default option design
- Social proof mechanisms
- Framing effects
- Commitment devices
- Expectation management
- Endowment effect mitigation
- Context analysis
- A/B testing for irrational responses

## When to Stop

Recognize when analysis paralysis sets in, when countermeasures become more costly than biases, when overcorrecting misses opportunities, when social norms should dominate, when emotional decisions have value, or when immediate action is required.
