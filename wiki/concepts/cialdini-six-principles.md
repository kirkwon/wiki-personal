# Cialdini's Six Principles of Influence

Robert Cialdini identified six foundational principles that drive human compliance and persuasion. These principles provide the core mechanics of influence, and pre-suasion adds a preparatory layer that amplifies their effectiveness.

## The Six Principles

1. **Reciprocation**: People feel obligated to return favors. Giving something first creates a psychological debt that increases compliance.
2. **Commitment and Consistency**: Once people commit to a position or action, they are driven to behave consistently with that commitment, even when circumstances change.
3. **Social Proof**: People look to the behavior of others — especially similar others — to determine what is correct or desirable.
4. **Authority**: Individuals comply more readily with requests from perceived experts or authority figures.
5. **Liking**: People are more easily persuaded by those they like — familiarity, compliments, cooperation, and physical attractiveness all increase likability.
6. **Scarcity**: Items or opportunities perceived as rare or diminishing are valued more highly, increasing the urgency to act.

## Pre-Suasion and the Principles

Pre-suasion enhances these principles by priming the audience's receptivity before the persuasion attempt. For example, creating a sense of scarcity *before* presenting an offer, or establishing authority through context *before* making a request, amplifies the underlying principle.

## Relationship to Cognitive Biases

These principles map onto well-documented cognitive biases: reciprocation relates to the [[endowment-effect]] and social obligation; social proof connects to [[confirmation-bias]] and herd behavior; scarcity relates to [[fear-of-missing-out-fomo]] and loss aversion.
