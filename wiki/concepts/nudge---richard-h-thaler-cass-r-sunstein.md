# Nudge - Richard H. Thaler & Cass R. Sunstein

Nudge is a framework arguing that small, subtle environmental changes can influence decisions without restricting options or changing incentives. The book's central claim is that traditional economic models assume rational agents, but real humans are systematically biased by cognitive limitations, emotions, and heuristics.

A nudge works by altering the context in which choices are made—through defaults, ordering, framing, or the number of options presented. The key insight is that these changes can improve outcomes while preserving individual freedom, forming the basis of what Thaler and Sunstein call [[libertarian-paternalism]].

## Core Ideas
- Humans are not rational agents; they exhibit predictable biases including status quo bias, loss aversion, and present bias.
- Choice environments can be deliberately designed—this is [[choice-architecture]].
- Defaults are enormously powerful: people tend to stick with whatever is pre-selected ([[default-effects]]).
- Too many options can lead to decision paralysis, connecting to [[paradox-of-choice]].
- Nudges often operate through framing, linking to [[framing-effects]].

## Applications
Nudges have been applied in public policy across healthcare, education, and finance. Examples include opt-out organ donation programs, automatic enrollment in retirement savings, and simplified choice architectures in public services. Different countries have adopted behavioral insights into governance, with bodies like the UK Behavioural Insights Team demonstrating global relevance.

## Ethical Tensions
The book raises concerns about autonomy and manipulation. Nudging assumes that designers know what is best for people, which can backfire. The tension between guidance and freedom is central to the ethical chapter and connects to broader debates about [[ethics-and-decision-making]] and [[freedom-vs-control]].

## Relation to Other Concepts
Nudge theory is closely related to [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]]—both document systematic departures from rationality—but Nudge extends the analysis into applied policy design. It operationalizes many biases found in the [[cognitive-biases-library]] and aligns with the [[environment]] mental model, since choice architecture is essentially environment design for decisions.
