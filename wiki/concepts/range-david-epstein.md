# Range - David Epstein

David Epstein's Range argues that broad cross-disciplinary experience produces better problem-solving and innovation outcomes than narrow specialization. The book provides the foundational thesis for the cross-training concept.

## Key Ideas

- **Breadth over narrow depth**: People who explore widely across fields develop transferable mental models and problem-solving strategies.
- **Cross-disciplinary transfer**: Methods and frameworks from one domain can be applied effectively to another, improving overall productivity.
- **Comfort with uncertainty**: Range-oriented learners are more comfortable navigating ambiguity because they have encountered diverse situations.
- **Knowledge patterns and fields**: Understanding patterns across multiple fields enables more creative and robust solutions.

## Connection to Cross-training

Epstein's work directly supports the practice of [[Cross-training in different skills or disciplines]] — the idea that systematically transferring methods across disciplines maximizes productivity and learning.
