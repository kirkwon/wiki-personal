# Terrain Analysis

The systematic assessment of the operating landscape—physical, metaphorical, or competitive—to understand advantages, vulnerabilities, and opportunities. From *The Art of War* Chapters 9 and 10, which classify terrain types and discuss navigation and fighting in each.

## Key Ideas
- **Terrain classification**: Sun Tzu identifies types including dispersive ground (home territory), entangling ground (contested), key ground (strategic chokepoints), and desperate ground (no retreat).
- **Read the landscape before acting**: Understanding terrain shapes strategy—fighting on your own ground is fundamentally different from fighting on the enemy's.
- **Metaphorical terrain**: In business and life, "terrain" means market conditions, organizational dynamics, resource availability, and competitive landscape.

## Connections
Connects to [[planning]] (terrain is a factor in strategic assessment), [[environment]] as a mental model, [[situation]] (terrain is one factor among many), and [[water-as-strategy]] (water flows according to terrain). Also relates to [[superforecasting]] in that understanding your environment is foundational to accurate prediction.

## Application
Before any strategic decision, map your terrain: what advantages do you hold? Where are the chokepoints? What ground is contested? Where is the high ground?
