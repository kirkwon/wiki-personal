# Intelligence as a Mental Model

Intelligence is a universal conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern and relationship recognition. Rather than being defined solely as cognitive ability or IQ, intelligence functions as a mental shortcut—a framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world across all domains.

## Core Idea

Intelligence provides a way to understand and navigate complex situations by recognizing patterns and relationships. This reframing moves intelligence from a fixed trait to a universal principle applicable everywhere, from individual decision-making to collective systems and organizational fields.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

Four key sources converge on this model:

- **Brian Christian & Tom Griffiths** — From [[the-most-human-human]], intelligence is what makes humans distinct, explored through the lens of human uniqueness in language and cognition.
- **Peter M. Senge** — From [[presence]], intelligence connects to human purpose and the field of the future, emphasizing organizational awareness and collective intention.
- **Kevin Kelly** — From [[out-of-control]], intelligence emerges from complex systems and is not centrally controlled, pointing to emergent and distributed forms of intelligence.
- **James Surowiecki** — From [[the-wisdom-of-crowds]], collective intelligence arises when diverse opinions are aggregated, showing that intelligence can be a property of groups rather than individuals alone.

## Relationship to Other Concepts

This model overlaps with and connects to several existing wiki concepts:

- [[solutions-mental-model]] — Both aim to navigate complexity, but intelligence emphasizes pattern recognition as the mechanism.
- [[biases]] — Intelligence as a model provides a counterweight to biases by focusing on recognizing patterns rather than relying on shortcuts that distort perception.
- [[superforecasting]] — Both involve probabilistic thinking and pattern recognition, though superforecasting is more specific to prediction accuracy.

## Tensions and Open Questions

An internal tension exists between framing intelligence as individual pattern-recognition (Christian/Griffiths) and as a collective or emergent property (Kelly, Surowiecki). This blurs the distinction between personal cognitive skill and systemic intelligence. The model is exploratory with moderate confidence (0.7), and its relationship to [[thinking-mental-model]] and [[problem-solving]] deserves further clarification.
