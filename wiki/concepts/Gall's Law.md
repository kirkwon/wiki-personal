# Gall's Law

Gall's Law states: **"A complex system that works is invariably found to have evolved from a simple system that worked."** This is the central prescription of John Gall's *The Systems Bible* and one of the most widely cited principles in systems thinking.

## Core Idea
Complex systems cannot be designed whole. They must evolve incrementally from simple, working foundations. Attempting to build a complex system from scratch will almost always fail or produce something that doesn't work as intended.

## Implications
- Start simple and evolve complexity gradually
- If you need a complex system, first build a simple version that works
- Never design complexity upfront
- The path from simple to complex is the only reliable route
- This applies to software, organizations, processes, and any domain involving emergent complexity

## Connection to Other Concepts
Gall's Law is closely related to [[antifragility]]—both recognize that systems gain resilience through incremental adaptation rather than top-down design. It also connects to [[decomposing-and-recombining]] and [[systematic-decision-making]], though Gall frames the insight specifically around system lifecycle rather than problem-solving technique.

## When to Stop
Gall warns against using his law to justify never building complex systems, or to abandon working systems prematurely. The law is a guide for evolution, not a reason for stasis.
