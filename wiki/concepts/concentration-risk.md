# Concentration Risk

Concentration risk occurs when investments are excessively exposed to a single asset, fund, manager, stock, company, or sector, creating a single point of failure. In the context of alternative investments, this means a single property, fund, or commodity position represents the entire exposure to that asset class.

## Examples

- Single property = 100% of real estate exposure
- Single private equity fund = 100% of private equity allocation
- Gold = 100% of commodity exposure
- Single stock: excessive exposure to one company
- Single-sector portfolio
- Employer stocks

## Guidelines

- Single stock: <5% (behavioral), <10% (aggressive)
- Single-sector portfolio: <20%
- Employer stocks: <10% (total optimization of uncertainty)

## Mitigation

- Diversify within each asset class (multiple properties, multiple funds, multiple commodities, multiple collectibles)
- Spread across different geographies and sectors
- Diversification beyond companies, sectors, geography, and company size
- Maintain a core-satellite approach: traditional investments as core, alternatives as satellite
- Follow the source recommendation: diversify across alternatives (real estate 40-60%, private equity 20-40%, commodities 10-20%, collectibles/crypto 0-10%)
- Limit position size through [[Position Sizing]]
- Diversify across multiple companies and sectors
