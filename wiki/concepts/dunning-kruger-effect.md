# Dunning-Kruger Effect

The Dunning-Kruger effect describes the cognitive bias where people overestimate their ability when they are incompetent and underestimate their ability when they are competent. Covered in Part VII of *Super Thinking* on learning and growth.

## Core Idea

Those with limited knowledge in a domain often lack the metacognitive ability to recognize their own incompetence, leading to inflated self-assessment. Conversely, experts may underestimate their abilities because they are acutely aware of how much they don't know.

## Applications

- **Self-assessment**: Recognize when your confidence may not match your competence.
- **Team dynamics**: Be wary of confident but inexperienced voices dominating discussions.
- **Learning**: Use the effect as motivation — if you know little, there is much to learn.

## Connection to Growth Mindset

The Dunning-Kruger effect connects to [[growth-mindset]] — a growth mindset provides a framework for acknowledging gaps in knowledge without ego attachment, and serves as a psychological buffer against Dunning-Kruger overconfidence by framing ability as developable rather than fixed. Weinberg includes it alongside growth mindset, deliberate practice, and spaced repetition in the learning section of *Super Thinking*.
