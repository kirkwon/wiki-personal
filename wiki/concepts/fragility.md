# Fragility

Fragility is a universal mental model that serves as a conceptual tool for understanding and navigating complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.^[raw/articles/Fragility.md]

Fragility describes a state where systems are harmed by volatility, stress, disorder, or uncertainty. The opposite of fragility is antifragility — systems that benefit from or grow stronger under stress.^[raw/articles/Fragility.md]

## Key Distinctions
- **Fragility**: A system that breaks down, degrades, or is damaged when exposed to stress, volatility, or disorder.
- **Antifragility**: A system that improves, adapts, or becomes stronger when exposed to stress, volatility, or disorder.^[raw/articles/Fragility.md]

## Relationship to Antifragility
The concept of fragility is directly tied to Nassim Nicholas Taleb's work on antifragility. Where antifragile systems gain from disorder, fragile systems suffer from it. Understanding this spectrum helps in assessing risks, designing resilient systems, and making better decisions under uncertainty.

## Applicability
This mental model is applicable across general domains — from personal decision-making to organizational strategy to financial planning. Recognizing fragility in a system allows one to either protect it from stress or redesign it to become antifragile.
