# Explanations Mental Model

Explanations is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

Drawing from David Deutsch's *The Beginning of Infinity*, the core insight is that explanation itself is a foundational mental model applicable universally across domains. A good explanation is one that is **hard to vary** — it pinpoints why something happens rather than offering vague or unfalsifiable claims. The reach of good explanations is infinite because we can always construct better ones, meaning the growth of knowledge is unbounded.

Bad explanations, by contrast, are easy to vary. Phrases like "god did it" or "it's just the way things are" feel satisfying but are explanatory dead ends because they cannot be refined or tested. This connects directly to [[confirmation-bias]] and [[overconfidence-and-certainty]] — bad explanations often feel satisfying precisely because they are unfalsifiable.

The "hard to vary" criterion serves as a practical filter for evaluating theories, hypotheses, and mental models. When faced with a complex situation, asking "is this explanation hard to vary?" helps distinguish between genuine understanding and mere hand-waving.

This model sits alongside [[theory-as-a-mental-model]], [[ideas-mental-model]], and [[thinking-mental-model]] as abstract frameworks for navigating complexity, and connects to [[problem-solving]], [[polya-four-steps]], and [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]] where explanations are the output of the reasoning process.
