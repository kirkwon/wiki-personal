# Illusion of Pattern in Randomness

The illusion of pattern in randomness describes the human tendency to perceive meaningful patterns, structures, or causes in data that is actually random. Humans have a strong propensity for finding patterns even when none exist. This cognitive bias causes us to impose order on random events, leading us astray from recognizing randomness' true nature.

This drives us to construct causal narratives from coincidences and to believe we understand systems that are fundamentally governed by unpredictable variation. Taleb identifies this as a core cognitive failure: humans are wired to find patterns, even when none exist. This is a fundamental limitation of human cognition that structured decision-making frameworks cannot fully overcome, and it is particularly dangerous in domains like financial trading, where apparent trends may be nothing more than random fluctuation.

## Definition
Apophenia — the tendency to perceive meaningful connections between unrelated things. In the context of randomness, we see signals where there is only noise, and we attribute outcomes to causes that are actually random. ## Key Points
- Cognitive biases drive pattern-seeking behavior
- Random events can appear systematic with small samples
- Recognizing true randomness is counterintuitive
- Overfitting to noise leads to poor decision-making

## Practical Implications
- Before accepting a perceived pattern, ask whether it could arise from randomness alone
- Be especially wary of patterns in small datasets or short time periods
- Use statistical tests to distinguish signal from noise rather than relying on narrative intuition
