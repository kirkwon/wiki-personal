# How the Economic Machine Works - Ray Dalio

Ray Dalio's "How the Economic Machine Works" is a simplified model of how economies function, widely cited as an accessible framework for understanding macroeconomic dynamics.

## Core Framework

Dalio's model centers on the relationship between spending and income, driven by:
- **Credit availability**: The ability to borrow and lend shapes economic activity.
- **Productivity growth**: Long-term growth depends on increases in output per worker.
- **Money printing**: Central banks can stimulate demand by creating new money.
- **Debt burdens**: High levels of debt relative to income create vulnerability and may necessitate deleveraging.

## Economic Phases

The model describes cyclical phases:
1. **Expansion**: Credit is abundant, spending rises, and the economy grows.
2. **Peak**: Debt burdens become unsustainable, and the gap between spending and income widens.
3. **Deleveraging**: Debt must be reduced through austerity, debt restructuring, or money printing.
4. **Normalization**: The economy stabilizes at a new equilibrium.

## Limitations

Dalio's framework is a heuristic, not a rigorous model. It simplifies complex dynamics into cyclical patterns, which may not fully capture tail risks, structural breaks, or the antifragile properties that [[antifragility]] highlights. The confidence rating of 0.7 reflects moderate uncertainty about the model's completeness.
