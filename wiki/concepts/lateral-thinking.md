# Lateral Thinking

Lateral thinking is a non-linear, generative approach to problem-solving developed by Edward de Bono that involves looking at problems from unexpected angles to generate creative solutions.^[raw/articles/Barriers.md] It challenges established mental patterns and explores alternative possibilities, contrasting with vertical thinking, which follows logical, step-by-step reasoning.^[raw/articles/Barriers.md] Rather than proceeding through known information, lateral thinking deliberately breaks out of conventional frameworks to produce novel solutions. It is covered in Part V of *Super Thinking* and is distinct from both vertical logical analysis and pure brainstorming.

The concept is central to de Bono's framework and serves as the foundation for several of his mental models, including the [[barriers]] model and the [[six-thinking-hats]].^[raw/articles/Barriers.md]

## Core Principles

- **Teachable methodology**: Lateral thinking is not an innate gift but a structured skill that can be developed through specific techniques
- **Deliberate departure from established patterns** — deliberately seeking to move beyond the obvious and the routine
- **Problem finding matters**: Identifying the right problem is as important as solving it; many failures stem from solving the wrong problem
- **Random entry points** — using random stimuli to provoke new associations
- **Challenge assumptions** — questioning the implicit constraints that shape how a problem is framed
- **Breaking barriers**: Mental barriers constrain thinking and can be overcome through deliberate techniques like zooming out and considering alternative possibilities; this connects to the [[barriers]] model
- **Alternatives and possibilities** — generating multiple solutions rather than seeking a single correct answer
- **Language shapes thought**: The words we use constrain or enable new ideas; replacing language can shift perspective entirely

## Key Techniques

Lateral thinking involves specific movements including "what if," "wonder why," and "undo what you did" — all designed to expand the range of possibilities considered. These connect to [[lateral-movements-in-thinking]] and [[breaking-barriers-to-thinking]].

## Relationship to Analytical Thinking

Lateral thinking complements analytical approaches like those in [[how-to-solve-it---george-polya]]. Where Polya emphasizes structured understanding, planning, and verification, de Bono emphasizes generative exploration and challenging assumptions. The two approaches serve different phases of the problem-solving process.

## Relationship to Six Thinking Hats

Lateral thinking is the broader methodology from which the [[six-thinking-hats]] framework is derived. While Six Thinking Hats assigns specific cognitive roles (facts, emotions, caution, optimism, creativity, process control), lateral thinking provides the underlying philosophy of deliberately switching cognitive modes to escape habitual thinking patterns. The [[six-thinking-hats]] method provides a structured group framework for applying these principles in collaborative settings.

## Connection to Edward de Bono

Edward de Bono is the psychologist who pioneered lateral thinking as a formal discipline. His work *Lateral Thinking: Creativity Step by Step* outlines the systematic techniques for structured creative thinking, distinguishing it from both vertical logical analysis and pure brainstorming.

## Open Questions

The boundary between lateral thinking and general creativity techniques remains somewhat blurry. De Bono's framework provides useful structure, but the degree to which it offers unique insights beyond well-established creative problem-solving methods is debated.
