# Rule of Thirds

The rule of thirds is a fundamental composition technique that divides the frame into a 3x3 grid of nine equal parts. Place important elements at the four intersections where the grid lines cross, or along the lines themselves, rather than in the center of the frame. This creates more dynamic and visually engaging images.

See [[composition-techniques]] and [[photography-fundamentals]] for context.

## Why It Works

Creates balance and natural tension, mimicking how humans naturally view scenes. It avoids the static feeling of center-placement and provides a more dynamic, engaging composition than centering the subject. ## How to Apply

1. Enable the grid on your camera (most cameras have this feature)
2. Place the subject at the intersections (4 points where lines cross) or along the lines
3. Align the horizon with the top or bottom grid lines, not the middle

## Examples

- Portrait with horizon: Subject at intersection, horizon at top or bottom third
- Landscape: Horizon at top or bottom third, interesting element at intersection
- Street scene: Main subject at intersection, leading lines to that point

## When to Break the Rule

Center composition can work for symmetry or a strong central subject. Breaking the rule intentionally creates tension, imbalance, or surprise when the effect demands it.
