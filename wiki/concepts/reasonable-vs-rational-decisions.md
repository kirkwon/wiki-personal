# Reasonable vs. Rational Decisions

The distinction between "reasonable" and "rational" is central to the psychology of money. Rational decisions optimize for mathematical optimality, while reasonable decisions optimize for psychological sustainability. In financial decision-making, "reasonable" (emotionally sustainable) decisions often beat "rational" (mathematically optimal) ones over time.

## The Distinction

**Rational**: Pay off low-interest debt with cash (mathematically optimal).
**Reasonable**: Keep emergency fund even with debt (sleep better at night).^[raw/articles/The Psychology of Money.md]

A technically correct decision that causes stress, anxiety, or behavioral spillover costs is worse in practice than a slightly suboptimal decision you can sustain without psychological harm.

## Spillover Costs

Bad decisions made under stress from poor financial psychology create cascading negative effects—emotional selling, revenge trading, fear-based avoidance. These spillover costs compound over time. A "reasonable" decision that prevents these spillover costs is more valuable than a marginal improvement in expected return. A mathematically optimal decision you can't stick with emotionally is worse than a slightly suboptimal decision you'll actually follow through on.

## Tension with Systematic Frameworks

This concept partially conflicts with the wiki's emphasis on [[systematic-decision-making]] and [[expected-value-ev]] frameworks, which prioritize mathematical rigor. The psychology of money argues that emotional sustainability matters more than mathematical optimality in personal finance.

## Connection to Decision Quality

This connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by emphasizing that process quality matters more than outcome quality. It also relates to [[systematic-decision-making]] by suggesting that the best systematic approach is one you can actually follow consistently.

## Practical Application

- Make financial decisions you can live with emotionally
- Prioritize the "sleep-at-night factor" over theoretical optimization
- Focus on what you'll actually stick with over time
- Build in margin of safety to accommodate human error
