# Judgments

Judgments is a universal mental model—a conceptual shortcut for navigating complex situations through pattern recognition and relationship mapping. It functions as a meta-framework that helps make sense of how people assess, decide, and evaluate across domains.

## Core Idea

Judgment provides a mental shortcut for understanding patterns and relationships in the world. Rather than treating every decision as unique, the Judgments model encourages recognizing recurring structures in how humans assess uncertainty, weigh evidence, and arrive at conclusions.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

### Daniel Kahneman: Noise

Kahneman's work on noise identifies a distinct category of error in human judgment—systematic variability in how different people or the same person in different contexts arrives at judgments. This is separate from bias (which is a consistent directional error). Noise represents random scatter around a target judgment, and it is pervasive in professional and personal decisions.^[raw/articles/Judgments.md]

### Rolf Dobelli: Cognitive Biases

Dobelli's "The Art of Thinking Clearly" provides a comprehensive catalog of cognitive biases and thinking errors that distort judgment. This complements Kahneman's noise concept by cataloging systematic errors that lead people astray.^[raw/articles/Judgments.md]

## Key Distinction: Noise vs. Bias

A critical insight from Kahneman is the difference between:
- **Bias**: Systematic, directional error (e.g., consistently overestimating risks)
- **Noise**: Random variability in judgment (e.g., two doctors giving different diagnoses for the same symptoms)

This distinction is underexplored in the wiki and provides a useful lens for understanding why judgment quality varies even among competent decision-makers.

## Applicability

Judgments is applicable universally—across professional decisions, personal choices, team evaluations, and any context requiring assessment under uncertainty.
