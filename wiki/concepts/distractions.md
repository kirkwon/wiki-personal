# Distractions

Distractions is a mental model that frames distraction as a navigational concept rather than purely a negative force. It provides a conceptual shortcut for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex situations, helping to identify when and why attention is pulled away from focus areas.

## Key Ideas
- Distractions function as signals about priorities, environments, and cognitive load
- The model connects to attention management frameworks already in the wiki, including [[Hyperfocus - Chris Bailey]], [[scatterfocus]], [[attention-residue]], and [[attention-budget]]
- Cal Newport's work on [[deep-work---cal-newport]] and [[digital-minimalism]] provides the primary theoretical foundation for this model
- Rather than treating all distractions as failures, the model encourages recognizing distraction patterns as useful information for navigating complexity

- How does this model interact with [[attention-residue]] — the leftover mental focus from previous tasks?
- Where does the line blur between productive cognitive wandering and unproductive distraction?
