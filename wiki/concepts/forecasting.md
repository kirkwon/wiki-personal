# Forecasting

Forecasting is a universal mental model that serves as a conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern recognition and sense-making. It provides a framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in uncertain environments, distinguishing itself from mere prediction by emphasizing probabilistic reasoning and iterative belief updating.

## Core Principles

Forecasting as a mental model draws on Philip E. Tetlock and Dan Gardner's research on superforecasting. Key principles include:

- **Calibration** — Aligning subjective probability estimates with actual outcomes over time. Well-calibrated forecasters know when they are uncertain and express confidence levels that match reality.
- **Base rates** — Using historical frequencies and reference class data as starting points before incorporating specific case information.
- **Diverse aggregation** — Combining insights from multiple perspectives and viewpoints to improve accuracy, recognizing that group forecasts often outperform individual ones.
- **Intellectual humility** — Maintaining openness to updating beliefs when new evidence emerges, resisting the urge to anchor too strongly on initial impressions.
- **Tracking accuracy** — Systematically recording predictions and outcomes to measure and improve forecasting performance over time.

## Relationship to Superforecasting

While superforecasting describes the practice of making highly accurate probabilistic predictions, forecasting as a mental model is the broader conceptual framework. Superforecasting is the application; forecasting is the lens through which complex situations are interpreted and navigated. This connects to [[prediction-markets]] as a mechanism for aggregating diverse forecasts into probability estimates.
