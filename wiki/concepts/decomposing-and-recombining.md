# Decomposing and Recombining

Decomposing and recombining is a heuristic for managing complexity: break a complex problem into simpler, manageable parts, solve each part separately, then reassemble the solutions into a coherent whole.

Polya identifies this as one of the most fundamental strategies in problem-solving. It appears in the "devising a plan" phase and connects to Descartes' Rule of dividing each difficulty into as many parts as possible. The approach is widely applicable beyond mathematics—to engineering, business strategy, and [[decision-making-frameworks]] generally.

After solving the sub-problems, the recombination phase requires attention: the parts must fit together correctly, and interactions between them may introduce new complications.
