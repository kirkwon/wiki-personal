# Surgeons (mental model)

The **Surgeons** mental model is a conceptual tool that uses surgical thinking as a framework for understanding and navigating complex, uncertain situations. It provides a mental shortcut for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world, particularly in domains where outcomes are imperfect and information is incomplete.

## Core Principles
- **Accepting messiness**: Complex systems do not yield to clean, predictable solutions. The surgeon learns to work within constraints rather than against them.
- **Decision-making under incomplete information**: Surgical decisions are often made without full knowledge of outcomes, requiring comfort with ambiguity.
- **Learning from failure**: Adverse outcomes are treated as data points for improvement, not as definitive judgments of competence.
- **Epistemic humility**: Recognizing that expertise does not eliminate uncertainty—it merely provides better tools for navigating it.

## Origin
The model is drawn from [[atul-gawande]]'s clinical experience and his book *[[Complications: A Surgeon's Notes on an Imperfect Science|Complications: A Surgeon's Notes on an Imperfect Science]]*, which argues that the surgeon's mindset of embracing complication and imperfection is broadly applicable beyond the operating room.

## Relationship to Other Mental Models
- Overlaps with [[checklists]] and [[the-checklist-manifesto---atul-gawande]], which address systematic approaches to managing complexity.
- Connects to [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]] and [[systematic-decision-making]].
- Complements [[embracing-failure-for-personal-growth]] by framing failure as an inherent feature of complex work rather than an aberration.
- Shares terrain with [[being-mortal]], Gawande's exploration of end-of-life care and the limits of medical intervention.

## Applicability
Universal—applicable to any domain involving complex systems, incomplete information, and the need to act despite uncertainty.
