# Boot

In the context of 1031 exchanges and real estate transactions, **boot** refers to cash or mortgage relief received by a taxpayer that triggers a taxable event, preventing full tax deferral.

## Types of Boot

### Cash Boot
Cash received by the taxpayer during an exchange. This can include net proceeds after acquisition, cash received in addition to replacement property, or mortgage relief that is not fully replaced. Cash boot is taxable at capital gains rates.

### Mortgage Boot
Debt relief that exceeds the new debt assumption. Calculated as:

```
Mortgage Boot = Old Mortgage − New Mortgage
Taxable Amount = Mortgage Boot × Capital Gain Percentage
```

Mortgage boot is also subject to capital gains taxation.

## Avoiding Boot

Strategies to avoid boot include:
- Replacing 100% of debt with new debt
- Using all equity to acquire higher-value properties
- Taking minimal cash distributions
- Structuring exchanges as reverse exchanges when needed

## Connection to Return of Capital

Boot is related to [[return-of-capital]] in that both involve the return of the investor's own capital in a manner that has tax implications. However, boot in a 1031 exchange specifically triggers capital gains taxation, whereas return of capital reduces cost basis. The [[yield-trap]] concept similarly deals with tax complexity in income-producing assets, but 1031 boot is specifically about capital gains deferral mechanics.
