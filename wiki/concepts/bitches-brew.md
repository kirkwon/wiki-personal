# Bitches Brew

**Bitches Brew** (1970) is the landmark fusion album by Miles Davis that birthed jazz-rock fusion. Featuring electric instruments, rock rhythms, and extensive studio experimentation, it represents Davis's radical reinvention of jazz in the late 1960s.

## Background
Recorded in 1969-1970, the album assembled a large ensemble of electric musicians. Key tracks include "Bitches Brew" and "Spanish Key."

## Musical Characteristics
- Electric instruments (electric piano, electric bass, drum machines)
- Rock-influenced rhythms and dense, layered textures
- Studio experimentation with overdubbing and extended improvisation
- Shift from small-group jazz to large-scale, almost orchestral arrangements

## Influence
Bitches Brew directly inspired fusion bands including Weather Report, Mahavishnu Orchestra, and Return to Forever. It opened the door for jazz to incorporate rock energy and electronic production.

## Legacy
As one of Miles Davis's two most consequential innovations (alongside [[kind-of-blue]] and [[modal-jazz]]), Bitches Brew demonstrated that jazz could evolve beyond acoustic traditions without losing its improvisational core.
