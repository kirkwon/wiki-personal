# Zero-Cost Bias

The disproportionate psychological pull of "free" — when zero is involved in pricing or offers, people make irrational choices that override normal cost-benefit analysis.

## Key Insight

"Free" triggers an emotional response that is not proportional to the actual value of the offer. People will choose a free option over a better-value paid option, accept inferior products when they are free, and make decisions that are not in their rational self-interest simply because the price is zero.

## Applications

- Understanding why "free shipping" or "free trial" promotions are so powerful
- Recognizing when zero-cost offers are distorting your purchasing decisions
- Designing incentive systems that account for the irrational weight of free offerings
- Identifying when "free" is being used as a manipulation tool in marketing

## Related Concepts

Connects to [[prospect-theory]]'s asymmetric valuation of gains and losses, and to Ariely's broader argument that irrationality is systematic and therefore manageable through better system design.
