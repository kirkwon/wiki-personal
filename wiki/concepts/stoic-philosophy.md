# Stoic Philosophy

Stoic philosophy is an ancient Greek and Roman school of thought emphasizing virtue, rationality, self-discipline, and acceptance of what is beyond one's control. Key figures include Marcus Aurelius, Seneca, and Epictetus. Modern interpreters like Ryan Holiday have popularized Stoic ideas for contemporary life, connecting them to decision-making, emotional regulation, and personal mastery.

## Core Principles
- **Dichotomy of control** — Focus only on what you can control; accept what you cannot
- **Inner citadel** — Cultivate an inner fortress of calm and resilience regardless of external circumstances
- **Virtue as the highest good** — Moral character matters more than outcomes or external success
- **Amor fati** — Embrace fate and obstacles as opportunities for growth
- **Memento mori** — Remember mortality to prioritize what truly matters

## Connection to Stillness
Stoicism provides the philosophical foundation for stillness as described by Holiday. The Stoic emphasis on mental discipline, pause before reaction, and acceptance of uncertainty maps directly onto the practice of cultivating inner quietude for clearer decisions and healthier relationships.
