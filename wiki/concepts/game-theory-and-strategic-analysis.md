# Game Theory and Strategic Analysis

Game theory studies strategic decision-making in situations where outcomes depend on the choices of multiple agents. It provides frameworks for analyzing competition, cooperation, and conflict.

## Core Models
- **Prisoner's Dilemma** — A classic scenario where individual rationality leads to collectively suboptimal outcomes. Connects to [[prisoner-s-dilemma]].
- **Tragedy of the Commons** — Individual rationality leading to collective irrationality over shared resources. Connects to [[tragedy-of-the-commons]].
- **Network Effects** — Products or services becoming more valuable as more people use them. Connects to [[network-effects]].
- **Competitive Advantage** — Factors that give one entity an advantage over others.

## Applications
- Business strategy and competitive positioning
- Negotiation and bargaining
- Platform and marketplace design
- International relations and policy

Game theory connects to [[strategic-bluffing]], [[decision-making-under-uncertainty]], and [[bayesian-thinking]] (updating beliefs about others' strategies). Weinberg places it in the strategic thinking section of *Super Thinking*.
