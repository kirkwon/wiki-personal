# Roth Conversion Ladder

A Roth conversion ladder is a multi-year strategy for converting traditional IRA assets to Roth IRA accounts while managing tax impact. Rather than converting large lump sums (which risk triggering tax torpedoes), the ladder spreads conversions across several years, staying below key tax thresholds.

## How It Works

1. Convert a portion of traditional IRA to Roth each year.
2. Keep annual conversion amounts below income thresholds that would trigger IRMAA surcharges, Social Security taxation, or higher tax brackets.
3. Over time, reduce or eliminate traditional IRA balances before Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) begin at age 73.
4. In retirement, withdraw from Roth accounts first (tax-free) to minimize taxable income.

## Benefits

- Removes RMD burden from the converted amount (Roth IRAs have no RMDs during the owner's lifetime).
- Provides tax-free growth and withdrawals in retirement.
- Allows fine-grained control over annual taxable income.
- Can reduce the "tax on tax" effect where RMDs trigger additional tax liabilities.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]

## Risks and Cautions

- Converting too much in any single year can trigger IRMAA surcharges or Social Security taxation cliffs.
- The $51,000 example in the source shows how a $200,000 conversion can backfire if it pushes provisional income above thresholds.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
- State tax implications vary—Roth conversions may not be beneficial in high-tax states.
- The optimal conversion rate is individual-specific; there is no universal threshold.

## Timing

- **Pre-retirement (10+ years out)**: Begin early conversions to build Roth balance.
- **Transition (5-10 years before)**: Maximize final conversions below thresholds.
- **Early retirement (first 5 years)**: Execute the ladder strategy systematically.
- **Late retirement (5+ years after)**: Remove remaining traditional IRA balances.^[Tax Torpedoes.md]
