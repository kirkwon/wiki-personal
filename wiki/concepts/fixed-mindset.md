# Fixed Mindset

A fixed mindset is the belief that personal qualities — intelligence, talent, character — are innate and unchangeable. According to Carol S. Dweck's research, this contrasts with a growth mindset and has wide-ranging effects on how people approach challenges, failure, and learning. Individuals with a fixed mindset tend to avoid challenges, fear failure, and interpret criticism as a threat to their identity.

## Key Characteristics
- Certainty that abilities are static and predetermined
- **Avoidance of challenge**: Difficulty is seen as a threat to self-image rather than an opportunity to grow. Individuals avoid challenges that risk exposing their limitations.
- **Fear of failure**: Setbacks are interpreted as evidence of inherent inadequacy. Individuals become fragile in the face of failure, seeing setbacks as confirmation of their fixed limits.
- **Defensiveness**: Criticism is received as a personal attack rather than constructive feedback. Useful negative feedback is ignored.
- Reduced effort when success is uncertain; giving up easily when facing obstacles. Effort is viewed as fruitless if talent is lacking, and as a sign of inadequacy rather than a path to improvement.
- **Tendency to plateau**: Without belief in development, effort may be abandoned when initial attempts don't yield results.
- Feeling threatened by others' success
- Viewing failure as evidence of inherent inadequacy rather than a learning opportunity
- Vulnerability to negative self-evaluation after setbacks
- Seeking validation through performance outcomes rather than learning processes

Fixed mindset thinking can be reinforced by person-focused praise (e.g., "You're so smart") and undermined by process-focused feedback that emphasizes strategies and effort.

## Manifestations
Dweck identifies fixed mindsets across domains:
- **Education**: students avoiding difficulty
- **Business**: leaders resisting innovation
- **Relationships**: partners judging rather than understanding
- **Personal development**: adults believing they're "too old" to learn

## Connection to Growth Mindset
The fixed mindset contrasts directly with the [[growth-mindset]], where abilities are viewed as developable through dedication and hard work. Research by Carol S. Dweck demonstrates that shifting from a fixed to a growth mindset can improve motivation, resilience, and performance across education, workplace, and personal development contexts. Understanding both perspectives is essential for the broader [[mindset-mental-model]], which recognizes that beliefs about ability shape behavior, learning, and outcomes across all domains.

## Connection to Cognitive Biases
Fixed mindset thinking connects to cognitive biases like [[overconfidence-and-certainty]], where individuals overestimate the stability of their traits and believe current abilities are fixed and accurately known. It also connects to [[ascription-bias]], where individuals attribute success to innate qualities rather than effort or strategy.
