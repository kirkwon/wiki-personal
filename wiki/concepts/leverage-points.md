# Leverage Points

Leverage points are places within a system where a small shift in one thing can produce large changes in everything. Meadows identified 12 categories of leverage points, ranging from parameters and buffer sizes at the lower end to system goals, paradigms, and the power to transcend paradigms at the highest level.

## Hierarchy of Leverage Points

Meadows describes a hierarchy from least to most powerful:
- Parameters and constants (weakest)
- Buffer sizes and stabilizing stocks
- Structure of material stocks and flows
- Length of delays and timing
- Strength of negative feedback loops
- Gain around positive feedback loops
- Information flows and access to information
- Rules of the system
- Structure of the system (power over who does what)
- Goals of the system
- Paradigms and mindsets (strongest)

## Key Insight

Effective change requires identifying the right leverage point. Interventions at higher-level leverage points — changing goals, mindsets, or the rules of the system — are far more impactful than tweaking parameters. Working at the level of paradigms and goals produces the most durable and transformative change, while tweaking parameters often has minimal effect.

Meadows connects leverage points to feedback loops and nonlinear behavior — because systems are nonlinear, small inputs at the right point can trigger disproportionate effects. This concept relates to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] by suggesting that the quality of an intervention depends on understanding system structure, not just predicting outcomes.
