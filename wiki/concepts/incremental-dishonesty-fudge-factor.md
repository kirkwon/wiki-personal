# Incremental Dishonesty (Fudge Factor)

The tendency for people to cheat just enough to benefit without breaking their self-image as honest. Rather than being binary (completely honest or completely dishonest), human dishonesty operates on a spectrum where people engage in small, incremental lies that maximize personal gain while preserving their moral identity.

## Key Findings
- People do not typically lie in extreme ways; instead they fudge just enough
- The amount of dishonesty scales with opportunity and incentive structures
- Cognitive effort required for lying creates a natural limit on how much people cheat
- Situational factors like peer pressure, authority, and cultural norms shift the threshold

## Experimental Evidence
Dan Ariely's matrix task experiments and other carefully designed studies demonstrate that most people will cheat to some degree when given the opportunity, but rarely to the maximum possible extent. This "fudge factor" is consistent across cultures and contexts.
