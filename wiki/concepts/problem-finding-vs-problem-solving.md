# Problem Finding vs. Problem Solving

Edward de Bono distinguishes between two critical phases of creative work: problem finding (identifying what the real problem is) and problem solving (executing solutions once the problem is clearly defined). This distinction is central to his lateral thinking methodology and has significant implications for how we approach challenges.

## The Distinction

- **Problem finding**: Determining what needs to be addressed, reframing questions, questioning assumptions, recognizing hidden constraints or opportunities, and identifying the actual obstacle rather than symptoms.
- **Problem solving**: Generating and implementing solutions once the problem is clearly defined.

## Why It Matters

Many failures stem from solving the wrong problem. People often rush to solutions without adequately exploring whether they are addressing the root cause. De Bono argues that spending more time on problem finding can dramatically improve outcomes. Without clear problem finding, even excellent solutions address the wrong question.

## Techniques

- Zooming out to see the bigger picture
- Asking "what if" and "wonder why" questions
- Challenging the language used to describe the problem
- Using metaphors and analogies to reframe the situation
- Questioning assumptions and reframing the issue
