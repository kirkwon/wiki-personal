# Yield Trap

A yield trap occurs when a high headline yield masks return of capital (ROC) distributions, resulting in principal erosion rather than genuine income.

## The Problem

An investment yielding 10% with 60% ROC provides only 4% true income. Your principal (cost basis) is declining by 6% annually while you believe you're earning 10%.

- A fund yielding 10% with 60% ROC delivers only 4% true income.  
- The remaining 6% is the investor's own capital being returned.  
- Cost basis declines by 6% annually while the investor may not realize it.  
- If fund NAV declines faster than ROC, principal is destroyed.

## The Math

If a fund pays a 10% distribution and 60% of it is ROC:

- **True income**: 10% × 40% = 4%  
- **Principal erosion**: 10% × 60% = 6% annually  
- **Net effect**: You're losing 2% of your investment value per year after accounting for the “income” you received.

## Warning Signs

- Headline yield significantly higher than peers.  
- Distribution breakdown shows a large ROC component.  
- Fund NAV is declining or flat despite distributions.  
- Fund is a CEF, covered‑call ETF, or REIT with aggressive distributions.  

**Common culprits**

- Closed‑End Funds (CEFs) with high distribution rates  
- Covered Call ETFs (e.g., QYLD, JEPI, NUSI)  
- REITs distributing ROC due to depreciation  
- MLPs in energy infrastructure  
- Bond funds returning principal  

## Avoiding the Trap

1. **Check the distribution breakdown** – Income vs ROC vs capital gains.  
2. **Calculate true yield** – Only count the income portion.  
3. **Assess NAV trend** – Is ROC eroding principal?  
4. **Consider your tax bracket** – Does deferral benefit you?  
5. **Compare alternatives** – Is there a more tax‑efficient option?  

> **Key warning**: Always check the distribution breakdown, not just the headline yield.
