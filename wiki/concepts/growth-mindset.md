# Growth Mindset

A growth mindset is the belief that abilities, intelligence, and talents can be developed through dedication, effort, learning, persistence, and hard work, as opposed to a fixed mindset which holds that these traits are innate and static. As a mental model, it functions as a conceptual shortcut for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex situations, helping navigate challenges by reframing effort and failure as opportunities for development rather than evidence of inadequacy. Originally developed by psychologist Carol S. Dweck through extensive research on self-theories and motivation, it stands as a specific instance of the broader mindset mental model, which serves as a universal conceptual tool for navigating complexity through pattern recognition. It has implications across education, organizations, and personal relationships. Covered in Part VII of *Super Thinking*.

Growth mindset can be cultivated through interventions including process-focused praise, reframing of effort, and explicit teaching about the brain's capacity for development.

This belief fosters resilience in the face of obstacles, an eagerness to learn from failures, and a proactive approach to challenges. Dweck's research shows that individuals with a growth mindset approach challenges with eagerness, invest effort in the face of difficulty, and treat failure as informative rather than threatening. Individuals with a growth mindset tend to:

- Embrace challenges as opportunities for growth
- Persist through obstacles and view failure as informative rather than definitive
- Invest effort strategically, seeing it as the path to mastery
- Learn from criticism and seek out feedback

## Origin

The concept was developed by psychologist Carol S. Dweck through extensive research on self-theories and motivation. Her work demonstrates that individuals who adopt a growth mindset tend to embrace challenges, learn from criticism, and persist in the face of setbacks.

## Core Principles

- **Belief in developability** — Abilities are not fixed; they can grow through sustained effort and effective strategies.
- **Effort as path to mastery** — Intelligence is not fixed; it can be cultivated through sustained effort. Focus on process and learning over fixed outcomes.
- **Embracing challenges** — Challenges are opportunities for growth rather than threats to self-worth.
- **Failure as learning** — Setbacks are natural parts of the growth process, reframed as information rather than verdicts on ability, building resilience and creativity.
- **Constructive criticism is valuable feedback** — Openness to feedback and new strategies.
- **Inspiration from others' success** — Finding inspiration in the success of others and their effort.
- **Persistence** — Sustained effort over time leads to improvement and achievement. Embracing failure for personal growth and reframing effort as the path to mastery.

People with a growth mindset view challenges and failures as opportunities to learn and improve, rather than as evidence of inherent limitation. This orientation supports sustained effort and resilience, making it a foundational component of [[grit-concept]].

## Key Characteristics

- Belief that abilities are developable through effort and strategy
- Belief that effort and strategy drive improvement
- Resilience in the face of obstacles
- Eagerness to learn from failures and setbacks
- Persisting through setbacks and failures
- Openness to feedback and new strategies
- Finding inspiration in the success of others
- Focus on process and learning over fixed outcomes
- Embracing challenges as opportunities to grow
- Learning from criticism and feedback

## Process vs. Person Praise

A key actionable distinction: praising process (effort, strategy, approach) promotes growth mindset, while praising person (intelligence, talent) reinforces fixed mindset. This connects to [[process-praise-vs-person-praise]].

## Research and Applications Across Domains

Carol S. Dweck's research demonstrates that growth mindset influences outcomes across multiple domains:

- **Education** — Students with a growth mindset show improved learning experiences and attitudes toward lifelong learning. Teachers foster growth mindsets through collaborative, self-improvement-focused practices that promote learning over performance metrics. Educators foster learning, collaboration, and self-improvement.
- **Workplace and Organizations** — Mindsets affect employee performance, job satisfaction, and workplace relationships. Organizations cultivate cultures of continuous improvement through change, innovation, and employee development.
- **Personal development** — Mindsets can evolve across life stages, influencing self-esteem and well-being. Continual skill development and adaptation to change characterize adult learning.
- **Home and Parenting** — Parents instill growth mindset through effort-focused praise and encouragement, creating environments that value effort over talent.
- **Relationships** — Understanding, empathy, and forgiveness strengthen connections. Growth mindset enhances understanding, empathy, and forgiveness by prioritizing growth over judgment.

## Connection to Neuroplasticity

Dweck ties growth mindset to neuroplasticity — the brain's ability to restructure itself through learning. Embracing a growth mindset can lead to changes in brain structure that support lifelong learning and adaptability. This provides biological support for the concept, and explicit teaching about the brain's capacity for development is one of the interventions that can cultivate a growth mindset.

## Relationship to Grit

Angela Duckworth identifies growth mindset as essential to developing grit. Without believing that effort leads to improvement, perseverance becomes fragile—individuals may quit when faced with difficulty because they interpret struggle as a sign of inadequacy rather than a normal part of growth.

## Fixed vs. Growth Mindset

The tension between fixed and growth orientations affects how people approach goals, setbacks, and the effort required for long-term achievement. A fixed mindset can lead to avoidance of challenges and helplessness in the face of difficulty, while a growth mindset encourages continued engagement and adaptive response.
