# Alignment

Alignment is a conceptual tool and principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world. In the context of artificial intelligence, alignment refers specifically to the challenge of ensuring that AI systems behave in ways that are consistent with human values and intentions—without it, AI systems may optimize for objectives that diverge from what humans actually want or need.

## Definition

Alignment functions as a universal mental model applicable across domains. It is a framework for recognizing patterns and relationships in complex situations, enabling better navigation of uncertainty and interconnection. The core idea is that aligning one's understanding, goals, or systems with underlying structures produces clearer outcomes. This technical usage—ensuring AI objectives match human objectives—connects to the broader mental model by emphasizing that alignment is fundamentally about coherence between what we intend and what actually happens.

## Key Dimensions of Alignment

As discussed in [[the-alignment-problem---brian-christian-tom-griffiths]], alignment encompasses several key dimensions:

- **Value alignment**: Ensuring AI systems encode and respect human values, including nuanced concepts like wellbeing and social good
- **Behavioral alignment**: Matching learned behaviors from reinforcement learning to human expectations
- **Consistency alignment**: Maintaining reliable decision-making across diverse and novel situations without becoming brittle or overgeneralized
- **Social alignment**: Building systems that contribute to human wellbeing and social good at scale

Alignment is distinct from mere capability—a system can be extremely powerful yet misaligned if it pursues objectives that conflict with human welfare. This makes alignment both a technical and philosophical challenge, touching on questions about what humans value and how those values can be encoded in computational systems.

## Connection to AI Alignment

The concept is most prominently developed in Brian Christian and Tom Griffiths' *The Alignment Problem*, which examines how to ensure AI systems behave in accordance with human values and intentions. This technical usage connects to the broader mental model by emphasizing that alignment is fundamentally about coherence between what we intend and what actually happens.

## Cross-Domain Applications

- **Decision-making**: Aligning decisions with values and long-term goals rather than short-term impulses
- **Systems thinking**: Ensuring components of a system work toward consistent objectives
- **Communication**: Aligning messages with audience understanding and context
- **Personal strategy**: Aligning daily actions with overarching purpose
