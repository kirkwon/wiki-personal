# Self-Control and Addiction

Thaler's chapter on self-control examines how hyperbolic discounting—valuing immediate gratification more than future benefits—leads to procrastination, under-saving, and addictive behaviors. The chapter argues these failures are predictable and can be mitigated through better choice architecture and commitment devices.

## Key Mechanisms

- Present bias causes undervaluation of future outcomes
- Temptation is stronger when options are salient and immediate
- Self-control is a limited resource that can be depleted

## Solutions

- Commitment devices (Ulysses contracts) that pre-commit to future actions
- Choice architecture that makes future benefits more salient
- Removing tempting options from the immediate environment

This connects to [[hyperbolic-discounting]] and the broader theme of designing institutions that work with human psychology rather than against it.
