# Processes

Processes is a universal mental model that provides a shortcut for navigating complex situations by recognizing patterns and relationships. Rather than relying on pure intuition or chasing outcomes, systematic processes offer a framework for making sense of uncertainty.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

Five independent sources converge on the value of processes:

- **Daniel Kahneman** (*Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment*) highlights how human judgment is inconsistent and subject to noise, making systematic processes essential for reliable decisions.
- **Nassim Nicholas Taleb** (*Fooled by Randomness*) emphasizes the role of randomness in outcomes, arguing that focusing on process rather than outcome is the only defensible approach.
- **Dan Ariely** (*The Upside of Irrationality*) demonstrates human irrationality through behavioral research, supporting the case for structured processes over ad-hoc reasoning.
- **Shane Parrish** (*Clear Thinking*) advocates turning ordinary moments into results through disciplined, process-oriented thinking.
- **Annie Duke** (*Quit: The Power of Knowing When to Walk Away*) addresses the decision to stop a process versus persist, showing that process awareness includes knowing when to walk away.
