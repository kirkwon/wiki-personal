# Master vs. Slave Morality

Nietzsche's framework distinguishing two origins of moral value systems. Master morality originates from nobility and is characterized by values of strength, creativity, pride, and self-affirmation. Slave morality originates from resentment and is characterized by values of meekness, equality, pity, and humility. This distinction is central to Nietzsche's critique of Christianity and conventional ethics. He argues that slave morality gained dominance through historical circumstances and psychological mechanisms, not because it is more true or rational.

## Master Morality

Master morality originates among the strong and noble. Its values include:
- Strength, creativity, and self-affirmation
- Nobility, pride, and magnanimity
- A life-affirming orientation that says "yes" to existence
- Values derived from what the noble type experiences as good
- Self-overcoming

Master morality does not need an opposing "evil" — it simply affirms what it values without requiring the weak as a contrast.

## Slave Morality

Slave morality originates from ressentiment — the resentment of the powerless toward the powerful. Its characteristic moves include:
- Inverting the values of the master: weakness becomes virtue, humility becomes spiritual superiority, meekness and submission are elevated
- Introducing the concept of "evil" as a reaction against the "good" of the masters
- Equality, pity, patience, and humility elevated as moral ideals
- Morality as a strategy of the weak to gain power through moral condemnation
- Psychology: Reactive, life-denying, based on inversion of master values

This framework helps analyze how moral systems can serve power dynamics rather than universal truth.
