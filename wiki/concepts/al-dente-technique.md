# Al Dente Technique

Al dente — "to the tooth" in Italian — describes the ideal pasta doneness: firm to bite with slight resistance in the center, never mushy.

## Key Principles

- Cook 1-2 minutes less than the package time
- Pasta continues cooking after draining due to residual heat
- Reserve 1/2 cup of starchy pasta water before draining — this starchy water is crucial for emulsifying sauces
- Never rinse pasta after draining (unless making cold pasta salad), as rinsing removes the surface starch needed for sauce adhesion

## Process

1. Use a large pot with 4-6 quarts of water per pound of pasta
2. Salt the water generously — 1-2 tablespoons — the pasta absorbs salt from within
3. Bring to a rapid boil before adding pasta
4. Add pasta all at once and stir immediately to prevent sticking
5. Begin tasting 1-2 minutes before the package-recommended time
6. Drain and reserve pasta water
7. Finish cooking the pasta in the sauce, adding reserved water as needed

## Fresh vs. Dried

Fresh pasta cooks dramatically faster: thin fresh pasta in 1-2 minutes, thick in 2-3 minutes, stuffed pasta in 3-5 minutes. Dried pasta requires longer cooking times but should still be removed at the al dente stage.

## Common Mistakes

- Overcooking — the most common error, resulting in mushy pasta
- Under-salting — water should taste like the sea
- Rinsing — removes essential starch
