# Gambler's Fallacy

The gambler's fallacy is the mistaken belief that past random events affect the probability of future independent events.

## Definition

**Gambler's Fallacy**: Believing past random events affect future independent events. For example, "Red has come up 5 times, so black is due" in roulette.

## Example

After flipping a coin 10 times and getting heads each time, believing the next flip is more likely to be tails. Each coin flip is independent — the coin has no memory.

## Impact

Poor gambling and investment decisions. This fallacy leads people to misallocate risk and make irrational bets based on patterns that don't exist.

## Mitigation

Understand that independent events have no memory. The probability of each event remains constant regardless of past outcomes.
