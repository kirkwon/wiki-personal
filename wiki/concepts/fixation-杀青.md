# Fixation (杀青)

Fixation is the heat treatment step in tea processing that halts enzymatic oxidation, preserving the natural compounds and delicate flavors of green tea. The method of fixation fundamentally shapes the flavor profile of the final tea.

## Chinese Method: Stir-frying (炒青)
- **Temperature**: 212-302°F (100-150°C)
- **Time**: 5-10 minutes (short)
- **Effect**: More complex, slightly roasted flavors
- **Used for**: Dragon Well, Biluochun

## Japanese Method: Steaming (蒸青)
- **Temperature**: 176-212°F (80-100°C)
- **Time**: 30-90 seconds (brief)
- **Effect**: More vegetal, fresh, bright flavors
- **Used for**: Sencha, Gyokuro, Matcha

## Significance
The choice between stir-frying and steaming creates the fundamental divide in green tea flavor profiles between Chinese and Japanese traditions. Chinese stir-frying at higher temperatures develops roasted, complex notes, while Japanese steaming at lower temperatures preserves brighter, more vegetal character.
