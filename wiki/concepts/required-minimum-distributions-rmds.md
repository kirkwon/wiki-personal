# Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) are the minimum amounts you must withdraw from your retirement accounts annually once you reach a certain age. Failing to take these distributions can result in a 25% penalty (reduced to 10% if corrected promptly).

## RMD Rules and Age Thresholds

The age at which RMDs begin has changed over time:
- **Previous Rule**: 70½
- **Current Rule**: 73 (for those born 1950–1959)
- **Future Rule**: 75 (for those born 1960+)

## Account Types Subject to RMDs

RMDs apply to:
- Traditional IRAs
- 401(k), 403(b), 457(b) plans
- SEP IRAs, SIMPLE IRAs
- Inherited retirement accounts

RMDs do **not** apply to:
- Roth IRAs (during the original owner's lifetime)
- Roth 401(k) (if still with employer)
- Roth conversions in progress

## RMD Calculation Formula

The RMD is calculated as:

```
RMD = Account Balance ÷ Distribution Period
```

### Life Expectancy Tables

The distribution period is based on IRS life expectancy tables. For example, at age 73:
- Single Life Expectancy: 17.34 years
- Joint Life Expectancy: 21.8 years (with a younger spouse)

**Example**: A $500,000 account at age 73 with single life expectancy yields an RMD of $500,000 ÷ 17.34 = $28,840.

## Deadlines and Penalties

- **Annual Deadline**: December 31st of each year
- **Exception**: First RMD due April 1st of the year you turn 73
- **Penalty**: 25% of the shortfall (reduced to 10% if corrected promptly)
- Quarterly installments are permitted (25% per quarter), but the total must be taken by December 31st

## Strategies to Minimize RMD Tax Impact

### Roth Conversion Strategy
Convert traditional IRA assets to Roth before RMDs start to reduce future RMD amounts. Pay taxes during working years when income is lower. After RMDs begin, convert up to the top of your tax bracket while being mindful of provisional income.

### Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs)
Up to $105,000 per year (2024) can be directed directly to charity, counting toward the RMD requirement while reducing taxable income.

### Aggressive Withdrawal Strategy
Withdraw more than the RMD during strong market years and less during poor market years to maintain a consistent income stream and avoid selling assets at market bottoms.

### Asset Location Strategy
Hold growth assets in Roth accounts (tax-free growth) and income-generating assets in traditional accounts. Withdraw from traditional accounts first when tax rates are lower.

### Inherited IRA Strategy
Beneficiaries must take RMDs based on their life expectancy. Stretch IRA rules changed for non-spousal beneficiaries — most must deplete the account within 10 years under the SECURE Act.

## Common Mistakes

- Forgetting RMDs entirely (25% penalty)
- Using the wrong life expectancy table
- Missing the December 31st deadline
- Incorrectly calculating quarterly amounts
- Misunderstanding which accounts are subject to RMDs (e.g., Roth IRAs are exempt during the owner's lifetime)

## Planning by Age

- **Ages 50–60**: Max out Roth conversions, consider mega backdoor Roth, build taxable accounts
- **Ages 60–70**: Final Roth conversions, start planning account balances, consider QCDs
- **Ages 70–73**: Final year for Roth conversions, account balance optimization, estate planning
- **Age 73+**: Systematic withdrawal planning, tax optimization, legacy planning
