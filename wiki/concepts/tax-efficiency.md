# Tax Efficiency

Tax efficiency refers to how effectively an investment vehicle minimizes taxable events and capital gains distributions in taxable accounts. The goal is to defer taxes as long as possible and avoid unnecessary tax drag that erodes returns.

## Key Factors

- **Capital gains distributions**: Funds that distribute net capital gains force shareholders to pay taxes even if they didn't sell any shares
- **Turnover rate**: Higher portfolio turnover generates more taxable events through buying and selling
- **Structure**: In-kind creation/redemption mechanisms allow ETFs to adjust share count without realizing gains
- **Index rebalancing**: When indices change, funds must trade holdings, potentially triggering gains

## Tax Inefficiency Ratio

A metric estimating annual tax drag due to distributions relative to ideal tax-deferred growth:
- Index ETFs: Low (0.0-0.3x)
- Index Mutual Funds: Medium (0.3-0.8x)
- Actively Managed ETFs: Medium-High (0.5-1.0x)
- Actively Managed Mutual Funds: High (1.0-1.5x)

## Practical Implications

In taxable accounts, tax efficiency matters because gains are taxed annually. In tax-advantaged accounts (IRAs, 401(k)s), tax efficiency is less relevant since gains aren't taxed until withdrawal. This is why tax-inefficient funds like actively managed mutual funds are better suited for retirement accounts.
