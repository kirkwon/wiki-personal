# Irrational Decision-Making

Irrational decision-making refers to systematic deviations from the predictions of rational economic models. Rather than being mere errors, these deviations follow predictable patterns rooted in emotions, social context, cognitive biases, and situational cues.

## Core Idea

Dan Ariely argues that human irrationality is not just a flaw—it has functional value when its patterns are understood. Irrational decisions are driven by factors that purely rational models ignore: emotions, social pressure, defaults, fairness perceptions, overconfidence, contextual cues, and first impressions.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]

## Key Drivers of Irrationality

- **Emotions**: Override logic in decision-making, leading to choices that contradict economic predictions based solely on logic.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Social Pressure**: Conformity, groupthink, and peer influence drive irrational behavior.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Default Options and Inertia**: Defaults and established patterns shape decisions regardless of optimality.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Illusion of Choice**: Abundance of options paradoxically decreases satisfaction.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Fairness Perception**: Perceived injustice leads to irrational retaliation or behavior.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Overconfidence**: Overconfidence in abilities causes poor decisions across industries.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Contextual Cues**: Context dramatically reshapes decisions, contradicting models based on isolated variables.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **First Impressions**: Initial judgments overshadow objective evaluation.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Blind Trust in Authority**: Authority manipulation leads to irrational compliance.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]
- **Money and Social Status**: Societal expectations drive decisions away from rational self-interest.^[raw/articles/the-upside-of-irrationality---dan-ariely.md]

## Relationship to Prospect Theory

Like [[prospect-theory]], irrational decision-making documents systematic deviations from rational choice. Ariely's framework is broader, encompassing social and emotional dimensions beyond Kahneman and Tversky's focus on loss aversion and framing effects.
