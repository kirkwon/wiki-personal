# Capital Gains vs. Property Tax Tradeoff

The tension between lower property taxes and potential higher capital gains taxes when transferring or inheriting real estate, particularly relevant under California's Proposition 19.

## The Tradeoff
When property is reassessed at market value upon transfer (as under Prop 19), the annual property tax is higher but the cost basis for capital gains purposes is also higher. Conversely, inheriting at the original lower basis keeps property taxes low but may result in a larger capital gains tax liability when the property is eventually sold.

## Implications
- **Proposition 19 Benefit**: Lower ongoing property taxes on inherited property
- **Proposition 19 Cost**: Potentially higher capital gains tax at sale
- **1031 Integration**: A [[1031-exchange-strategy]] may be preferable to defer both taxes through exchange
- **Hold Period**: Longer holding periods can mitigate the immediate tax impact

## Strategic Planning
Real estate investors must weigh the cash flow impact of higher property taxes against the deferred capital gains liability. The optimal strategy depends on the investor's time horizon, portfolio composition, and tax situation.
