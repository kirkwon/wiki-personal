# Italian Pasta

Pasta is the foundation of Italian cuisine — versatile, affordable, and beloved worldwide. From long strands to short shapes, fresh to dried, each pasta shape has its ideal sauce and regional tradition.

## Types of Pasta

### Fresh vs. Dried

- **Fresh (pasta fresca)** — Soft, tender, cooks quickly (1-5 minutes). Used for special occasions and stuffed pasta.
- **Dried (pasta secca)** — Firm, shelf-stable, cooked al dente. Used for everyday cooking.

### Long Pasta

- **Spaghetti** — Round, thin; best with tomato, oil-based sauces
- **Linguine** — Flat, thin; best with seafood, clam sauces
- **Fettuccine** — Flat, wide; best with creamy sauces
- **Tagliatelle** — Flat, wider; best with meat ragù
- **Pappardelle** — Very wide; best with game ragù
- **Bucatini** — Thick, hollow; best with amatriciana, carbonara

### Short Pasta

- **Penne** — Angled cut, hollow; best with chunky tomato sauces
- **Rigatoni** — Wide, ridged; best with meat ragù, baked dishes
- **Ziti** — Smooth, straight; best for baked ziti
- **Fusilli** — Corkscrew shape; best with pesto, cream sauces
- **Orecchiette** — "Little ears"; best with broccoli rabe, sausage
- **Conchiglie** — Shells; best with chunky sauces, baked

### Stuffed Pasta

- **Ravioli** — Square, various fillings (cheese, meat, spinach); nationwide
- **Tortellini** — Ring-shaped; meat, cheese; Emilia-Romagna
- **Agnolotti** — Half-moon; meat, herbs; Piedmont
- **Cappelletti** — Hat-shaped; meat, cheese; Emilia-Romagna

## Cooking Pasta

### Al Dente

The perfect doneness: firm to bite, not mushy, with slight resistance in the center. Cook 1-2 minutes less than package time — pasta continues cooking off heat.

### The Process

1. Use a large pot — 4-6 quarts water per pound
2. Salt liberally — 1-2 tbsp (should taste like sea)
3. Bring to rapid boil
4. Add pasta all at once
5. Stir to prevent sticking
6. Taste test 1-2 minutes before package time
7. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water for sauce
8. Drain — never rinse (removes starch)

### Fresh Pasta Cooking Time

Much faster than dried: thin pasta 1-2 minutes, thick pasta 2-3 minutes, stuffed pasta 3-5 minutes.

## Classic Pasta Dishes

### Roman Classics

- **Carbonara** — Guanciale, egg yolks, Pecorino Romano, black pepper. No cream.
- **Cacio e Pepe** — Pecorino Romano, black pepper, pasta water for emulsion.
- **Amatriciana** — Guanciale, tomato, onion, Pecorino Romano.

### Genovese

- **Pesto alla Genovese** — Basil, pine nuts, Pecorino, Parmesan, garlic, olive oil.

### Emilia-Romagna

- **Ragù alla Bolognese** — Ground beef and pork, tomato, onion, celery, carrot, wine, milk. Slow-cooked 3-4 hours.

### Southern

- **Pasta alla Norma** (Sicily) — Eggplant, tomato, basil, ricotta salata.
- **Spaghetti alle Vongole** — Clams, garlic, olive oil, parsley, white wine.

## Sauces by Pasta Shape

Long shapes suit oil, tomato, and seafood sauces. Short shapes suit chunky and baked preparations. Stuffed pasta pairs best with light sauces like brown butter sage or broth.

## Common Mistakes

- Overcooking pasta — cook al dente, reserve cooking water
- Not salting water — salt liberally
- Rinsing pasta — never rinse (unless for cold pasta salad)
- Wrong sauce for shape — match shape to sauce type
- Not tossing in sauce — finish cooking pasta in the sauce

## Making Fresh Pasta

Ingredients: flour (all-purpose or semolina), eggs (2 per cup flour), pinch of salt. Mound flour, make a well, crack eggs in, mix gradually, knead 10 minutes until smooth, rest 30 minutes, roll thin, cut into desired shape.

## Key Principle

Quality ingredients (guanciale, Pecorino Romano, good eggs) and proper technique matter more than elaborate recipes. Al dente is non-negotiable.
