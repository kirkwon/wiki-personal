# Meaning Finding Framework

Viktor Frankl's core thesis that the primary human motivation is the search for meaning, rather than the pursuit of pleasure (Freud) or power (Adler). This framework holds that meaning can be discovered through creative work, loving relationships, or the attitude one takes toward unavoidable suffering. Frankl developed this insight from his experiences in concentration camps, where he observed that prisoners who maintained a sense of purpose were more likely to survive.

## Key Principles
- Meaning is the central human drive, not pleasure or power
- Meaning can be found through three avenues: creative expression, experiencing something (love, beauty, truth), and the attitude taken toward unavoidable suffering
- The framework operates independently of external circumstances — meaning can be cultivated even under extreme conditions
- Consistent application of these principles leads to personal and professional growth

## Connection to Other Concepts
Relates to [[growth-mindset]] through the idea that attitude and orientation can be chosen regardless of conditions.
