# Chess

Chess is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

## Core Principles

Chess thinking translates to several key mental model principles:

- **Pattern Recognition**: Identifying recurring configurations and positions that signal strategic opportunities or threats. This connects to [[recognition-primed-decision-model]] and [[problem-solving]].
- **Multi-Move Lookahead**: Thinking several steps ahead, anticipating consequences of actions before committing. Relates to [[scenario-planning]] and [[working-backwards-in-problem-solving]].
- **Sacrifice for Positional Advantage**: Willingness to give up short-term resources for long-term strategic positioning. Connects to [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] and [[expected-value-ev]].
- **Tempo and Initiative**: Controlling the pace and sequence of actions to maintain advantage. Relates to [[strategic-vs-reactive-thinking]].
- **Endgame Thinking**: Focusing on how the situation resolves rather than intermediate states. Connects to [[working-backwards]] and [[planning]].
- **Resource Management**: Allocating limited resources (pieces, time, energy) efficiently across the unfolding situation.
