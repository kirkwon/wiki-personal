# Debt Avalanche vs. Debt Snowball

Two strategies for prioritizing debt payoff, each with different psychological and financial trade-offs.

## Debt Avalanche (Recommended by Sethi)
- **Method**: Pay minimums on all debts; throw all extra money at the debt with the highest interest rate.
- **Result**: Saves the most money in interest over time.
- **Requires**: Discipline to continue targeting high-rate debt even when the balance may not shrink as quickly.
- **Best for**: Mathematically optimal payoff; people who can maintain motivation without quick wins.

## Debt Snowball
- **Method**: Pay minimums on all debts; throw all extra money at the debt with the smallest balance.
- **Result**: Quick wins build momentum and psychological confidence.
- **Cost**: Pays more in total interest over time because the highest-rate debt may not be addressed first.
- **Best for**: People who need psychological wins to stay motivated; those for whom adherence matters more than optimal math.

## Debt Hierarchy (Sethi's Priority Order)
1. **Credit card debt** (18-25%+ APR) — highest priority
2. **Personal loans** (8-15%)
3. **Car loans** (4-8%) — can invest instead if rate is below 6%
4. **Student loans** (3-7%) — invest instead unless peace of mind is the goal
5. **Mortgage** (3-7%) — generally don't pay early; invest instead

## Balance Transfer Strategy
For credit card debt over $5,000 at rates above 18%, transferring to a 0% APR card (12-21 months) with a 3-5% fee can save $500-2,000 in interest. Requires discipline not to use the card for new purchases.

## Connection to Automation
Both strategies should be automated: minimum payments are automatic, and extra payments to the target debt are set up as recurring transfers.
