# Dichotomy of control

The dichotomy of control is a foundational Stoic practice originating from Marcus Aurelius' *Meditations*. At its core, it is the discipline of distinguishing between what is within one's control and what is not, and directing energy exclusively toward the former.^[raw/articles/Dichotomy of control.md]

## Stoic Foundation

Marcus Aurelius articulated this principle as the basis for maintaining inner tranquility in the face of external events. The Stoic version emphasizes *acceptance* of uncontrollable factors — other people's actions, outcomes, external circumstances — while committing full effort to one's own thoughts, choices, and responses.^[raw/articles/Dichotomy of control.md]

## Productivity Recontextualization

This source reinterprets the dichotomy as a productivity skill: auditing workflow for efficiency losses, eliminating low-value tasks, and iterating on results. While the philosophical emphasis is on acceptance, the productivity framing emphasizes *control* over processes and outputs. This tension between acceptance and optimization is a notable distinction not fully acknowledged in the source.^[raw/articles/Dichotomy of control.md]

## Application Framework

1. **Audit your current workflow** — identify where efficiency losses occur and track how time and attention are spent.
2. **Restructure your approach** — focus on high-impact activities and eliminate or delegate low-value tasks.
3. **Iterate and refine** — measure impact of changes, adjust based on results, build consistency through habit formation.
4. **Scale what works** — standardize effective approaches, document processes, teach others to multiply impact.^[raw/articles/Dichotomy of control.md]
