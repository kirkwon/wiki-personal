# Human High-Frequency Trader

The Human High-Frequency Trader is a metaphor Timothy Ferriss uses to describe someone who constantly adjusts life decisions for optimal outcomes—treating life like a series of small, rapid trades rather than long-term bets. This concept appears in the context of the [[lifestyle-design-formula]] and reflects a philosophy of continuous adaptation, embracing uncertainty, and making frequent small adjustments rather than rigid long-term plans.

The metaphor connects to concepts in decision science: Annie Duke's [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] emphasizes probabilistic thinking and bet sizing, while [[real-options-analysis]] treats decisions as flexible options in dynamic environments. Ferriss's trader metaphor extends this by suggesting that life optimization is an ongoing process of small adjustments rather than a single strategic plan.
