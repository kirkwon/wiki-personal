# Culture Change in Healthcare

Culture Change in Healthcare is Gawande's argument that the problems of end-of-life care cannot be solved by better protocols alone — they require a fundamental shift in the culture and identity of medicine. The profession was built around curing disease, and palliative approaches were historically seen as failure. Changing this requires changing what doctors see as success, how they spend their time, and how they relate to patients and families.

This connects to broader organizational dynamics. The resistance to change mirrors patterns seen in [[building-a-cohesive-team]] and systemic dysfunction: professionals default to established routines, avoid difficult conversations, and measure success by metrics (survival rates, procedure volume) that may not reflect what patients value. Changing culture means confronting these defaults and building new norms around honest communication, patient-centered values, and comfort as a legitimate medical goal.

Gawande points to examples where culture change has occurred — palliative care programs that reduced suffering and costs simultaneously — as evidence that the shift is possible. But it requires leadership, training, and systemic incentives aligned with the new values.
