# Death of God (Nietzsche)

The Death of God refers to the collapse of traditional metaphysical foundations — particularly the Christian God — as a source of moral authority. Nietzsche announced this "death" not as celebration but as a crisis that demands new values.

## Consequences

- Without divine foundations, moral claims lose their ultimate justification
- This creates both freedom (from inherited moral constraints) and danger (potential nihilism)
- The need for new values becomes urgent — hence the project of revaluation and the possibility of the Übermensch
- The collapse of metaphysical certainty connects to questions about the [[will-to-truth]] and whether truth-seeking itself requires foundations

## Relevance

The Death of God sets the stage for why Nietzsche's analysis matters: if traditional moral systems are not grounded in universal truth, then understanding their actual origins (through genealogical analysis) becomes essential rather than merely interesting.
