# Confidence Score Transparency

Confidence score transparency refers to the practice of explicitly communicating the level of certainty or confidence behind a claim, prediction, or decision. Rather than presenting conclusions as absolute truths, transparency about confidence levels helps calibrate expectations and enables better downstream decision-making.

This concept connects to [[superforecasting]], where practitioners assign probabilistic estimates to uncertain outcomes and track calibration accuracy over time. It also relates to [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]], which examines how humans assess and communicate uncertainty. In the context of [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]], transparent confidence scores help distinguish between a well-reasoned decision and a lucky outcome, supporting the evaluation of decision processes rather than just results.

Key considerations include:
- **Calibration**: Ensuring that stated confidence levels match actual accuracy over time.
- **Communication**: Expressing uncertainty in ways that recipients can interpret and act upon.
- **Feedback loops**: Using confidence scores to identify systematic overconfidence or underconfidence patterns.

The concept is relevant to frameworks like [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] and [[systematic-decision-making]], where making the reasoning process explicit is central to improving judgment.
