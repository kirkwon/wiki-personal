# Six Thinking Hats

The Six Thinking Hats is Edward de Bono's parallel thinking method for structured group deliberation, developed by [[edward-de-bono]]. It uses six colored hats to represent distinct modes of thought, enabling groups and individuals to consider all angles systematically rather than engaging in adversarial debate. Each hat encourages a specific perspective during decision-making and problem-solving.

## The Six Hats

- **White Hat** – Focuses on objective facts and data gathering. [[white-hat-facts-and-data]]
- **Red Hat** – Expresses emotions, intuitions, and gut feelings. [[red-hat-emotions-and-intuition]]
- **Black Hat** – Identifies risks, problems, and cautionary concerns. [[black-hat-cautionary-perspective]]
- **Yellow Hat** – Promotes positive, optimistic viewpoints and constructive brainstorming. [[yellow-hat-positive-viewpoints]]
- **Green Hat** – Encourages creativity, divergent thinking, and new ideas. [[green-hat-new-ideas]]
- **Blue Hat** – Manages the process, coordinating all perspectives during discussions. [[blue-hat-process-control]]

## Core Principles

The framework promotes **parallel thinking**—where all participants explore the same topic from the same perspective simultaneously—rather than traditional adversarial argument. This ensures balanced consideration of facts, emotions, risks, optimism, creativity, and process control. The method prevents the confusion that arises when a group tries to be emotional, analytical, and creative simultaneously. By directing attention to one mode at a time, it improves clarity and ensures all perspectives are heard.

The core innovation is parallel thinking: rather than debating opposing viewpoints, the group systematically cycles through each perspective. This reduces polarization, ensures all dimensions of a decision are explored, and creates a structured process that can be managed by a facilitator.

## Application

The hats can be used in sequence or selectively, depending on the needs of the discussion. The Blue Hat serves as metacognition over the process, encapsulating and coordinating the other perspectives to achieve comprehensive problem analysis and resolution.

## Connection to Lateral Thinking

The Six Thinking Hats is one practical application of de Bono's broader lateral thinking methodology. The [[green-hat-new-ideas]] and [[red-hat-emotions-and-intuition]] perspectives are particularly aligned with lateral thinking's emphasis on generative exploration.

## Connections to Decision-Making Theory

This framework connects to [[emotion-and-intuition-in-decisions]] by legitimizing intuitive input through the Red Hat, and relates to [[dual-process-theory]] in its acknowledgment that both rational analysis and gut feeling play roles in decision quality. It also serves as a practical tool for addressing team dysfunction described in [[the-five-dysfunctions-of-a-team]], since structured perspective-taking can counteract absence of trust and fear of conflict.
