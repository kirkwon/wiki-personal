# Meals

Meals is a universal mental model for navigating complex situations through pattern and relationship recognition. It functions as a conceptual tool that provides mental shortcuts for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.

Drawing from Timothy Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Chef*, the Meals framework treats cooking and meal preparation as a meta-learning vehicle. The core idea is that breaking complex skills into digestible portions—much like preparing a meal—enables accelerated learning across domains. Ferriss's DSSS method (Deconstruct, Select, Sequence, Stakes) operationalizes this by breaking any skill into its component parts, selecting the most impactful elements, sequencing them for optimal learning, and adding stakes to maintain commitment.

The "tiger cooking method" referenced in Ferriss's work emphasizes immersive, high-stakes practice environments where learners engage with the full complexity of a skill rather than isolated drills. This connects to broader principles of [[experiment-and-exploration-forms-of-world-disclosure---snke-ahrens|world disclosure]] and [[how-to-take-smart-notes---snke-ahrens|smart note-taking]], where structured frameworks help manage complexity.

Meals bridges the cooking domain with meta-learning and decision-making, connecting to [[how-to-solve-it---george-polya|problem-solving]] frameworks and [[hyperfocus---chris-bailey|deep focus]] techniques for mastering new skills efficiently.
