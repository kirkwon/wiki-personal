# Implementation Intentions

Implementation intentions are pre-commitment strategies for behavior change. They involve specifying when, where, and how you will perform a desired behavior in advance, which increases the likelihood of following through.

## How They Work

Rather than relying on willpower at the moment of decision, you plan the behavior in advance: "When [cue happens], I will [perform routine]." This bridges the gap between intention and action by embedding the decision into the habit loop.

## Relationship to Habit Science

- Implementation intentions formalize the cue identification step in the [[habit-loop]]
- They work alongside [[habit-stacking]] by anchoring new behaviors to specific cues
- Complement [[habit-testing]] by providing a structured way to experiment with routine substitutions

## When to Use

When you need to change a specific behavior, when willpower is depleted, or when designing organizational processes for consistent execution.
