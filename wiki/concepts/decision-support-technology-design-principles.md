# Decision Support Technology Design Principles

Based on his research into naturalistic decision-making, Gary Klein offers principles for designing decision support systems that work with how people actually think rather than against it.

Core design principles:
- **Augment, don't replace**: Systems should support natural patterns of recognition and simulation rather than forcing users through rationalistic procedures they don't follow naturally.
- **Provide rich data**: Experts need contextual information they can scan for cues, not just summarized metrics.
- **Support mental simulation**: Interfaces should allow users to mentally run through courses of action—showing consequences, timelines, and dependencies.
- **Adapt to expertise level**: Novices need more structure; experts need flexibility. One-size-fits-all interfaces fail both groups.
- **Avoid unnecessary procedural constraints**: Over-automation and rigid workflows can degrade decision quality by interfering with intuitive pattern matching.

This framework connects to [[mental-simulation-in-decision-making]] and [[recognition-primed-decision-model]], emphasizing that technology should be designed around the cognitive realities of expert decision-making rather than idealized rational models.
