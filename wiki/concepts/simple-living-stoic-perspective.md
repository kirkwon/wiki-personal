# Simple Living (Stoic Perspective)

Seneca's advocacy for simple living, articulated in *Letters from a Stoic* Chapter 10, distinguishes Stoic simplicity from general minimalism. The focus is not on owning fewer things for its own sake, but on avoiding luxury and extravagance that distract from inner development and virtue.

## Core Arguments

- Luxury creates dependency and anxiety rather than satisfaction.
- Simple living frees mental energy for philosophical reflection and meaningful engagement.
- The goal is moderation — not asceticism that harms health or relationships.
- Connects to [[balancing-ambition-with-well-being]] by prioritizing inner peace over external accumulation.

## Guardrails

Seneca warns against simple living becoming asceticism that harms health or relationships, or philosophy becoming mere intellectual exercise without application. This tension is also noted in the [[when-to-stop]] conditions of the source analysis.
