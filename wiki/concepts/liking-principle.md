# Liking Principle

One of Cialdini's six principles of influence. The liking principle states that people are more easily persuaded by individuals they like. When we perceive someone as likable, we lower our critical defenses and become more receptive to their requests and messages.

## How It Works

Liking operates through several sub-mechanisms: physical attractiveness, similarity, compliments, cooperation, familiarity, and conditioning through repeated positive association. These factors create rapport that lowers resistance to persuasion. We tend to like people who are similar to us, who have complimented us, or who we have cooperated with toward shared goals.

## Applications

- Building rapport before making requests
- Leveraging genuine similarities to increase persuasive effectiveness
- Using attractive spokespersons or emphasizing shared values and experiences
- Recognizing when likability is being manufactured to manipulate

## Ethical Considerations

The liking principle connects to [[ethical-design-manipulation-question]]—building genuine rapport differs from manufactured likability designed to exploit trust. Understanding this distinction is central to the ethical application of persuasion tactics.
