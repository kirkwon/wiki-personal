# Heuristics in Problem Solving

Heuristics are mental shortcuts that simplify judgment and decision-making. While they are often useful and efficient, they can lead to systematic errors known as cognitive biases.

Kahneman identifies several key heuristics:
- **Availability heuristic** — judging probability by how easily examples come to mind
- **Representativeness heuristic** — judging probability by how similar something is to a prototype
- **Anchoring** — relying too heavily on the first piece of information encountered
- **Affect heuristic** — letting emotions guide judgments about risk and benefit

These heuristics are products of [[system-1-vs-system-2-thinking]] — System 1 deploys them automatically, while System 2 can detect and correct their errors, though often fails to do so. The resulting biases form the core of the [[cognitive-biases-library]].

Heuristics connect to [[generalization-in-problem-solving]] and [[analogy-in-problem-solving]] as forms of intuitive reasoning that can be powerful but imprecise.
