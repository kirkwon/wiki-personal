# Recipes as Mental Model

Recipes is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world.^[raw/articles/Recipes.md]

The core idea draws from Timothy Ferriss's *The 4-Hour Chef*, which uses recipe-based thinking—decomposing cooking into learnable components—as a framework for learning any skill. Just as a culinary recipe combines specific ingredients and methods to produce a predictable outcome, the mental model of "recipes" suggests that complex domains can be broken into structured, repeatable procedures that reveal underlying patterns.^[raw/articles/Recipes.md]

This concept connects to [[decomposing-and-recombining]], which also emphasizes breaking complexity into manageable parts, and to [[polya-four-steps]], which provides a step-by-step approach to problem solving. It is distinct from [[management]] and [[planning]] in that it emphasizes the structured, deconstructable nature of procedures themselves rather than the higher-level navigation or organizational frameworks those models provide.

## Applicability
- **Universal**: Can be applied across domains—cooking, skill acquisition, decision-making, writing, and any area where structured procedures exist.
- **Confidence**: 0.7 — single-source basis; the concept is still being developed and needs further articulation to differentiate it from existing decomposition models.
