# Neglected Probability

The flaw of neglected probability describes how decision-makers routinely overlook or underestimate the likelihood of unlikely but consequential events. This bias is particularly dangerous when the stakes are high and the probabilities are small but non-trivial.

The tendency to overlook or underestimate probabilities when making decisions about uncertain events stems from cognitive biases or a lack of understanding. This can manifest as ignoring tail risks, underweighting rare events, or overestimating the probability of familiar outcomes.

Taleb connects this to the broader human difficulty in assessing [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]], where people systematically misjudge tail risks. Combined with [[overconfidence-and-certainty]], it creates a dangerous tendency to ignore low-probability, high-impact scenarios — the very events that matter most for risk management.

This concept relates to [[black-swan-events]] and reinforces why frameworks like [[real-options-analysis]] and [[scenario-planning]] must explicitly account for neglected tail probabilities rather than assuming normal distributions.

## Key Points
- Cognitive biases cause systematic probability misestimation
- Rare events are particularly prone to being neglected
- Base rates are frequently ignored
- This connects to [[black-swan-events]] — we underestimate the probability of extreme outcomes
- Tail risks are systematically misjudged, especially when combined with [[overconfidence-and-certainty]]
