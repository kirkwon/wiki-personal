# Resource Depletion

Resource depletion refers to the consumption of finite natural resources—such as fossil fuels, minerals, freshwater, and arable land—at rates that exceed regeneration or discovery. The 30-Year Update examines how resource scarcity interacts with technological innovation, finding that technological advances alone have not been sufficient to offset accelerating demand.

Key points from Meadows:
- Many critical resources show depletion trajectories consistent with the original Limits to Growth predictions
- Technological innovation can extend resource availability but cannot eliminate physical limits
- Sustainable futures require both technological solutions and fundamental changes in consumption patterns

This concept is central to the [[world3-model]] and connects to [[planetary-boundaries]] as a driver of environmental threshold breaches.
