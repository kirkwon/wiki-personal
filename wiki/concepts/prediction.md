# Prediction

Prediction is the process of making forecasts about future outcomes based on available information. In the context of calibration training and model selection and validation frameworks, prediction is both a skill to develop and a metric to evaluate — the ability to anticipate outcomes informs better framework selection and systematic improvement of efficiency and output. A core concern in this domain is aligning one's confidence levels with actual accuracy, which connects directly to superforecasting and probabilistic thinking.

## Key Ideas

- Effective prediction requires distinguishing signal from noise, a theme central to Nate Silver's work in [[the-signal-and-the-noise-nate-silver]].
- Prediction accuracy can be improved through systematic approaches: tracking assumptions, measuring outcomes, and iterating on models.
- The skill connects to [[superforecasting]], which emphasizes probabilistic thinking, intellectual humility, and diverse perspectives for making highly accurate predictions.

## Connection to calibration

Calibration training, as described in the source, extends into prediction territory by emphasizing systematic improvement of efficiency and output. The related mental model of [[noise]] and the source reference to [[the-signal-and-the-noise-nate-silver]] suggest that the underlying concept involves reducing random error in judgment.

## Connections

Prediction is related to [[uncertainty]] (the awareness that outcomes are not certain) and [[noise]] (the interference that degrades forecast quality). It also relates to [[decision-making-frameworks]] and [[systematic-decision-making]] as practical applications.
