# Super Thinking - Gabriel Weinberg

*Super Thinking* (2024) by Gabriel Weinberg is a beginner-level compendium of 35 mental models organized into 7 parts, designed to improve decision-making, problem-solving, and learning across domains. The book's core thesis is that combining multiple mental models yields "super thinking" — deeper insight and better outcomes than relying on any single framework.

## Core Thesis

The power of the book lies not in any single mental model but in combining multiple models for deeper insight. Weinberg organizes models into seven parts covering core concepts, decision making, strategic thinking, systems thinking, creativity, social intelligence, and learning.

## Structure

### Part I: Core Concepts
Chapters on the Map/Territory distinction, Occam's Razor, First-Order and Second-Order Thinking, and the Pareto Principle.

### Part II: Decision Making
Covers Bayes' Rule, Base Rate Fallacy, Sunk Cost Fallacy, Opportunity Cost, and Margin of Safety.

### Part III: Strategic Thinking
Game Theory, Prisoner's Dilemma, Tragedy of the Commons, Competitive Advantage, and Network Effects.

### Part IV: Systems Thinking
Feedback Loops, Law of Unintended Consequences, Systems Are Dynamic, Emergent Properties, and Equilibrium.

### Part V: Creativity and Innovation
Lateral Thinking, Mental Sandbox, Inversion, First Principles, and Thought Experiments.

### Part VI: Social and Emotional Intelligence
Reciprocity, Authority Bias, Social Proof, In-Group/Out-Group Bias, and Empathy.

### Part VII: Learning and Growth
Growth Mindset, The Feynman Technique, Spaced Repetition, Deliberate Practice, and the Dunning-Kruger Effect.

## Practical Application

The book serves as a curated mental model toolkit with practical triggers for when to apply models — important decisions under uncertainty, complex problems across domains, system analysis, strategic planning, and learning new subjects.

## Guardrails: When to Stop

The book importantly includes "when to stop" criteria to prevent model imperialism:
- When mental model application becomes analysis paralysis
- When forcing models where they don't fit
- When overthinking prevents necessary action
- When using complexity to sound smart rather than add value
- When models become a crutch rather than a tool
- When missing common sense in favor of sophisticated models

## Key Takeaways

- Apply relevant models to the situation rather than forcing all models everywhere
- Combine multiple models for deeper insight
- Build a repertoire over time
- Watch for analysis paralysis and model overuse
- The value is in synthesis and accessibility of established concepts
