# Explanatory power analysis

Systematic practice of improving efficiency and output by identifying methods that maximize productivity while maintaining sustainable work practices. Rooted in David Deutsch's epistemological concept that good explanations are those with high explanatory power — accounting for more phenomena with fewer assumptions.

## Core Practice

1. **Audit Your Current Workflow** — Identify where efficiency losses occur, track how time and attention are spent, note patterns leading to wasted effort
2. **Apply the Framework** — Restructure approach around high-impact activities, eliminate or delegate low-value tasks
3. **Iterate and Refine** — Measure impact of changes, adjust based on results, build consistency through habit formation
4. **Scale What Works** — Standardize effective approaches, document processes for reuse, teach others to multiply impact

## Application Domains

- **Personal Productivity**: Planning weeks more effectively, managing competing priorities, breaking complex projects into manageable tasks
- **Professional Context**: Team coordination, meeting efficiency, decision-making processes
- **Learning and Growth**: Accelerating skill acquisition, organizing and retaining information, identifying and filling knowledge gaps

## Related Concepts
Connects to [[scatterfocus]] and [[hyperfocus]] as attention modes, [[error-correction-mechanisms]] as a related skill, and [[peer-accountability-systems]] for maintaining standards. Ties to Deutsch's framework of conjecture and refutation through the emphasis on iterative refinement.

## Open Questions
The relationship between Deutsch's epistemological concept (explanatory power as a criterion for scientific quality) and this productivity application is not fully elaborated in the source material. The skill card may conflate the epistemological framework with general productivity optimization.
