# Basil Flavor Profile

Basil's flavor is characterized by a unique combination of sweet, peppery, and anise-like notes with grassy and citrus undertones depending on the variety.

## Primary Flavor Notes

- **Sweet**: Honey-like sweetness
- **Peppery**: Mild black pepper heat
- **Anise**: Subtle licorice undertones
- **Grassy**: Fresh, green notes
- **Citrus**: Hint of lemon in some varieties (especially Lemon Basil)

## Varietal Differences

Each basil variety has distinct flavor characteristics that suit different culinary traditions:

- **Sweet Basil**: Classic sweet-anise profile, the standard for Italian cuisine
- **Thai Basil**: Licorice and clove notes, essential for Thai curries and stir-fries
- **Holy Basil** (Tulsi): Clove and peppery character, used in Indian cooking and traditional medicine
- **Lemon Basil**: Citrus-forward with sweetness, pairs well with fish and poultry
- **Purple Basil**: Sweeter than green varieties, primarily used for garnish and infused vinegars

## Heat Sensitivity

Basil's flavor is heat-sensitive—prolonged cooking causes loss of color and development of bitterness. The herb should be added late in cooking (last 1-2 minutes) or used raw. Even in warm applications like pesto, basil should be combined with warm rather than hot ingredients.

## Flavor Preservation

Fresh basil frozen in oil retains flavor significantly better than dried basil, which loses much of its character after 1-2 weeks. Refrigeration in water extends shelf life to 3-5 days with daily water changes.
