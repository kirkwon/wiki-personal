# Model selection and validation frameworks

Model selection and validation frameworks is a productivity skill focused on systematically improving efficiency and output. It involves auditing workflows, applying structured methods and techniques, iterating on results, and scaling what works to maximize personal or organizational productivity while maintaining sustainable work practices.

## Core Practice

The skill breaks down into four phases:

1. **Audit** — Identify where efficiency losses occur, track how time and attention are spent, and note patterns that lead to wasted effort.
2. **Apply** — Restructure approach to focus on high-impact activities and eliminate or delegate low-value tasks.
3. **Iterate** — Measure the impact of changes, adjust based on results, and build consistency through habit formation.
4. **Scale** — Standardize effective approaches, document processes for reuse, and teach others to multiply impact.

## Applications

- **Personal productivity**: planning weeks more effectively, managing competing priorities, breaking down complex projects into manageable tasks.
- **Professional context**: team coordination, improving meeting efficiency, enhancing decision-making processes.
- **Learning and growth**: accelerating skill acquisition, organizing and retaining information, identifying and filling knowledge gaps.

The skill is primarily discussed in a single source, offering a focused but limited perspective. Confidence in the framework's robustness is moderate, and its relationship to statistical model selection (as discussed by Nate Silver) involves different contexts — here it refers to personal productivity frameworks rather than statistical modeling.
