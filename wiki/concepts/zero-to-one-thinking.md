# Zero-to-One Thinking

Zero-to-one thinking is Peter Thiel's core philosophical framework, articulated in his book *Zero to One* with Blake Masters. The central distinction is between "zero to one" — creating something entirely new — and "one to n" — copying or iterating on what already exists.

This concept is the core mechanism of the [[thiel-mental-model]], providing the framework for understanding how new value is created in complex situations. While "one to n" progress is horizontal (expanding across existing categories), "zero to one" progress is vertical (creating entirely new categories).

## Key Ideas

- Creating new things is fundamentally different from copying existing ones
- True innovation requires contrarian thinking and discovering secrets
- Monopolies emerge from creating entirely new categories rather than competing in existing ones
- Focus on long-term value creation through unique insights

## Implications

Thiel argues that the most valuable forms of progress come from building genuinely novel products, technologies, or markets rather than making incremental improvements to existing ones. Key implications include:

- True innovation requires thinking beyond existing markets and categories
- Building something new creates durable competitive advantages
- Imitation, even of successful models, stifles genuine creativity
- The most impactful companies are those that create entirely new value propositions
- The most valuable forms of progress come from building genuinely novel products, technologies, or markets

## Connections and Contrasts

This concept connects to [[infinite-game-mindset]] in that both emphasize long-term thinking over short-term competitive moves, though Thiel's framework specifically prioritizes creation and originality over adaptation. It also contrasts with [[incremental-change-as-a-trap]], which Thiel identifies as a dangerous strategy that allows competitors to catch up.

Zero-to-one thinking emphasizes the value of original creation over imitation, and its relationship to [[monopoly-vs-competition]] underscores that durable advantage comes from category creation rather than head-to-head competition.
