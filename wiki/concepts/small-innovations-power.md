# The Power of Small Innovations

Because complex systems are governed by positive feedback loops and nonlinear dynamics, small-scale innovations can trigger large-scale changes. Meadows describes how a minor intervention — if placed at the right leverage point — can cascade through a system, amplifying through reinforcing loops and fundamentally altering system behavior.

This principle reframes how change is approached: rather than relying on large-scale top-down mandates, effective systemic change often comes from identifying the right small intervention and letting the system's own dynamics carry it forward. This connects to [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]] by emphasizing that the impact of an action depends on its position within system structure, not just its magnitude.
