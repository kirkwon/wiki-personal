# Habit

Habit is a conceptual tool or principle that helps understand and navigate complex situations. It provides a mental shortcut or framework for making sense of patterns and relationships in the world. As a universal mental model, it applies across all domains—from personal productivity to team dynamics to communication effectiveness.

## Definition

Habit functions as a mental model for pattern recognition and sense-making. Rather than prescribing a single technique, it offers a lens for understanding how repeated behaviors, routines, and automatic responses shape outcomes across contexts. This framing connects to the [[hook-model]] of behavior design, [[external-triggers]] and [[internal-triggers]] that cue actions, and the [[user-investment-habit]] that deepens engagement over time.

## Cross-Source Perspectives

The habit model is referenced across multiple authoritative sources, suggesting broad consensus on its foundational importance:

- **James Clear** — In [[atomic-habits-james-clear]], habit formation is broken into a four-step loop (cue, craving, response, reward) and emphasized as the compound interest of self-improvement.
- **Charles Duhigg** — In [[smarter-faster-better---charles-duhigg]], habits are explored as drivers of team productivity and personal effectiveness, including the role of mental models in shaping routine behavior.
- **Charles Duhigg** — In [[supercommunicators-charles-duhigg]], habit patterns are examined in the context of communication and how habitual response modes affect listening and understanding.
- **Stephen R. Covey** — In [[the-8th-habit-from-effectiveness-to-greatness---stephen-r-covey]], habit is connected to moving beyond effectiveness toward greatness, framing purposeful habits as expressions of unique personal voice.

## Applicability

Habit is applicable universally. It connects to existing behavior models like the [[fizzgogg-behavior-model]] and [[habit-testing]], and to attention-related concepts such as [[attention-budget]] and [[attention-residue]], where managing habitual focus patterns determines cognitive performance. The model also relates to [[Hyperfocus - Chris Bailey]], where intense habitual concentration can be both a tool and a trap.
