# Digital Minimalism

Digital minimalism is a philosophy and practice of intentional, purpose-driven technology use, in contrast to default digital habits that are filled with distractions. It was popularized by Cal Newport in his 2024 book *Digital Minimalism*. The framework builds on the principles of [[deep-work---cal-newport]] by arguing that solitude, leisure, and focused attention are essential human needs that constant connectivity erodes.

## Core Idea
The central claim is that our default digital habits drain mental energy, reduce deep thinking, and ultimately lead us away from a fulfilling life. A minimalist approach restores cognitive capacity by eliminating tools and behaviors that don't serve meaningful goals.

## Key Principles
- **Intentional selection** — Use technology only when it clearly supports important goals; before adopting any tool, assess whether it serves significant purposes
- **Quality over quantity** — Focus on meaningful interactions and content rather than volume; prioritize depth over breadth in digital engagement
- **Boundaries** — Create technology-free zones, periods, and habits to protect personal time and wellbeing; protect unstructured time free from digital input
- **Batching communication** — Reduce constant context-switching from notifications and messages
- **Critical evaluation** — Before adopting any tool, assess whether it serves significant purposes

## Practical Applications
- Purposeful social media use — quality connections over quantity, curated content
- Purposeful email management — filters, labels, unsubscribing, inbox boundaries
- Purposeful content consumption — curated feeds, subscribing to quality sources, avoiding mindless scrolling
- Purposeful technology investment — critically evaluating new tech before adopting
- Focus tools — website blockers, technology-free zones
- Solitude and leisure — actively preserving time free from digital input

## Connection to Distractions Model
Digital Minimalism provides the practical application layer for the [[distractions]] mental model, offering concrete strategies for managing attention in complex digital environments.

## Tension with Habit-Forming Design
Digital minimalism directly opposes the design philosophy described in [[hooked---nir-eyal]], which focuses on building habit-forming products through triggers, variable rewards, and user investment. Newport's framework asks practitioners to step back from these very mechanisms and evaluate whether engagement-driven design serves their actual goals.

## Connection to Cognitive Science
This overlaps with [[thinking-fast-and-slow---daniel-kahneman]]'s distinction between System 1 (automatic, shallow) and System 2 (deliberate, deep) thinking. Digital minimalism aims to reduce System 1-driven distraction and create space for the deep, focused cognition that Newport calls "deep work."
