# Property Tax Transfer Rules

Rules governing how property tax basis (base year value) is transferred upon property change of ownership, particularly in California under Proposition 13 and Proposition 19.

## Proposition 13 Foundation (1978)
Established California's 1% property tax rate with a 2% annual increase cap. Protected long-term homeowners from dramatic tax increases by preserving base year value on transfers between parent-child, disability transfers, or inherited property.

## Proposition 19 Changes (2021)
Modified transfer protections:
- **Full protection** limited to primary residences, family farms ≤$1M, and disabled child transfers
- **Reassessment at market value** for most other transfers
- **Senior transfers** expanded to same-county moves with no transfer limit
- **Wildfire victim** protections for replacement properties within 2 years

## Base Year Value vs. Market Value
Base year value is the original assessed value determining ongoing property taxes. Under Prop 13, this was preserved on transfers. Under Prop 19, most transfers trigger reassessment at current market value, significantly increasing annual property taxes.

## Capital Gains Tradeoff
Lower property taxes on inherited property (due to reassessment) may come at the cost of higher capital gains taxes at eventual sale, since the reassessed value becomes the new cost basis for capital gains calculations.

## Strategic Considerations
- [[1031-exchange-strategy]] may defer both property tax and capital gains taxes
- Entity structure (trusts, LLCs) impacts transfer rules
- County-specific rules vary in implementation
- Timing and documentation are critical for eligibility
