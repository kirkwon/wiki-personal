# Ressentiment

Ressentiment is the French-German term Nietzsche uses for a specific form of resentment — a deep, enduring bitterness felt by the powerless toward the powerful. It is the psychological engine that drives slave morality.

## How It Works

- The powerless cannot directly challenge the strong, so they invert values: what the powerful value (strength, pride, wealth) is redefined as evil, while what the powerless possess (humility, patience, poverty) is redefined as virtuous.
- This inversion is not a conscious strategy but an unconscious psychological mechanism — the weak cannot admit their weakness, so they make weakness itself a moral good.
- Christianity, in Nietzsche's analysis, is the ultimate expression of ressentiment — transforming the suffering of the oppressed into a moral victory.

## Why It Matters

Ressentiment explains how moral systems can arise from psychological need rather than rational assessment. It connects to [[genealogical-analysis-of-morality]] as the method for uncovering these origins, and to [[master-vs-slave-morality]] as the framework it produces.
