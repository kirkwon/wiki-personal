# Capital Gains Distributions

Capital gains distributions occur when an investment fund realizes net capital gains and distributes them to shareholders. In taxable accounts, these distributions are taxed as capital gains even for shareholders who didn't sell any shares.

## How They Work

When a mutual fund sells securities at a profit, the fund realizes capital gains. By law, funds must distribute net capital gains to shareholders at least annually. This means all shareholders—regardless of how long they've held the fund—are taxed on gains they didn't personally realize.

## Why ETFs Distribute Less

ETFs use the in-kind creation/redemption mechanism. Authorized participants handle share flow by exchanging baskets of securities rather than cash, so the fund itself rarely sells holdings. This dramatically reduces the frequency and size of capital gains distributions.

## Typical Distribution Rates (2023 Vanguard Data)
- VTSAX (Index Mutual Fund): ~0.18% of NAV
- VTI (Index ETF): 0%
- VFIAX (Index Mutual Fund): ~0.05% of NAV
- VOO (Index ETF): 0%

## Timing and Impact

Capital gains distributions often occur in December, creating year-end tax surprises. Even long-term holders who never sold are taxed, and the distribution reduces the fund's NAV by the distribution amount.
