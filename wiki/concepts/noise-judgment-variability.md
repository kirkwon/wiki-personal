# Noise (Judgment Variability)

Noise refers to the irreducible variability in human judgment and decision-making, even among experts assessing the same situation. It is distinct from bias—while bias is a systematic deviation in one direction, noise is random dispersion around a mean.

## Kahneman's Findings

Daniel Kahneman, in *Noise: A Flaw in Human Judgment*, demonstrates that expert assessments contain significant variability. Different professionals evaluating the same case reach different conclusions not because of bias but because of noise—random fluctuations in how evidence is weighted, interpreted, and integrated.

## Key Characteristics

- **Irreducible**: Unlike bias, some degree of noise cannot be trained away or standardized out.
- **Quantifiable**: Noise can be measured by comparing judgments across individuals or repeated assessments.
- **Distinct from Bias**: A system can be unbiased on average while still being noisy.

## Implications

Recognizing noise as a feature of judgment rather than a flaw changes how we approach decision-making. It suggests that improving decision quality requires both reducing bias and managing noise—through structured processes, calibration, and awareness of individual judgment variability.
