# Ethical Design and the Manipulation Question

The central ethical question in habit-forming design is whether products are empowering users or exploiting them. This tension runs through the entire Hook Model framework.

## The Core Tension

Habit-forming mechanisms can be used for both beneficial and harmful purposes. A meditation app that builds a daily mindfulness habit is ethically different from a social media platform that exploits loneliness to increase screen time. The same psychological principles — [[internal-triggers]], [[variable-reward-design]], [[user-investment-habit]] — can create either empowering routines or addictive loops.

## Designer Responsibility

Designers bear moral responsibility for the impact of the habits they create. This includes considering:
- Whether the product genuinely serves user needs
- Whether habit formation occurs transparently
- Whether engagement metrics are prioritized over user well-being
- Whether dark patterns are being used to manipulate behavior

## User Responsibility

Users also share responsibility for being aware of habit-forming mechanisms and making informed choices about their engagement. This connects to broader themes in the wiki around [[decision-quality-vs-outcome-quality]] — distinguishing between the quality of the decision process and the outcome.

## Connection to Broader Ethics

This theme overlaps with [[ethics-in-exploration-and-experimentation]] from Sönke Ahrens' work and the wiki's general emphasis on ethical decision-making. The manipulation question asks whether designers should optimize for engagement or for user welfare, echoing tensions in [[decision-making-frameworks]].
