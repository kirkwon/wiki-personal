# Affect Heuristic

The affect heuristic is the tendency to make decisions based on emotional response rather than objective analysis. A positive feeling toward something leads to favorable judgments of its other attributes, and vice versa.

## Definition

**Affect Heuristic**: Making decisions based on emotional response. If something "feels" good, people assume it is good, and vice versa.

## Example

- "I like this company, so it's a good investment" — the positive feeling toward the brand overrides analysis of fundamentals
- Choosing a product because it feels good rather than evaluating objectively
- "This food looks gross" leads to rejecting it without tasting

## Usefulness and Limitation

Useful for intuition in familiar domains; can lead to poor decisions in unfamiliar domains where emotional responses are unreliable. In unfamiliar domains, affect-driven decisions lack the calibration that comes from experience. When emotional responses are misleading, they can lead to poor decisions where actual quality or risk is not correlated with the felt response.
