# Freedom Number

The amount of money needed to achieve financial freedom, defined by Ramit Sethi using the 4% Rule: you need 25 times your annual expenses saved to withdraw 4% annually in retirement without running out of money.

## Calculation
- Track annual expenses honestly.
- Multiply by 25.
- The result is your Freedom Number.

## Examples
- Annual expenses: $60,000 → Freedom Number: $1.5 million
- Annual expenses: $50,000 → Freedom Number: $1.25 million
- Annual expenses: $80,000 → Freedom Number: $2 million

## Alternative Framing
Using 80% of current income as the retirement target:
- $100k income → need $80k/year → $2M Freedom Number

## Time Horizon
At a 7% return, saving $1,000/month reaches a $1.5M Freedom Number in approximately 26 years. The key variable is the savings rate and time horizon, not investment returns.

## Connection to the 4% Rule
The Freedom Number is directly derived from the 4% Rule, a widely used withdrawal rate in financial planning. This connects to [[retirement-planning]] and [[real-options-analysis]]'s emphasis on dynamic decision framing.
