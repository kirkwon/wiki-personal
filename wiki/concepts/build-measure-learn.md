# Build-Measure-Learn

The Build-Measure-Learn feedback loop is the central mechanism of the lean startup methodology. It describes a rapid cycle in which a team builds a Minimum Viable Product, measures customer response, and learns from the data to inform the next iteration.

The loop emphasizes speed: the faster a team can cycle through Build-Measure-Learn, the faster they learn what customers actually want. This connects to ideas of small batch production—doing work in small increments to shorten feedback cycles and reduce waste.

The "Measure" phase requires distinguishing between vanity metrics (which feel good but don't inform decisions) and actionable metrics (which reveal whether the team is making progress toward validated learning goals). Innovation accounting provides a framework for tracking meaningful progress.

When evidence accumulates that current assumptions are wrong, the team must decide whether to **pivot** (change direction) or **persevere** (stay the course). This decision framework prevents teams from either stubbornly clinging to failing strategies or pivoting chaotically without learning.

The Build-Measure-Learn loop shares philosophical roots with probabilistic decision-making approaches like those in [[thinking-in-bets---annie-duke]]—both treat decisions as hypotheses to be tested and updated based on evidence. It also parallels real-options thinking, where decisions are treated as flexible options rather than irreversible commitments.
