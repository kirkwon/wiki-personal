# Explanatory Reach

Explanatory reach is the ability of an explanation to account for phenomena beyond the specific cases from which it was originally derived. It is a central concept in David Deutsch's epistemology, introduced in *The Beginning of Infinity* as a criterion for evaluating the quality and universality of explanations.

## Definition
An explanation has reach when it can be applied to new domains, predict novel phenomena, or account for situations that were not part of its original evidence base. This distinguishes genuine explanatory knowledge from mere descriptions or pattern-matching.

## Key Properties
- **Universality**: Explanations with reach are not limited to particular contexts; they can be generalized.
- **Fertility**: They generate new predictions and open up new areas of inquiry.
- **Depth**: They explain not just what happens but why, at a fundamental level.

## Connection to Knowledge Growth
Deutsch argues that the growth of knowledge depends on finding explanations with high explanatory reach. Such explanations solve not only existing problems but also reveal new problems and new possibilities. This contrasts with inductivist approaches that generalize from observed cases without providing deep explanatory mechanisms.

## Related Concepts
- [[conjecture-and-criticism]]: Reach is evaluated through the process of proposing explanations and refuting errors.
- [[knowledge]]: Knowledge, in Deutsch's framework, is explanatory information with reach.
- [[principle-of-optimism]]: High-reach explanations expand the scope of solvable problems.
- [[problem-solving]]: Identifying knowledge gaps is the first step toward creating explanations with reach.

## Tension with Bayesian Thinking
The wiki's existing [[bayesian-thinking]] framework relies on updating probabilities from observed data. Deutsch's reach criterion suggests that some explanations are valuable precisely because they extend beyond observed data, which challenges purely data-driven epistemologies.
