# Revocable Living Trusts

A trust created during the settlor's lifetime that can be modified, amended, or terminated during the settlor's lifetime.

## Key Features

- **Revocability**: Settlor can change or terminate the trust
- **Living Trust**: Takes effect during settlor's lifetime
- **Probate Avoidance**: Assets in trust avoid probate at death
- **Privacy**: Trust documents generally private, unlike wills
- **Incapacity Planning**: Can include disability provisions

## Advantages and Disadvantages

### Advantages
- **Probate Avoidance**: Assets transfer quickly and privately; avoids probate costs and delays
- **Incapacity Planning**: Built-in disability provisions via successor trustee
- **Privacy**: Trust documents remain private; beneficiary information remains private
- **Flexibility**: Can be modified during lifetime
- **Cost Efficiency**: Avoids probate costs and delays
- **Asset Management**: Centralized management of assets
- **Privacy**: Beneficiary information remains private

### Disadvantages
- **No Asset Protection**: Assets not protected from creditors
- **Tax Inefficiency**: No income tax benefits
- **Administrative Burden**: Requires proper funding and maintenance
- **Cost**: Initial setup costs can be significant
- **Complexity**: More complex than simple will
- **Irreversible Changes**: Once irrevocable, cannot be changed

## Funding and Administration

### Funding Requirements
Complete funding is critical:
- Transfer all intended assets to trust
- Re-title real estate, financial accounts, and vehicles
- Update beneficiary designations for insurance and retirement accounts
- Transfer business interests (partnership, corporate interests)
- Continue funding as new assets are acquired

### Administrative Considerations
- Select qualified successor trustee
- Maintain investment strategy and policies
- Keep detailed accounting and documentation
- Communicate regularly with beneficiaries
- Plan for trustee succession and changes
