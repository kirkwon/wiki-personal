# Groundless Fears

Seneca addresses groundless fears in *Letters from a Stoic* Chapter 13, advising against fearing things that are unlikely or beyond one's control. This concept connects directly to the [[cognitive-biases-library]] and modern research on irrational anxiety.

## Key Ideas

- Many fears are based on assumptions that have no foundation in reality.
- Focusing on what is beyond control amplifies anxiety unnecessarily.
- Rational examination of feared outcomes often reveals they are improbable or manageable.
- Connects to [[dread-aversion]] and [[probability-and-judgment-under-uncertainty]].

## Practice

When a fear arises, Seneca recommends asking: Is this fear based on something I can control? Is the feared outcome likely? If not, release the anxiety and redirect attention to actionable concerns.
