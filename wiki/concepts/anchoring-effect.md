# Anchoring Bias

Anchoring bias is the tendency to rely too heavily on the first piece of information encountered (the "anchor") when making decisions. Subsequent judgments are distorted toward that initial reference point, even when the anchor is arbitrary.

## Examples
- A $100,000 starting salary negotiation makes $90,000 seem like a deal
- Initial price estimates influence final negotiated prices

## Mitigation
- Start negotiations with objective data, not arbitrary numbers
- Deliberately consider multiple reference points
