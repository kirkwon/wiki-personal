# Good Judgment Project

The Good Judgment Project was a large-scale research initiative that identified and studied "superforecasters" — individuals who consistently outperform average forecasters, domain experts, and prediction markets in accuracy. The project ran forecasting tournaments where participants made probabilistic predictions about geopolitical, economic, and social events.

The project's key finding was that superforecasters use specific, learnable techniques: probabilistic thinking, multiple hypotheses, Bayesian updating, and active avoidance of confirmation bias. These findings are documented in *Superforecasting* by Philip E. Tetlock and Dan Gardner.

## Relationship to Prediction Markets

The Good Judgment Project demonstrated that individual superforecasters can rival or exceed the accuracy of prediction markets, which aggregate diverse opinions. This connects to [[prediction-markets]] as discussed in the book's fifth chapter on alternative forecasting methods.
