# Knowledge Representation

## Definition
Knowledge representation (KR) is the field of artificial intelligence dedicated to representing information about the world in a form that a computer system can utilize to solve complex tasks such as diagnosing a medical condition or having a dialog in natural language.

## Historical Context
- **1950s-60s**: Early AI used symbolic logic and rules (e.g., Logic Theorist, General Problem Solver).
- **1970s**: Development of semantic nets, frames, and scripts (Minsky's frames, Schank's scripts).
- **1980s**: Rise of rule-based systems and expert systems (MYCIN, DENDRAL); introduction of description logics.
- **1990s**: Growth of ontologies and the Semantic Web (RDF, OWL, DAML+OIL).
- **2000s**: Probabilistic graphical models (Bayesian networks, Markov logic networks) combined with KR.
- **2010s-Present**: Neural-symbolic integration, knowledge graphs (Google Knowledge Graph, Wikidata), and large language models as implicit knowledge bases.

## KR Techniques
1. **Symbolic Logic** – propositional and first-order logic, resolution, theorem proving.
2. **Semantic Networks** – nodes and labeled edges representing concepts and relations.
3. **Frames** – structured records with slots and fillers for stereotypical situations.
4. **Scripts** – sequences of events for typical activities (e.g., restaurant script).
5. **Rule-Based Systems** – IF-THEN rules with forward/backward chaining.
6. **Ontologies** – formal vocabularies (RDFS, OWL) defining classes, properties, individuals.
7. **Description Logics** – decidable fragments of first-order logic for ontology reasoning.
8. **Probabilistic KR** – Bayesian networks, Markov logic networks, probabilistic soft logic.
9. **Knowledge Graphs** – large-scale networks of entities and relations (e.g., Wikidata, DBpedia).
10. **Embedding-Based KR** – TransE, DistMult, ComplEx, RotatE for learning vector representations of entities/relations.

## Applications
- Expert systems (medical diagnosis, financial advice).
- Natural language understanding (semantic parsing, question answering).
- Semantic web and linked data.
- Robotics and planning (situation calculus, action languages).
- Computer vision (scene graphs, visual question answering).
- Bioinformatics (pathway ontologies, gene ontologies).
- Enterprise information integration.
- AI safety and interpretability (causal models, argumentation frameworks).
