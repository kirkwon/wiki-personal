# Sources of Power: How People Make Decisions - Gary Klein

_by Gary Klein_

## Chapters

### Chapter 1: Chronicling the Activities of Fire Ground Commanders
Klein describes his study of fireground commanders making life-or-death decisions under extreme time pressure. He observes that experienced commanders rarely compare options but rely on recognition of familiar patterns from past incidents.

### Chapter 2: The Recognition-Primed Decision (RPD) Model
This chapter introduces the core model: experts recognize a situation as typical, retrieve a typical response from memory, and quickly evaluate it via mental simulation. The RPD model contrasts sharply with rational choice theory by emphasizing pattern matching over option comparison.

### Chapter 3: The Power of Intuition
Intuition is shown to be based on vast experience stored as patterns in memory, not mysticism. Klein argues that what we call intuition is rapid, unconscious pattern recognition honed over years of practice.

### Chapter 4: The Power of Mental Simulation
Experts use mental simulations to "run" a course of action forward in time to check for flaws before committing. This chapter details how simulation helps evaluate options quickly without real-world consequences and how it can be trained.

### Chapter 5: The Power of Metaphor and Analogy
Decision makers often compare novel situations to familiar experiences using analogies. This cognitive shortcut helps transfer expertise across domains and allows people to leverage past lessons in new settings.

### Chapter 6: The Power of Storytelling
Stories are a natural way to convey expertise and lessons learned. Klein shows how narrative not only communicates decisions but also helps build shared mental models in teams and preserves knowledge in organizations.

### Chapter 7: The Power of Conscious Thinking
While intuition drives many decisions, conscious reasoning still plays a role in unfamiliar situations, critique, and justification. The chapter discusses balancing intuitive and analytical thinking and when each is most appropriate.

### Chapter 8: The Power of Experience
Experience builds the rich repertoire of patterns that experts use. Klein explores how experience is developed through deliberate practice and why it is irreplaceable by rules, checklists, or algorithms alone.

### Chapter 9: Naturalistic Decision Making in the Wild
The NDM framework is applied to domains beyond firefighting: nursing, chess, military command, and business. Each case reinforces the RPD model's generality and demonstrates the universal nature of expert decision making.

### Chapter 10: A New View of Decision Making
This chapter contrasts NDM with the classical "decision tree" model, arguing that real-world decisions are not about picking among options but about sizing up situations and recognizing the appropriate course of action.

### Chapter 11: Implications for Training
Training should focus on building experience through simulations, case studies, and feedback rather than abstract principles. Klein recommends "learning by doing" with intentional practice and exposure to a wide variety of scenarios.

### Chapter 12: Implications for Technology Design
Decision support systems should augment natural patterns, not force rationalistic procedures. Design principles include providing rich data, supporting mental simulation, and adapting the interface to the user's expertise level.

### Chapter 13: Implications for Organizations
Organizations should create cultures that value expertise, allow for storytelling, and minimize unnecessary procedural constraints. Leaders should trust the intuition of experienced practitioners and avoid over-reliance on formal decision-making models.

### Chapter 14: Final Reflections
Klein summarizes the power of the human mind in decision making and urges respect for the expertise developed over years of practice. He calls for research and design that align with how people actually make decisions rather than forcing artificial rationality.
