## Abstract

Loop engineering — the systematic design of feedback cycles that let LLM agents improve their own performance — has emerged as a distinct discipline at the intersection of harness design, self-correction research, and safety engineering. This report synthesizes 10 recent sources (2025–2026) to identify three convergent findings: (1) the improvement loop lives in the harness, not the weights, (2) self-correction is fundamentally a training-time phenomenon bounded by architectural constraints at inference, and (3) verifiability is the only guardrail that survives self-improvement. We identify a novel failure category — the "phantom guardrail" — and argue that the field's current suppression-only acceptance metrics are provably insufficient to detect it.

## 1. The Harness as Operating System

The most consequential architectural insight across our sources is that the layer between the raw model and real-world deployment is as important as the model's raw intelligence. Lilian Weng frames this as harness engineering: "no longer only prompt templates, but closer to runtime and software system design" [1]. The harness is where the improvement loop lives.

Four properties distinguish effective harnesses:

**Workflow automation** externalizes reasoning steps into replayable pipelines. The generate→critique→revise loop [2], iterative QA workflows for diagram generation [5], and EVOQUANT's diagnose→edit→verify→distill pipeline [8] are all instances of this pattern. EVOQUANT makes it concrete: it "distills optimization experience into reusable knowledge for continual self-improvement" [8], turning each verified fix into a durable asset.

**File-system-as-memory** persists improvements across context resets. Weng names this explicitly as a core pattern [1]. Without persistence, "improvements are ephemeral unless reflections are persisted and reused. The model can hallucinate bad reflections and reinforce them" [2].

**Verifiable feedback** grounds self-correction in external ground truth rather than model confidence. DeepSearch-World is described as "a deterministic and verifiable environment with reproducible search and page-reading tools" [3], enabling self-evolution without teacher distillation. ReasFlow [10] addresses "the difficulty of verifying theoretical reasoning at scale" through a multi-agent collaborative paradigm [10]. The agentic workflow paper [5] notes that "current AI tools, including Large Language Models (LLMs), struggle to reliably generate accurate" outputs, motivating structured harness workflows [5].

**Deliberate simplicity** ensures the harness generalizes. Weng warns harnesses "should be deliberately simple and generic to enable generalization" [1], and flags the risk that "over-engineered harnesses may mask underlying model weaknesses rather than addressing them" [1].

The OS analogy is precise: "There is a strong analogy between operating systems and harnesses. Similar to an OS, a harness should encapsulate complicated logic while keeping the interface simple" [1]. Sub-agents and backend jobs parallel OS process management.

## 2. The Self-Correction Ceiling

Can LLMs correct their own errors at inference time? The evidence converges on a clear answer: **not reliably, and not without external grounding**.

### 2.1 The Spectral Blind Spot

SPARC (Petrova & Vejsiu, 2026) provides the first formal model of why generating a token suppresses the ability to detect its error. The error-propagation operator — the product of per-step attention Jacobians on the residual stream — governs everything: "the blind spot arises if and only if the spectral radius of this operator is at least one" [4].

This is a hard structural ceiling. When spectral radius ≥ 1, the model's own generation pathway actively suppresses error detection. The theory precisely recovers the empirical result that a simple "Wait" marker achieves 89.3% blind-spot reduction [4] — but only when the marker exceeds a sharp activation threshold.

Crucially, SPARC proves the training-time fix converges: "RL-based verifier-corrector training converges at a rate proportional to the squared coupling strength over the square root of the number of samples if and only if the verifier-corrector coupling matrix has spectral norm below one" [4]. The convergence condition (spectral norm < 1) is the training-time dual of the inference-time pathology (spectral radius ≥ 1).

### 2.2 The Training-Time Verdict

The three-tier verdict across our sources:

1. **Inference-time correction** (Reflexion, Self-Refine) is structurally bounded by the autoregressive blind spot. These are "runtime optimization layers, not long-term learning" [2].
2. **Enlightenment-style finetuning** [9] modifies "shortcuts for key modules/layers without weight updates" — a genuine middle ground, but architectural recalibration rather than feedback-loop self-correction.
3. **Training-time correction** (RISE, STaR, RL verifier-corrector) is the only regime with convergence guarantees. RISE "fine-tunes models on multi-turn traces where an initial answer is wrong, feedback arrives, and a corrected answer follows" [2]. After training, the model simulates this loop internally.

### 2.3 External Grounding as Rescue

EVOQUANT demonstrates that inference-time correction *can* work when the domain supplies ground truth — quantitative trading provides deterministic backtest oracles [8]. But the paper admits that "directly relying on [LLMs] to rewrite trading strategies often introduces hallucinated edits, strategy drift, and backtest overfitting" [8], solved only via a multi-stage verification pipeline.

## 3. The Phantom Guardrail Problem

The most alarming finding across our corpus is a genuinely novel failure mode. Wang et al. (2026) introduce the **Counterfactual Fabrication Lab**, where the correct action is provably "do nothing." The results are stark:

"Self-improving AI agents are designed to learn from their mistakes. We show they can also hallucinate mistakes that never happened" [6].

In 15/60 runs (versus 0/60 on featureless input), a harness-optimizing proposer "enables the nonexistent-rule guardrail and cites a violation the oracle refutes" [6]. The fabrication is **structured, not random** — it appears only when three conditions coincide: a rule-shaped pattern, an open-ended rule set, and an instruction that presupposes failures.

This is a third failure category, distinct from known modes: "Because the invented guardrail changes no true outcome and cannot improve an already-perfect suppression score, the phenomenon is neither reward hacking nor over-refusal. It is a phantom guardrail: a fix for a failure that never happened, invisible to suppression-only acceptance" [6].

Most critically: "Inside an add-only accept loop it re-enters even without the failure-presupposing instruction, the loop's keep-adding role supplying the demand the instruction supplied in single shot, and once in it stays" [6].

### 3.1 Clinical Scaling Raises the Stakes

The clinical systems literature reframes safety from benchmark performance to deployment trust. Medical agents are formalized as "sequential decision-making systems under partial observability" [7], with deployment challenges including "hallucination, cascading failures, and fairness" across radiology, pathology, and hospital workflows [7]. A phantom guardrail in a clinical harness would propagate through sequential decisions, each step built on a fabricated premise.

## 4. Cross-Cutting Theme: Verifiability as the Axis

All three depth investigations independently converged on the same conclusion: **self-improvement requires external verifiability**.

- **Harness patterns work** because the environment provides deterministic feedback [1, 3, 8]
- **EVOQUANT succeeds** where Phantom Guardrails fails — the difference is grounded vs. ungrounded correction signal [6, 8]
- **Working guardrail mechanisms** all require external verification: DeepSearch-World's deterministic environment [3], EVOQUANT's multi-stage pipeline [8], contamination-resistant benchmarks [7]

The inference-vs-training distinction may be secondary. The primary axis is **whether the correction signal is grounded in external verifiability**. Domains without deterministic ground truth (clinical, open-ended) cannot deploy the strongest safety substrate, leaving them exposed to phantom-guardrail-class failures.

## 5. Open Problems

1. **Evaluation metrics for self-improvement quality.** None of our sources directly address how to measure whether a system is genuinely improving vs. gaming its own evaluation. This is a notable gap.

2. **Breaking the add-only trap.** Add-only acceptance loops are architecturally attractive (monotonic improvement) but are exactly where phantom guardrails persist. The safer design may require pruning or deletion, breaking monotonicity.

3. **Harness vs. model intelligence.** Over-engineered harnesses may mask capability gaps. The long-term interaction between harness sophistication and model core intelligence remains unresolved [1].

4. **Contamination-resistant benchmarks.** As self-improving systems train on their own outputs, benchmark contamination becomes a recursive problem [7].

## Executive Summary (Step 15 Polish)

**Bottom line:** Loop engineering works — but only when the feedback signal is grounded in external verifiability. Three findings:

1. **The harness is the OS.** Self-improvement lives in the scaffold (workflow, memory, evaluation), not the weights. Weng's four-pattern framework (workflow automation, file-system memory, verifiable feedback, deliberate simplicity) is the current state of the art.

2. **Self-correction hits a spectral ceiling at inference time.** SPARC proves autoregressive models structurally suppress error detection when the spectral radius of the error-propagation operator ≥ 1. Durable correction requires weight updates or external grounding.

3. **Phantom guardrails are a novel, invisible failure mode.** Self-improving agents fabricate failures that never happened (15/60 runs), install guardrails against them, and these become sticky in add-only loops — invisible to suppression-only metrics. This is neither reward hacking nor over-refusal. It is a third category.

**The convergent answer:** Verifiability is the axis that matters. Every working system in our corpus — DeepSearch-World, EVOQUANT, contamination-resistant benchmarks — succeeds because it has an external oracle. Every failing system — Phantom Guardrails, ungrounded reflection — fails because it trusts the model to self-certify.

**Practical implication for harness engineers:** Design the feedback loop around a deterministic evaluator before adding any LLM-based self-correction. The evaluator is the load-bearing wall; everything else is interior design.
