## Overview
This wiki serves as a personal knowledge base covering cooking, photography, personal finance, and general second-brain topics for lifelong learning and reference. The cooking section spans coffee brewing methods, rice varieties, seasonings, regional cuisines including Chinese and Italian, and fundamental techniques like braising and wok cooking. Photography covers fundamentals, composition, lighting, and post-processing. Personal finance addresses investing, retirement planning, tax strategies, trusts, and real estate.

Decision-making is a throughline across the wiki, integrating cognitive science, behavioral economics, and practical frameworks. Key concepts include prospect theory, dual process theory, Bayesian thinking, cognitive biases, and decision quality versus outcome quality. Recent sources include Annie Duke's *Thinking in Bets*, which introduces a betting framework for decision-making under uncertainty, connecting to expected value, bet sizing, strategic bluffing, and decision tracking. Grit, framed as a universal mental model, adds a lens for understanding sustained effort and passion as tools for navigating complex situations, drawing on Angela Duckworth's research with moderate confidence.

The jazz and music theory section has expanded with bebop — a revolutionary jazz style of the 1940s–1950s characterized by fast tempos, complex harmonies, and chord-based improvisation. Pioneers including Charlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, Thelonious Monk, and Bud Powell are documented, alongside the classic quintet lineup. Bebop connects to jazz harmony, music theory fundamentals, and provides the stylistic context for Miles Davis's emergence as a major figure.

Cantonese cuisine has been added as a comprehensive concept, emphasizing the philosophy of ingredient freshness, the five cooking principles, and core techniques such as steaming and wok cooking. Classic dishes including dim sum, roast duck, char siu, and steamed fish are documented, along with the unique soup culture and yum cha tea traditions. This complements the existing Sichuan cuisine coverage, highlighting the contrast between Cantonese mild-fresh philosophy and Sichuan's numbing-spicy character.

Planning, Stillness, Settings, Environment, and Grit have been added as mental models. Planning is defined as a universal conceptual tool for navigating complex situations through pattern and relationship recognition. Stillness, introduced through Ryan Holiday's *Stillness Is the Key*, functions as a navigational principle applicable across all domains. Settings connects to existing mental models like planning, solutions, group, and stillness. Environment provides a framework for understanding how surroundings and context shape performance and outcomes. Grit functions as a conceptual tool for making sense of patterns and relationships in complex situations.
